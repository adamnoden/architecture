# Boundary Service Lodge and accessible site-service raceway

**Owner direction:** 31 October 2026. **Name adopted:** **Boundary Service Lodge**. **Architecture owner:** [HAD](house-architectural-design-programme.md) with [PAT-001 — Controlled Utility Entry](../../docs/patterns/controlled-utility-entry.md) as the transferable pattern. **Status:** owner-house design brief and candidate comparison; neither a selected location/installation nor permission from any undertaker.

## Organising proposition

**Converge from the designed house outward toward the real site.** The permanent inhabited house should be deliberately stable, internally maintainable and serviceable; its interfaces with shifting soil, tree roots, outside weather and public utilities should be intentionally designed transition systems, rather than ad hoc penetrations forcing compromises back into permanent fabric.

This is a design order, **not** permission to ignore geology, existing trees, easements, neighbour rights, sewer invert or provider specifications. Site facts determine how the interfaces must be engineered and what remains feasible. The building does not assume the street networks converge at a single boundary point.

### Preferred, conditional two-stage topology

```text
PUBLIC NETWORKS / STREET
        │  separate statutory/operator handoffs as actually required
        ▼
BOUNDARY SERVICE LODGE  ── separate foul / site drainage inspection and outfalls
  owner-operable isolation + appropriate meters / indications
        │
        ▼
ACCESSIBLE EXTERNAL SERVICE RACEWAY
  protected, segregated, removable covers; soil-motion accommodation
        │
        ▼
CONTROLLED HOUSE ENTRY / INTERNAL SERVICE HUB
  independent house-side isolation, inspection and distribution
```

Do not imply all utility companies have the same entry location, ownership boundary or approved enclosure. It may be **one architectural volume with independently protected provider-compatible compartments**, several adjacent small enclosures, or a more modest cluster of boxes: evaluate, do not force centralisation. Existing PAT-001 and RH-06/RH-08 controlled service geography stay authoritative until a formal source-owned change.

## Boundary Service Lodge — architectural ambition

A **small, beautiful, secure brick-and-mortar utility outbuilding or very substantial service cabinet** just inside the site boundary near the provider entry points. Think of it as a miniature architectural service lodge rather than an enlarged plastic meter box. It must be composed in the same restrained Georgian-derived language as the house, with genuine masonry, sound joints, durable doors/grilles, restrained fittings and appropriate patina. **No flat roof:** assess a simple pitched slate/metal roof with honest eaves and controlled water shedding. A secure brick shell should not force more concrete, foundation excavation or roof complexity than its function earns.

Its size is determined by **real person-and-tool access**, meter service/withdrawal clearances, ventilation, access rights and isolation operation — not by an arbitrary 'shed-size' label. Compare:

1. Compact front-access architectural cabinet with outward-opening secure doors and safe working reach;
2. Small walk-up masonry lodge, where equipment and maintenance genuinely warrant it;
3. A short row of separate, coordinated masonry compartments if providers disallow common housing.

The site-side placement must preserve the approach, garden composition, tree/root protection, visibility and security. Keep public-facing details harmonious and discreet, but do not conceal emergency isolation under decorative ironwork.

### Service-by-service function schedule

| Service | At or adjacent to lodge | Critical boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Potable water | Owner-operable upstream shut-off for the private run; optional pressure gauge, flow/leak indication and independently maintainable filtration *only if it earns its burden* | Supplier may retain its own external stop tap and meter at the highway/property boundary; do not assume the provider-owned meter can move into the lodge |
| Electricity | A provider-approved intake/meter compartment **if permitted**, with a clearly identified customer-side isolator and useful load/energy indication | The customer's switch generally **cannot** make the incoming DNO cable or provider apparatus dead; operator-only work and emergency actions remain distinct |
| Fibre / communications | Suitable network demarcation, protective ducts, labelled patch/termination, perhaps passive status labels | Keep active kit out of a damp/flood-prone box unless properly powered/cooled/secured |
| Gas (optional, preferably omitted) | A separately approved, externally ventilated metering / emergency-control arrangement if the house eventually uses gas | Never put gas and electrical equipment in an unsegregated common closed volume; appropriate operator-access and gas-safety requirements prevail |
| Foul drainage | A **separate** accessible external inspection/rodding point and correctly supported sealed drain | Not a valve or unsealed foul chamber in the dry electrical lodge; gravity fall and actual sewer invert still govern |
| Roof/court/surface water | Visible, separately inspectable collection/outfall provisions when useful nearby | Do not connect an open roof/court drain to a dry service chamber or make the lodge the lowest unpumpable sump |

Retain **independent isolation and diagnosis within the house** at its service hub. These are two deliberate stages, not guaranteed two independent sources or an all-services master switch. Avoid duplicating meters, actuators or monitoring devices that offer no credible maintenance benefit.

### Light, indications, information and safe use

- Protected **built-in task lighting** for hands-on work; an ordinary reliable fixed fitting and sensibly positioned manual switch are the baseline. An emergency light is a candidate if it earns a genuine outage/repair benefit, not mandatory gadgetry.
- Durable **physical service index**: which valve/switch controls which run, network vs owner ownership, whether a conductor remains live, how to isolate, and the route towards the house. Labels must be legible in daylight and darkness and survive software obsolescence.
- **Simple, long-life indications where useful**: potable pressure, totalised flow/leak pattern, electric energy/load via approved equipment. Compare passive analogue gauges with replaceable digital displays; no promise of 'one gauge per service' where the measurement is meaningless.
- Secure, robust, safe doors/covers, anti-slip approach, appropriate drainage/ventilation/earthing, frost/flood/heat/pest resistance, key access for authorised workers and escape/working access as applicable.
- Fail safely without connectivity or automation. Prevent a burst pipe or foul leak from damaging incoming electrics. Keep wet and electrical service compartments appropriately separated.

## External raceway — below grade need not mean buried in soil

**Preferred investigation:** a continuous **inspectable external service corridor** through private land, with durable modular/precast or masonry trough sections and independently removable **cast-iron or steel covers**. Compare heavy decorative open grates with weather-protective solid covers. The owner values the form and craft of real iron grates; they should be used where the physical duty allows, not as decoration over an unmaintainable trench.

| Option | What it earns | What it must defend |
|---|---|---|
| Directly buried ducts/pipes with discrete inspection chambers | Conventional benchmark; small visible intrusion | Future digging, hidden connections, root interference and changing soil |
| Open-grated trench | Excellent visible access and potential deliberate drainage | Admits cold air, runoff, leaves, grit and contaminated surface water; cannot be assumed dry |
| Solid removable covered raceway | Better weather/frost/security protection; still accessible | Lid seals, condensation, ventilation, flooding, lifted-cover weight and retained access |
| Separate dry electrical/telecom raceway and wet/water/drain route | Clears many incompatible mixing risks | More space, trenching, root impact, crossings and cost |
| Segmented, articulated troughs with deliberate joints | Can tolerate site movement better than one rigid concrete trench | Joint soil/water ingress, uneven covers, actual service support and inspection |

**Gravel is not the default fill.** Covering fittings and joints with gravel destroys the access advantage; gravel cannot serve as a proven gravity outfall and may become a silt trap. If a granular filter layer has a genuine hydraulic duty, put it in an independently cleanable drainage subassembly, not as an unquestioned substitute for a cover or water escape.

The trench must not become a **below-grade bathtub**. Prove groundwater/flood/blocked-outfall behaviour, passive overflow and access after wetting, especially where a separate dry compartment contains electric service. Choose solid liftable covers where frost, debris and rain exposure demand them; grates where the route is **intended** to receive and discharge water. A watertight trough is not a guarantee of dryness when receiving levels and cover joints are unresolved.

### Ground movement, trees and services

An external trench is soil-supported unless specifically engineered otherwise. It will move **differently** from the pile-supported house, road and lodge. Detail relative motion at each endpoint and at segmented trough joints; distinguish intended soil movement from tolerable pipe/cable joint translation and rotation. Keep pipe supports and clamps renewable and capable of accommodation. Cross the house's permanent foundation at an **authorised opening** with the correct gas/water/air/thermal/fire boundaries; do not chase a beam or cast pipes rigidly into it.

A long excavation can injure mature tree roots; root-preserving alignment, local bridging, interrupted trenches or limited trenchless sections must be compared, not presumed. The utility corridor may need to bypass important trees or be broken into appropriately accessible sections.

Water service depth, frost protection and supplier-approved ducting remain live: an open air-filled trench at 750 mm depth is **not** thermally equivalent to a pipe buried under 750 mm of soil. Electricity ducts/covers and provider access are separately controlled. For foul drainage, access does **not** remove required gravity gradient to the real sewer; keep separate sealed pipes, inspection and usable movement fittings.

## Primary evidence and restrictions worth preserving

- **Water Regs UK, [concealed vs accessible](https://www.waterregsuk.co.uk/topics/all-faqs/what-is-meant-by-concealed-and-accessible/):** explicitly distinguishes below-ground-but-unburied accessible chambers from pipework concealed in fabric; water undertaker agreement still controls shared ducts and fitting protection.
- **Water Regs UK, [entry into buildings](https://www.waterregsuk.co.uk/topics/all-faqs/how-should-pipework-access-a-building/):** typical 750 mm entry and insulation/duct/sealing duties, especially under ventilated floors.
- **UK Power Networks, [EDS 08-2101 LV Customer Supplies Up To 100A](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/media/jyrlrrir/eds-08-2101-lv-customer-supplies-up-to-100a_08-12-25.pdf), v14 (8 December 2025), §5.8.2:** **brick-built freestanding kiosk** is a described alternative to approved GRP kiosks for an individual dedicated supply, subject to EDS 08-2110 drawings; an electrical meter cabinet has a restricted equipment list. This is **not approval** to co-house gas/water/foul systems with DNO equipment.
- **Thames Water, [leakage responsibility](https://www.thameswater.co.uk/media-library/home/about-us/governance/our-policies/codes-of-practice/leakage-code-of-practice.pdf):** company-owned communication pipe/meter/outer stop tap and privately owned supply pipe have distinct locations and legal rights. Meter location alone does not determine ownership.
- **Forest Research, [tree roots and trenching](https://www.forestresearch.gov.uk/tools-and-resources/fthr/urban-regeneration-and-greenspace-partnership/practical-considerations-and-challenges-to-greenspace/tree-roots-and-trenching/):** preserve root systems, avoid unnecessary continuous trenches and use trenchless techniques where useful.
- **DPL-08C, [foul/site-water handoffs](../dpl/dpl-08c-foul-surface-site-water-interface.md):** existing Reference House assumes below-ground foul exit in service-side foundation area and a short court-drain crossing. These are not demonstrated compatible with the P0 movement-isolated proposal.

## Work required before selection

1. **Site/ownership boundary diagram:** provider metering/isolators vs owner equipment, any supplier-required direct access; distinguish services not arriving from the same street point.
2. **Three built typology comparisons:** simple approved meter cabinet vs masonry lodge vs small multi-compartment pavilion, with sensible maintenance envelopes, appearance, roof drainage, fire/gas/ventilation/privacy/security costs and construction.
3. **Raceway plan, 1:10/1:20 sections and two failure-state drawings:** open grate vs solid cover; frozen water, blocked outlet, flooding, tree roots, moving clay, corrosive media, inaccessible lifting and loss of lighting.
4. **Connected boundary-to-house proof:** complete water/duct/service support and movement geometry, foul gravity/invert, actual pipe replacement route, electrical separation, optional gas ventilation and no illicit structural piercing.
5. **Material and inspection schedule:** brick/stone, mortar, metal grates/covers, safe lifting mass and hardware, fittings/joints/liners/insulation/coatings and all residual polymers; avoid nominally 'plastic-free' claims from metal external appearance.
6. **Proportionality and maintenance review:** first-cost, civil disturbance, embodied material, operating burden and what actually becomes inspectable/replaceable. If a protected cabinet is the better answer, adopt it rather than inflating the lodge for romance.

No fixed lodge footprint, load class, cover thickness, pile type, service alignment, upstream meter position, lighting product or utility approval is selected here. Do not turn an appealing architectural image into an unevidenced complete installation.
