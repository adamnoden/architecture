# The table, chair and the unproved 100 mm insertion

**31 October 2026 · bounded HAD-03 continuation · no furniture selection.** Starting source main: 1486df666d2c6758ccc0e93212f9d5290f7da7dd. This continues the [dining-chair and garden-opening comparison](had-03-dining-garden.md). It tests the product evidence behind one positioning assumption, without changing the room, the previous drawing or the household brief. HAD-03 remains planned.

## Result

**The authored 100 mm chair insertion remains unverified.** One real table supplies useful underside and support-gap values, but those values do not describe a continuous three-dimensional opening into which the chair and a diner can move.

The current four-side-place comparison uses two 490 mm chair envelopes at 600 mm centre spacing on each long side. Their outer span is 1090 mm. Against the recovered table's 1120 mm leg-gap scalar, that leaves only a **conditional 30 mm total**. This is a reason to obtain the actual support and chair sections before inheriting the positioning assumption. It is not a fit result, a tolerance allowance or a seating-capacity rating.

Keep the earlier room comparison conditional. A real table's smaller overall footprint does not automatically validate its chair entry, occupied posture, withdrawal or extra end seats.

The existing table is an authored 1600 × 800 × 750 trial. The 7382 is 80 shorter, 40 narrower and 20 lower in the corresponding overall dimensions. It is a different object; neither its top outline nor its supports can be substituted silently into the existing calculation. The manufacturer warns that dimensions can vary slightly, without supplying tolerances for this fit comparison.

The table page describes tapered, outward-facing legs. A gap reported at one height cannot establish the gap at another. No absence of an apron, unobstructed insertion strip or 20 mm tabletop thickness is inferred from the two vertical values.

## What the width arithmetic can test

For equal 490 mm-wide chair rectangles with centre spacing P, the total outer span of n aligned chairs is 490 + (n − 1)P. This uses the same overall-envelope screen as the predecessor; it does not model the chair's actual solids at entry height.

The following is a **conditional scalar screen only**: suppose that 1120 were a continuous clear interval in the relevant direction and at the heights where those chair envelopes enter. That premise is not established by the product text.

| Authored row comparison | Outer envelope span | 1120 minus that span |
|---|---:|---:|
| Two chairs at the four-side trial's 600 centre spacing | 1090 | 30 |
| Two chairs at the earlier six-station trial's 800 centre spacing | 1290 | −170 |
| Three non-overlapping 490-wide rectangles, touching with no gaps | 1470 | −350 |
| Three chairs at 600 centre spacing | 1690 | −570 |

For the first row, centring within the assumed interval would leave 15 at each end. The 110 between the two chair rectangles is not a shoulder or elbow allowance. None of these remainders includes manufacturing variation, placement error, the full splayed supports or occupied movement.

A negative remainder rejects containment of those rectangles within that assumed interval. It does not prove a collision between the actual three-dimensional objects, prohibit every six-seat arrangement or rate the table for a particular number of diners. Chairs at the table ends or in different positions require their own actual support and occupied-use test.

## The vertical subtraction does not establish insertion

On a common floor datum, the reported 710 under-clearance value minus the reported 450 seat height gives **260 mm**. This is a difference between published heights, not measured space above a diner's thighs or knees. The corresponding table-top-to-seat difference is 730 − 450 = 280; neither quantity is an ergonomic acceptance criterion.

The predecessor's 100 mm insertion places part of a 600-deep overall chair rectangle beneath a table outline. The real question is whether the actual leading chair profile and seated user can occupy that strip throughout approach and withdrawal. The recovered values do not locate the table legs, underside fittings, chair seat edge, front legs, bow or the user's knees in that strip.

To validate the assumed insertion, the next fit record must show the actual table and chair sections at the intended stations and the complete movement between outside and inserted states. It must also state the occupied-use basis. A static height difference, a photograph or two compatible-looking product names cannot supply those missing coordinates.

## Regular and occasional seating remain different decisions

The predecessor's four-side-place trial is an unadopted arrangement, not an adopted daily household capacity. Required regular and guest seat counts remain unknown. Additional chairs being available or stored elsewhere does not demonstrate their use at the table.

The earlier added south-end chair entered the court-corner territory by 217.5 mm under that study's table position, 100 insertion and 400 straight translation. That finding belongs to the authored 800 × 1600 table and its stated action. It is retained, but is not transferred to the differently sized 7382. Its shorter top does not earn an occasional six-place result.

If occasional end seating is required, show the actual table/support pairing, end-chair entry, occupied state, withdrawal and any temporary furniture rearrangement together with the applicable room and opening operations. This continuation does not reduce the seat requirement to preserve the previous route or relax the route because the use is occasional.
