# HAD-03 — court walking level, lower water plane and threshold proposal

**Unadopted architectural proposal, not a water-boundary or discharge pass.**
The house needs an ordinary usable court and a credible route for water when its
primary outlet blocks. The existing front-threshold detail explicitly excludes
this enclosed court. Its +15/−60 geometry and open landing-edge spill are not
reused as evidence here. This proposal separates the walking surface from the
lower drained surface, then identifies the actual threshold and floor interfaces
that must be designed before RWT can admit the route. The [A3 plan and section crop](studies/court-levels-proposal.svg) separates retained source geometry from these proposed interfaces.

The [current first-failure and renewal gate](had-03-court-threshold-first-failure.md) tests the trial's actual sill, pan, jamb, fixing, floor and carrier interfaces. In particular it rejects the 5 mm nominal underside-to-water comparison as any kind of threshold pass. It retains the preferred step-free pan-led direction as unadopted pending a matched section and RWT hydraulic result.

## Located spatial choice

Retain the source 3500 square court, x 4250–7750/y 4250–7750, and the paired
north/south doors centred x 6000, nominal width 2400. Keep the principal through
route at the house's ground FFL, without introducing a 150 mm court step.
Compare removable, self-supporting pavers over a lower waterproofed, supported
surface. The walking level is an **authored 0 datum**; the substrate starts at
**−180** at its perimeter and falls toward a separate primary inlet. A 2% fall
over a 1750 half-width gives **35 mm** drop to −215. Those levels refer to the top of the waterproofing, not the structural substrate: membrane, protection and supporting construction below it still need actual allocated thickness. The final pan must have real
falls to its actual inlet, including corners; this one section does not prove
a four-way paving or drainage layout.

With a comparison 20 mm paver and 6 mm protective pad, the pedestal/support
territory is **154–189 mm**. This is within the dimensional range of a real
modular support family, not an invented solid platform. The documented PLV60 adjustment of 60–130 plus a PLA70 extension provides a 130–200 comparison range; the separate PLS6 protective pad is already counted above. [Schlüter TROBA-LEVEL manufacturer data sheet 7.6](https://eu.schluter.com/statics/www_schlueter_pl_pl_2x/downloads/cumulus/schlueter_db_7_6_troba_level_uk.pdf)
requires a load-bearing substrate, recommends 1.5–2% substrate fall, explains
open-joint drainage and access to the void, and provides modular extensions
above its basic 60–130 adjustment. Actual paver thickness, support spacing,
point loads, slip resistance, perimeter restraint and membrane protection
remain product/design duties. Raised walking grade must also reappraise the wall base, DPC, cavity closure and splash exposure; old lower-grade evidence cannot simply be inherited. The court's future maintenance scaffold cannot
inherit a pedestal-deck bearing pass.

Leave an independently liftable threshold strip across each door and access to
both inlets without disturbing the other. A blocked paver joint cannot be the
sole route from the walking surface: the threshold strips and separately
located relief opening must remain exposed to the surface flow. Raised paving
conceals debris and leaks unless panels can actually be lifted, handled, staged
and reinstated. The lifting tool, panel mass, stable standing area and opening
protection belong in that task; the word *removable* proves none of them.

## The water boundary is a construction, not the floor line

The candidate threshold is a drained renewable doorset supported independently
of its removable court strip. Develop a continuous sill pan with a rear upstand
and end dams, connected to the court waterproofing and wall-base boundary.
Frame fixing must not puncture an unprotected wet pan. Frame drainage discharges
outward into the independently exposed strip; a cosmetic seal must not block it.
The sill pan, wall-base transition, cavity closure and floor air/thermal boundary
must remain coordinated when the doorset is renewed.

The section crop marks a deliberately authored **140 deep × 60 high threshold reserve**, not the outline of a selected product. Drawn from +15, its bottom is **−45**, only **5** above the uncomputed −50 water target. That underside is an example level to investigate, not a verified vulnerable boundary or an adequate margin. Its pedestal blocks are dimensional support envelopes with the protective pad counted, not manufacturer component sections.

For an explicit next drawing, test a **+15 rear pan upstand** and **+75 end/wall
returns**, with the near-door walking surface 0. These are fresh project trial
levels, not adoption of the front detail and not a statutory access claim.
They do **not** establish that the lowest vulnerable interface is +15. A sill
pan seam, frame drain, low anchor, concealed cavity closure or threshold underside
can be vulnerable lower down. The next joint detail must list each one and its
actual level, material continuity, support and failure path. If the rear upstand
is crossed by a lower unprotected joint, that joint controls. No threshold body
is presumed to withstand immersion because it has a rain-test rating.

Use RWT's **−75 relief activation / −50 maximum operating-water target** only
as an explicit comparison. Neither is computed capacity. If a completed pan
really retained all vulnerable fabric above +15, the nominal vertical difference
to that target would be 65; if its first vulnerable joint were −60, the target
would already exceed it by 10. This sensitivity explains why an installed
threshold section is indispensable. It is not a 65 mm safety allowance earned
by this document. Splash, transient head, tolerances and local blockage remain
additional duties.

## Northward route: reject the easy-pier assumption

A northward carrier reaches outside over 4250, from court edge y 7750 to outer
face y 12000. The initial idea was x 7400 through an apparent spare pier.
However, the garden door ends at x 7200 and the adjacent outer window starts at
x 7600. A 300-wide strip leaves only **50 mm either side** within that interval.
This cannot be treated as a ready-made support opening.

A distinct comparison uses the **x 6000 threshold axis**, through both existing
paired doors and the clear central family-room route. It avoids the extra
service partition crossed by RWT's eastward pantry trial. It still crosses the
court and outer threshold/support territories, each 417.5 deep. Both thresholds
need real independent bridging and thermal/moisture continuity above and around
the carrier; the door aperture does not remove that obligation. The family-room
floor must provide accessible, safely supported release panels without fixed
furniture over them. No channel is silently cut into the current floor.

Do not assign the source **300 mm ground reserve** twice. A shallow carrier
near the floor surface competes with load-bearing covers and insulation; putting
it below continuous insulation reduces its discharge level. A deliberately
deeper local floor/service section is an available design option, but must be
shown with actual layers and support rather than disguised as spare depth.
Preserve a separate primary route and receiving identity: the relief cannot
rejoin the blocked throat that it is meant to bypass.

## What can and cannot move forward

The raised walking surface is a credible conventional family for further design;
it removes the assumed 150 mm occupied step while retaining inspectable lower
water territory. The north threshold-axis option removes one partition crossing,
but adds explicit threshold-support and floor-renewal tasks. The narrow-pier
shortcut is rejected. None of these observations establishes hydraulic capacity,
waterproofing, structural capacity, safe access or a compatible real plot.

Before adoption, HAD/OAS/IAS must produce one actual court sill/wall-base section
with its lowest vulnerable interface and a fully allocated crossing floor.
RWT then tests inlet/operating head, primary obstruction, combined roof/court
inflow, common debris and external backwater against those levels. A site-facing
handoff must state the required receiving level and maximum external water level;
it must not invent a lower garden, lawful outfall or dry receiver. Existing
RWT-06 eastward studies remain historical alternatives with their own source
identity. The bounded front OAS detail is not reopened or falsely described as
defective because it never claimed to solve this court.
