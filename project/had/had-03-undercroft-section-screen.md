## The critical section, not another pipe catalogue

RWT's latest architectural screen offers a **genuinely accessible, dry suspended-floor undercroft** with a normal north crossing and separate passive east escape. That is a plausible **building morphology** on some sites, but not a void that can be inserted beneath the present floor by choosing thinner fittings. The current Reference House draws ground FFL **z=0** and general outside ground **z=−150 mm**, only **150 mm** apart. There is no selected ground-floor package, external site high-water datum or undercroft height. This is why the source's `−150` cannot be used as a guaranteed garden grade, groundwater limit or available void.

For a true accessible undercroft above the assessed exterior high-water/grade boundary, define:

- `D`: finished-floor height **above** the relevant external level, not absolute building elevation.
- `T`: complete occupied floor thickness, including structure, thermal/water/gas/fire layers and load-bearing finishes.
- `H`: genuinely usable service-access clear height **below the complete floor**, after supports and local protrusions.
- `F`: justified additional vertical separation for assessed water, movement, contamination, drainage or inspection constraints, **not yet known**.

The elementary geometry is **`D ≥ T + H + F`**. Its shortfall against the *drawn* `D=150` is **`max(0, T + H + F − 150)`**. This is a *necessary geometric condition*, not sufficient to show gravity drainage, safe access, flood independence or structural support.

| Authored section sensitivity (not requirements) | Floor `T` | Clear access `H` | Minimum `D`, before `F` | Extra floor-to-**fixed-grade** elevation vs drawn 150 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Limited crawl route | 250 mm | 600 mm | 850 mm | **700 mm + F** |
| Crouched renewal route | 300 mm | 900 mm | 1,200 mm | **1,050 mm + F** |
| More workable low service space | 350 mm | 1,200 mm | 1,550 mm | **1,400 mm + F** |

`T` and `H` are declared scenario inputs, **not** an allocated ground-floor thickness, access standard or full working-method clearance. The `+ F` notation is algebraic only; a real floor needs actual joists/beams, openings, stiffness, thermal/gas/moisture boundaries, floor/paver repairs, structure/ground differential and undercroft access geometry. For the middle comparison, at the unchanged reference datum the hypothetical soffit would be **z=−300**, already **150 mm below** the drawn garden ground `z=−150`; a 900 mm dry service corridor is not present. Achieving it by lowering the ground alone creates a **1,050 mm deeper local basin** relative to the same garden datum, directly confronting the no-pump/no-wet-well policy. Achieving it by raising the building relative to *fixed* ground requires a substantial plinth and a new front/rear accessibility, entrance, garden-level, court, external service and masonry/arch story. Raising the garden together with the house gives **no relative gain**.

The [section sensitivity figure](studies/court-undercroft-section-screen.svg) depicts the middle row only. It is deliberately schematic: it does **not** draw an invented foundation or suggest that the selected site can provide the relative height.

## The court remains a two-exit wet system

The existing trial places raised walking pavers at z=0, an **unadopted** court waterproofing top at z=−180 near the perimeter and −215 at its illustrated low point. That low pan is 30–65 mm **below** the schematic outer ground z=−150. Therefore a direct gravity *free surface* path from the pan to that **same** nominal grade is not self-proving; a viable normal carrier needs actual hydraulic grade, losses, outlet level and receiving water, and may be submerged/pressurised when it operates. A deeper trench underneath a suspended floor is **not** automatically a dry accessible undercroft.

The alternative high relief crest `z=−75` is nominally **75 mm above** that schematic outside grade, but its proposed operating-water target `z=−50` is only **5 mm below** the *hypothetical* sill-support underside `z=−45`. Neither is a verified lowest first-harm joint. That sketch cannot earn water safety at the first frame drain, jamb, end dam, wall base, cavity or penetration, still less at a nominated event and blocked primary. The separate normal **N** and emergency **E** routes must each cross the actual structure, be renewable, and end in genuinely independent harmless receiving territories for the claimed failed state. Opposite compass directions and a pretty section are not enough.

## Decision and dependency consequences

**Reject the unqualified assumption that an accessible dry undercroft fits the unchanged 150 mm ground separation.** Retain a *raised* house/undercroft as conditional **major architectural and plot choice**, not current leading-detail acceptance; keep the separate daylight-relief and open-passage options visible, not as false hydraulic proof.
