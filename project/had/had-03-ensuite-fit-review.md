# HAD-03 — small en-suite fit review

**Result:** reject two product-sized arrangements of U-ENS3. Preserve the accepted
source as an **unfitted reservation**. HAD-03 remains incomplete. Neither a
smaller symbol nor a nominal room area resolves the fixture, service and
operation conflicts. This review does not prove every possible compact-room
arrangement impossible.

**Starting authority:** main `f75668d39bdebb389b12721c28230549566bf8b9`,
[layout source](../../compiler/reference-house/georgian/layout-revision.ts),
[shared interface](../triad-interface-contract.md),
[wet-variant obligations](../ias/ias-02w-wet-kitchen-variants.md) and
[existing HAD study](had-03-furnished-design-study.md). No source geometry,

## What was tested

The [A3, 1:20 diagnostic](studies/ensuite-fit-study.svg) places actual manufacturer
**external envelopes**, rather than generic reduced symbols, in two opposing
fixture arrangements. The paper scale is explicit. The dashed door rectangle
is a conservative operation reservation, **not an exact leaf-sweep test**:
intersection with it calls for handing/operation investigation and alone does
not prove an actual leaf collision.

The test applies an authored 600 mm unoccupied front task field as a comparison
question for using/cleaning a WC. It is not a regulatory requirement, an
accessibility classification or a manufacturer clearance. No relaxed field is
introduced after seeing the result. Actual human operation, assembly support,
shower enclosure, controls and release routes have not been demonstrated.

A pan dimension is not the installed WC dimension. A tray's external plan is
not the usable standing area, enclosure opening, trap or waterproofing detail.
The 85 mm tray height is especially material: it cannot be described as occupying
only the source's 20 mm floor-finish allocation. A raised or recessed installation
would need an explicit section and source/layer decision; this review adopts
neither. No product here earns the project's selected material or moisture duties
merely by being small.

## Arrangements and dispositions

### A — west shower, east WC

Place the 800 × 1000 tray at x9562.5–10362.5 / y2750–3750. It intersects the
held lining over x9862.5–10112.5 / y2762.5–3750: **250 × 987.5 mm**. Removing
that lining in the picture would erase a real service reservation. Its function
and replacement route must be re-authored before this can be a candidate.

The east WC comparison reserves a 155 mm carrier at x11427.5–11582.5 and the
480 mm ceramic at x10947.5–11427.5, y3000–3370. The cross-room remainder to the
shower is 2020 − 800 − 155 − 480 = **585 mm**. It fails this review's 600 mm
front-field test by 15 mm before board, finish, enclosure and tolerance costs.
The carrier's 500 mm width is shown; its compatibility/support, connections and
whole-cistern exchange are not established. The small basin at x10400–10760,
y3517.5–3787.5 does not confer useful approach or trap access by fitting its box.

**Reject A as drawn.** Service overlap is decisive independently of the chosen
front-field benchmark. This does not mean no alternative shower size, WC type,
orientation or service arrangement could ever work.

### B — east shower, west WC

Place the tray x10782.5–11582.5 / y2787.5–3787.5. Put the pan against the east
face of the existing lining: x10112.5–10592.5 / y2780–3150. The remaining front
space to the tray is **190 mm**, short of the authored field by 410 mm. This
already grants the unproved favourable assumption that a compatible carrier,
finish and connections could be accommodated within the existing lining.

The basin at x10200–10560 / y3517.5–3787.5 fits as an envelope only. The east
shower also occupies much of the nominal door-operation reservation. Reversing,
sliding or narrowing the door needs a genuine bedroom-side and opening study;
it does not fix the 190 mm front-space result.

**Reject B as drawn.** Do not count shared shower floor as a permanently free
WC operation field without an explicit wet-room/enclosure/ordinary-use design.

## Next design decision, not another cosmetic study

Prefer a **coordinated local wet-stack redesign** over continuing to shrink
symbols inside U-ENS3. Retain the 12 × 12 m envelope and the four-bedroom brief.
Test altered internal service geography and door operation against the real
bedroom, gallery and adjacent bathroom. If a dimension change reaches the east
window, explicitly revise and reconcile its aperture/composition; do not simply
slide the wall through it. A target such as 1800 mm clear depth would require
725 mm more than the current depth, well beyond the 187.5 mm north-only
translation limit. That target is an illustrative design trial, not a requirement.

This review does not select a rewind: a local change must demonstrate all
three wet rooms, bedroom furniture/approach, gallery passage, service tasks and
window margins together. If no credible retained-programme solution survives,
reducing/reallocating the small en suite becomes an **explicit owner programme
decision**. An agent may prepare the comparison, but must not silently remove it.

| Open evidence | Owning next action |
|---|---|
| Adopted room/door/fixture arrangement and effects on neighbours | HAD/house source, coordinated with IAS and OAS |
| Actual wet-floor/wall section, support and service release | IAS with BCR wet/detail/occupation resolution |
| I00-C1 clear opening, tool approach and replacement | IAS; a shifted panel remains insufficient |
| Ordinary use and emergency release after fixture installation | HAD/IAS with competent evidence where required |
| Other unfinished furnished/private/service studies | HAD-03; this review does not claim whole-house convergence |

The BCR scaffold receives these as wet/detail/occupation obligations. No
performance class, maturity score, physical test result or whole-house PASS
changes. HAD-03 must remain open until the intended architectural claim is earned.

The subsequent [relocation trial](had-03-relocation-trial.md) pursues a retained-
programme alternative above the ground guest WC. It identifies a further
connected-route failure and the specific bed, opening and service decisions
needed before any source rewind. The two rejected arrangements were a checkpoint,
not the end of design exploration.
