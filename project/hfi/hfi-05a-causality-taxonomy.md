**Packet:** HFI-05A  
**Programme:** [`docs/development/human-factors-low-friction-inhabitation-programme.md`](../../docs/development/human-factors-low-friction-inhabitation-programme.md)  
**Execution plan:** [`docs/development/human-factors-low-friction-inhabitation-execution-plan.md`](../../docs/development/human-factors-low-friction-inhabitation-execution-plan.md)  
**Owning issue:** #195  
**Research-start baseline `main`:** `6db87f9fc2e2e20d67b9004fd6ada53aac117d1d`  
**Prior gate:** HFI-04 complete  
**Question:** which frictions identified across HFI-02/03/04 genuinely share a causal mechanism, which similarities are merely topical or linguistic, and what smallest taxonomy can organise later proportionality/conflict/target work without collapsing person, task, state, consequence and remedy into one category?  
**Boundary:** synthesis/taxonomy only. This packet does **not** choose interventions, rank candidate propositions, apply HFI-01D proportionality, resolve technical conflicts, derive quantitative targets, redesign the Reference House, create doctrine/patterns, add compiler proof or change publication maturity.

---

## 1. Why HFI needs a causal taxonomy now

HFI-02 through HFI-04 deliberately researched topics separately:

- bodies, reach, force and stairs;
- life-course and carrying states;
- circulation, thresholds, object routes and domestic logistics;
- bathing, openings/hardware, environmental controls and fixed kitchen/storage work.

That decomposition was useful for research. It is dangerous if carried forward unchanged.

A topic list can make the project believe it has many independent problems when several are manifestations of one cause. The opposite error is just as serious: a fashionable phrase such as “accessibility”, “generosity” or “universal design” can merge unlike mechanisms and then justify one oversized remedy everywhere.

HFI-05A therefore separates five things that must not be confused:

1. **causal mechanism** — what property of the building interaction creates the burden;
2. **domain** — where the burden happens;
3. **scenario modifier** — what person/state/load makes the mechanism more or less demanding;
4. **consequence** — what happens to the person/task if the burden remains;
5. **candidate intervention** — one possible way to change the mechanism.

The core rule is:

> **Group frictions only when the same building-side mechanism explains them. Shared room type, shared user population, shared symptom or shared remedy is not enough.**

---

## 2. The causal test

Two findings belong in the same causal family only if all of the following survive scrutiny.

### 2.1 Same building-side mechanism

The fixed/durable condition producing compensation is materially the same.

Example:

- a sofa failing a corridor turn;
- a wheelchair/mobility device failing the same turn;
- a loaded person needing repeated body rotation at the same pinch point.

These may share **envelope/path mismatch**.

Counter-example:

- a shallow stair tread;
- a narrow corridor.

Both can feel “cramped”, but the stair problem is foot placement/balance on repeated level change, while the corridor problem is lateral/swept clearance. They are not one causal mechanism merely because “more space” might improve both.

### 2.2 Same direction of intervention

A causal grouping is stronger if changing the mechanism predictably helps all grouped cases without requiring contradictory geometry.

If one group needs lower controls while another needs controls higher, “reach” is not yet a sufficient causal family. The cause may instead be **interface placement relative to usable reach and approach**.

### 2.3 Same evidence logic

The same kind of evidence should be capable of testing the mechanism.

For example:

- door/appliance open-state conflicts can be tested geometrically with swept/occupied envelopes;
- thermostat comprehension requires mapping/state-feedback evidence and cannot be proved by geometry alone.

### 2.4 No hidden scenario substitution

A family may be exposed by many scenarios without being caused by those scenarios.

“Older occupant”, “wheelchair user”, “pregnancy” and “occupied hands” are not causal categories. They are representative conditions under which a building mechanism becomes more or less consequential.

### 2.5 No remedy-defined category

“Wider”, “step-free”, “automated”, “lever handle”, “lift-ready” and “adjustable” are remedies or strategies, not causes.

HFI-05A does not organise evidence around solutions.

---

## 3. Taxonomy architecture

Every later HFI finding should be representable as:

`domain × causal family × scenario modifier(s) × task event × consequence × candidate remedy`

Only the **causal family** is the primary synthesis axis.

The other axes remain independent so that later HFI-05B can ask whether a remedy is proportionate for the actual frequency/consequence/irreversibility profile.

---

# 4. Primary causal families

HFI-05A recognises **nine** causal families.

They are intentionally broader than rooms and narrower than slogans.

---

## CF-01 — Envelope / path mismatch

### Definition

The body, assisted pair, carried load or object needs a swept/occupied envelope that the fixed route/interface does not credibly accommodate.

### Typical manifestations

- corridor pinch point;
- corner that cannot be negotiated without multi-stage rotation;
- doorway plus adjacent return preventing approach/turn;
- furniture or mattress route failure;
- pram/wheelchair/object route failure;
- two-person assistance envelope conflict;
- open door narrowing an otherwise adequate route.

### What this family is **not**

- not “all width problems”;
- not stair foot-placement quality;
- not worktop reach;
- not level discontinuity;
- not general desire for large rooms.

### Evidence / representation

- clear geometry;
- swept path;
- body/object envelope;
- approach direction;
- dynamic state where relevant.

### Representative source packets

HFI-03A, HFI-03C, parts of HFI-04B and HFI-04D.

---

## CF-02 — Support / level-change movement mismatch

### Definition

The building requires repeated vertical or support-surface movement whose rise/going/landing/edge/hand-support relationship imposes unnecessary foot-placement, balance, descent-control or attention demand.

### Typical manifestations

- stair going too shallow for robust foot placement in a tested scenario;
- rise/going/pitch combination increasing descent demand;
- inadequate landing recovery/turn condition;
- handrail relationship poorly supporting the movement sequence;
- isolated internal step creating a balance event;
- shower/threshold level discontinuity where step/edge is the dominant movement problem.

### Why separate from CF-01

A person can have ample lateral clearance and still have poor support geometry. The evidence base and hazard mechanism differ.

### Why not merge all thresholds here

A nominally flush threshold can still fail because of drainage/weathering or wheel interruption; those belong partly to CF-03. CF-02 is specifically the embodied support/level-change movement mechanism.

### Representative source packets

HFI-02B, HFI-02D, HFI-03B, parts of HFI-04A.

---

## CF-03 — Ground-plane / rolling-surface discontinuity

### Definition

A route that should operate as a continuous walking/wheeling surface is interrupted by a lip, abrupt level change, incompatible material junction, drainage detail or other local discontinuity that forces extra lift, wheel negotiation, attention or detour.

### Typical manifestations

- entrance threshold upstand;
- raised transition strip between internal finishes;
- garden/courtyard threshold lip;
- shower entry lip;
- local ramp/step arrangement that breaks otherwise continuous entrance-level movement.

### Why separate from CF-02

CF-03 is not principally about the geometry of an intentional stair/vertical journey. It concerns a local discontinuity embedded in an otherwise continuous route.

### Technical firewall

“Flush” is not itself the solution. Moisture, drainage, weathering, movement, thermal and doorset duties remain hard constraints for HFI-05C.

### Representative source packet

HFI-03B, with 04A interface consequences.

---

## CF-04 — Reach / posture / operating-effort mismatch

### Definition

A fixed interface places the required grasp, control, load or work point outside a comfortable/credible relationship to the user’s approach, posture, obstruction depth, grip and force capability for the tested task.

### Typical manifestations

- window handle requiring extended reach over obstruction;
- heavy/stiff latch or closer;
- shower/bath control reached across a hazard zone;
- deep upper or lower kitchen storage;
- sink/work interface causing sustained forward lean;
- appliance loading forcing repeated deep bend;
- control mounted inside a nominal height band but unreachable from the actual operating position.

### Internal distinctions retained

This family shares a body–interface mismatch but does **not** erase:

- reach vs force;
- standing vs seated use;
- vertical vs horizontal reach;
- bend/kneel vs overhead extension;
- one-handed vs two-handed action;
- frequent vs rare interaction.

Those remain task/modifier fields.

### Representative source packets

HFI-02A, HFI-04A/B/C/D.

---

## CF-05 — Sequence / consequence-zone mismatch

### Definition

The fixed arrangement forces the occupant to perform actions in an order or location that exposes them to the consequence before they can safely/controlably establish the desired state, or creates unnecessary regrip/crossover between linked steps.

### Typical manifestations

- shower control located such that the user must enter the spray/consequence zone before setting flow/temperature;
- hot item extracted before a stable landing point is available;
- wet/hot task requiring crossover through an unrelated active zone;
- door/hardware sequence forcing the user to release/regrip a carried load unnecessarily;
- a control that can only be operated after entering the space/hazard it is intended to control.

### Why separate from CF-04

A control may be physically reachable yet sequenced badly. A landing surface may be close enough to reach yet occur on the wrong side of the task. The defect is relational/order-based, not simply reach distance.

### Representative source packets

HFI-02D, HFI-03D, HFI-04A/B/C/D.

---

## CF-06 — Staging / load-handling discontinuity

### Definition

A task predictably changes grip, load state or handling mode but the building provides no credible place/condition for set-down, regrip, transfer or temporary support, causing avoidable carrying, balancing or repeated handling.

### Typical manifestations

- groceries at entrance/kitchen with no staging point before opening storage;
- laundry/waste route requiring repeated door operation while loaded with no usable pause point;
- hot/heavy oven item with no adjacent landing surface;
- furniture/object move requiring an impossible intermediate rotation because no staging/turn zone exists;
- cleaning equipment extraction requiring unloading unrelated objects first.

### Why separate from CF-05

CF-05 concerns ordering/hazard relationship. CF-06 concerns **support during a handling-state transition**. They often co-occur but can exist independently.

### Representative source packets

HFI-02D, HFI-03C/D, HFI-04D.

---

## CF-07 — Dynamic occupied-state conflict

### Definition

The building is usable in its nominal/closed state but becomes materially obstructive when ordinary movable elements or people occupy the positions required by the task.

### Typical manifestations

- door leaf blocks the route or control;
- dishwasher door/rack consumes the user’s operating space;
- oven door conflicts with standing/landing position;
- opposing drawers/cupboards cannot support the required sequence;
- window opening hardware creates body/curtain/furniture conflict;
- two ordinary users repeatedly collide because active work zones overlap.

### Why this is not just CF-01

CF-01 describes envelope/path mismatch generally. CF-07 exists to preserve a critical causal fact: **the failure appears only when the system changes state**. Closed-plan drawings routinely hide it.

CF-07 may be tagged with CF-01 where the dynamic state specifically destroys path clearance.

### Representative source packets

HFI-03A/C, HFI-04B/D.

---

## CF-08 — Mapping / state-feedback / legibility mismatch

### Definition

The occupant cannot efficiently infer which control/route/interface performs the intended function, whether the action registered, what state the building is in, or what will happen next.

### Typical manifestations

- switch bank with weak mapping to loads;
- thermostat mode/setpoint/schedule ambiguity;
- automated system with unclear override duration;
- fault/isolation controls with poor labels;
- unfamiliar visitor cannot identify ordinary route/control;
- multi-way/local controls behave inconsistently;
- low-light use relies on searching rather than predictable placement.

### Why separate from physical reach

A perfectly reachable control can remain unusable because the user cannot identify or interpret it.

### Why not call this “cognitive accessibility”

HFI does not create a diagnostic user category. Legibility is tested through ordinary unfamiliarity, fatigue, low light and reduced processing reserve in the frozen scenario set.

### Representative source packets

HFI-04B/C, with HFI-03A route-legibility overlap.

---

## CF-09 — Topological / life-course mismatch

### Definition

Functions that must interact repeatedly, or functions needed under a foreseeable life-course state, are separated by a building topology whose permanent arrangement creates recurring cross-level/cross-house burden or makes later adaptation disproportionately destructive.

This family has two tightly related subtypes that share **durable topology rather than local interface geometry**.

### CF-09A — repeated functional separation

Examples:

- laundry cycle repeatedly connecting upper sleeping/bathing with ground-floor utility;
- waste/grocery/cleaning tasks whose fixed destinations cause unnecessary repeated level changes;
- garden-serving function routed through unrelated rooms/levels.

The cause is not raw distance. It is the **relationship among repeated task destinations, level changes and handling events**.

### CF-09B — life-course/adaptation topology

Examples:

- no credible entrance-level sleeping/bathing route when stairs become temporarily/permanently unusable;
- structural/service layout making later level-access bathing or stair-lift/lift adaptation disproportionately destructive;
- adaptation option requiring shell reconstruction because no neutral reservation was preserved.

### Why one family

Both subtypes concern durable spatial allocation of functions and the cost of changing those relationships. They differ in time horizon and are kept distinct at subtype level.

### Why not merge with “more space”

The intervention may be relocation, duplication, reserve-now, demand-led equipment or accepting the burden because another technical benefit dominates. Area is only one possible consequence.

### Representative source packets

HFI-02C, HFI-03D, HFI-04A/D.

# 5. Cross-cutting scenario modifiers — not causes

The following are **modifiers** in the HFI model.

They can expose several causal families but should never become substitute taxonomies.

| Modifier | What it changes |
|---|---|
| larger/taller body | envelope, foot placement, joint angles, head/shoulder clearance |
| smaller/shorter body | reach, leverage, visual access |
| older occupant | balance, reach/posture tolerance, strength/dexterity variation, fatigue |
| wheelchair/mobility aid | approach, turning, knee/foot clearance, seated reach, rolling continuity |
| temporary lower-limb impairment | stair/level-change capacity, turning/support demand |
| temporary upper-limb impairment | reach side, grip/force, bilateral action |
| pregnancy | abdominal clearance, bend tolerance, balance, fatigue/load handling |
| occupied hands | door/control ability, balance support, set-down need |
| carried child/load/object | envelope, visibility, balance, force and staging need |
| wet hands/floor | grip, slip consequence, desire to avoid consequence zone |
| fatigue/distraction | attention reserve, balance margin, mapping tolerance |
| low light | edge/control/route perception and legibility |
| unfamiliar user | learned mapping unavailable; route/control inference dominates |
| assistance/care pair | combined occupied envelope and sequencing |

A design change that helps several modifiers **because it changes the same causal mechanism** has broad benefit. A change that merely helps the same named group in unrelated ways does not become one proposition.

---

# 6. Consequences — not causes

HFI-01D consequence classes remain separate from the taxonomy.

The same causal family can produce different consequences depending on task/state.

Example:

- CF-04 reach mismatch at an occasional high shelf may be `C1 — repeated burden` or even negligible;
- CF-04 reach mismatch over a hot bath/hob may contribute to `C3 — material hazard exposure`.

Do not encode consequence into the family name.

Common outcomes include:

- extra attention;
- extra steps/detour;
- repeated bend/overreach;
- regrip/set-down;
- dependence on another person;
- task delay/failure;
- segregated/undignified route;
- fall/collision/burn exposure;
- destructive future retrofit.

These are outputs of the causal mechanism under a scenario, not categories of architecture.

---

# 7. Domains — not causes

The following remain useful **domains** but are no longer primary synthesis groups:

- stairs;
- corridors/galleries;
- entrances/thresholds;
- bathrooms/WCs;
- doors/windows;
- controls;
- kitchen/storage;
- domestic logistics;
- object movement;
- life-course adaptation.

A bathroom can contain CF-03, CF-04, CF-05, CF-07 and CF-09 mechanisms simultaneously.

A corridor can contain CF-01, CF-07 and CF-08.

This is why “bathroom accessibility” or “circulation generosity” is too coarse for HFI-05B.

---

# 8. Candidate interventions — not causes

HFI-02/03/04 produced many plausible interventions. HFI-05A deliberately refuses to organise around them.

Examples:

- increase stair going;
- widen a route locally;
- flatten a threshold;
- move a control;
- use lever hardware;
- motorise a window/blind;
- add a landing/set-down surface;
- relocate an appliance;
- reserve a future lift/adaptation zone;
- duplicate a function at entrance level;
- provide app control;
- raise an appliance;
- enlarge a turning zone.

Each must be evaluated later as a **bounded proposition** against alternatives under HFI-05B.

The same causal family may have several remedies with radically different burdens.

# 9. False unifications explicitly rejected

## 9.1 “Accessibility”

Too broad.

Visitability, ordinary usability, adaptability, wheelchair usability and maintenance access remain distinct project concepts. They can expose different causal families and have different burdens.

## 9.2 “Generosity”

Too vague.

Deeper stair treads, wider galleries, larger turns, more landing surface and extra future-adaptation area do not share a cause merely because all consume space.

HFI-05B must compare each proposition separately.

## 9.3 “Width”

Too geometric and one-dimensional.

- corridor width relates to CF-01;
- door clear opening may relate to CF-01 plus CF-07;
- stair width may relate to assistance/carrying and is not a substitute for stair going;
- kitchen aisle width may be driven by CF-07 open states.

One width target cannot be justified across these domains without separate evidence.

## 9.4 “Reach”

Too coarse.

Reach to a light switch, window handle, bath control, upper shelf and deep sink differ in obstruction, posture, consequence, frequency and force.

CF-04 preserves the common body–interface mechanism while requiring those fields to remain explicit.

## 9.5 “Step-free”

Not synonymous with wheelchair usability or universal accessibility.

A step-free route can still fail on width, turning, surface, controls, assistance or dignity. Conversely, an upper floor reached only by stairs can still be proportionately designed for robust ordinary use while preserving later adaptation options.

## 9.6 “Automation”

Not a causal family and not automatically low-friction.

Automation can remove manipulation while creating CF-08 mapping/state uncertainty or failure dependency.

## 9.7 “One-handed use”

A scenario/task condition, not a universal product rule.

It can expose CF-04 force/grip mismatch, CF-05 sequence mismatch or CF-06 staging deficiency.

## 9.8 “Older users”

A population modifier, not a defect category.

The goal is not an “elderly architecture” layer; it is to reveal mechanisms that become more consequential as capability changes.

## 9.9 “Wheelchair design”

A legitimate category-specific design context, not a single remedy family.

Wheelchair scenarios can expose CF-01, CF-03, CF-04, CF-07 and CF-09 simultaneously.

## 9.10 “More space”

Not a causal model.

Some frictions are solved by mapping, relocation, sequencing, hardware, feedback or later adaptability with **no area increase**. More space can even lengthen repeated tasks.

# 11. Causal chains and stopping rule

HFI findings can form chains.

Example:

`door leaf opens into narrow arrival zone`  
→ CF-07 dynamic conflict  
→ destroys usable approach envelope (CF-01)  
→ user must rotate/regrip load (CF-06)  
→ repeated burden / possible collision consequence

The project should identify the **earliest architectural cause that can be changed without inventing a broader rule than necessary**.

Stopping rule:

> **Do not keep abstracting upward once the common cause stops predicting the same intervention logic.**

For example, “the house is insufficiently generous” explains almost nothing and predicts indiscriminate area growth. The taxonomy stops at the more specific causal families above.

---

# 12. Multi-cause findings

Some frictions are genuinely compound.

HFI must not force them into one bucket.

Examples:

### Shower use

- CF-03 if the entry has a lip;
- CF-04 if controls are hard to reach/operate;
- CF-05 if control position forces entry into spray first;
- CF-07 if door/screen blocks assistance/exit;
- wet/fatigue modifiers affect consequence.

### Kitchen dishwasher task

- CF-07 open door/rack occupation;
- CF-04 bending/reach;
- CF-06 staging/transfer;
- CF-08 if controls/state are unclear.

### Stair under carried load

- CF-02 foot placement/balance;
- CF-04 handrail reach/grip if relevant;
- CF-06 if no credible pause/landing at a long sequence;
- occupied-hands/carrying modifier.

The taxonomy allows compound findings while preventing vague “accessibility failure” labels.

---

# 13. Architectural ownership remains independent

Causal synthesis does not transfer authority to HFI.

A CF family may cross several RH gates, but any accepted architectural change still routes to the narrowest correct owner.

Examples:

- CF-01 ground-floor turn defect → likely RH-02;
- CF-02 stair geometry → RH-03;
- CF-03 occupied threshold → RH-09 / technical detail owner;
- CF-04 door/window/control interface → RH-09;
- CF-09A laundry topology → likely RH-02/RH-03 depending required change;
- CF-09B whole-house life-course strategy → RH-10 boundary, with upstream geometry owners as required.

HFI-05A is a reasoning layer, not a shadow architecture gate.

---

# 14. What HFI-05B must evaluate

HFI-05B should take **bounded propositions**, each tagged to one or more causal families, and apply HFI-01D.

A proposition record should now be able to state:

- `cause_family`;
- `domain`;
- `scenario_ids`;
- `task/event`;
- `frequency`;
- `consequence`;
- `evidence_confidence`;
- `baseline/counterfactual`;
- `proposed intervention`;
- `alternatives`;
- `irreversibility/option_value`;
- `burden axes`;
- `architectural owner`;
- `known unknowns`.

This prevents a proposal such as “make corridors wider” from reaching proportionality review without naming the actual CF-01 failure it is intended to solve.

# 15. Candidate proposition families passed to HFI-05B

05A does not decide them, but the research to date leaves the following **bounded proposition families** worth proportionality review.

### Movement / support

- stair going / tread-foot robustness beyond minimum correspondence where evidence supports a material benefit;
- hand-support/landing geometry that improves carried/reduced-attention stair use;
- route role classification with local clearance generosity at high-frequency/high-conflict pinch points rather than blanket widening.

### Continuous ground plane

- preserve substantially level principal entrance-level movement where technically viable;
- avoid gratuitous internal finish-transition upstands;
- solve external/door/shower continuity without weakening moisture/drainage/weathering.

### Dynamic movement

- analyse and protect critical door/corner/object swept paths;
- alter door swing/position rather than defaulting to wider corridors where that removes the actual CF-07 cause.

### Controls / interfaces

- locate frequent controls around task sequence and operating position, not nominal mounting band alone;
- preserve intelligible state/mapping and proportionate local/manual fallback where control consequence justifies it;
- prefer hardware/operation that avoids unnecessary grip/force/compound action for frequent use.

### Domestic work

- provide task-specific staging where predictable load-state changes make it valuable;
- avoid fixed storage/work geometry that forces frequent/heavy use into extreme reach/bend zones;
- represent appliance open states before accepting fitted-work geometry.

### Life-course / topology

- preserve credible entrance-level fallback living/bathing strategy;
- test low-burden neutral reservations for future adaptation against speculative installed mechanisms;
- explicitly evaluate the upper sleeping/bathing ↔ ground utility laundry topology rather than assuming either “utility downstairs” or “laundry upstairs” is self-evidently correct.

This list is deliberately shorter than the full research inventory. Similar observations have been compressed only where causality survived.

# 16. What is **not** passed forward as a proposition family

The following are rejected as synthesis outputs:

- “design for everyone”;
- “make everything accessible”;
- “make corridors generous”;
- “make doors wide”;
- “make all controls low”; 
- “use lever handles everywhere”;
- “make the whole ground floor wheelchair accessible” without a defined category/claim;
- “future-proof everything”;
- “automate everything”;
- “avoid all thresholds” without moisture/drainage context;
- “make all storage reachable”;
- “make the kitchen bigger”;
- “provide a lift”; 
- “make every repeated route shortest”.

Each either confuses cause with remedy, ignores countervailing scenarios/technical owners, or is too broad to survive HFI-01D.

---

# 17. Evidence-confidence consequence

Causal taxonomy does not upgrade evidence.

If several weak hypotheses share a plausible mechanism, the family remains weak until applicable evidence supports it.

Likewise, a strong family can contain one weak proposition.

Examples:

- CF-08 mapping/state feedback has strong general human-factors support, but a specific preferred domestic control layout may remain unproven;
- CF-09B adaptation option value is structurally plausible and supported by inclusive-housing practice, but the case for a particular future lift reservation depends on real burden and geometry;
- CF-02 stair mechanisms have substantial evidence, but an exact Ovid’s House preferred going remains HFI-06 work.

05B must carry evidence confidence at proposition level.

---

# 18. Reference House consequence — still no verdict

05A does not audit the Reference House.

It changes how the later audit will describe findings.

Instead of:

> “corridor is too narrow”

HFI-07 should be capable of saying:

> “CF-01 envelope/path mismatch for scenario X at turn Y under represented clear geometry; candidate owner RH-02.”

Instead of:

> “controls are inaccessible”

it should distinguish:

- CF-04 reach/force mismatch;
- CF-05 sequence mismatch;
- CF-08 mapping/state-feedback mismatch;
- or `NOT-MODELLED` if the fixed interface is absent from source.

Instead of:

> “laundry is inconvenient”

it should state the CF-09A repeated functional-separation mechanism and the full dirty→wash→dry→fold→clean-return task evidence.

This sharpens later rewind ownership and prevents cosmetic fixes.

# 19. Computational consequence

The taxonomy is representable as structured metadata later, but 05A authorises no compiler change.

Potential future fields:

- `cause_family: CF-01..CF-09`;
- optional `cause_subtype`;
- `domain`;
- `scenario_ids[]`;
- `task_event_ids[]`;
- `modifiers[]`;
- `consequence_class`;
- `candidate_owner`;
- `evidence_confidence`.

Important constraints:

- many-to-many tagging is allowed;
- a cause tag does not imply a failure verdict;
- a domain is not inferred from a cause;
- no cause family maps automatically to one remedy;
- no family is assigned a numeric weight;
- `CF-09` does not create a compiler right to redesign topology.

# 20. Red-team of the taxonomy

### Is nine families too many?

No obvious pair can be merged without losing intervention logic:

- CF-01 vs CF-07: static/swept envelope vs state-triggered conflict;
- CF-02 vs CF-03: intentional support/vertical movement vs local continuous-plane interruption;
- CF-04 vs CF-05: physical capability/position vs order/consequence relationship;
- CF-05 vs CF-06: action ordering vs support during load-state transition;
- CF-08 vs CF-04: information/state understanding vs physical operation;
- CF-09 vs CF-01: durable function allocation/life-course topology vs local envelope geometry.

### Is nine families too few?

Potential omitted themes were tested:

- **privacy/acoustics** — adjacent discipline unless a human-factors proposition later demonstrates a distinct mechanism;
- **lighting/contrast** — often a modifier or CF-08 legibility mechanism; detailed visual-environment performance remains its technical domain;
- **slip resistance** — technical surface/falls property, not yet a separate causal family because current HFI task findings use wet state mainly as a modifier; 05C can preserve the technical owner;
- **thermal comfort** — environmental performance rather than HFI control-interface causality;
- **security** — constraint/owner, not a human-friction family;
- **maintenance** — separate project concept unless ordinary occupant task is involved.

No tenth family is justified now.
