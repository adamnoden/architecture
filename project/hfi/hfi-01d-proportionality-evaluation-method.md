# HFI-01D — Proportionality and Evaluation Method

**Packet:** HFI-01D  
**Programme:** [`docs/development/human-factors-low-friction-inhabitation-programme.md`](../../docs/development/human-factors-low-friction-inhabitation-programme.md)  
**Execution plan:** [`docs/development/human-factors-low-friction-inhabitation-execution-plan.md`](../../docs/development/human-factors-low-friction-inhabitation-execution-plan.md)  
**Owning issue:** #195  
**Baseline `main`:** `13b86f65a5824f8b0c22edfa6c9a56cf7f97110f`  
**Prior packets:** HFI-01A terminology + scope; HFI-01B normative/evidence register; HFI-01C representative scenario matrix  
**Question:** how must HFI compare a claimed human-factors benefit with the permanent spatial, technical, environmental, economic and architectural burden of providing it?  
**Boundary:** evaluation method only. This packet does not judge any current Reference House dimension, select an intervention, derive a target, create a doctrine/pattern, or make a cost/carbon claim that has not been quantified elsewhere.

---

## 1. Purpose

HFI can easily become an argument for indiscriminate generosity:

- deeper stairs;
- wider corridors;
- larger turns;
- more adaptation reservations;
- more accessible controls;
- more support space;
- more duplicated routes.

Some of those may prove valuable. Some may consume permanent area, cost, carbon and architectural coherence for little real benefit. HFI therefore needs a proportionality method **before** topic research begins producing attractive interventions.

The constitutional rule already exists:

> **Serviceability is pursued proportionately. Permanent provision should reflect the frequency, consequence and retrofit difficulty of the future event it is intended to support.**

HFI applies that rule to embodied occupation.

The core method is:

> **Trace the benefit to representative scenarios; expose the complete burden; test hard constraints first; compare alternatives explicitly; quantify what can honestly be quantified; and record the final judgement without fabricating a common numerical score.**

---

## 2. Why HFI does not use a weighted score

HFI-01D rejects a generic weighted scoring model such as:

`benefit score × 0.4 + accessibility score × 0.2 − carbon score × 0.1 ...`

That would create false precision for quantities that are often not commensurable.

Examples:

- 0.8 m² of permanent floor area cannot be honestly converted into the same unit as a reduction in stair-fall exposure without an explicit valuation model;
- a moisture-management failure cannot be averaged away by a high usability score;
- architectural damage cannot be made objective by giving it `6/10`;
- a weak evidence base does not become strong because several subjective weights sum favourably;
- a statutory or life-safety conflict is not a negative point inside an otherwise positive total.

HFI may use real numbers where real numbers exist—square metres, pounds, kilograms CO₂e, operating energy, clear dimensions, frequency estimates, replacement intervals—but **does not collapse unlike quantities into an arbitrary master score**.

The method is structured judgement with an auditable evidence trail.

## 3. Evaluation unit: one proposition against explicit alternatives

Every proportionality review must evaluate a **bounded proposition**, not an aspiration.

Examples of valid proposition forms later in HFI might be:

- increase a defined stair going from current value A to candidate value B;
- provide X additional clear width at a named high-frequency turn;
- reserve a future adaptation zone of defined size and location;
- relocate a fixed control outside a hazard zone;
- preserve a level movement route while satisfying a defined drainage strategy.

Invalid forms include:

- “make circulation generous”;
- “design for everyone”;
- “make the house accessible”;
- “future-proof it”.

Each review must state at least:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| `Proposition` | exact change or rule under consideration |
| `Baseline/counterfactual` | what happens if the proposition is not adopted |
| `Scenario IDs` | HFI-01C scenarios expected to benefit |
| `Mechanism` | why the intervention is expected to improve those scenarios |
| `Evidence` | sources supporting the mechanism and any quantitative claim |
| `Architectural owner` | gate that would own an accepted change |
| `Timing` | build-now, reserve-now, demand-led, or replaceable-later |
| `Alternatives` | credible lower-burden and higher-burden ways of addressing the same mechanism |
| `Known unknowns` | missing site, cost, product, technical or evidence facts |

A review with no explicit counterfactual cannot establish proportionality.

## 4. The benefit side

HFI evaluates benefit through the scenario matrix, not generic desirability.

### 4.1 Scenario relevance

For each proposition, identify:

- the scenario(s) materially affected;
- whether the scenario is ordinary use, visitability, adaptability, wheelchair usability or equivalent/dignified use;
- whether the benefit applies across several genuinely different scenarios or merely repeats one mechanism under several labels.

Scenario count is **not** itself a score. Ten near-duplicate scenarios do not outweigh one serious scenario.

### 4.2 Frequency

Classify the relevant event/task using the best available evidence or bounded project assumption.

Suggested descriptive bands:

| Band | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `F-CONTINUAL` | encountered repeatedly through ordinary daily occupation |
| `F-DAILY` | normally encountered one or more times most days |
| `F-REGULAR` | recurring weekly/monthly or otherwise predictable use |
| `F-OCCASIONAL` | foreseeable but infrequent ordinary event |
| `F-EPISODIC` | temporary life-course state or occasional visit/use condition |
| `F-RARE` | unusual event with low expected recurrence |
| `F-UNKNOWN` | frequency cannot yet be responsibly characterised |

These are descriptive categories, not probability values.

A rare event can still justify provision where consequence and retrofit difficulty are high. A daily event can still fail proportionality where the friction is trivial and the burden is large.

### 4.3 Human consequence

Record the **human consequence if the friction remains**, not merely that friction exists.

Suggested HFI consequence classes:

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `C0 — negligible` | preference-level inconvenience or difference with no material task effect |
| `C1 — repeated burden` | additional attention, posture, handling, detour, grip/force or choreography |
| `C2 — material task degradation` | meaningful difficulty, repeated dependence, avoidable exclusion, or task failure for a representative scenario |
| `C3 — material hazard exposure` | plausible increase in fall, collision, burn, unstable movement or similar embodied hazard requiring technical attention |

External statutory/life-safety/technical duties are **not a consequence class**. They remain a separate authority/viability axis under §7, preserving the HFI-01B firewall between evidence, human consequence and external authority.

HFI must not invent medical severity or accident probabilities where evidence does not support them.

### 4.4 Magnitude of improvement

State what the proposition actually changes:

- removes the friction;
- materially reduces it;
- improves robustness/margin;
- improves equivalent/dignified participation;
- reduces future adaptation work;
- only marginally changes the interaction;
- benefit unknown pending evidence.

Do not treat a dimension change as a benefit merely because it is larger.

### 4.5 Breadth and distribution of benefit

Record whether the benefit:

- helps ordinary occupation broadly;
- helps a bounded but foreseeable human-variation scenario;
- prevents avoidable exclusion for a smaller group;
- mainly benefits one rare object/task;
- creates equivalent use rather than a segregated workaround.

Breadth does not erase consequence. A serious exclusion affecting fewer users can outweigh a small convenience affecting many users, but that judgement must be stated rather than hidden inside arithmetic.

### 4.6 Evidence confidence

Every claimed benefit receives an evidence-confidence statement:

- `E-HIGH` — convergent, applicable evidence supports the claimed mechanism with limited material uncertainty;
- `E-MODERATE` — useful applicable evidence exists with meaningful limitations;
- `E-LOW` — mechanism is plausible but evidence is weak, indirect, sparse or poorly matched to the scenario;
- `E-HYPOTHESIS` — project trigger or expert intuition not yet externally substantiated.

This confidence statement does **not** replace HFI-01B source classification or authority. A law/standard can create an external duty without establishing empirical mechanism confidence; an excellent empirical study can support a mechanism without creating legal authority.

A high permanent burden should not be justified by `E-HYPOTHESIS` unless another hard duty independently requires the intervention.

### 4.7 Adverse scenario effects

A proposition may improve one scenario while making another worse. Every review must therefore ask:

- does added width/clearance remove useful room area or storage for ordinary tasks?
- does a changed door/route create new handling, privacy or security friction?
- does an adaptation provision obstruct ordinary furniture/use?
- does a control strategy improve reach for one population while worsening another?
- does an “accessible” alternate route become less equivalent or dignified?

Benefits cannot be counted without recording material **HFI regressions or displaced friction** elsewhere in the frozen scenario set.

## 5. Irreversibility, retrofit asymmetry and option value

Irreversibility is central because HFI often deals with geometry that is cheap to choose early and expensive to change later.

For each proposition, record separately:

1. **How hard is the current condition to change later?**
2. **How permanent/costly is the proposed provision if installed now?**
3. **Can the need instead be met later by a replaceable or demand-led layer?**
4. **Can a smaller neutral reservation preserve future options without pre-selecting one future solution?**

Use this qualitative matrix:

| Burden now | Difficulty later if omitted | Default posture |
|---|---|---|
| low | high | strong candidate for early provision if benefit is credible |
| low | low | choose the simpler solution; preserve optionality where useful |
| high | low | prefer demand-led / replaceable provision unless another reason dominates |
| high | high | requires strong evidence, explicit trade-off and architectural ownership |

Examples of **potentially irreversible** moves include shell geometry, stair geometry, structural openings, fundamental wet-room location and major circulation topology.

Examples of **potentially demand-led** moves include some hardware, controls, support equipment and replaceable fittings where the permanent architecture preserves a credible later route.

A neutral reservation can sometimes have **option value**: it may preserve several future responses while imposing less current burden than installing one speculative solution. Option value must still be real rather than rhetorical—name what future choices the reservation actually preserves.

The matrix is a starting posture, not an automatic verdict.

## 6. The burden side

Every intervention must expose its costs beyond the immediate HFI benefit.

### 6.1 Area and spatial opportunity cost

Record where relevant:

- additional gross internal area;
- additional clear area inside a fixed shell;
- area transferred from another room/function;
- increased circulation-to-usable-room ratio;
- footprint/envelope expansion;
- lost storage, servicing, structure or furniture opportunity;
- whether the intervention changes one local zone or propagates through the whole plan.

A proposal that “only adds 100 mm” may have a much larger system consequence if repeated around the building.

Area is not automatically bad. Hidden area inflation is.

### 6.2 Initial and whole-life cost

Later project-specific evaluation should distinguish where evidence exists:

- construction cost;
- specialist product/fabrication cost;
- coordination/design cost;
- maintenance/replacement consequence;
- future adaptation cost avoided;
- demolition/rework avoided;
- cost uncertainty.

HFI does not invent monetary values. Where no credible cost exists, record `UNKNOWN` and do not pretend the burden is zero.

### 6.3 Embodied carbon and material consequence

Record where relevant:

- additional structure/envelope/finishes caused by area growth;
- extra material mass or specialist assemblies;
- replacement-cycle consequence;
- demolition/rework avoided by early provision;
- carbon uncertainty.

No generic “more area = unacceptable carbon” rule is created here. Later LCA/QS evidence owns quantities.

### 6.4 Operational energy / environmental consequence

A larger or more complex permanent geometry may affect:

- heated/cooled volume;
- exposed envelope area;
- ventilation demand;
- daylight/shading strategy;
- passive environmental performance.

Conversely, some HFI interventions have negligible operational consequence. Record the actual mechanism; do not add “energy” mechanically to every review.

### 6.5 Moisture, weathering and drainage

HFI movement continuity cannot purchase usability by creating an unresolved water problem.

Any proposition touching entrances, external thresholds, bathrooms, showers, ground interfaces or garden/courtyard access must identify the relevant moisture/drainage owner and state whether the HFI condition is technically compatible, unresolved or conflicting.

A known unresolved water-management mechanism is not a tradeable minus point. It is an **open technical dependency**.

### 6.6 Fire, security, structure and services

For each affected discipline ask:

- does the proposition create a new demand?
- does it remove required separation, guarding, security or support?
- does it obstruct service or maintenance geography?
- does it require a structural span/opening/reservation change?
- does it create a control/electrical/service constraint?

HFI cannot close these disciplines itself. The project assurance matrix supplies the owner.

### 6.7 Acoustics, privacy and environmental quality

Wider/open circulation, additional openings or alternate routes may alter:

- acoustic separation;
- visual privacy;
- retreat;
- environmental zoning;
- daylight/overheating relationships.

These effects are real burdens where the mechanism exists, not generic objections to openness or generosity.

### 6.8 Workmanship and delivery burden

Record whether an intervention:

- uses ordinary competent construction;
- demands unusually tight tolerance;
- introduces many interacting details;
- relies on specialist installation or proprietary adjustment;
- creates failure modes that ordinary site work is likely to mishandle;
- makes later inspection/repair harder.

The project deliberately values robustness to real delivery. A theoretically excellent HFI detail that is fragile in ordinary construction may be disproportionate.

### 6.9 Architectural effect

HFI is not entitled to treat architecture as residual space after functional optimisation.

Record material effects on:

- room proportion;
- sequence and hierarchy;
- compression/release;
- threshold character;
- privacy/retreat;
- façade/opening composition;
- stair as architectural element;
- selected G-01 grammar where relevant;
- overall domestic rather than institutional character.

Architectural effect is not a veto based on taste. It is a project value that must be named and argued when materially affected.

Historic appearance alone is not sufficient reason to retain an avoidable barrier.

### 6.10 Future flexibility and lock-in

Ask whether the proposed solution:

- preserves multiple future adaptations;
- commits the house to one specialist product family;
- narrows future room use;
- creates permanent geometry around a short-lived assumption;
- makes a future intervention easier or harder.

A “future-proofing” intervention that locks the house to one future can be less adaptable than a simpler neutral reservation.

### 6.11 Propagation and cumulative portfolio burden

A local intervention can look proportionate in isolation while a family of similar interventions collectively bloats the house.

For every accepted candidate, record:

- how many times the intervention repeats;
- whether it propagates shell dimensions or structural/service grids;
- cumulative area/cost/carbon/material effect where quantifiable;
- whether several individually marginal provisions compete for the same spatial budget;
- whether one shared intervention could satisfy several scenarios more efficiently.

HFI-05B must therefore perform both **local proportionality** and a **portfolio check across the candidate HFI package** before final principle/target formation.

There is no entitlement to keep every intervention merely because each passed separately.

## 7. Hard constraints versus tradeable burdens

The most important control in HFI-01D is that some conditions are **not candidates for averaging**.

### 7.1 Hard external constraints

Where applicable, the following must be closed by their owner before HFI can treat an option as viable:

- law / Building Regulations / adopted planning requirements;
- life safety and fire strategy;
- structural safety and stability;
- essential weathering, waterproofing and drainage;
- required security performance;
- essential service/electrical safety;
- any other project assurance item whose owner says the proposition is not technically viable.

HFI can expose the conflict but cannot “win” by scoring more usability points.

### 7.2 Open dependencies are not zero burden

If cost, carbon, drainage, structural feasibility, fire compatibility or another **material** input is unknown, mark that input `UNKNOWN` and keep the proposition conditional or `UNRESOLVED` until the missing information is sufficient for the decision.

Do not assume:

- unknown cost = cheap;
- unknown carbon = negligible;
- unmodelled geometry = adequate;
- product not selected = feasible;
- absent site = favourable site.

An unknown that is demonstrably immaterial to the proposition need not block it; it must be marked `NOT-MATERIAL`, not silently omitted.

### 7.3 Competing architectural benefits remain trade-offs

Where no hard duty is breached, HFI may legitimately compete with:

- room size;
- storage;
- privacy;
- compactness;
- architectural sequence;
- ordinary construction;
- cost/carbon restraint.

Those conflicts are resolved by explicit project judgement, not by claiming HFI has higher authority merely because it concerns people.

### 7.4 Uncertainty is not a one-way ratchet

Uncertainty does not automatically justify either overprovision or underprovision.

Where omission is cheap to reverse, uncertainty tends to support demand-led action. Where omission creates irreversible geometry and the current burden of preserving options is small, uncertainty may support modest early reservation. Where both current and future burdens are large, the correct result may remain `UNRESOLVED` rather than defaulting to “future-proofing”.

## 8. Comparison protocol

Later HFI-05B proportionality reviews must follow this order.

### Step 1 — state the friction/mechanism

Name the interaction, not the preferred solution.

### Step 2 — identify scenarios and evidence

List the affected HFI-01C scenario IDs and the HFI-01B evidence supporting the mechanism.

### Step 3 — freeze the counterfactual

Describe current/baseline condition and credible no-change consequence.

### Step 4 — generate alternatives

Include, where plausible:

1. no change;
2. low-burden local change;
3. stronger architectural change;
4. demand-led/replaceable adaptation;
5. alternate-route or alternate-interface solution.

Do not compare a favoured intervention only against an obviously bad strawman.

### Step 5 — screen hard constraints

Remove or mark unresolved any alternative that cannot satisfy mandatory technical duties.

### Step 6 — characterise benefit and regressions

Record:

- scenario relevance;
- frequency;
- human consequence if unaddressed;
- magnitude/breadth/equivalence benefit;
- evidence confidence;
- retrofit asymmetry/option value;
- scenarios or ordinary uses made worse by the proposition.

### Step 7 — characterise burden

Record only material burdens, including:

- area;
- initial/whole-life cost;
- embodied carbon/material consequence;
- operational energy/environmental consequence;
- moisture/weathering;
- fire/security/structure/services;
- acoustics/privacy;
- workmanship/delivery;
- architectural effect;
- future flexibility/lock-in;
- propagation/cumulative effect.

### Step 8 — test dominance before judgement

If option A delivers materially equal or better HFI benefit with lower burden and no new conflict than option B, B is normally rejected as **dominated**.

This simple dominance test should eliminate needless overprovision before nuanced judgement begins.

### Step 9 — perform the portfolio check

Before finalising a family of recommendations, aggregate their material spatial/technical/environmental burden and ask whether:

- repeated local allowances have accumulated into hidden plan inflation;
- two provisions can be combined;
- a lower-burden shared response exists;
- the combined package still preserves architectural and environmental coherence.

A local pass does not guarantee a portfolio pass.

### Step 10 — make the proportionality judgement

Record the reasoning in prose with quantities alongside it. Explain why the selected disposition follows despite residual trade-offs.

### Step 11 — state sensitivity / dependency

Name the facts that could reverse the judgement—for example real-site level, cost estimate, product envelope, LCA result, fire advice or stronger later evidence.

A conclusion that depends on an unknown must remain visibly conditional.

## 9. Proportionality dispositions

HFI-05B may use the following bounded outcome vocabulary.

| Disposition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `EARLY-PROVISION-EARNED` | credible benefit + severe retrofit difficulty make modest permanent provision proportionate now |
| `PREFERRED` | intervention is the best current architectural response after trade-offs, but not an external mandatory duty |
| `PERMITTED` | intervention is defensible but not clearly superior; project/site preference may choose it |
| `DEMAND-LED` | need is better met later through replaceable/adaptable provision than permanent speculative infrastructure |
| `REJECT-DISPROPORTIONATE` | burden materially exceeds evidenced benefit, creates unacceptable regression, or a lower-burden alternative dominates |
| `UNRESOLVED` | material evidence, technical closure, site fact, cost/carbon input or architectural decision is missing |
| `EXTERNAL-DUTY` | applicable law/technical authority independently requires or prohibits the condition; HFI records but does not trade it away |

These are proportionality dispositions, **not Reference House audit results**. HFI-07 retains its separate `PASS / IMPROVE / CONFLICT / UNRESOLVED / NOT-MODELLED` vocabulary.

`PREFERRED` must not be described as evidence-mandated when the final choice contains Ovid's House judgement.

## 10. No hidden threshold rules

HFI-01D deliberately does **not** say:

- daily friction automatically deserves permanent provision;
- `C3` automatically wins over area/carbon;
- any wheelchair scenario automatically requires whole-dwelling wheelchair geometry;
- future adaptation automatically justifies a lift shaft;
- wider/deeper/larger is safer and therefore better;
- a low-cost intervention is automatically proportionate;
- an intervention must benefit the majority to be justified;
- an inclusive route must be literally identical in every circumstance;
- every HFI benefit must be monetised;
- cost or carbon can justify breach of law/life safety;
- historical form can justify avoidable exclusion;
- a planning/statutory minimum caps Ovid's House ambition;
- any current Reference House geometry has incumbency advantage;
- every locally proportionate HFI intervention can be accumulated without limit.

The method exists to make these trade-offs inspectable rather than replace them with a different ideology.

## 12. Worked method examples without design conclusions

These examples demonstrate the method only. They do not judge the Reference House.

### Example A — modest permanent geometric allowance

Hypothetical proposition: a small increase in a stair/turn reservation materially improves several ordinary/carrying scenarios.

The method asks:

- Which exact scenarios improve?
- Does evidence show meaningful compensation/hazard reduction?
- Is the event daily or occasional?
- Is later geometric retrofit destructive?
- Does the change propagate into room loss, shell growth or structure?
- Does it make another frozen scenario or ordinary room use worse?
- Is there a smaller local alternative with equivalent benefit?
- What happens when all similar allowances are accumulated across the plan?
- Does the resulting stair/turn remain architecturally coherent?

If benefit is credible, current burden low and later retrofit severe, `EARLY-PROVISION-EARNED` may become defensible.

No such conclusion is made here for the current stair or upper turns.

### Example B — speculative future lift reservation

Hypothetical proposition: reserve substantial permanent area for a future private lift.

The method asks:

- Which life-course scenarios actually require it rather than entrance-level living or stair-lift adaptation?
- How foreseeable is the need?
- What permanent area/carbon/structural/service burden is created now?
- Does the reservation damage ordinary rooms for every occupant throughout the building's life?
- Can a neutral structural/plan allowance preserve several future options with lower burden?
- What happens if the assumed future product family changes?

High current burden plus uncertain need prevents “future-proofing” from being self-justifying.

### Example C — control relocation

Hypothetical proposition: move a shower control to a position operable before entering the spray.

The method asks:

- Does the scenario/evidence show meaningful sequence/hazard improvement?
- Is the change spatially cheap?
- Does it create plumbing, waterproofing, service-access or usability conflict?
- Does the new position worsen reach/sequence for another representative scenario?
- Is it robust to ordinary products and workmanship?

Where benefit is recurring and burden genuinely trivial, the method should allow that asymmetry to be visible instead of demanding artificial compromise.

Again, this packet makes no Reference House bathroom finding.

## 14. Red-team checks before accepting a proportionality judgement

A reviewer must attack at least these failure modes:

1. **solution-first reasoning** — was the preferred dimension chosen before the scenario/mechanism?
2. **scenario stuffing** — were near-duplicate scenarios counted to exaggerate benefit?
3. **median-body default** — did the analysis ignore relevant variance?
4. **edge-case absolutism** — did a rare scenario silently dominate all ordinary use without consequence analysis?
5. **frequency worship** — was a high-consequence rare event dismissed only because it is rare?
6. **standards worship** — was an external minimum treated as optimum?
7. **bigger-is-better bias** — was more area treated as a benefit without a mechanism?
8. **accessibility exceptionalism** — were real cost/carbon/architectural conflicts suppressed because the intervention was labelled inclusive?
9. **cost veto** — was an important benefit dismissed using unquantified claims of expense?
10. **carbon theatre** — was “carbon” invoked without identifying the material/area mechanism or quantity?
11. **technical hand-waving** — were moisture, fire, security, structure or services assumed solvable without the owning discipline?
12. **architectural hand-waving** — was a material effect dismissed as mere aesthetics rather than analysed?
13. **heritage/style veto** — was historical appearance used to preserve avoidable friction without testing alternatives?
14. **future-proofing theatre** — was speculative infrastructure justified only because future change is imaginable?
15. **product lock-in** — was permanent geometry designed around one current product family?
16. **unknown-as-zero** — were missing cost, site, carbon or technical facts treated as harmless?
17. **incumbency bias** — was current Reference House geometry protected merely because it is already coordinated/drawn/compiled?
18. **sunk-cost bias** — was downstream publication/drawing work used as a reason not to rewind architecture?
19. **false precision** — were qualitative uncertainties disguised by scores, weights or exact thresholds?
20. **institutionalisation** — did provision for occasional assistance/special use make ordinary domestic space needlessly clinical or cumbersome?
21. **local-optimum / global-bloat** — did individually reasonable allowances accumulate into disproportionate whole-house area/cost/carbon?
22. **friction displacement** — did the proposal reduce one scenario's friction by moving equal or greater friction onto another scenario or ordinary use?
23. **authority/evidence collapse** — was a legal/standard duty accidentally treated as empirical proof, or empirical evidence treated as legal authority?

A judgement failing this red-team remains `UNRESOLVED` or must be reformulated.

## 16. Findings and authority consequences

### Findings

1. HFI proportionality cannot be reduced responsibly to one weighted score.
2. Scenario traceability is mandatory: a claimed benefit must name the interactions it improves and any frozen scenarios it worsens.
3. Permanent geometry deserves stronger justification when benefit is weak or evidence sparse, but cheap early provision can be strongly favoured where later retrofit is destructive.
4. Hard technical/statutory constraints are closure gates, not human-consequence classes or negative scoring factors.
5. `UNKNOWN` burdens must remain visible and cannot be silently treated as zero.
6. Alternative generation and dominance testing are necessary controls against spatial generosity becoming self-justifying.
7. Local proportionality is insufficient: the final HFI candidate package must survive a cumulative portfolio test.
8. Architectural effect is a legitimate project trade-off but not a style veto over established human need.
9. HFI may conclude that no intervention, demand-led adaptation or established standard practice is the proportionate answer.
