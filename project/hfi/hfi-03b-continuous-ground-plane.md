## 1. Purpose

The phrase **continuous ground plane** is useful only if it describes usable continuity rather than visual flatness.

A route can look level and still fail because:

- a door sill or track catches a small front caster, walking aid or foot;
- a local lip is difficult to see;
- the approach cross-falls enough to create steering effort;
- water ponds at the weather line;
- a drainage grate or slot catches a cane tip, wheel or narrow heel;
- the external landing is technically level but too small or obstructed to use the door;
- a nominally flush threshold sacrifices weathering and leaks;
- an external route depends on site levels that have never been established;
- a later replacement doorset reintroduces an upstand because the opening did not reserve a workable threshold zone.

Conversely, a robust step-free route does **not** require every surface to be geometrically horizontal. Approved Document M itself distinguishes `level`, `gently sloping` and `ramped`, and expressly warns that accessibility measures must remain compatible with moisture resistance.

The controlling proposition is:

> **A continuous ground plane is a movement-and-water-management system, not a visual promise of flatness.**

And the project corollary is:

> **Level access should be achieved by designing the water out, not by pretending water does not exist.**

This packet tests that corollary against current statutory guidance, specialist housing guidance, contemporary house-building technical guidance and bounded empirical evidence.

---

## 2. Keep five ground-plane problems separate

HFI must not collapse all changes of level into one “threshold” problem.

### 2.1 Ordinary internal dry-plane continuity

Between rooms sharing the same construction and environmental condition, a decorative strip, lip or raised saddle can create movement friction without performing a necessary technical function.

Current RH-09 already selects **no decorative threshold strip** where the same floor build-up genuinely continues through an internal doorway.

This remains a Reference House selection, not yet a universal HFI rule.

### 2.2 Dry-to-dry construction transition

A transition between different internal floor build-ups may need a joint, cover or movement interface even where no water boundary exists.

The HFI question is not “can the joint be deleted?” but:

> **Can the necessary interface remain traversable, legible, durable and replaceable without an avoidable abrupt upstand?**

### 2.3 External weather-line threshold

At an external door the plane crosses a demanding interface among:

- indoor finished floor datum;
- frame/sill/track;
- external landing or paving;
- rain and wind exposure;
- surface-water falls and interception;
- DPC/cavity/waterproofing strategy;
- thermal and airtightness continuity;
- door operation and replacement;
- maintenance/cleaning access.

This is the main focus of HFI-03B.

### 2.4 Internal wet-area drainage plane

A level-access shower demonstrates an important principle: **step-free does not mean zero fall**. Approved Document M defines an installed level-access shower as step-free with no lips/upstands while the floor is laid to shallow falls toward a gulley.

Detailed bathroom/shower geometry remains owned by **HFI-04A** and the relevant moisture/drainage technical disciplines. HFI-03B carries only the general ground-plane inference.

### 2.5 Real-site approach

The generic Reference House can express an intention and reserve a credible threshold condition, but it cannot prove the route from street/parking/gate to entrance without actual:

- plot levels;
- point of access;
- gradients;
- drainage/flood conditions;
- boundary geometry;
- parking/drop-off relationship;
- local public-realm interface.

Real-site approach proof remains real-site work.

# 5. Evidence additions and applicability

These records extend the HFI-01B evidence architecture. Existing `HFI-SRC-N01/N02` remain the parent Part M law/statutory-guidance records and are not duplicated as new authorities.

## `HFI-SRC-GP01` — Approved Document M Volume 1: approach/threshold clauses

- **Source:** HM Government, *Approved Document M: access to and use of buildings, Volume 1: Dwellings*, 2015 edition incorporating 2016 amendments; particularly paragraphs 0.14–0.15, 2.6–2.10, 2.20–2.21, 3.22–3.23 and Appendix A.
- **Date/version:** 2015 edition incorporating 2016 amendments; current Volume 1 listed on GOV.UK and checked 2026-10-18.
- **Jurisdiction / population:** England; new dwellings/material alterations within the document’s stated scope; M4(2)/M4(3) only where the optional requirement applies.
- **Class:** `STATUTORY-GUIDANCE`.
- **Authority / status:** official guidance supporting Part M; not itself an ergonomic optimum.
- **Applicability:** approach-route continuity, gradient categories, accessible thresholds, external landings and the explicit interface with moisture-resistance duties.
- **Key guidance / useful findings:** M4(2) calls for a safe/convenient step-free approach using the shallowest gradient reasonably achievable; private approach routes are level, gently sloping or where necessary ramped. Appendix A defines `level` as gradient not exceeding `1:60`, `gently sloping` as between `1:60` and `1:20`, and `ramped` as between `1:20` and `1:12`. An `accessible threshold` is level or, if raised, no more than `15 mm` total height, with a minimum number of upstands/slopes and upstands above `5 mm` chamfered. M4(2) principal entrance has a level external landing at least `1200 × 1200 mm`; M4(3) uses `1500 × 1500 mm`. Other external doors connected to the dwelling are also subject to accessible-threshold provisions in their respective categories.
- **Critical cross-discipline clause:** paragraph 0.15 explicitly says that when providing a level/ramped approach and level threshold to meet Part M, the dwelling must still satisfy the moisture-resistance requirements C2/C4.
- **Known limits / nonclaims:** `15 mm` is an accessible-threshold statutory-guidance ceiling, not an Ovid’s House preferred upstand; Part M does not by itself prove a chosen threshold’s weather performance; the optional categories are not universally applicable nationally.
- **Access state:** official GOV.UK PDF publicly accessible and text inspected. Screenshot requests were attempted as required by the research workflow, but the source endpoint returned a cache miss; no visual-only fact is relied on.
- **Checked:** 2026-10-18.
- **Canonical location:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-and-use-of-buildings-approved-document-m

## `HFI-SRC-GP02` — DETR 1999 Accessible Thresholds in New Housing

- **Source:** Department of the Environment, Transport and the Regions, *Accessible thresholds in new housing — Guidance for house builders and designers*, The Stationery Office, ISBN 0-11-702333-7.
- **Date/version:** first published 1999; still expressly referenced by Appendix A of current Approved Document M Volume 1 as a source of other acceptable accessible-threshold solutions.

- **Applicability:** the complete external-landing → drainage → sill/threshold → internal-floor interface.

- **Known limits / nonclaims:** published in 1999; specific construction examples and materials are period-specific and must not be copied uncritically into a 2026 technical detail. HFI uses the document primarily for its integrated problem model and durable mechanisms, not as a contemporary product specification.
- **Access state:** original publisher copy is no longer conveniently available online; current Approved Document M cites the document, and bibliographic/archival copies plus indexed text were checked. No unsupported contemporary status is claimed.
- **Checked:** 2026-10-18.
- **Canonical identifier:** ISBN 0-11-702333-7.

# 6. Evidence synthesis

## 6.1 Step-free is not the same as perfectly horizontal

Approved Document M provides a useful vocabulary firewall:

- **level:** no steeper than `1:60`;
- **gently sloping:** between `1:60` and `1:20`;
- **ramped:** between `1:20` and `1:12`.

A successful ground plane can therefore drain and still be step-free.

This matters because “flush” can otherwise become a false binary:

`perfectly flat = accessible`  
`any fall = inaccessible`

The technical evidence rejects that model.

The HFI question is instead:

> **Can water be moved deliberately without creating an unnecessary movement obstacle or steering burden?**

## 6.2 The weather line is a system, not a sill detail

The current regulatory and technical sources converge on an assembly model:

`approach → landing → surface fall → interception/drainage → sill/track → waterproofing/DPC → internal floor`

A low sill without the surrounding system is not a solved accessible threshold.

This matters to Ovid’s House because its existing interface doctrine already says:

`permanent opening → declared fixing/adjustment zone → replaceable doorset → removable architrave`

HFI adds a human-factors constraint to that interface:

> replacement of the doorset must not casually destroy the traversable ground-plane condition that the opening was designed to support.

That remains a later integration question, not a new RH-09 requirement in this packet.

## 6.3 Part M explicitly rejects the false accessibility/moisture trade

Paragraph 0.15 is unusually direct: providing a level/ramped approach and level threshold for Part M must still leave the dwelling compliant with moisture-resistance requirements C2/C4.

That yields a strong project inference:

> **“We need an upstand because rain exists” and “we need no weather defence because accessibility exists” are both incomplete arguments.**

The interface must satisfy both duties.

This is a genuine cross-discipline design problem, not a reason for either discipline to win by default.

## 6.4 A 15 mm regulatory ceiling is not a preferred target

Current Approved Document M defines an accessible threshold as either level or, if raised, no more than `15 mm`, with controlled upstands/slopes and chamfering above `5 mm`.

HFI must preserve four distinctions:

1. `15 mm` is a **maximum within one statutory-guidance definition**, not a recommendation to create a 15 mm obstacle;
2. a lower or level solution may be possible where the water/weather detail supports it;
3. a low but abrupt edge can still matter mechanically to small wheels/casters and to people with reduced foot clearance;
4. removing the edge without resolving drainage/weatherproofing can create a worse building.

Therefore this packet **does not nominate 15 mm as an Ovid’s House target**.

## 6.5 Small obstacles are not operationally trivial

The caster-resistance literature and wheelchair-skills evidence point in the same direction without needing a false universal threshold:

- abrupt obstacles create rolling resistance governed by wheel/load/obstacle geometry;
- a `20 mm` square threshold is large enough to become a separately trained wheelchair skill;
- the skill may require momentum and deliberate caster management.

That does not prove that `10`, `12` or `15 mm` is unacceptable.

It does prove that the architectural phrase “only a small lip” is not an adequate human-factors argument.

## 6.6 The landing is part of the threshold

The door user needs enough stable space to:

- arrive;
- stop;
- reach/operate the door;
- remain clear of leaf movement;
- cross the sill;
- recover/reorient after the crossing.

Current Part M therefore couples threshold requirements with external landing dimensions and door geometry.

HFI should preserve that coupling. It should not audit the sill profile in isolation from the landing and immediate route.

## 6.7 Drainage should be deliberate and maintainable

The legacy DETR guide remains valuable because it treats drainage as a maintainable system:

- exposed landing falls away from the door;
- driven-water risk determines whether drainage is needed;
- slots/channels should not themselves impede mobility;
- drainage should be clearable rather than a hidden one-time construction gesture.

The maintenance principle is important for Ovid’s House. A beautifully flush threshold whose drainage can silt up invisibly or cannot be cleaned without dismantling the doorset is not a robust low-friction interface.

## 6.8 Crossfall is a real human-factors burden

Falls are necessary for water management, but they are not free from the user’s perspective.

Manual wheelchair evidence indicates that cross slopes can add propulsion/steering difficulty, particularly with adverse surface conditions.

This supports a design preference for:

- directing fall **with intent** rather than accepting accidental compound slopes;
- avoiding needless crossfall through the most demanding manoeuvre zone;
- separating the need to drain a surface from the assumption that every movement line should sit on the steepest available fall.

No numeric HFI crossfall target follows yet.

## 6.9 Canopies and exposure reduction are first-order accessibility tools

NHBC’s accessible-threshold guidance includes precipitation protection as part of the solution.

This suggests a useful first-principles move:

> **reduce the amount of water the threshold has to fight before solving the remaining water at the sill.**

A canopy, recess or other geometrically honest weather protection can improve both weathering and human use without creating an upstand.

This is not a universal requirement; exposure, facade composition and site conditions still matter.

## 6.10 The ground plane must survive product replacement

A threshold is not only the installed sill on day one.

For Ovid’s House, the permanent opening and surrounding floor/drainage geometry should avoid unnecessary dependency on one proprietary frame profile if reasonably possible.

Later integration should therefore ask:

- what vertical tolerance does a replacement doorset inherit?
- can the sill/track be replaced without rebuilding the permanent floor edge?
- can drainage remain inspectable/cleanable?
- can a future doorset preserve the accessible threshold without bespoke demolition?

This is strongly aligned with the project’s replaceability doctrine but remains a later architectural-integration question.

# 8. Rejected shortcuts

HFI-03B explicitly rejects the following.

## 8.1 “Flush everywhere”

Rejected. It ignores drainage, moisture, product geometry and legitimate construction transitions.

## 8.2 “15 mm is accessible, therefore use 15 mm”

Rejected. `15 mm` is a statutory-guidance ceiling within a defined threshold geometry, not a preferred design value.

## 8.3 “Any slope breaks level access”

Rejected. Current Part M expressly permits level/gently sloping step-free approaches and defines `level` as a small gradient band, not mathematical zero.

## 8.4 “Waterproofing requires a step”

Rejected as a universal proposition. Technical guidance demonstrates accessible-threshold assemblies that manage water through exposure reduction, falls, drainage, sill/drip geometry and waterproofing coordination.

## 8.5 “Drainage is someone else’s problem”

Rejected. Drainage design belongs to its technical owner, but HFI cannot claim a threshold works while treating the water-management system as irrelevant.

## 8.6 “A line drain solves everything”

Rejected. The channel can itself create rolling/cane/maintenance problems; it also needs discharge capacity, support and cleanability.

## 8.7 “The generic Reference House can prove street-to-door step-free access now”

Rejected. The real site is intentionally absent.

## 8.8 “A level shower proves all thresholds should be flat”

Rejected. A level shower is a useful demonstration of step-free geometry with purposeful falls; detailed wet-room geometry belongs to HFI-04A and moisture/drainage technical work.

# 9. Candidate target architecture for HFI-06

03B does not set project targets. It establishes what later targets must describe.

A credible ground-plane target cannot be only `maximum threshold = X mm`.

It should carry at least:

| Target family | Necessary variables |
|---|---|
| `GP-INTERNAL-DRY` | whether transition is necessary; finished discontinuity; edge profile; loose/replaceable finish effects |
| `GP-EXT-THRESHOLD` | threshold height/profile; number of upstands; approach/departure geometry; sill/track relationship |
| `GP-LANDING` | usable local plane; door-swing interaction; longitudinal/crossfall; surface characteristics |
| `GP-DRAINAGE` | exposure reduction; interception location; fall direction; drain/grate traversability; cleanability |
| `GP-ROUTE` | start/end point; continuity; gradient class; local obstacles; gates/doors; equivalent-route dignity |
| `GP-REPLACEMENT` | tolerance for future doorset/frame replacement without rebuilding permanent fabric |

Every value later admitted must retain:

- source/authority class;
- applicable scenario;
- technical owner;
- whether it is a statutory minimum, evidence-supported range, Ovid’s House target/minimum or site-specific value;
- known weather/site/product assumptions.

| Human-factor objective | Coupled discipline | Conflict that must not be hidden |

| low/level threshold | envelope/moisture | reduced upstand vs water ingress |

| small-wheel traversal | drainage | narrow/smooth grate vs drainage capacity/cleanability |
| weather protection | architecture/facade | canopy/recess usefulness vs facade composition/daylight |
| external landing | site/landscape | usable plane vs plot gradient/boundary/flood strategy |
| replaceable doorset | envelope/thermal/airtightness | product change vs sill/DPC/air-barrier continuity |

| level outdoor access | structure/envelope | usable finished plane vs waterproofing/upstand/deck build-up |

The project assurance ownership matrix remains controlling: HFI diagnoses embodied-use consequences but does not seize technical ownership of moisture, drainage, structure, fire or product certification.

# 11. Reference House questions preserved for HFI-07

03B creates audit questions, not findings.

## Principal entrance

1. What is the real start point of the step-free route once a site exists?
2. Can the entrance landing, door operation and threshold be used as one sequence?
3. What finished threshold profile does the selected doorset actually create?
4. Where does water go before it reaches the sill?
5. Can the drainage feature be cleaned without specialist dismantling?
6. Does the weathering detail preserve the permanent-opening / replaceable-doorset doctrine?

## Garden / courtyard doors

1. Does “substantially flush” survive translation into an actual sill, paving and drainage section?
2. Is the garden route an ordinary high-frequency route deserving stronger continuity than a secondary external door?
3. Does the courtyard geometry create any special ponding/splash/exposure condition?

# 13. Evidence gaps retained deliberately

1. **Current 2026 NHBC detailed clauses:** current annual Standards exist but detailed access is gated; the public 2025 chapters are useful dated technical comparators, not claimed as the current clause text.
2. **Modern empirical threshold-height preference:** the available literature demonstrates obstacle mechanics/skills but does not establish a clean contemporary UK domestic optimum below the statutory ceiling.
3. **Ambulant trip risk for very small doorway lips:** direct domestic evidence is thinner than general obstacle/gait literature; do not manufacture an exact ambulant threshold from analogy.
4. **Mobility-aid diversity:** manual wheelchairs, powered chairs, scooters, walkers and rollators respond differently to lips, slots and surface falls.
5. **Real-site drainage/exposure:** impossible to prove generically.
6. **Product-specific sill geometry:** intentionally deferred until a doorset/system family is technically evaluated.
7. **Construction tolerance/settlement:** nominal drawings do not guarantee as-built finished levels.
8. **Wet-room geometry:** HFI-04A owns bathing/toileting/dressing use; technical moisture/drainage owners close waterproofing.

Absence of a single optimal number is not a reason to fall back to arbitrary visual flatness.

# 14. Packet conclusion

HFI-03B supports the umbrella concept **continuous ground plane**, but narrows what the phrase means.

It does **not** mean:

- perfectly horizontal everywhere;
- no technical joints;
- no sill profile;
- no fall;
- no drainage channel;
- “flush” at any weathering cost.

It means:

> **ordinary movement should encounter no unnecessary discontinuity, and necessary environmental transitions should be resolved as complete, traversable, drainable, weather-resistant and maintainable interfaces.**

The strongest current project formulation remains:

> **Level access should be achieved by designing the water out, not by pretending water does not exist.**

The statement is now supported by the regulatory/technical evidence architecture, but is **not promoted to doctrine by this packet**.

The Reference House’s intended step-free ground floor and substantially flush external thresholds remain **credible intentions, not HFI PASS results**. Detailed threshold profiles, falls, drains, doorsets and site approach are still unresolved and receive no incumbency advantage.

No Reference House rewind is authorised by HFI-03B.
