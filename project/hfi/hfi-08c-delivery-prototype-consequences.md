**Question:** what delivery requirements and physical tests are actually earned by HFI, without converting established practice, `NOT-MODELLED` questions or speculative equipment into a prototype programme?

---

## 1. Result

HFI earns **one delivery verification family** and **no standing physical prototype family**.

The accepted delivery consequence is:

> High-frequency or high-consequence domestic routes, controls and interfaces must be verified as complete actions in their relevant occupied states, using finished/net geometry and the evidence authority appropriate to the claim.

This is implemented as a reusable delivery module with proposed project non-negotiable **N17**.

The packet expressly rejects the idea that every HFI concern deserves a mock-up. Ordinary stairs, conventional doors/hardware, established threshold systems and standard spatial clearances remain design/detail/inspection questions unless a genuinely non-standard assembly creates uncertainty that drawings, established evidence and product information cannot resolve proportionately.

---

## 2. Why a separate delivery module is earned

HFI-07 demonstrated a recurrent representation boundary:

- topology can pass while the finished interface remains unproved;
- a nominal width can exist while strings, guards, frames, ironmongery or fixed projections consume the usable condition;
- a room label can exist while the complete task remains unknown;
- an external-guidance calculation can pass without proving the stronger project preference;
- `NOT-MODELLED` is often the correct architectural state until later design resolution supplies the missing facts.

Those are not reasons to invent earlier geometry. They are reasons to define **when and how later delivery must prove the condition**.

The existing RIBA implementation brief already owns repose, prototypes, tolerance control and commissioning. HFI therefore adds a bounded module rather than creating a parallel delivery system.

## 4. What HFI changes in delivery practice

### 4.1 Nominal → finished / net proof

Where the human-use claim depends on usable space, Stage 4/5 evidence must account for the assembled condition.

Relevant examples include:

- stair going/rise/headroom and actual clear width after strings/guards/handrails;
- door clear opening after frame, ironmongery and stops;
- threshold geometry after weathering/drainage/floor finishes;
- corridor and corner clear width after fixed projections;
- sanitary/wet-room geometry where selected by the brief;
- appliance open-state geometry;
- fixed storage reach/bending geometry where material;
- large-object or replacement-component swept paths where relevant.

This does not mean every example is required on every project.

### 4.2 Dimension → complete action

Selected interfaces are reviewed as sequences such as:

`approach → align/turn → reach/grip → operate/open → pass/transfer → set down/regrip → feedback → close/reset/leave`.

Only foreseeable states that materially affect the task are used. Occupied hands, wet hands, low light, fatigue, temporary injury, assisting another person and carrying an ordinary bulky object are prompts, not universal simultaneous design conditions.

### 4.3 Product/component → installed assembly

A handle force, closer rating or nominal door leaf does not prove the complete door action. Where operation quality matters, final evidence must consider the installed assembly including seals, thresholds, closers, latches and approach geometry.

### 4.4 Isolated threshold → coupled environmental interface

Level transition, drainage, weathering, door operation, movement, durability and approach geometry are reviewed together. HFI does not authorise “flat at any cost” threshold design that creates moisture/envelope failure.

### 4.5 Missing early detail → stage-owned proof obligation

`NOT-MODELLED` survives until the correct project stage makes the fact knowable. Delivery schedules must name the later proof rather than converting uncertainty into invented early geometry.

## 5. RIBA-stage disposition

### Stage 1

Brief the relevant occupants/scenarios, high-frequency/high-consequence actions and present versus future specialist-equipment needs.

### Stage 2

Create the initial Human-Factors Verification Schedule and identify geometry expensive to change later. Apply proportionality before buying permanent area.

### Stage 3

Prove selected actions at coordinated planning resolution: principal routes, stairs/landings, door approaches, critical corners, wet-room clearances, object paths, appliance open states, control locations and set-down/staging where relevant.

Unresolved finished detail may remain explicitly `NOT-MODELLED`.

### Stage 4

Replace nominal assumptions with finished-interface evidence and coordinate tolerances, frames, strings, guards, handrails, ironmongery, thresholds, drainage/weathering, controls and selected product-specific assumptions.

### Stage 5

Protect critical human-use geometry from substitutions/site drift. Measure or operate representative installed conditions where documentary evidence cannot establish the result reliably.

### Stage 6

Confirm the selected as-built conditions and occupant-facing controls. Material deviations remain visible.

### Stage 7

Look for recurring workarounds/choreography in use and feed material findings back into the evidence base without promoting anecdote directly into universal doctrine.

## 6. Prototype disposition

### No new standing HFI prototype

HFI-08C creates **no new P-series prototype** and no generic stair/door/threshold mock-up requirement.

Reasons:

1. the selected 300 mm Reference House stair uses ordinary established construction;
2. mainstream stair/door/threshold questions already have substantial standards, product and professional-practice evidence;
3. HFI’s main unresolved questions are often final geometry/product/site facts rather than novel physical principles;
4. unnecessary prototype creation would confuse research novelty with implementation discipline and consume cost/material without answering a unique uncertainty.

### When HFI can earn a project prototype

A project-specific first article/mock-up becomes justified only where:

- a genuinely bespoke/non-standard interface is selected;
- coupled action sequences cannot be resolved credibly through coordinated design information;
- operating force/feedback depends on a novel assembled mechanism;
- tolerance/movement materially threatens the human-use result;
- repeated fabrication/installation creates workmanship uncertainty; or
- the project makes a stronger performance claim that lacks another proportionate verification route.

If earned, prototype the **complete interface/action**, not a decorative fragment.

## 7. Reference House consequence

HFI-08B selected 300 mm principal-stair going at architectural-source level.

HFI-08C adds no further Reference House geometry.

For later technical development of that stair, the delivery obligation is to prove the finished system:

- final rise/going consistency;
- net clear width after strings/guards/handrails;
- headroom;
- landing geometry;
- handrail/guard continuity and graspability;
- relevant lighting/conspicuity;
- door conflicts;
- carrying/reduced-attention sequence.

The current nominal ≈1000 mm width still does not prove stairlift compatibility. Any later stairlift proposition remains product/family-specific and demand-led.

## 8. Rejected prototype / delivery hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Disposition | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| build a physical stair mock-up because 300 mm was selected | **REJECT** | conventional geometry; established evidence + later finished construction verification answer the question |
| create a generic door-hardware prototype programme | **REJECT** | use product/assembly evidence and installed operation checks unless a bespoke mechanism is selected |
| prototype every flush threshold | **REJECT** | established envelope/threshold design is available; prototype only if a novel coupled detail creates unresolved risk |
| reserve a lift/stairlift as a delivery default | **REJECT** | no adopted target; specialist equipment remains demand-led |
| convert every `NOT-MODELLED` item into Stage-3 geometry | **REJECT** | violates representation lock; later proof must wait for the facts it genuinely needs |
| verify only numeric clearances | **REJECT** | complete-action burden can survive a passing isolated dimension |
| enlarge circulation globally to avoid later checks | **REJECT** | violates proportionality; fix causal bottlenecks |

## 9. Relationship to existing delivery system

The new module complements rather than supersedes the existing RIBA implementation brief.

Existing controls remain authoritative for:

- repose (N15);
- repeated non-standard prototype/first-article work (N06/N13);
- tolerance/workmanship schedules (N12/N14);
- boundary coordination;
- commissioning and maintainability;
- physical prototypes already justified by tectonic uncertainty.

N17 supplies the missing human-use verification lens.

The delivery index now registers the module so it can be issued independently or incorporated into a project-specific implementation brief.

---

## 10. Authority / maturity consequences

This packet changes **delivery requirements**, not architectural doctrine or Reference House geometry.

It does not:

- create a new principle, pattern or strategy;
- create a new prototype family;
- promote a HFI numerical target to universal doctrine;
- claim specialist accessibility compliance;
- broaden compiler proof scope;
- change Reference House maturity;
- change physical-prototype maturity;
- claim external professional validation.

Delivery maturity is not promoted merely because the module exists. It still requires application on a real/project-specific delivery process.
