**Packet:** HFI-04A

**Question:** how should HFI represent and judge bathing, toileting and dressing as complete sanitary-use sequences — including wet conditions, transfers, control/reach, dry staging, assistance and future support — without turning every domestic bathroom into a specialist institutional room or importing wheelchair-category dimensions as universal targets?  
**Boundary:** evidence synthesis and sanitary-use method only. This packet does **not** redesign `G-WC-01` or `U-BATH-01`, set preferred bathroom dimensions, select final fixtures, mandate a bath or shower type, locate final shower/bath controls, specify grab-rail positions, prove M4 compliance, resolve waterproofing/drainage details, create doctrine/patterns, add compiler proof or change publication maturity.

---

## 1. Purpose

A bathroom can contain the required fixtures and still be awkward, unsafe or dependent in use.

The relevant unit is not the room area and not the fixture list. It is the **complete sanitary task sequence**:

`approach → enter → stage aids/items → undress → establish control → transfer/enter → support → wash/toilet → exit/transfer → dry → dress → store/clean → leave`

Different tasks use different parts of that sequence. A WC transfer is not a shower transfer. A bath introduces a persistent rim-crossing event that a level-access shower does not. A person showering independently has different geometry from a person being assisted. A room may be nominally large enough while placing the control, basin, support or dry towel exactly where it forces unstable reach.

The controlling proposition is:

> **Sanitary usability is a property of the complete sequence of approach, control, support, transfer, washing, drying and departure — not of fixture count or room area alone.**

A second proposition follows from HFI-01D:

> **Build permanent capacity where omission is expensive and later need is credible; keep user-specific support equipment demand-led where the architecture can accept it cleanly.**

This packet tests both propositions against current England dwelling guidance, contemporary government research and bounded empirical evidence.

## 4. Existing evidence reused

### 4.1 `HFI-SRC-N02` — Approved Document M Volume 1

Current England statutory guidance keeps three dwelling categories distinct. Categories M4(2) and M4(3) apply only where required by planning permission; their sanitary dimensions and arrangements are therefore **not** universal HFI targets.

Relevant category-2 guidance nevertheless establishes important adaptation mechanisms:

- walls, ducts and boxings to WC/cloakroom, bathroom and shower rooms should be capable of supporting grab rails, seats and adaptations up to the stated loading, with local strengthening where needed;
- a two/three-storey dwelling with three or more bedrooms uses an entrance-storey WC/basin room that also provides an installed or potential level-access shower under the category-2 route;
- the WC room door opens outwards.

Relevant category-3 guidance demonstrates complete-task concerns rather than a single room-area rule:

- sanitary fixtures are paired with clear access zones;
- wheelchair-accessible bathrooms include an installed level-access shower;
- the level-access shower is positioned to permit a seat and controls on adjacent walls;
- a clear turning zone is required within that category.

The important HFI inference is **not** “copy M4(3) everywhere”. It is that fixture use, transfer space, support, control position and future adaptation must be coordinated as one system.

### 4.2 `HFI-SRC-N04` — Approved Document G

Current Approved Document G is a separate technical authority. It includes hot-water/scalding provisions, including the current guidance limiting hot-water supply temperature to a fixed bath to `48°C` through appropriate temperature control.

HFI-04A does not convert that technical duty into an ergonomic control-position rule. It does establish that bath/shower usability cannot be reasoned about while ignoring hot-water safety.

### 4.3 `HFI-SRC-R01` — 2025-published Part M demographic/ergonomic research

The government-commissioned Arup study remains `GOVERNMENT-RESEARCH`, explicitly **not government policy or guidance**.

Its residential qualitative findings are unusually relevant to this packet:

- bathroom space was a frequently reported barrier, including insufficient space for independent use and for an assistant/helper;
- basin approach/reach was frequently reported as problematic;
- level-access showers and wet rooms were commonly identified as helpful;
- among bath users, getting out of the bath appeared as a recurring difficulty;
- bathroom adaptations were the most common adaptations reported by interview participants;
- one chronic/long-term-condition subgroup was more likely than the overall survey average to identify lack of **dry space to store items when showering** as a barrier and extra shower-room space as helpful.

These are self-reported/qualitative findings across a heterogeneous population. They support the existence of sequence/space/staging problems; they do not produce a preferred room dimension.

### 4.4 `HFI-SRC-R07` — Wellecke et al. 2022 occupational-therapist survey

The survey of 144 Australian occupational therapists identified step-free access, large step-free showers and ground-floor bathroom/bedroom provision among important accessible-design features, and highlighted reinforcement for future support/adaptation.

This is expert-practice empirical evidence, not UK regulation and not causal dimensional proof. It is useful mainly as convergence for **early irreversible provision + later adaptation**.

## 5. Additional topic evidence

The following records extend the HFI-01B evidence architecture for this packet.

### `HFI-SRC-SAN01` — Whitehead & Golding-Day 2019 BATH-OUT qualitative study

- **Source:** Phillip J Whitehead, Miriam R Golding-Day, *The lived experience of bathing adaptations in the homes of older adults and their carers (BATH-OUT): A qualitative interview study*.
- **Date/version:** 2019; *Health & Social Care in the Community* 27(6), 1534–1543; DOI `10.1111/hsc.12824`.
- **Population/context:** one English local-authority housing-adaptations service; 21 older adults and five carers drawn from the BATH-OUT feasibility RCT.
- **Class:** `PEER-REVIEWED-EMPIRICAL`.
- **Applicability:** lived effect of replacing a bath/inaccessible shower with level/easy-access showering provision.
- **Useful findings:** themes included ease of use, safety, cleanliness, independence/choice/control and confidence/quality of life. Removing the physical bathing barrier was reported to restore bathing ability and autonomy for participants.
- **Known limits/nonclaims:** small purposively sampled older-adult/carer qualitative cohort; adaptation context rather than new-build optimisation; no universal shower dimension or fixture arrangement follows.
- **Access state:** PubMed metadata/abstract and open full text available.
- **Checked:** 2026-10-19.
- **Canonical location:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31373420/

### `HFI-SRC-SAN02` — King & Novak 2017 bathing-transfer balance experiment

- **Source:** Emily C King, Alison C Novak, *Effect of Bathroom Aids and Age on Balance Control During Bathing Transfers*.
- **Date/version:** 2017; *American Journal of Occupational Therapy* 71(6); DOI `10.5014/ajot.2017.027136`.
- **Population/context:** 26 healthy adults — 12 younger, 14 older — stepping into/out of a slippery bathtub under several support conditions.
- **Class:** `PEER-REVIEWED-EMPIRICAL`.
- **Applicability:** bath-rim crossing, wet/slippery transfer and environmental support mechanisms.
- **Useful findings:** older participants showed greater instability; vertical grab-bar use and wall touch produced the best balance control among tested conditions. A bath mat did not remove the obstacle-crossing problem in the direction perpendicular to the bath rim.
- **Known limits/nonclaims:** small healthy sample, laboratory task and specific support geometry. It supports the mechanism that rim crossing + support geometry matter; it does not establish a domestic rail location or prove all baths unsafe.
- **Access state:** PubMed abstract/metadata.
- **Checked:** 2026-10-19.
- **Canonical location:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29135427/

### `HFI-SRC-SAN03` — Murphy et al. 2006 observed bath transfers

- **Date/version:** 2006; *Journal of the American Geriatrics Society* 54(8), 1265–1270; DOI `10.1111/j.1532-5415.2006.00814.x`.

## 6. Sanitary-use sequence model

HFI-04A adopts a **sequence ledger**, not a numerical friction score.

Relevant events are:

| Event | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `APPROACH` | reach the sanitary room and intended fixture |
| `DOOR` | open/close/lock/unlock without destroying required use space |
| `AID-PARK` | place/retrieve mobility aid, stool, shower chair or similar where applicable |
| `UNDRESS` | remove clothing from a stable position |
| `DRY-STAGE` | keep towel/clothing/essential items reachable and dry |
| `CONTROL` | establish/adjust flow, temperature, flush or other fixed control |
| `ENTER-XFER` | enter shower/bath or transfer to WC/seat |
| `SUPPORT` | use designed support or stable bearing surface where needed |
| `WASH-TOILET` | perform the principal task |
| `EXIT-XFER` | leave/stand/transfer under the changed post-task condition |
| `DRY-DRESS` | dry and redress without avoidable wet-floor/reach choreography |
| `CLEAN-STORE` | dispose/store/clean ordinary items and aids |
| `LEAVE` | exit and restore ordinary room state |

Not every scenario contains every event. The ledger exists to expose where architecture imposes a friction or hazard transition.

**Do not sum event counts into a sanitary-friction score.** A single unstable wet transfer may matter more than several trivial actions.

## 7. Showering

### 7.1 Level access removes one barrier, not all barriers

The evidence converges on a useful but bounded conclusion:

> **Where a shower is intended to support reduced mobility, a level-access entry removes a persistent step/rim-crossing event and has strong adaptation value.**

It does **not** follow that every visually open wet room is low-friction. The complete sequence still depends on:

- drainage falls and local slip condition;
- stable approach/exit;
- screen/curtain/door interaction;
- control reach and feedback;
- support/seat compatibility where needed;
- dry towel/clothing staging;
- room temperature/ventilation/moisture duties;
- whether another person must assist.

HFI-03B remains controlling for movement + water-management compatibility.

### 7.2 Control-before-exposure remains a valid HFI mechanism

The frozen scenario `HFI-SC-USE-05` asks whether the user must enter the direct spray/hazard zone before establishing flow/temperature.

The packet does **not** derive a universal coordinate for shower controls. It does establish the sequence requirement that later target formation must test:

> **A shower-control arrangement should not unnecessarily require the user to become wet, cross an unstable zone or adopt an extended reach merely to establish safe flow/temperature before ordinary showering begins.**

This is a **sequence requirement**, not a mandate that controls sit outside every enclosure. A control reachable from a stable dry-side or threshold position may satisfy it; so may another arrangement that demonstrably preserves stable operation and seated/standing use.

Final control position, operating force, feedback and consistency remain coordinated with HFI-04C and HFI-06.

### 7.3 Dry staging is part of shower use

The post-wash state is materially different from the pre-wash state: the occupant and local floor may be wet, balance reserve may be reduced and dry clothing/towels now matter immediately.

R01 provides direct contemporary evidence that lack of dry storage during showering is a reported barrier for some users. Therefore later bathroom audit must represent `DRY-STAGE` explicitly rather than assume a towel rail or distant linen cupboard solves it.

No universal shelf/rail dimension is derived here.

## 8. Baths

A bath is not rejected by HFI-04A. It provides a different domestic use and may be strongly preferred by some occupants.

But the bath introduces a distinctive, persistent geometry:

`approach → control/reach → rim crossing → lower/transfer → rise/transfer → rim crossing → wet exit`

The evidence supports three mechanisms:

1. **rim crossing is a real balance/transfer event**, especially when wet;
2. **purpose-provided support can improve the transfer**, while improvised reliance on screens/fixtures is a bad substitute;
3. **assistance over/around a bath can create substantial helper posture burden**.

Therefore:

- bath provision must not be treated as equivalent to a level-access shower for reduced-mobility scenarios;
- a bath-control arrangement that requires deep leaning across the bath/hot-water zone remains a legitimate HFI concern;
- support capacity and access beside/at the bath are legitimate later audit fields where the scenario warrants them;
- none of this justifies banning baths or converting every bathroom into a shower room.

The current Reference House receives no bath/shower selection change from this packet.

## 9. Toileting

Toileting must be audited as the whole chain:

`approach → door → aid position → orient/transfer or sit/stand → support → hygiene → flush → basin → exit`

A nominal WC clear zone alone can miss:

- door leaf conflict with a fallen/assisted occupant or manoeuvre zone;
- transfer/support side being blocked by basin/radiator/joinery;
- a mobility aid having no credible place to remain;
- support being absent so the user reaches for basin/radiator/door hardware;
- flush/control requiring awkward twist/reach;
- basin use failing after the WC task even though the WC itself is reachable.

The toilet-transfer evidence is not strong enough to derive a universal rail arrangement. It is strong enough to preserve the distinction:

> **Permanent architecture should be capable of taking correctly located support; the exact support configuration may remain user/task-specific unless an applicable category requires it at completion.**

This distinction is central to avoiding both under-provision and institutional over-fitting.

## 10. Drying and dressing

Dressing is not spare space left over after fixture placement.

For bathing/showering scenarios, the sequence may require:

- a dry reachable location for clothing/towel;
- a stable standing or seated posture for drying/dressing;
- enough local envelope to bend, turn and manage garments without contacting wet/hot/fragile fixtures;
- separation between wet and dry items;
- assistance envelope where assistance is actually part of the scenario.

The evidence base for an exact domestic dressing zone is thinner than the evidence for shower/bath transfer. HFI therefore records **the task**, not an invented dimension.

Later audit should fail neither way:

- it must not ignore dressing because the fixtures fit;
- it must not allocate a speculative universal dressing bay unsupported by evidence.

## 11. Assistance is a distinct two-body state

`HFI-SC-USE-09` and `LC-06` require assistance to remain visible without assuming permanent dependency.

Assistance can change:

- approach/body envelope;
- transfer direction;
- helper reach and trunk posture;
- need for access to the side/front of a fixture;
- privacy and door operation;
- location of dry items and controls.

R01 reports insufficient helper space as a real residential bathroom barrier for some interviewees. SAN06 shows that assisted bath transfers can impose substantial helper posture burden.

The correct conclusion is **not** “provide carer clearances around every fixture”. It is:

> **Where an HFI scenario claims assisted use, test the actual two-person task geometry rather than assuming ordinary one-person clearance scales automatically.**

This preserves domestic character and area discipline while preventing fictitious “assistable” rooms.

## 12. Permanent capacity vs demand-led fittings

This packet finds a particularly strong example of HFI-01D retrofit asymmetry.

### 12.1 Wall/support capacity

Strengthening/pattressing concealed within bathroom/WC construction is:

- relatively cheap and unobtrusive when designed at build time;
- difficult/disruptive to add later behind finished wet-room construction;
- compatible with ordinary domestic appearance when unused;
- able to preserve multiple future support positions rather than pre-select one rail today.

Current M4(2) guidance already encodes this logic through strengthened sanitary walls. Empirical evidence also supports the value of properly provided supports.

Therefore **sanitary-wall support capacity is a strong HFI candidate for early permanent provision**, subject to HFI-06 target formation and structural/wet-room detail ownership.

### 12.2 Rails, seats and specialist equipment

Actual rails/seats can be highly beneficial but their best type/location depends on:

- transfer method;
- side preference;
- body size and capability;
- wheelchair/mobility-aid geometry;
- standing vs seated showering;
- assistance method;
- fixture selection.

Unless an applicable regulatory/design category requires installation at completion, HFI's default posture is therefore:

> **capacity now; user-specific hardware when needed.**

That is not a prohibition on installing rails early for a known user. It is a proportionality rule against speculative specialist hardware where neutral permanent capacity preserves the option more cleanly.

## 14. HFI-07 audit representation

Later Reference House audit should represent sanitary rooms through named task facts rather than a single `bathroom_size` field.

At minimum, where source geometry exists, an audit should be able to ask:

### Room/approach

- door clear opening and swing/state;
- approach/manoeuvre relation;
- internal dry/wet transition;
- mobility-aid parking/staging where scenario-relevant.

### WC

- approach and sit/stand/transfer side(s);
- fixture conflicts with use zone;
- support-capable wall/zone;
- flush and basin continuation of the task.

### Shower

- step/rim condition;
- usable entry/exit path;
- control-operating position before direct spray;
- seat/support capacity where scenario-relevant;
- drainage/slip interface status;
- dry staging.

### Bath

- rim-crossing sequence;
- control reach from stable position;
- support opportunity;
- access for assistance only where claimed.

### Dressing/drying

- reachable dry staging;
- stable local dressing posture or seating option where scenario-relevant;
- conflict with door/fixtures/wet zone.

The representation may use discrete states, geometry and event relationships. **No combined bathroom-friction score is authorised.**

## 16. Rejected hypotheses and shortcuts

HFI-04A explicitly rejects the following:

1. **“A bigger bathroom is an accessible bathroom.”** Area without usable fixture/control/support relationships proves little.
2. **“Copy M4(3) dimensions into every bathroom.”** Regulatory category geometry is not a universal ergonomic optimum.
3. **“Wet room = solved.”** Level entry can coexist with poor drainage, slippery transitions, unreachable controls and no dry staging.
4. **“Baths are inherently bad.”** They introduce a harder transfer geometry for some scenarios but remain a legitimate domestic choice.
5. **“Fit grab rails everywhere now.”** Permanent support capacity has strong option value; actual rail geometry is frequently user-specific.
6. **“A WC clear zone proves toileting works.”** The task includes approach, transfer/sit-stand, support, hygiene, flush, basin and departure.
7. **“Dressing happens elsewhere.”** Post-bathing drying/dressing is part of the sanitary sequence where the room layout requires it.
8. **“Assistance means institutional carer space around everything.”** Assistance must be tested as a bounded two-body scenario, not used to inflate every room.
9. **“Shower controls under the head are merely an annoyance.”** If their position forces entry into spray or unstable reach before control is established, it is a real sequence defect candidate; exact target/location remains to be derived.
10. **“The Reference House already passes because it reserves a shower.”** A reservation without fixture/use/support/drainage/control proof is not a pass.
