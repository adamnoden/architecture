## 1. Why this packet exists

HFI crosses several mature vocabularies—accessibility, inclusive design, ergonomics, human factors, anthropometry, adaptability and the project's own Repose language. If those terms remain loose, later evidence can appear to agree while answering different questions.

This packet therefore freezes **operational HFI meanings** before HFI-01B registers external sources.

These definitions are not claims that standards, regulators or research disciplines use the same wording. HFI-01B must record the authoritative terminology used by each source and its jurisdiction. Where an external source defines a term differently, preserve that source meaning inside its own applicability and state the distinction explicitly rather than silently rewriting either vocabulary.

The governing methodological spine remains:

`person × task × state × route/interface × consequence`

A valid HFI proposition must be traceable to this interaction rather than to a free-floating preference for “more space”, “accessibility” or “ergonomics”.

## 2. Core HFI operational vocabulary

### Friction

**Friction** is an additional demand imposed by the building on a person performing a task, beyond the demand intrinsic to the task itself.

It may appear as additional:

- attention or monitoring;
- body choreography, contortion or careful placement;
- reach, grip or operating force;
- route detour or repeated repositioning;
- object manipulation or rotation;
- hazard exposure;
- foresight, setup or recovery action;
- dependence on assistance that the task would not otherwise require.

A friction is a **research signal**, not automatically a defect. Some demands are unavoidable, technically necessary or proportionate. HFI must test frequency, consequence, alternatives and trade-offs before treating a friction as an architectural failure.

Aesthetic dislike alone is not HFI friction. Nor is the inherent effort of the task—for example lifting a heavy object—unless the building adds avoidable burden to that task.

### Compensation

**Compensation** is a user-side change in posture, pace, route, grip, sequence, attention, assistance or object handling made in order to overcome or work around a building condition.

Examples can include turning sideways, shortening a step, ducking, pre-positioning the body, freeing one hand, taking an indirect route, rotating an object repeatedly or entering a hazard zone before a control can be operated.

Compensation is evidence about the interaction, not proof that the architecture is wrong. A proportionate design may still require occasional compensation. The programme asks whether the compensation is **necessary, foreseeable and proportionate**.

### Sequence of use

**Sequence of use** is the ordered chain through which a person completes an architectural interaction.

For HFI it includes, where relevant:

1. approach / starting position;
2. perception and identification of the route or control;
3. reach / access;
4. operation or transition;
5. immediate feedback or changed condition;
6. continuation to the intended task state;
7. recovery, reversal or fallback if the action fails.

The sequence includes the condition the person is exposed to **before** control is gained. This is why a control can be reachable yet badly located: reach height alone does not describe the action sequence.

### Movement generosity

**Movement generosity** is useful clear geometry beyond a narrowly feasible or minimum condition that makes movement more robust to representative human variance, carrying, assistance, turning, passing or imperfect alignment.

It is measured in **effective usable geometry**, after realistic intrusions and interaction effects—not nominal room or corridor dimensions alone.

Movement generosity does **not** mean that wider or larger is automatically better. Additional area must earn itself through HFI-01D proportionality and later evidence. Compression, intimacy and narrow secondary spaces remain legitimate where they do not impose disproportionate friction.

### Continuous ground plane

A **continuous ground plane** is a connected movement surface between intended same-storey destinations that avoids unnecessary abrupt vertical barriers and allows the relevant route to be negotiated without an avoidable step-over manoeuvre.

“Continuous” does not mean:

- perfectly horizontal everywhere;
- one material or finish everywhere;
- zero drainage fall;
- zero threshold construction depth;
- permission to compromise weathering, moisture or thermal performance.

Gradients, drained interfaces and technically necessary transitions may still form part of a continuous route if the movement remains coherent and proportionate. Moisture and drainage retain their own technical authority.

### Principal route

A **principal route** is the primary ordinary-use route by which an HFI task is expected to occur between principal destinations.

It is defined by use, not by visual grandeur. A ceremonial axis, formal promenade or compositional route is not automatically the HFI principal route if occupants normally use another all-weather path.

Where an inclusive/accessible route is needed, HFI should prefer equivalent and dignified participation in the principal ordinary-use system rather than a conspicuously inferior service route, unless a different route is genuinely necessary and proportionate.

### Object route

An **object route** is the complete path by which a representative object reaches or leaves its intended destination, including the handling geometry required to translate, rotate, tilt, pause and pass through intermediate spaces.

An isolated door clearance does not prove an object route. The route includes:

- the object's representative envelope;
- human handling position where relevant;
- corners and turning space;
- doors and door leaves;
- stairs / landings where applicable;
- temporary staging or rotation space;
- destination entry and final positioning.

Object routes are distinct from plant-maintenance withdrawal routes, although they may share physical geography.

### Visitability

**Visitability** is the ability of a visitor to arrive, enter and participate in the principal entrance-level life of the dwelling, including access to an appropriate entrance-level WC where that is part of the applicable brief.

HFI uses visitability as a **bounded entrance-level category**. It does not imply:

- whole-dwelling wheelchair usability;
- independent use of every room;
- lifetime adaptability;
- satisfaction of a particular statutory category merely because the word is used.

Real-site approach levels remain real-site facts.

### Ordinary usability

**Ordinary usability** is the ability of the relevant occupant population to carry out repeated domestic tasks safely, intelligibly and with proportionate effort, attention and choreography under representative conditions.

“Ordinary” refers to **foreseeable recurring domestic use**, not a median body or one idealised able-bodied user.

Usability is stronger than mere geometric access. A space can be reachable yet awkward to operate in; a control can be technically reachable yet force a poor sequence of use.

Ordinary usability is also narrower than universal suitability: HFI does not claim that every person must be able to perform every task without adaptation or assistance.

### Adaptability

**Adaptability** is the capacity of the dwelling to accommodate a foreseeable change in occupant need or use later through bounded, intelligible work without disproportionate damage to permanent fabric or wholesale architectural reconstruction.

Adaptability may be achieved by:

- making an irreversible geometric allowance early where later correction would be difficult;
- providing a credible future conversion route;
- reserving attachment/support capacity where justified;
- leaving replaceable components demand-led rather than installing them speculatively.

Adaptability is not the same as current accessibility or current usability. It must also survive proportionality: speculative provision that consumes excessive area, carbon, cost or technical complexity is not automatically good adaptation.

### Wheelchair usability

**Wheelchair usability** means that a wheelchair user can actually perform the relevant residential tasks in the relevant space or route, not merely cross one threshold or fit within one nominal dimension.

HFI keeps this separate from visitability and ordinary usability because the necessary route, turning, transfer, reach and assistance conditions may be materially different. It is not a claim of whole-dwelling M4(3) or other regulatory compliance unless the applicable technical route independently proves that.

### Maintenance access

**Maintenance access** is access for inspection, servicing, repair, replacement and associated tools/components. It remains primarily owned by maintenance geography and the relevant technical discipline.

HFI intersects maintenance only where the same route/interface is also part of ordinary occupation or where human/object handling creates a directly relevant embodied-use question. HFI does not absorb maintenance engineering into this programme.

## 3. Adjacent disciplines and design frames

These terms are deliberately distinguished rather than treated as synonyms.

| Term | HFI working use | What HFI must not infer |
|---|---|---|
| **Accessibility** | The reduction or removal of barriers that prevent people with differing capabilities, including disabled people, from approaching, entering, using or leaving relevant parts of the dwelling. Regulatory accessibility remains source/jurisdiction specific. | That one accessibility standard exhausts human factors; that a regulatory pass proves low-friction ordinary usability; or that HFI may redefine statutory categories. |
| **Inclusive design** | A design approach that deliberately considers human diversity and seeks to avoid unnecessary exclusion or segregated use. | That every need can be satisfied by one undifferentiated solution, or that inclusion requires maximal area everywhere. |
| **Universal design** | An aspirational evidence/design tradition seeking use by the widest practicable range of people without unnecessary special adaptation or stigmatizing separate arrangements. | Literal universality, zero assistance, or a project rule that every space must serve every body/state. |
| **Ergonomics** | The fit between people, tasks, tools/interfaces and immediate physical/cognitive demands; especially useful for reach, force, posture, control placement and sequence of use. | That an ergonomic optimum automatically becomes an architectural target without spatial, technical and proportionality review. |
| **Human factors** | The broader study of person × task × environment/system interaction, including workload, error, feedback, comprehension, degraded states and system consequences. HFI borrows this systems frame for domestic architecture. | That Ovid's House is making clinical, occupational-safety or general system-engineering claims beyond the architectural scope stated here. |
| **Anthropometrics** | Descriptive measurement and distribution of human body dimensions used as an input to route, reach, clearance and interaction questions. | That a percentile is a design answer; that the 95th percentile should automatically become a minimum; or that one population dataset represents everyone. |
| **Life-course design** | Designing irreversible geometry and future change paths with ageing, temporary impairment, care and changing household needs in view. | That every possible future adaptation should be installed on day one, or that ageing is equivalent to disability. |
| **Repose / low vigilance** | The parent architectural objective that ordinary domestic background conditions should minimise unnecessary vigilance. HFI develops its embodied/operational side. | That HFI owns the whole Repose programme, environmental comfort, neuroarchitecture or every source of attentional demand. |

### Relationship among them

HFI is **broader than statutory accessibility** because it also studies ordinary task friction, carrying, object movement, temporary states and sequence of use.

HFI is **narrower than human factors in general** because it is confined to architectural/domestic interactions that Ovid's House can legitimately influence.

HFI uses **anthropometrics and ergonomics as evidence inputs**, not as independent architectural authorities.

HFI treats **inclusive/universal design as relevant traditions**, not as claims that every project decision can be universalised.

HFI remains a development of **Repose**, not a competing philosophy.

## 4. Method-axis definitions

The programme's `person × task × state × route/interface × consequence` model is interpreted as follows.

### Person

The relevant human characteristics that materially affect the interaction: body dimensions, reach, strength, grip/dexterity, balance, vision, mobility mode, age-related capability or need for assistance.

A `person` is not a stereotype. Later scenarios should vary only characteristics relevant to the task rather than create caricatured user personas.

### Task

The goal the occupant or visitor is trying to achieve: enter, ascend, carry, turn, bathe, reach, operate, move an object, assist another person and so on.

The task must be described by its actual purpose rather than by the building component encountered on the way. “Take laundry downstairs” is a task; “use stair” is only one part of its route.

### State

A temporary or contextual condition that changes capability, attention or hazard while the person performs the task—for example wet hands, occupied hands, fatigue, low light, carrying a child, temporary injury or partial system failure.

State is separate from stable body characteristics so HFI does not treat temporary impairment or ordinary distraction as a different class of person.

### Route / interface

A **route** is the connected sequence of spaces, openings, level changes and turning conditions through which the task proceeds.

An **interface** is a bounded place where the person must transition or act on the building—for example a threshold, handle, shower control, window, switch or stair tread/landing condition.

Many tasks contain both.

### Consequence

The material outcome if the interaction demands compensation or fails: trivial inconvenience, repeated attentional burden, inability to complete the task, loss of dignity/equivalence, damage to an object/building, or injury risk.

HFI-01D will later determine how consequence combines with frequency, irreversibility and project burden. HFI-01A does not rank those consequences yet.

## 5. Equivalent and dignified use

**Equivalent use** means that a person can participate in the same ordinary domestic function without being needlessly diverted into a materially inferior mode of access or control solely because of their body/capability.

Equivalent does not mean geometrically identical. A different mechanism or route may be legitimate where it gives genuinely comparable access and is technically or spatially justified.

**Dignified use** is used narrowly in HFI to reject unnecessary architectural segregation or conspicuous service treatment—for example making the only workable mobility route feel like back-of-house circulation when the principal route could reasonably have accommodated it.

This is not an aesthetic veto on visible accessibility measures. A visible handrail, ramp, lift or support can be completely dignified when it is honestly integrated and useful.

## 6. HFI scope boundary

### HFI owns research/diagnosis of

- anthropometric variance relevant to domestic geometry;
- embodied movement and fall-related interaction questions;
- horizontal and vertical route geometry as experienced in use;
- clearances, turning, passing and local pinch points;
- level changes and movement continuity;
- furniture/object and repeated domestic-task logistics;
- reach, grip, operating effort and one-handed use where architecture fixes the interface;
- sequence of use, mapping and ordinary control feedback;
- bathing/toileting/dressing geometry where fixed architecture materially constrains use;
- fixed kitchen/storage/work-zone geometry where it materially constrains use;
- life-course and temporary-impairment consequences of irreversible architectural choices;
- embodied/comprehension friction directly tied to routes, level changes, controls and ordinary actions;
- route/use equivalence and unnecessary segregation where HFI conditions are involved.

HFI owns **research and diagnosis**, not the architectural authority to change every item above. Accepted findings return to the existing owners identified in HFI-00A and the project assurance matrix.

### HFI intersects but does not own

| Concern | Legitimate HFI question | Owning closure route |
|---|---|---|
| thermal comfort / overheating | Does heat materially change a representative human-use scenario? | building physics / energy owner and RH-11 route |
| acoustics | Does noise alter comprehension/use or force an HFI-relevant route/control response? | acoustic / MEP owner and RH-11 route |
| daylight / glare | Does visibility materially affect HFI route/control legibility? | building physics / environmental owner |
| fire / escape | Does proposed HFI geometry conflict with escape, guarding or fire strategy? | fire / Building Control owner |
| structure | Does an HFI geometric proposal require structural change? | structural owner |
| moisture / weathering / drainage | Can the desired level/threshold condition be made physically robust? | envelope / drainage / building-physics owners |
| services / electrical safety | Can a control/interface be placed and operated as HFI proposes without violating technical duties? | MEP / electrical owner |
| security | Can accessible/openable hardware and HFI use coexist with security duties? | architect + Part Q/product/security owner |
| maintenance / replacement | Does ordinary human/object circulation share or conflict with a maintenance route? | maintenance geography + technical owner |
| product performance | Does the architectural interface allow a plausible product family? | later technical/product design |
| site approach / parking / levels | What HFI route should a real project test? | real-site design and statutory/access route |
| cost / carbon / area | Is an HFI intervention proportionate to its project burden? | HFI-01D informs; client/architect/QS/LCA owners close real-project trade-offs |

This table operationalises the project-assurance invariant:

> **Nothing may be declared out of scope without identifying where it is in scope.**

### Outside HFI unless a fixed architectural interaction creates a bounded handoff

- clinical diagnosis or medical treatment;
- general wellbeing claims detached from an architectural mechanism;
- thermal, acoustic, IAQ or daylight performance as independent disciplines;
- structural sizing and capacity;
- fire engineering / life-safety signalling design;
- drainage capacity and waterproofing design;
- detailed MEP/electrical design;
- threat/security strategy beyond the architectural interface;
- product selection and detailed product ergonomics;
- movable furniture or decorative interior-design specification;
- construction-site ergonomics except where already owned by workmanship/prototype programmes;
- maintenance engineering as a separate field;
- invented real-site facts.

## 7. Statutory and project-language firewall

HFI working categories are **not aliases for regulatory categories**.

In particular:

- `visitability` is not automatically `M4(1)`;
- `ordinary usability` is not automatically `M4(2)`;
- `wheelchair usability` is not automatically `M4(3)`;
- `continuous ground plane` is not a compliance statement about an approach, ramp or threshold;
- `movement generosity` is not a hidden project minimum;
- `adaptability` is not evidence that a future adaptation is technically feasible;
- `accessible` is not interchangeable with `low-friction`.

HFI-01B must preserve the exact meanings and applicability of statutory/guidance terms rather than forcing them into this project vocabulary.

## 8. Anti-drift interpretations rejected now

| Rejected interpretation | Reason |
|---|---|
| “Any annoyance is friction, therefore a defect.” | Friction is a research signal; materiality and proportionality still need evidence. |
| “If the user compensates, the building has failed.” | Some compensation is unavoidable or proportionate. HFI studies whether it is unnecessary. |
| “Movement generosity means bigger rooms/corridors.” | It means useful effective margin for an identified task/variance; extra area must earn itself. |
| “Continuous ground plane means perfectly flat and no threshold construction.” | Movement continuity must coexist with gradients, drainage, weathering, thermal and other technical duties. |
| “The principal route is the architecturally grand route.” | HFI principal route is defined by ordinary task use. |
| “If an object fits through the narrowest door, its route works.” | Route feasibility includes turns, handling, landings, doors and staging. |
| “Visitability means wheelchair-liveable house.” | Visitability is deliberately entrance-level and bounded. |
| “Accessibility pass equals ordinary usability.” | HFI tests repeated task interaction beyond formal access. |
| “Adaptability means pre-install every future adaptation.” | Demand-led replaceable measures may be more proportionate than speculative provision. |
| “95th-percentile body dimensions are automatic project minima.” | Anthropometric distributions are evidence inputs, not targets. |
| “The current 280 mm stair going / 1300 mm gallery / other RH dimensions are HFI reference targets.” | HFI-00 froze them only as current baseline facts. |
| “A user anecdote establishes evidence.” | It establishes a trigger hypothesis to research. |
| “HFI can settle drainage, fire, structure, acoustics, security or MEP conflicts itself.” | Those disciplines retain their own authority and closure routes. |
| “A downstream drawing or compiler representation can settle an HFI architectural question.” | Drawings/compilation may expose the issue; accepted material findings rewind the architectural owner first. |

## 9. Scope tests for later packets

Before admitting a question into HFI research, ask:

1. **Is there a person performing a plausible domestic task?**
2. **Does an architectural route, geometry or fixed interface materially affect that task?**
3. **Can the demand be described as friction, compensation, exclusion, hazard exposure or adaptability burden without inventing a medical claim?**
4. **Is HFI actually the research owner, or only an interface to another discipline?**
5. **If another discipline owns technical closure, is that owner named?**
6. **Would the question survive if the current Reference House geometry were replaced by another house?** If not, keep it project-specific rather than promote it into general doctrine.
7. **Is the proposition still only a trigger?** If yes, do not express it as a preferred dimension or requirement before the evidence stages.

A question that fails these tests should be excluded, reframed or handed to the appropriate owner.

## 11. Findings and authority consequences

### Findings

1. HFI requires three distinct levels of language: **parent architectural objective** (Repose), **research disciplines/traditions** (accessibility, inclusive/universal design, human factors, ergonomics, anthropometrics) and **HFI operational terms** (friction, compensation, sequence, routes, usability/adaptability categories).
2. `Friction` is the central diagnostic term, but it must remain a signal rather than a verdict until evidence and proportionality are applied.
3. `Compensation` is the observable/user-side response that makes many frictions legible; it is not synonymous with failure.
4. `Movement generosity` is task-specific effective margin, not a general instruction to enlarge the house.
5. `Continuous ground plane` is a movement concept, not authority to override water, thermal or threshold engineering.
6. `Principal route` is an ordinary-use concept and must not be confused with a formal architectural promenade.
7. `Visitability`, ordinary usability, wheelchair usability and adaptability answer different questions and must remain separate throughout the programme.
8. The project-assurance ownership matrix now provides the required destination for every major discipline excluded from HFI technical ownership.

### Rejected hypotheses / interpretations

The anti-drift table above is binding for later HFI packets unless an explicit evidence-backed revision is recorded. In particular, HFI does not begin from “bigger is better”, “step-free at any cost”, “one percentile solves human variance” or “regulatory accessibility equals usability”.
