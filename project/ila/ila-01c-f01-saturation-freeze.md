# 1. Decision

F01 is **FROZEN** at discovery stage, but the model frozen here is materially different from the model entering this packet.

The adversarial pass successfully falsified two tempting simplifications:

1. there is no defensible single architectural genealogy in which Archigram leads to Duffy, Duffy leads to Brand, and Brand supplies the first architectural account of destructive interaction between differently paced layers; and
2. Ovid's House cannot plausibly position either the general `destructive coupling` mechanism or the physical replacement-destruction test as a conceptual discovery of this project.

The strongest bounded conclusion is instead:

> **By the late 1960s, architects and building-systems researchers were already treating unequal component lives, independent replacement, physical dependence and the destruction caused by fixed integration as one architectural problem. Duffy/DEGW subsequently produced an especially influential general layer model for buildings; Japanese housing research independently operationalised life-cycle separation at component and interface level; Brand later synthesised Duffy's layers with an explicit slow/fast systems hierarchy. Ovid's House inherits this field rather than originating it.**

This is a better result for the project than the cleaner genealogy with which ILA began. It removes a false originality risk and leaves a more precise question for later stages: what, if anything, is distinctive about Ovid's **particular synthesis, domestic execution discipline and governing restraints**, rather than the underlying proposition that differently lived parts should not be destructively bound together?

# 2. What changed after ILA-01B-F01

ILA-01B had already rejected the linear `O'Neill → Duffy → Brand → Ovid` story and provisionally proposed two broad branches converging in Brand:

- an architectural/workplace branch centred on Duffy/DEGW; and
- a systems/ecology branch running through Simon / hierarchy theory / O'Neill.

That remains useful, but it was still too neat on the architectural side.

ILA-01C found two sources/traditions that materially change the family:

## 2.1 Helmut C. Schulitz / SCAG

The March 1971 *Architectural Forum* article **`Structure for Change and Growth`** by Helmut C. Schulitz describes SCAG, a residential building system developed in 1968 for Los Angeles.

It is not merely a flexibility project. It explicitly states all of the following before Duffy's published layer diagram, ORBIT, CHS or Brand:

- buildings commonly have useful lives of roughly 40–60 years while component life cycles range much more widely;
- fixed construction is uneconomical when the obsolescence of the entire package is governed by its shortest-lived components;
- component replacement cycles should therefore be allowed to differ;
- uniform adaptability is wasteful because different parts require different degrees and frequencies of change;
- short-cycle units should be capable of easy replacement;
- implementation requires autonomous components with as little physical dependence on other components as possible;
- components should be independently exchangeable;
- disassembly/reuse should not require alteration or destruction of other elements;
- the supporting structure should have the longest lifespan of the component hierarchy.

Primary / near-primary records:

- Helmut C. Schulitz, `Structure for Change and Growth`, *Architectural Forum*, vol. 134 no. 2, March 1971, pp. 60–63. Scanned issue: https://www.usmodernist.org/AF/AF-1971-03.pdf
- *Architectural Forum* 1971 annual index confirms Schulitz, title and page 60: https://www.usmodernist.org/AF/AF-1971-12.pdf
- An earlier German publication exists as Helmut C. Schulitz, `SCAG — Anpassungsfähigkeit in der Architektur`, *Bauen + Wohnen*, 24 (1970), pp. 389–393. Proposition-level comparison with the 1971 English text is deferred under #646 rather than guessed.

### Consequence

SCAG materially predates the current discovery model's strongest C02 formulations. It proves that **differential component lives + shortest-lived-component obsolescence + independent replacement + minimised physical dependency + selective rather than universal adaptability** were already combined in an architectural proposition by 1971.

The project therefore must not imply that Brand, ORBIT, CHS or Ovid originated that combination.

SCAG is a clear `EARLIER_ANTECEDENT` candidate for both ILA-C01 and ILA-C02, with `NO_DOCUMENTED_PROJECT_CONTACT` before ILA.

It also materially narrows several candidate Ovid-delta claims discussed below.

## 2.2 Century Housing System / Utida / Japanese building-production lineage

The **Century Housing System (CHS)** began as a Japanese government-led research initiative in 1980–82 and was described by Yositika/Yoshichika Utida in 1983 as a systems approach to component coordination.

Strong secondary reconstruction of the primary system shows that CHS:

- divided a dwelling into component groups partly by durability/life expectancy;
- used five broad durability bands from a few years to roughly a century;
- treated the component group as including the component, its external connections and the work needed to design, construct and maintain it;
- used interface matrices to classify relationships between component groups;
- made the ability to change a component without damaging other parts a central criterion;
- held that long-life components should not be damaged when short-life components are replaced;
- ordered installation according to durable life;
- treated services as a particularly important source of conflicting relationships;
- developed interface, sequencing and access rules to make renewal possible;
- led in practice to measures such as raised floors / accessible service routes and rules against embedding shorter-lived services in longer-lived construction.

Key records:

- Robert Schmidt III, Toru Eguchi & Simon Austin, `Lessons from Japan: A Look at Century Housing System`, DSM 2010: https://www.designsociety.org/publication/30379/lessons_from_japan_a_look_at_century_housing_system
- Yositika Utida, `Century Housing System — A Systems Approach to Component Coordination`, *Micro IF* 3211(4), 1983, p. 41 — bibliographic identity recovered through later scholarly sources; primary proposition-level reading remains for ILA-02.
- Stephen Kendall & Jonathan Teicher, *Residential Open Building* (2000), including the CHS `durable years` / `disentangling` account: https://www.uceb.eu/DATA/CivBook/43.%20Residential%20Open%20Building%2C%20Stephen%20Kendall%20and%20Jonathan%20Teicher.pdf
- Shuichi Fukao, `The History of Developments toward Open Building in Japan`, 2008, for KEP/CHS institutional context.
- Kazunobu Minami, `The Efforts to Develop Longer Life Housing with Adaptability in Japan`, *Energy Procedia* 96 (2016), pp. 662–673, DOI `10.1016/j.egypro.2016.09.124`.

### Consequence

CHS is an independent **domestic** antecedent that gets very close to Ovid C02 at mechanism and implementation level. It falsifies any easy claim that Ovid's distinct contribution is simply:

- transferring differential-life thinking from offices into housing;
- noticing that replacement should not damage longer-lived material;
- turning layer theory into interface rules;
- protecting structure from short-lived services;
- or providing service access because services change faster than fabric.

Those propositions already existed in a serious housing research programme decades earlier.

The historical scholarship around Utida further suggests that Japanese building-production research had begun organising modular/open-system thinking around building-part life cycles through the 1960s–70s. The exact proposition-level date is not sufficiently established here, so a pre-CHS priority claim is **not** made. The unresolved primary-source question is durable under #648.

# 3. Revised F01 topology

F01 should no longer be pictured as one or two neat lines progressing toward Ovid.

The discovery-saturated topology is better represented as several partly independent traditions that converge around a recurring mechanism.

## 3.1 Early adaptability / replaceability field — multiple convergences

### Habraken / SAR

N. John Habraken's support/infill distinction (1961/62 onward) establishes a durable collective support and more changeable resident-controlled infill. This is central to F02 because the primary problem is **control, levels and allocation of change**, not a quantified rate hierarchy.

F01 retains Habraken as a neighbouring antecedent, not the source of C02.

### Systems-building traditions — CLASP / SCSD / related programmes

1960s systems-building work created coordinated subsystems, interchangeable components, service distribution strategies and the expectation of later change. SCSD is especially relevant as a demonstrable system in which structure, partitions, services and environmental systems were coordinated for change.

The F01 search did not find a sufficiently explicit pre-SCAG proposition combining **different component lives** with **replacement dependency/destruction**. These remain important contextual antecedents rather than the strongest C02 source.

### Archigram / Plug-In City

Plug-In City (1964) explicitly assigned different expected lives to support infrastructure and plug-in components. This is a genuine `EARLIER_ANTECEDENT` for differentiated architectural lifespans.

However, no proposition-specific evidence currently proves `Archigram → Duffy` as a documented derivation of Duffy's layer model. Later scholarship reasonably notes conceptual echoes, but the ILA contract forbids upgrading broad intellectual debt or resemblance into a causal edge.

The edge remains `UNRESOLVED / TENTATIVE` until a primary or strong historical source demonstrates that the rate/lifespan proposition itself was transmitted.

### Propst / Action Office / facility based on change

Robert Propst's work treats the office as a facility continually altered by changing organisations, technology and work. It is a meaningful C01 antecedent and part of the North American office context Duffy encountered.

This pass found no comparably strong Propst articulation of the C02 rate/dependency mechanism. Keep Propst out of the core C02 lineage unless ILA-02 proves otherwise.

### Cedric Price / planned obsolescence

Price made planned obsolescence, change and short-lived architecture explicit design material. This is important to the history of architectural impermanence but is not, on current evidence, a stronger source for **differentiated component lives and destructive coupling** than SCAG.

Primary ownership therefore remains F02 unless a specific F01 proposition emerges.

### Metabolism / Nakagin and related core-capsule models

Metabolist work clearly distinguished longer-lived collective frameworks from shorter-lived replaceable elements. Nakagin Capsule Tower is an especially visible precedent: durable cores + intended replaceable capsules.

Its operational history is also a counterpoint: intended replaceability did not become a routine replacement practice. Access, ownership, coordination, economics and supply matter alongside a detachable joint.

Use as `PRECEDENT_EXEMPLAR` / potential `COUNTERPOINT`, not as evidence that the same theoretical chain led to Duffy or Ovid.

### KEP / Japanese open-component research

The Kodan Experimental Housing Project began in the 1970s and separated housing into structural/exterior/interior/sanitary/service subsystems with interface coordination and open components. It is an important predecessor to CHS and belongs strongly in F02/F03 as well.

Current evidence does not establish that KEP itself had CHS's explicit durability bands and replacement-damage classification. Do not back-project the mature CHS proposition into KEP without primary evidence.

### Schulitz / SCAG

SCAG is currently the **earliest strong proposition-level C02 antecedent located in the bounded English-accessible search**.

It combines:

`different component life cycles → avoid whole-building obsolescence at the shortest cycle → allow different replacement cycles → minimise physical dependence → independent exchange → no alteration/destruction of other elements → allocate adaptability selectively because universal adaptability is expensive`.

This is too close to C02 to remain a peripheral precedent.

The wording `earliest located in this bounded search` is mandatory. It is not a universal priority claim.

## 3.2 Duffy / DEGW — general building-layer model and direct Brand source

Duffy remains important despite SCAG and CHS.

His distinct historical role in F01 is not that nobody before him understood unequal lives. It is that Duffy/DEGW developed a particularly portable **general building-layer model** — Shell / Services / Scenery / Set — around the temporal and economic life of buildings, and that Brand explicitly takes this architecture-specific schema forward.

Relevant evidence now includes:

- Duffy & Worthington's original shell/scenery/set diagram is reported as first published in 1972 (`Design for Changing Needs`, *Built Environment*, pp. 458–463);
- *Planning Office Space* (Architectural Press, 1976) develops shell/services/scenery/set as distinct planning timescales;
- later Duffy formulations explicitly describe buildings as layers of components with different longevity;
- DEGW's ORBIT study (completed 1983) makes independent updating a design value and identifies inextricable integration of functional, structural and aesthetic systems as a cause of premature office obsolescence.

Strong historical study:

- Amy Thomas, `Architectural consulting in the knowledge economy: DEGW and the ORBIT Report`, *The Journal of Architecture* (2019/20), DOI `10.1080/13602365.2019.1698639`.

Duffy therefore remains a high-priority F01 dossier candidate because:

1. he is directly relevant to the current manuscript;
2. Brand explicitly derives his layer schema from Duffy;
3. his model is general, transmissible and directly concerned with building management over time;
4. the project must distinguish what Duffy already said from later Brand/Ovid formulations.

But Duffy must not be described as the unique origin of architectural rate-of-change thinking.

## 3.3 Japanese building-production / CHS branch — independent domestic operationalisation

The Japanese line now deserves its own branch rather than being treated as a side precedent to Open Building.

A bounded working topology is:

`post-war building-element / industrialisation research → open systems / KEP → CHS → later Skeleton-Infill / NEXT21 and associated research`

The exact source-to-source edges and dates inside this branch require primary work before a formal lineage graph.

What is already sufficiently clear for discovery is that CHS independently joins:

- component durability bands;
- coordination of installation and interfaces;
- replacement without damaging longer-lived components;
- service accessibility;
- physical separation of different-lived systems;
- practical housing production.

This makes CHS a major falsification source for simplistic Ovid-delta claims.

## 3.4 Systems / ecology branch

The systems/ecology branch remains useful, but its job is narrower after the architectural search.

### Herbert A. Simon

`The Architecture of Complexity` (1962) supplies near-decomposability: complex systems can remain connected while interactions are stronger within subsystems than across them. Time-scale separation also appears in the systems reasoning around hierarchy.

Ovid project history shows Simon was adopted only after C02 existed, so he is not a documented formative source for Ovid.

### Allen & Starr / ecological hierarchy

T. F. H. Allen & Thomas B. Starr, *Hierarchy: Perspectives for Ecological Complexity* (1982), develops hierarchy theory in ecology and the relation of processes operating at different scales/rates.

This is part of the intellectual context behind the later O'Neill work, but F01 does not need to prove every ecological genealogy edge before continuing.

### O'Neill et al.

R. V. O'Neill et al., *A Hierarchical Concept of Ecosystems* (1986), makes differential process rates central to hierarchy in ecosystems.

Brand explicitly cites this work in the `Shearing Layers` discussion and quotes its slow/fast component proposition. That makes O'Neill important as a **documented source for Brand's systems interpretation**, not as an architectural source and not as a source of Duffy.

### Consequence

The systems/ecology branch explains Brand's slow/fast hierarchy, but it is **not necessary to explain the existence of architectural rate/coupling thinking itself**, which is independently well established before O'Neill.

Therefore F01 should not overstate the importation story. Architecture did not need ecology in order to discover unequal component lives or physical independence. Brand's value is the synthesis and general systems interpretation.

# 4. Brand's revised position

Stewart Brand's 1994 *How Buildings Learn* remains an essential F01 source, but for a more precise reason than `popularised shearing layers`.

Brand explicitly:

- credits Duffy's four building layers;
- expands the schema to Site / Structure / Skin / Services / Space Plan / Stuff;
- invokes O'Neill's ecological hierarchy to explain slow and fast rates;
- argues for **slippage** between differently paced systems;
- warns that when systems of different rates are tightly embedded, the slow constrains the fast and the fast tears up the slow.

That last point is very close to Ovid's C02 mechanism.

Brand is therefore provisionally best treated as a **synthesis node**, not an origin node:

`Duffy architectural layer model + O'Neill slow/fast systems hierarchy → Brand's shearing/slippage synthesis`.

The arrow types must be proven source-by-source in ILA-02 before a final graph.

The architectural prior art from SCAG and CHS means Brand did not originate the broader proposition that different-lived components should be physically independent. His contribution is the particular synthesis, memorable six-layer framing and general slow/fast interaction account.

# 5. Later research that narrows Ovid's candidate delta

Later building research repeatedly turns layer theory into dependency analysis.

## E. Sarah Slaughter — 2001

`Design strategies to increase building flexibility`, *Building Research & Information* 29(3), 208–217, DOI `10.1080/09613210010027693`.

Slaughter's renovation research treats the **interrelationships among systems** as a determinant of renovation difficulty and shows that design strategies can modify those relationships to make later change easier.

This supports the view that dependency, not merely component lifespan, is the operational issue.

## Russell & Moffatt / IEA Annex 31 — 2001

The `lifetime compatibility` guidance includes the direct rule not to encapsulate or strongly interconnect short-lifetime components with longer-lifetime components.

That proposition is effectively the mature professional version of C02's mechanism.

## Schmidt III / Deamer / Austin — 2011

`Understanding Adaptability through Layer Dependencies`, ICED11, uses Brand's layers with a Design Structure Matrix to expose unwanted/hidden dependencies and improve layer placement.

Its historical review also points directly to CHS's interface classifications, including whether changing one component damages both components, one component, or neither.

This is especially important because it shows that **dependency analysis itself** had already been operationalised explicitly in adaptability research well before Ovid.

## Kamara et al. — 2020

`Change Factors and the Adaptability of Buildings`, *Sustainability* 12(16), 6585, DOI `10.3390/su12166585`.

The empirical counterpoint survives the saturation pass:

- actual building changes do not necessarily occur on the timetable implied by canonical layer-lifespan diagrams;
- strategic, organisational, regulatory, economic and use changes can dominate;
- layer classification remains useful, but it should not be mistaken for a predictive schedule.

### Freeze consequence

F01 therefore uses temporal bands **ordinally and relationally**, not prophetically.

Ovid should ask:

> if this thing changes earlier than the thing around it, what else must be destroyed, disabled, opened, remade or vacated?

It should not claim:

> this component will change in year N because its layer says so.

# 6. The candidate Ovid delta after saturation

The candidate delta is materially narrower than after ILA-01B.

This is intentional. ILA rewards accurate inheritance, not novelty preservation.

## 6.1 Candidate deltas rejected or substantially weakened

### `Domestic transfer`

**Rejected as an F01 novelty hypothesis.**

Habraken/Open Building, SCAG, Metabolism, KEP and CHS all place related change/lifecycle propositions in housing before Ovid.

Ovid may still make a distinctive **British detached-house application**, but `bringing lifecycle-layer thinking into housing` is not defensible as the delta.

### `Replacement-destruction test`

**Rejected as underlying conceptual novelty.**

SCAG explicitly requires independent exchange and disassembly without alteration/destruction of other elements. CHS explicitly protects long-life parts from damage when short-life parts are replaced and classifies interfaces by replacement damage.

Ovid's sentence:

> **A component's lifespan matters less than the lifespan of the things that must be destroyed to replace it.**

may still be a project-authored formulation and a particularly effective decision heuristic. It must not imply priority for the mechanism it names.

### `Physical service/interface operationalisation`

**Rejected as broad novelty.**

SCAG, Japanese open-system work, CHS and later lifetime-compatibility guidance all move beyond abstract diagrams into assembly, interfaces, access and separability.

Ovid may still be unusually specific in its chosen domestic details and maintenance geography, but `we turned layers into interfaces` is historically too broad.

### `Occupied-change consequences`

**Weakened.**

Renovation/adaptability research already considers disruption, loss of use and cost. Japanese housing/open-building work also makes occupied change a practical concern.

Ovid's sustained focus on dust, demolition, reinstatement and the inhabitant living through ordinary work may still be an important editorial/architectural emphasis, but it cannot yet carry a novelty claim.

## 6.2 Candidate deltas still worth testing later

These are **hypotheses only**.

### A. `SYNTHESISES` — whole-house serviceability architecture

Ovid may distinguish itself by joining several previously separate traditions into one domestic architectural discipline:

- differential component life;
- maintenance geography;
- withdrawal and replacement routes;
- isolation;
- visible/contained failure;
- designed interfaces and fixing zones;
- workmanship robustness;
- occupied-work disruption;
- evidence / compiler / pattern verification.

The possible contribution is the **coherent whole**, not invention of any one constituent idea.

This hypothesis cannot be settled within F01 because F03/F05/F06/F07 own several of the other strands.

### B. `NARROWS` — proportional serviceability rather than maximum adaptability

Ovid explicitly asks whether provision for future change earns its immediate spatial, material, carbon and complexity cost.

SCAG already contains a strikingly similar warning: homogeneous flexibility is expensive and may provide adaptability where it is not needed. CHS's later assessment also recognises that adaptability should be classified/applied according to actual need rather than maximised indiscriminately.

Therefore Ovid cannot claim to have invented selective/proportional adaptability either.

A possible later delta is the **specific decision structure** by which Ovid ties provision to event probability, consequence and retrofit difficulty. F02/F05 must test this before it is retained.

### C. `OPERATIONALISES` — unusually explicit domestic work sequences

Ovid repeatedly tests not just `can this component theoretically detach?` but:

`identify → isolate → access → release → disconnect → handle → withdraw → bring replacement → reconnect → reinstate boundary`.

The discovery search found many strong separability/interface antecedents but did not establish that this exact sequence is distinctive.

Keep it as a later method/operationalisation question, not an F01 originality claim.

### D. project-authored vocabulary / compression

Terms such as `destructive coupling`, `maintenance geography`, `proportional serviceability` and the replacement-destruction sentence may be effective **Ovid formulations** even where the concepts have antecedents.

The ILA contract allows authorship of a formulation without claiming historical priority for its mechanism.

# 9. Falsification tests and results

## Test A — Can an earlier source displace Duffy/Brand as the first strong architectural C02 antecedent?

**Yes. Falsified.**

SCAG's 1971 English primary publication does so decisively; the system was developed in 1968. CHS independently provides a domestic component/interface implementation from 1980–83.

Therefore neither Duffy nor Brand should be narrated as first discovery of the mechanism.

## Test B — Is Archigram the demonstrated source of Duffy's temporal layers?

**No. Not proved.**

Archigram is earlier and conceptually related. Later scholarship describes echoes/debts, but the proposition-specific derivation remains unresolved.

Do not draw the arrow.

## Test C — Is Duffy still material after SCAG is found?

**Yes.**

Duffy's importance changes from presumed priority to **generalisation + direct Brand source + office-life-cycle model**.

That is sufficient to keep him central to F01.

## Test D — Is Brand merely a populariser?

**No.**

Brand appears to perform a genuine synthesis of Duffy's architectural layers with O'Neill's slow/fast hierarchy and makes the slippage/destructive-embedding relationship unusually explicit.

He should not be reduced to the person who drew six S's.

## Test E — Does O'Neill explain the origin of architectural rate/coupling thinking?

**No.**

Architectural propositions predate O'Neill by decades. O'Neill explains a systems/ecology source that Brand explicitly uses.

## Test F — Is the replacement-destruction mechanism plausibly Ovid-original?

**No. Falsified.**

SCAG and CHS already articulate materially equivalent mechanisms.

Ovid may own its wording and specific execution rules, not the underlying idea.

## Test G — Is domestic transfer a meaningful F01 delta?

**No. Falsified.**

Residential antecedents are abundant before Ovid.

## Test H — Is maximum physical independence the implication of rate mismatch?

**No.**

SCAG itself warns against homogeneous flexibility because it is expensive and poorly matched to heterogeneous change needs. CHS later encounters system complexity/economic limits. This strongly supports Ovid's current proportional/selective posture but also means that posture has antecedents.

## Test I — Are canonical layer service lives predictive schedules?

**No.**

Later empirical work shows actual change triggers do not reliably track nominal layer lifetimes. Use broad temporal ordering as a design heuristic, not a calendar.

## ILA-C01 — Building as a temporal object

This is a deep, multiply sourced architectural premise with many antecedents. No single origin should be implied.

## ILA-C02 — Differential rates of change and destructive coupling

The combined mechanism is established prior art:

- different building components have different useful lives/change cycles;
- whole-building obsolescence can be driven by the shortest-lived component when assemblies are fixed together;
- physical dependence can force change in one component to alter/destroy others;
- shorter-lived systems can be made more independently exchangeable;
- interfaces, sequencing and service geography can materially determine whether that independence is real;
- universal adaptability has a cost and should not be assumed beneficial everywhere.

Strong pre-Ovid exemplars include SCAG, Duffy/DEGW/ORBIT, CHS and Brand, with later research making dependency/lifetime compatibility increasingly explicit.

**Ovid does not claim to have invented destructive coupling as a mechanism.**

## The rate model is not a clock

The family treats lifespan/rate bands as a way to expose likely mismatch and dependency. It does not assert deterministic replacement dates.
