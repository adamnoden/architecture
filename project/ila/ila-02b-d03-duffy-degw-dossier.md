# 1. Executive decision

Francis Duffy and the early DEGW / *Planning Office Space* line are a **FOUNDATIONAL earlier antecedent** to the temporal half of ILA-C01/C02.

The deep read makes Duffy materially closer to Ovid's House than the common Stewart Brand summary suggests.

The important primary source is not merely a later four-S diagram.

The 1976 *Planning Office Space* argument already states, in substance, that:

- an office building should be understood through the changes that occur across its useful life;
- physical elements relate to one another **through time**;
- some design decisions are long-term and others short-term;
- partitions belong to short-life scenery rather than long-life building;
- services should permit later change;
- office design comprises several levels of design activity laid over one another but capable of being carried out independently;
- those levels are distinguished by intended lifespan;
- shell, scenery, set and service components therefore operate on materially different cycles;
- the long-lived shell should leave options open because future users are uncertain;
- shorter-lived scenery mediates between coarse, long-life building capacity and precise, short-term organisational demand;
- statements of intention can be structured so one can be revised without automatically changing the others.

That is already a serious theory of **temporal decomposition** rather than a descriptive observation that components wear out at different dates.

The dossier therefore rejects any Ovid framing in which:

> Duffy merely noticed different component lifespans, while Ovid discovered that those timescales should be architecturally separated.

That would be false.

Duffy's 1976 material already gives architectural and organisational consequences to differing timescales and explicitly values independence between levels of design.

However, D03 does **not** collapse the whole C02 delta.

The strongest distinction still visible in Ovid is narrower:

> **Ovid evaluates the physical dependency itself: when a faster-changing component is renewed, what slower fabric must be cut, broken, stripped, opened, refinished or otherwise consumed because of the interface?**

The 1976 Duffy material strongly anticipates decoupling, but this dossier found no primary Duffy proposition equivalent to Ovid's explicit destructive-coupling test:

> `a component's lifespan matters less than the lifespan of the things that must be destroyed to replace it`.

Nor does Duffy's office-layer model by itself supply Ovid's later apparatus around:

- permanent-fabric boundary debt;
- replacement / withdrawal sequences;
- maintenance geography;
- designed physical interfaces;
- failure containment;
- domestic proportionality;
- pattern / semantic / compiler governance.

Those remain separate questions, many of which have other antecedents elsewhere in ILA.

The correct D03 result is therefore:

> **Duffy owns much of the temporal architecture and much of the generic independence principle. Ovid's residual interest is the domestic physical operationalisation of dependency — especially destructive replacement coupling — not the discovery of layers changing at different rates or the desirability of separating long- and short-cycle decisions.**

This residual is still a **candidate synthesis / operationalisation**, not a priority claim. D05 may further collapse the physical-dependency delta.

## 3.1 Original problem and domain

The source addresses **commercial office design**.

Its assumed actors include:

- developers;
- architects;
- landlords / providers of office shells;
- tenants;
- office / interior designers;
- office managers;
- organisations whose staff, departments, equipment and work patterns change;
- specialists designing building services.

Its temporal problem is created by several mismatches:

- shell life exceeds tenancy life;
- tenancy life exceeds many internal organisational arrangements;
- organisations change during a tenancy;
- services and fittings contain subcomponents with different useful lives;
- developers must make long forecasts while occupiers can make shorter, more precise ones.

This is not detached-house theory.

Its primary mechanism nevertheless transfers because the underlying proposition is architectural:

> different decisions and assemblies operate over different horizons, and forcing them into one temporal design act creates avoidable rigidity.

# 4. The 1976 source already owns a strong C01 proposition

The opening chapter makes the building's continuing life a first-class design object.

Section 3.04 describes tenants arriving and leaving, interiors being repeatedly altered, and smaller changes continuing inside each tenancy. It concludes that the building should be understood through the totality of those changes over its useful life.

That is a direct earlier antecedent to ILA-C01:

> design the building as something repeatedly maintained, altered and renewed rather than as an object frozen at completion.

### Relationship

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT`  
Confidence: `DOCUMENTED`  
Importance to C01: provisional `MAJOR`.

### Ovid consequence

Ovid cannot claim to have discovered the architectural posture that a completed building should be understood through subsequent change.

The domestic transfer remains material, but the temporal premise is inherited.

# 5. `Shell and scenery` is explicitly a theory of elements through time

Section 3 is titled **Shell and scenery**.

The source says its first major theme concerns building elements and how the physical elements of office design relate to one another **through time**.

It immediately distinguishes long-term from short-term office-design decisions and derives consequences from that distinction.

The consequences include:

- old buildings can be gutted and redesigned internally;
- architecture and interior design operate differently;
- **services ought to allow scope for later change**;
- short-lived partitions should be understood as scenery rather than long-lived building;
- building shell and interior require different user information and different prediction horizons.

This materially strengthens the F01 discovery finding.

Duffy/Cave/Worthington are not merely classifying components by life expectancy after the fact. They are using temporal difference to organise design responsibility and physical provision.

### Relationship to C02

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT`  
Confidence: `DOCUMENTED`  
Importance: provisional `FOUNDATIONAL` to the differential-rate half of C02.

---

# 6. Independence between temporal levels is already explicit

Section 3.06 is especially important.

The source treats office design as several levels of design activity laid on top of one another but carried out **independently**.

Those levels are distinguished by the length of time the designed thing is meant to last.

The example separates:

- a building shell intended for roughly forty years;
- tenancy requirements over a much shorter lease;
- continuing internal changes as departments, people and equipment move.

This directly defeats a weak Ovid novelty claim such as:

> `Earlier layer models described rates; Ovid adds the idea that the layers should be able to change independently.`

No. Independence is already part of the 1976 architecture.

### D03 correction

The surviving Ovid question is not generic independence.

It is the **physical criterion used to judge whether independence has actually been achieved**.

Ovid asks about:

- access;
- release;
- replacement path;
- physical destruction of slower fabric;
- reinstatement of interrupted boundaries;
- maintenance working geometry;
- failure and repair consequences.

That is a materially sharper operational test than the generic temporal separation alone.

But later D05 / F03/F05 sources may own substantial portions of that too.

---

# 7. The original temporal model is more nuanced than the later `four S` summary

A common historical retelling makes Duffy's theory appear fully formed as:

- Shell — about fifty years;
- Services — about fifteen years;
- Scenery — five to seven years;
- Set — days / months.

The 1976 primary source is more complicated.

It clearly distinguishes:

- **shell**;
- **scenery**;
- **sets**;
- and service elements by lifespan.

But the service layer is not yet the later neat, single category found in Brand's summary.

Section 3.09 says, approximately:

- shell design: about forty years;
- scenery design: about seven years;
- set design: about three months;
- major service installations such as lifts / trunking: around twenty years;
- outlets such as lights, extracts and power points: no longer-lived than the scenery with which they are integrated.

This is actually more intellectually interesting than the later four-S shorthand.

It recognises that **one technical system itself contains different temporal strata**.

### Consequence

Do not use Brand's later four-layer diagram as if it were a verbatim transcription of a single Duffy model that had always existed unchanged.

The primary record supports a development:

1. shell / scenery / sets plus differentiated service lives in 1976;
2. later consolidation into the familiar Shell / Services / Scenery / Set account;
3. Brand's 1994 reformulation into Site / Structure / Skin / Services / Space Plan / Stuff.

D04 must therefore treat Brand as a synthesis node, not merely a graphic re-publisher.

---

# 8. Scenery is not merely short-lived; it mediates between different resolution scales

The 1976 source gives scenery a specific function.

The long-lived shell is necessarily coarse and must support uncertain future users.

Shorter-lived scenery can respond more precisely to a tenant's actual requirements.

The source explicitly frames scenery as taking up the tolerance between long-life shell and short-term organisational demand.

This anticipates an important aspect of Ovid's platform thinking:

> a slower layer need not predict every future fast-layer requirement if an intermediate layer can mediate between them.

### C05 relevance

This is not yet Ovid's full `architectural platform`.

It lacks the later project combination of:

- permanent fabric;
- replaceable systems;
- designed interfaces;
- maintenance geography;
- workmanship robustness;
- failure / stewardship obligations.

But the structural idea of:

> **coarse long-lived capacity + more precise short-lived adaptation**

is inherited.

### Relationship

C05: `EARLIER_ANTECEDENT`  
Importance: provisional `MAJOR` ingredient, not source of the full synthesis.

---

# 9. Revision without propagation is also already present

Section 4 turns from physical layers to process and information.

It argues that office briefs cannot be treated as one definitive, frozen statement because organisations and occupancy needs change.

The source proposes different statements of intention which are **independent of one another and allow revision without affecting the others**.

That is an unusually close conceptual antecedent to the project's broader anti-propagation instinct.

The domain here is briefing / decision structure rather than a wall, pipe or junction.

Still, the abstract mechanism is clear:

> local change is valuable when it need not propagate unnecessarily into other levels.

### Relationship

For C02's generic coupling logic:

- `EARLIER_ANTECEDENT` — `DOCUMENTED`.

For Ovid's **physical destructive coupling**:

- related but not equivalent.

Do not collapse informational independence into proof of physical replaceability.

Duffy says that on returning from the United States to Britain in **1971**, he used the terms:

That matters to C01 because it rejects the architectural handover event as the end of the design problem.

# 12. Source record D03-S03 — *The Changing City* (1989) and the later four-layer crystallisation

## Bibliographic identity

**Francis Duffy & Alex Henney**, *The Changing City*, Bulstrode Press, 1989.

The exact pages most repeatedly reproduced in later technical literature are pp. 61–62.

### Recoverable proposition

Later literature consistently attributes to Duffy & Henney the now-familiar four temporal layers:

- Shell — long life / structure;
- Services — medium-cycle technical systems;
- Scenery — partitions / finishes / fit-out;
- Sets — rapidly rearranged movable elements.

The source is also associated with the cumulative-cost argument: over a long building life, repeated generations of services and space-plan change can outweigh the initial structure expenditure.

### Evidence discipline

This dossier did not obtain a complete authoritative digital edition of the 1989 primary text.

Therefore:

- bibliographic identity is secure;
- the four-layer / pp. 61–62 attribution is strongly corroborated by independent scholarly literature;
- D03 does **not** treat uninspected secondary reproductions as stronger evidence than the fully inspected 1976 primary source.

### Dossier use

The 1989 work is important mainly as evidence that the temporal decomposition later crystallised into the familiar four-layer account before Brand's 1994 synthesis.

It is **not necessary** to make 1989 the origin of the underlying theory.

The underlying architecture is already explicit in 1976.

# 16. The office-domain transfer limit is substantial

Duffy's mechanism is highly relevant, but the source assumes a building type unlike the ordinary house.

## 16.1 Different actors

The office model can separate:

- developer;
- landlord;
- tenant;
- architect;
- interior designer / space planner;
- facilities manager;
- service engineer;
- corporate management.

An ordinary detached house often collapses several of those roles into:

- one owner / household;
- occasional trades;
- no continuous facilities-management organisation.

Ovid therefore cannot simply import office layering and assume the organisational machinery survives.

## 16.2 Different economic cycles

Commercial leases create obvious fit-out cycles.

Tenant churn makes repeated scenery change normal and economically legible.

Domestic change is less regular:

- kitchens / bathrooms may persist for decades;
- services can be fragmented across many small interventions;
- ownership changes may or may not cause immediate fit-out change;
- aesthetic and emotional attachment to fabric is often stronger.

## 16.3 Different service intensity

Large offices contain:

- extensive mechanical systems;
- lifts;
- data distribution;
- dense power / communication infrastructure;
- professional maintenance regimes.

A house requires proportionality.

The Duffy lesson is therefore not:

> create office-scale service infrastructure in a house.

It is:

> distinguish temporal horizons and avoid making the slow layer solve every fast-layer problem permanently.

## 16.4 Different architectural value

A speculative office shell is deliberately asked to tolerate unknown future tenants.

Ovid permits a house to make stronger permanent spatial commitments where those commitments are worth preserving.

That is why Duffy does not by himself settle selective permanence.

# 24. Contradictory and negative evidence

## 24.1 The four-S story is historically too neat

The 1976 primary source does not present exactly the later standard four-S model.

Services are internally differentiated by lifespan, and some basic services sit conceptually with the longer-lived shell.

Therefore historical summaries that make the canonical four S appear fully stable from the beginning should be treated cautiously.

## 24.2 `Independent` is not the same as physically demountable

Duffy's 1976 independence language spans design activity, briefing and temporal responsibility.
