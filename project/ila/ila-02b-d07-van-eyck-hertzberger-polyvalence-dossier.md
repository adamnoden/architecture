# 1. Executive decision

D07 closes one of the most important remaining originality questions in ILA-C04.

Aldo van Eyck and Herman Hertzberger already occupy, by 1961–63, the precise architectural middle ground that current Ovid prose treats as a central part of **selective permanence**:

> architecture need not become neutral, physically transformable or non-committal in order to survive changed use; a specific, intelligible and architecturally committed form can remain itself while supporting more than one pattern of occupation.

The two sources contribute different pieces.

**Van Eyck** supplies the negative / critical proposition:

- flexibility should not become an architectural absolute;
- maximum neutrality can erase identity and meaningful commitment;
- a building can admit adaptation without pretending to accommodate every fundamentally different life-pattern equally well;
- significant form should possess enough scope for multiple meanings without requiring continual alteration.

**Hertzberger** supplies the positive proposition and vocabulary:

- `flexibility` conceived as perpetual physical / programmatic adjustment can become non-commitment;
- a neutral solution designed to accept every future may be the best solution to none;
- the constructive alternative is **polyvalence**;
- a form can remain physically itself and retain identity while accepting different uses / interpretations;
- changed use can therefore be achieved with little or no literal physical transformation.

This is extremely close to current Ovid language:

- `The result need not be a neutral container.`
- `It can remain recognisably itself while its technical life changes around it.`
- `Capacity and compatibility can matter more than literal movement.`
- `Change should be given deliberate places to occur so that architecture can afford to remain permanent elsewhere.`

D07 therefore removes any defensible Ovid claim to have originated the **conceptual middle position** between fixed functionalism and universal flexibility.

The underlying proposition:

> **committed form can remain itself while supporting changed use**

is established prior architectural theory.

Ovid may still own its terminology and its particular synthesis:

- `selective permanence` as project wording;
- `permanence through transformation` as project framing;
- the coupling of committed spatial order to lifecycle-separated technical systems;
- the explicit decision machinery deciding where permanence / change are worth buying;
- the whole-house integration with maintenance geography, failure architecture, interfaces, workmanship, stewardship and compiler / evidence governance.

But these are now **synthesis / governance hypotheses**, not a claim to discovery of the architectural middle ground.

D07 also creates meaningful reader-facing attribution pressure.

Current Part I explicitly names Open Building as the principal architectural precedent after making statements materially closer to Van Eyck / Hertzberger than to support/infill theory. That is not a false priority claim, but it produces an incomplete intellectual topology.

Provisional treatment pressure is therefore **T3 → T4**:

> likely a compact local acknowledgement or source-note correction around the `neutral container` / `capacity and compatibility` passage, subject to ILA-04.

No reader-facing edit is made in D07.

# 3. Chronology correction: the Van Eyck warning is already present in 1961

ILA-F02 discovery used 1962 as the convenient date for Van Eyck's `false neutrality` critique.

D07 finds an earlier primary-context articulation.

## D07-S01 — `The medicine of reciprocity tentatively illustrated`, 1961

**Aldo van Eyck**, introductory text to publication of the Amsterdam Municipal Orphanage, *Forum*, April–May 1961.

A reproduced primary text in scholarship / collected-writings transcription records Van Eyck explaining that the Orphanage's pattern-structure was derived from a particular daily-life pattern and could develop that pattern, but could not truthfully pretend to support a fundamentally different one equally well.

He then identifies **extreme flexibility** with `false neutrality` and uses the well-known glove analogy: a glove that fits every hand ceases to be a meaningful hand-fit.

Primary-text reproduction consulted:

- María Lidón de Miguel, *Aldo van Eyck y el concepto In-between*, reproducing `The medicine of reciprocity tentatively illustrated` from Van Eyck's collected writings: https://riunet.upv.es/bitstream/handle/10251/55348/LID%C3%93N%20-%20Aldo%20van%20Eyck%20y%20el%20concepto%20In-between%3A%20aplicaci%C3%B3n%20en%20el%20orfanato%20de%20Amsterdam.pdf

### Source quality

- original Forum item: `TIER_A_PRIMARY_SOURCE`;
- accessible reproduction: `TIER_B_STRONG_PRIMARY_REPRODUCTION` for D07 proposition checking.

### D07 correction

The anti-neutrality proposition should be dated **at least to 1961** in Van Eyck's published architectural argument.

The 1962 Forum text strengthens / generalises it; it is not the first recoverable appearance located by D07.

# 4. What Van Eyck actually owns

Van Eyck's proposition is more restrained than the slogan `flexibility is bad`.

## 4.1 Adaptability is admitted

The Orphanage text explicitly accepts that the building's pattern can develop.

The objection is not to all future change.

## 4.2 Architecture has a meaningful fit

The building derives from a particular pattern of life.

Its architectural specificity is therefore not an accidental defect to be erased in anticipation of every future.

## 4.3 Universal accommodation becomes false

A building that claims equal suitability for every fundamentally different use risks becoming neutral in the pejorative sense: it stops offering a meaningful architectural response to any particular one.

This is the exact intellectual fault line later occupied by Ovid.

## 4.4 Flexibility is a bounded value, not an absolute

Van Eyck's later 1962 argument makes the point explicitly: flexibility should not become another architectural absolute.

The problem is not change.

The problem is an architecture that treats uncertainty as a command to withhold commitment.

### Relationship to Ovid

`Van Eyck → C04 anti-neutrality = EARLIER_ANTECEDENT`  
Confidence: `HIGH`.

# 6. Van Eyck's mature critique: comprehensible structure versus amorphous neutrality

The 1962 argument generalises the 1961 position.

The recurrent propositions are:

- flexibility is useful but dangerous when absolutised;
- unlimited neutrality can be as architecturally damaging as excessive rigidity;
- significant / archetypal structures should support multiple meanings without needing continuous physical alteration;
- architecture should remain comprehensible rather than dissolve into an amorphous substrate for hypothetical futures.

That is very close to Ovid's current rejection of:

`uncertain future → neutral universal shell`.

### D07 consequence

Ovid cannot present that rejection as a novel architectural inference.

It is a direct continuation of an established post-war argument.

# 8. Hertzberger's critique of flexibility

Hertzberger's attack is sharper and more explicit than Van Eyck's.

His target is not the ability to change per se.

It is **architectural non-commitment** masquerading as adaptability.

The logic is approximately:

```text
future is uncertain
      ↓
architect refuses a committed solution
      ↓
space is made maximally neutral / adjustable
      ↓
no present state receives a genuinely apt architectural response
```

For Hertzberger, this is a category error.

Uncertainty does not remove the architect's responsibility to make a specific form.

### Ovid overlap

This directly precedes current Ovid statements that:

- maximum reconfigurability can make the present building pay for hypothetical change;
- a universal kit of parts is not automatically good architecture;
- neutral flexibility can prevent anything from becoming architecture.

The broad critique is inherited territory.

---

# 9. Polyvalence is the decisive positive proposition

Hertzberger does not stop at criticism.

He proposes **polyvalence**.

The important structure is:

> changefulness is accepted as permanent reality, but the architectural response can itself remain a stable form capable of more than one interpretation / use.

That means:

- the form can retain identity;
- the form need not physically transform for every changed use;
- specificity and future usefulness are not opposites;
- a relatively small amount of physical flexibility can coexist with strong capacity for different occupation.

This is almost exactly the conceptual structure of Ovid's current C04 prose.

### D07 verdict

`Hertzberger → committed adaptable form / polyvalence = FOUNDATIONAL earlier antecedent to central C04 proposition`.

# 10. `Capacity and compatibility can matter more than literal movement` is prior-art territory

Current Part I states:

> a room need not physically transform to remain adaptable; useful dimensions, openings and relationships can support different occupations; capacity and compatibility can matter more than literal movement.

After D07, that proposition cannot carry an originality implication.

Hertzberger's polyvalence is precisely the architectural proposition that:

- a stable form may accept changed use;
- the adaptability can reside in interpretation / occupation rather than physical transformation;
- identity can survive changed programme.

The later adaptable-housing literature uses different vocabulary, but the underlying architectural idea is clearly established by 1962.

### Ovid status

The sentence remains legitimate Ovid prose.

It should be treated as:

`PROJECT_FORMULATION_OF_INHERITED_PROPOSITION`.

# 11. `Remain recognisably itself` is also inherited territory

Current Ovid prose says the house need not be a neutral container and can remain recognisably itself while technical life changes around it.

Hertzberger repeatedly treats identity as central to polyvalence.

The point is not merely that a room can host several functions.

It is that **variation does not require erasure of identity**.

This collapses another possible residual after D06.

Weeks had shown:

`stable infrastructure + changeable subordinate parts`.

Hertzberger now shows:

`stable architectural form itself + multiple interpretations / uses`.

Together they occupy most of the conceptual territory Ovid C04 needs.

# 12. D07-S04 — Diagoon as a bounded built test

Hertzberger's Diagoon houses in Delft, designed from 1967, are a useful built development of the theory.

Author-maintained source:

- Herman Hertzberger, `Diagoon — a framework for polyvalence`, 2016: https://www.hertzberger.nl/images/nieuws/DiagoonHousingDelft2016.pdf

Hertzberger describes the houses as an explicit implementation of polyvalence:

- one articulated domestic volume rather than predetermined room-function zoning;
- sites / spatial positions interpretable for different uses;
- capacity to accommodate changing family composition;
- an architecture intended to generate relevant uses rather than merely receive a list of predetermined functions.

### Why this matters

The built example is domestic.

That sharply reduces any attempt to dismiss the Van Eyck / Hertzberger lineage as an institutional-scale precedent irrelevant to Ovid's house.

Unlike D06 Weeks, D07 does not require a large transfer from hospital campus to dwelling.

The argument is already residential.

---

# 13. Built counter-evidence: mechanisms are not the same as useful capacity

Later study of the Diagoon houses provides a useful negative finding.

Designed optional infill packages / moveable or purchasable elements were reportedly little used, while the underlying spatial composition remained the more durable source of appropriation and changed occupation.

TU Delft research records that residents did not use several designed options for partitions / finishes and concludes that the quality lay more strongly in the primary spatial composition than in optional infill machinery.

This is unusually close to Ovid's current anti-maximalist intuition:

> useful spatial capacity may outperform a larger inventory of change mechanisms.

### Important boundary

This does **not** prove Ovid's burden classes or proportionality framework.

It is one built-case signal that literal transformability can be overprovided relative to the value of good underlying form.

The two architects were not merely adjacent names.

Hertzberger's own retrospective describes the 1959–63 Forum period as a major education and explicitly identifies Van Eyck as an important teacher who helped him formulate and refine drafts.

Author-maintained timeline:

- https://www.hertzberger.nl/index.php/en/biografie/tijdlijn

This establishes strong direct intellectual contact.

### What it proves

`Van Eyck ↔ Hertzberger = DOCUMENTED_INTELLECTUAL_CONTACT / COLLABORATION_CONTEXT`.

### What it does not prove

D07 did not recover a primary statement in which Hertzberger says:

> my concept of polyvalence derives specifically from Van Eyck's false-neutrality argument.

The conceptual relationship is plausible and the texts appear in the same Forum milieu, but ILA does not convert plausibility into genealogy.

Therefore:

`Van Eyck → Hertzberger polyvalence = DERIVATION_NOT_PROVEN`.

Keep the two source nodes distinct in ILA-03.

# 15. Relation to Weeks — two different answers to uncertainty

D06 and D07 should not be collapsed.

## Weeks

The future is uncertain, so:

- establish stable geography;
- allow subordinate units to expand / alter / be replaced;
- leave final growth partly indeterminate.

## Van Eyck / Hertzberger

The future is uncertain, but:

- do not turn neutrality into an absolute;
- commit to meaningful form;
- make that form capable of multiple interpretations / uses without continual alteration.

These are different strategies.

Ovid borrows conceptually from the territory of **both** even without documented project contact:

```text
Weeks:
stable enabling order + changing subordinate parts

Van Eyck / Hertzberger:
stable meaningful form + changed interpretation / use

Ovid:
committed architectural order + changeable technical layers + bounded use capacity
```

The combination is important.

The combination is not yet proven historically unique.

# 16. C04 after D07

D07 materially changes the C04 delta.

## No longer defensible as Ovid contribution claims

Ovid should not claim priority for:

- rejecting universal / neutral flexibility;
- arguing that uncertainty does not justify architectural non-commitment;
- claiming that specific architecture can accept multiple future uses;
- claiming that form can retain identity through changed occupation;
- claiming that physical movement / transformation is not necessary for adaptability;
- treating good room / form capacity as an alternative to constant reconfiguration.

These are clearly established prior art.

## What remains

Ovid's live C04 field becomes narrower:

1. **terminological compression** — `selective permanence`, `permanence through transformation`;
2. **whole-house allocation** — explicitly coordinating permanent architectural order with faster technical / fit-out layers;
3. **proportional governance** — deciding where adaptability earns present burden through C03 machinery;
4. **physical dependency integration** — linking use adaptability to destructive-coupling / interface / replacement logic;
5. **maintenance and failure integration** — changeable domains must also be inspectable, isolatable, maintainable and recoverable;
6. **epistemic governance** — separating doctrine, pattern, evidence, implementation, semantic model and compiler authority.

These remain possible project contributions at synthesis / method level.

Do not elevate them to historical priority before ILA-03/04.
