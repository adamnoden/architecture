# 1. Executive decision

D10 closes the frozen ILA-02B deep-dossier queue with a deliberately unheroic result.

ILA-C13 is overwhelmingly inherited architectural territory.

Two source nodes matter for different reasons.

## Victor and Aladár Olgyay

The Olgyays' early-1950s bioclimatic programme establishes the foundational modern architectural method of translating climate and human comfort into decisions about:

- site;
- orientation;
- solar exposure;
- shading;
- form;
- openings;
- air movement;
- thermal behaviour;
- envelope / material response.

The programme is already substantial before Victor Olgyay's 1963 *Design with Climate*.

The 1963 book is the mature portable synthesis, not the beginning of the idea.

Olgyay therefore strongly owns the broad proposition:

> **environmental performance should first be treated as an architectural / climatic design problem rather than immediately delegated to mechanical plant.**

## Dean Hawkes

Hawkes is the closer antecedent to Ovid's **exact current ordering**.

His `selective` environmental model explicitly gives primary environmental work to:

- building form;
- fabric;
- orientation;
- openings;
- ambient environmental sources;
- occupant control.

Mechanical heating, cooling, ventilation and lighting remain legitimate but **supplementary** to the primary environmental control provided by the selective built form.

This is extremely close to Ovid Principle 9:

> use form, envelope, orientation, shading, mass and controllable openings to reduce demand on active systems before adding mechanical / electronic correction.

The intellectual position `first, not only` is therefore not an Ovid contribution.

## Banham remains the necessary counterpoint

Reyner Banham prevents the lineage from becoming a morality play in which passive architecture is `real architecture` and active systems are an embarrassment.

Mechanical environmental systems can themselves be architectural.

The appropriate Ovid position is therefore sequencing under evidence, not ideological purification:

```text
use durable form / fabric / ambient opportunity where it earns value
        ↓
measure remaining requirement
        ↓
add active systems where required
        ↓
allow health / performance / resilience to override simplicity
```

Every generic component of that sequence has prior art.

## Ovid delta after D10

Ovid cannot claim novelty for:

- climate-responsive architecture;
- architecture-before-plant ordering;
- fabric / built form as primary environmental control;
- ambient energy before generated energy;
- mechanical systems as supplementary rather than prohibited;
- occupant environmental control;
- passive environmental provision as resilience / service-loss fallback;
- future-climate stress testing;
- rejecting simplistic `fabric first` when health / moisture / overheating evidence says otherwise.

The remaining C13 contribution hypothesis is narrower:

> **Ovid makes passive-first a constitutional cross-family governance rule inside a century-scale domestic platform: permanent geometry is asked to earn environmental value before shorter-lived plant is added, but measured performance, health, failure resilience and future climate retain explicit authority to force active systems or rewind permanent design decisions.**

That is a coherent project synthesis.

It is not a historical priority claim.

No pre-ILA formative Olgyay or Hawkes contact is documented.

Current Part I already treats passive-first as established practice rather than an Ovid invention, so publication pressure is modest: **T1–T2**, likely source / evidence acknowledgement rather than substantive correction.

# 5. The Olgyay move is analytic architectural mediation

The strength of the Olgyay programme is methodological.

It links:

```text
climate
↓
human comfort / biological response
↓
required environmental correction
↓
architectural / technological response
```

The architectural response includes decisions that become slow, durable parts of the building:

- orientation;
- form;
- shading geometry;
- openings;
- thermal mass / material response;
- air-movement strategy.

### D10 consequence

Ovid cannot claim priority for asking permanent geometry to perform environmental work.

# 7. `Design with Climate` makes environmental analysis portable

The 1963 book systematises the earlier programme into a widely usable architectural method.

Its structure moves from:

- climatic analysis;
- human comfort;
- site / orientation;
- sun / shading;
- wind / air movement;
- building form / envelope response;
- technological means of correction;
- architectural synthesis.

The exact sequence should not be simplified into a slogan that `Olgyay always forbids mechanical systems until the end`.

Olgyay's method is broader:

> first understand the climate / comfort problem, then use the appropriate environmental / technological response, integrated architecturally.

### D10 relationship to Ovid

Olgyay is **foundational** to the general architectural ordering of climate-responsive design.

Hawkes is closer to Ovid's exact `primary passive / supplementary mechanical` formulation.

# 8. Olgyay is not anti-technology

A key attribution boundary is that bioclimatic design is not equivalent to anti-mechanical architecture.

The Olgyay method allows technological correction where climate / architecture alone cannot produce acceptable conditions.

The point is not:

`machines are bad`.

It is:

`architectural and climatic opportunities should be understood before correction is specified.`

### D10 consequence

Ovid's `passive-first does not mean passive-only` boundary is compatible with, rather than opposed to, the foundational bioclimatic lineage.

# 10. Hawkes's selective mode is the closest C13 antecedent

Hawkes distinguishes two broad environmental strategies.

## Exclusive mode

The internal environment is tightly separated from ambient conditions and maintained predominantly through mechanical / automatic control.

## Selective mode

The building selectively accepts, filters and exploits external / ambient conditions.

Its environmental strategy depends strongly on:

- orientation;
- form;
- fabric;
- windows / openings;
- natural light;
- passive solar gains;
- natural ventilation where appropriate;
- occupant control;
- generated energy only as needed.

### D10 judgement

`Hawkes → C13 exact ordering = FOUNDATIONAL / MAJOR earlier antecedent`.

---

# 11. The later Hawkes synthesis states `primary` and `supplementary` explicitly

Hawkes's later *The Selective Environment* makes the hierarchy exceptionally clear.

The selective model gives:

- primary temperature control to building fabric / built form;
- primary energy opportunity to ambient sources;
- mechanical heating / cooling / ventilation / lighting a **supplementary** role.

It also retains deliberate occupant control.

Useful source:

- Dean Hawkes with Jane McDonald & Koen Steemers, *The Selective Environment* (2002): https://www.routledge.com/The-Selective-Environment/Hawkes-McDonald-Steemers/p/book/9781138157309

Accessible text reproduction consulted for proposition checking records the general characteristics of selective design and explicitly says active systems should supplement the primary control provided by selective built form.

### Source-use boundary

The 2002 text confirms continuity / mature formulation.

D10 does not silently assign every later phrase verbatim to the 1982 article.

# 15. Banham is a counterpoint and terminology context

Reyner Banham's *The Architecture of the Well-Tempered Environment* (1969) is essential because it refuses to define architecture only through passive / structural form.

His environmental modes include conservative, selective and regenerative strategies and treat powered environmental systems as legitimate architectural means.

Hawkes's later selective/exclusive framework sits in an environmental-design field Banham helped articulate, and later scholarship explicitly treats Banham's `selective` language as an important earlier terminology context.

### D10 use

- `Banham → Hawkes terminology / environmental-mode context = STRONG_CONTEXTUAL_ANTECEDENT`;
- `Banham → Ovid C13 = COUNTERPOINT`.

### Ovid consequence

# 18. `Fabric first` can become bad doctrine — also prior art

Historic England's retrofit guidance provides direct professional counter-evidence to simplistic fabric-first ideology.

It warns that indiscriminate fabric interventions can create:

- moisture harm;
- overheating;
- poor indoor-air quality;
- technical incompatibility;
- damage to existing fabric.

It permits natural, mechanical or hybrid ventilation depending on evidence.

Sources:

- https://historicengland.org.uk/advice/technical-advice/retrofit-and-energy-efficiency-in-historic-buildings/improving-energy-efficiency-through-mitigation/
- https://historicengland.org.uk/advice/technical-advice/retrofit-and-energy-efficiency-in-historic-buildings/improving-energy-efficiency-through-mitigation/risks-of-energy-efficiency-interventions/

### D10 consequence

Ovid's constitutional limit:

> measured performance, health and resilience outrank ideological simplicity

is good governance but not a novel idea.

# 19. Passivhaus is a useful counterexample to `less plant = better architecture`

- extremely low heating demand;
- highly controlled envelope performance;
- deliberate mechanical ventilation;

This is useful to Ovid because its passive-first preference could otherwise drift into an unjustified mechanical-ventilation taboo.

# 20. C13 after D10

## No longer defensible as Ovid contribution claims

Ovid should not imply priority for:

- climate-responsive form;
- orientation / shading / opening strategy as environmental control;
- building fabric as primary control;
- ambient energy before generated energy;
- passive-first ordering;
- active systems as supplementary rather than forbidden;
- occupant control inside environmental strategy;
- passive fallback during service failure;
- future-climate testing;
- evidence overriding passive simplicity.

These are all established prior art / professional practice.

## Live residual

The surviving Ovid C13 field is almost entirely **governance / synthesis**:

1. **temporal ordering** — ask slow geometry / fabric to earn environmental value before faster plant is specified;
2. **proportionality** — do not oversize permanent passive measures or infrastructure merely to avoid active systems;
3. **failure integration** — prefer passive fallback where it materially improves recoverability;
4. **future-climate rewind** — permanent choices can be reopened when future-condition testing exposes avoidable liability;
5. **health override** — active systems are mandatory when evidence says passive provision is inadequate;
6. **whole-house integration** — environmental strategy is judged alongside maintenance, failure, Repose, workmanship and serviceability rather than as an isolated energy optimisation.

Every ingredient has antecedents.

The possible contribution lies only in the explicit project constitution tying them together.
