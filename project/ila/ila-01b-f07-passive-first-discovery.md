# 1. Question

Ovid's House currently states:

> **Use form, envelope, orientation, shading, mass, controllable openings, drainage and gravity to reduce demands on active systems before adding mechanical or electronic correction.**

The constitution immediately limits the proposition:

> **`First` does not mean `only`. Measured performance, health and resilience outrank ideological simplicity. Mechanical systems are the correct answer when the building, climate or use requires them.**

F07 therefore does not ask whether passive architecture exists.

The question is:

> **What work should durable architectural form, fabric and geometry perform before shorter-lived active systems are asked to correct the environment, and where does passive-first discipline become ideology rather than performance?**

The family has five materially different intellectual strands:

1. **bioclimatic design** — climate translated into site, orientation, form, shading, envelope, openings and mass;
2. **environmental-management modes** — built form and active plant understood as different, often combined, means of environmental control;
3. **selective / occupant-controlled environment** — ambient sources and building fabric primary, active systems supplementary where required;
4. **low energy as part of long-life architecture** — energy demand joined to durability and adaptability rather than treated as a separate engineering score;
5. **technical dependence / autonomy** — a separate question about how much infrastructure or machine dependence should be accepted.

# 3. Victor Olgyay — the closest foundational antecedent

## 3.1 _Design with Climate_ (1963)

Source:

- Victor Olgyay, *Design with Climate: Bioclimatic Approach to Architectural Regionalism*, Princeton University Press, 1963.
- Google Books bibliographic record: https://books.google.com/books/about/Design_with_Climate_Bioclimatic_Approach.html?id=AeZEAAAAYAAJ

The work systematically translates climatic conditions into architectural design decisions including:

- site selection;
- orientation;
- solar control;
- shading;
- building form;
- air movement / ventilation;
- thermal effects of materials;
- heliothermic planning.

The book's significance to Ovid is not merely that it discusses passive design.

It explicitly organises environmental correction as a **sequence**.

## 3.2 `Flattening the temperature curve`

Olgyay's well-known diagram orders environmental intervention as:

1. prevailing / ambient environmental conditions;
2. microclimatology;
3. climate balance of the structure;
4. mechanical heating or cooling.

Reliable secondary reproductions identifying the source as Olgyay 1963 include:

- Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory / UC Berkeley dissertation reproduction: https://windows.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/escholarship_uc_item_3rd2f2bg.pdf
- UCL teaching material reproducing the diagram from p. 11 of *Design with Climate*: https://www.ucl.ac.uk/research/domains/sites/research_domains/files/esa_labucl_cleung_esaworkshop_bartlettschoolofarchitecture.pdf

A recent UCL architectural-history study summarises the diagram as moving through microclimatology and structural climate balance to mechanical heating / cooling as the final supplement.

Source:

- https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10164123/1/Architecture%27s-Model-Environments.pdf

This is extremely close to Ovid's ordering principle:

> **permanent architecture should reduce the environmental demand before active correction is added.**

### Relationship type

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT`.

No formative project contact is currently established.

### Discovery consequence

Ovid cannot claim priority for:

- climate-responsive orientation;
- form responding to climatic region;
- architectural shading as first-line control;
- thermal mass / material response before plant;
- environmental modification by microclimate / structure before mechanical correction;
- a sequence in which mechanical heating/cooling completes what architectural response cannot.

Olgyay is the strongest F07 candidate discovered so far.

# 4. Olgyay's method is not passive-only

This point matters because Ovid's own boundary is `first, not only`.

Olgyay's framework does not treat technology as morally illegitimate.

His bioclimatic model explicitly combines:

- climatology;
- biology / human comfort;
- architecture;
- technology.

A historical review of ecological design summarises the Olgyay method as a linear process:

`climate → biology → technology → architecture`

while the `flattening the curve` diagram itself shows architectural / structural moderation followed by mechanical correction where required.

Source:

- Oxford Research Encyclopedia, `History of Ecological Design`: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61750/chapter/542861932

### F07 consequence

Even the **anti-dogmatic** version of passive-first has deep prior art.

Ovid's `passive-first but not passive-only` boundary is not a novel escape hatch added to an otherwise novel principle. It is compatible with the core bioclimatic lineage itself.

# 5. Reyner Banham — environmental control is architecture, including machines

## 5.1 _The Architecture of the Well-Tempered Environment_ (1969)

Sources:

- Canadian Centre for Architecture bibliographic record for the 1969 edition: https://www.cca.qc.ca/en/search/details/library/publication/00024952
- University of Chicago Press description of the revised edition: https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/A/bo5956755.html

Banham's importance to F07 is largely adversarial.

He systematically treats environmental engineering as part of architectural history rather than as something outside architecture.

The book distinguishes environmental-management modes commonly summarised as:

- **conservative** — retain / buffer desirable conditions through mass / enclosure;
- **selective** — use structure/openings to admit desirable external conditions while excluding undesirable ones;
- **regenerative** — use applied power / mechanical systems to create the required environment.

Later environmental-history literature uses this taxonomy to show that buildings often combine modes rather than belonging to a morally pure category.

### Relationship type

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT + COUNTERPOINT`.

### Why Banham matters

Banham blocks a simplistic Ovid story in which:

`architecture = passive / good`

and

`mechanical system = correction / regrettable`.

Active environmental systems can themselves be legitimate architecture.

The relevant Ovid proposition is therefore an **ordering / burden-of-proof rule**, not an ontological hierarchy in which passive form is always superior.

# 6. Dean Hawkes — an almost direct prior formulation of C13

F07 discovery finds Hawkes even closer to Ovid's exact current wording than Banham.

## 6.1 Selective versus exclusive environments

Source:

- Dean Hawkes, `The theoretical basis of comfort in the 'selective' control of environments`, *Energy and Buildings* 5(2), 1982: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0378778882900081
- Dean Hawkes, Jane McDonald & Koen Steemers, *The Selective Environment* (2002), Routledge record: https://www.routledge.com/The-Selective-Environment/Hawkes-McDonald-Steemers/p/book/9780419235309

Hawkes distinguishes **selective** environmental control from more sealed / mechanically controlled `exclusive` environments.

The later book's global characteristics of selective design state that:

- environmental standards relate to local climate;
- natural light is maximised;
- **primary temperature control is by building fabric**;
- building form and cross-section respond to climate;
- orientation responds to climate / sun path;
- energy should be primarily from ambient sources such as daylight, useful solar gain and natural ventilation where appropriate;
- **mechanical heating, cooling, ventilation and lighting are supplementary to the primary control provided by selective built form**;
- occupant control matters.

A secondary academic reproduction of the source table is available here:

- https://fileserver-az.core.ac.uk/download/pdf/60402173.pdf

### Relationship type

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT`.

### Discovery consequence

This is very close to C13's entire ordering proposition.

Ovid cannot claim to have invented:

- building fabric as primary environmental control;
- ambient environmental sources used first;
- mechanical systems used as supplements;
- orientation / section / fenestration as environmental architecture;
- occupant control as part of a selective environmental model.

### Provisional interpretation

Hawkes substantially collapses any meaningful conceptual novelty in C13 itself.

The remaining Ovid value must lie in how this established discipline is **bound into long-life architecture**, not in passive-first environmental ordering as such.

# 10. Technical dependence — Ivan Illich is relevant only in a bounded way

Source:

- Ivan Illich, *Tools for Conviviality* (1973).
- Full-text mirror: https://www.panarchy.org/illich/conviviality.html

Illich's concern is not building physics.

It is the relationship between people and tools / institutions.

He distinguishes tools that enlarge autonomous action from systems that increasingly determine the user's possibilities and convert people into dependent consumers.

### Relationship type

`EXPLICIT_PROJECT_SOURCE` at reading-list level + `CROSS-DOMAIN ANTECEDENT / ANALOGUE` to technical dependence.

### Why it matters

Ovid's C13 is partly motivated by the fact that:

- permanent form lasts longer than active plant;
- equipment fails / becomes obsolete;
- user control can become opaque;
- comfort can become dependent on invisible technical systems.

Illich can illuminate that dependence question.

### Why it must remain bounded

Illich does **not** establish:

- correct ventilation rates;
- passive design performance;
- the superiority of natural ventilation;
- the environmental adequacy of simpler systems.

Those require building science.

Do not use `conviviality` as a substitute for performance evidence.

# 11. Autonomous-house tradition — useful counterpoint to Ovid

Source:

- Brenda Vale, *The Autonomous House: Design and Planning for Self-Sufficiency* (1975), bibliographic record: https://openlibrary.org/books/OL5193907M/The_autonomous_house

Later literature describes the autonomous-house idea as seeking independence from public gas, water, power and sewer infrastructure through self-contained energy and resource systems.

### Relationship type

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT + COUNTERPOINT`.

### Why it matters

The autonomous-house tradition takes **reduced external dependence** much further than Ovid C13.

That makes it useful as a limit case.

Ovid is not proposing:

- infrastructure secession;
- self-sufficiency as a moral end;
- zero external inputs;
- maximal on-site systems.

Indeed, an autonomy-maximalist house can paradoxically acquire **more technical apparatus to maintain**.

### F07 lesson

> **Low dependence is not the same thing as autonomy.**

Ovid's burden remains proportional: architecture should reduce unnecessary active dependence, not manufacture a self-sufficiency ideology.

# 12. Passivhaus — a modern counterpoint to simplistic `passive = no machines`

Sources:

- Passive House Institute, building-certification principles: https://passivehouse.com/en/home/building-certification/
- Passive House Institute training / history: https://elearning.passivehouse.com/mod/book/tool/print/index.php?id=1039

Passive House combines strong passive / fabric measures with active systems.

Its core principles include:

- high insulation;
- high-performance windows;
- airtightness;
- thermal-bridge reduction;
- **controlled mechanical ventilation with heat recovery**.

The concept was developed by Wolfgang Feist and Bo Adamson from 1988; the Darmstadt-Kranichstein project followed in 1991.

### Relationship type

`CONTEMPORARY / LATER PROFESSIONAL COUNTERPOINT`.

Not an intellectual ancestor of Olgyay-era passive-first design.

### Why it matters to Ovid

Passivhaus shows clearly that:

> **extreme reduction of heating/cooling demand does not imply rejection of active ventilation.**

This supports Ovid's `first, not only` boundary.

It also creates a useful tension with the project's preference for direct / understandable occupant control: a high-performing building may legitimately use tightly controlled active environmental systems.

That tension belongs to design judgement, not ideology.

`CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL CONVERGENCE / EVIDENTIAL SUPPORT`.

# 14. `Fabric first` can itself become ideology

Historic England gives a strong contemporary counterposition.

Source:

- Historic England, `Improving Energy Efficiency Through Mitigation`: https://historicengland.org.uk/advice/technical-advice/retrofit-and-energy-efficiency-in-historic-buildings/improving-energy-efficiency-through-mitigation/

Historic England notes that `fabric first` is often used to prioritise fabric thermal upgrades over other interventions, but in historic buildings this can be:

- impracticable;
- undesirable;
- technically unsound;
- inefficient;
- a cause of unintended consequences.

It instead advocates a broader energy-efficiency hierarchy considering repair, maintenance, services, responsive controls and low/zero-carbon systems.

### Relationship type

`CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL COUNTERPOINT`.

### Why it matters to C13

This strongly supports Ovid's existing qualifier:

> **measured performance, health and resilience outrank ideological simplicity.**

Passive-first is an ordering heuristic, not permission to damage a building or refuse necessary plant.

# 16. What discovery already makes unsafe to imply as Ovid inventions

Ovid should assume that it did **not** invent:

- climate-responsive architecture;
- bioclimatic design;
- orienting buildings by climate / solar geometry;
- shaping form and section for environmental performance;
- using shading before cooling plant;
- using building mass / fabric to moderate climate;
- using natural/ambient sources before active correction;
- placing mechanical heating/cooling after microclimate / structural response in an environmental-control sequence;
- treating building fabric as primary environmental control;
- treating mechanical environmental systems as supplementary where appropriate;
- combining passive and active environmental-control modes;
- giving occupants direct environmental control in selective buildings;
- linking `low energy` with `long life` and `loose fit`;
- seeking reduced technical dependence / greater user autonomy;
- self-sufficient / autonomous-house concepts;
- fabric-first professional practice;
- low-energy buildings that still rely deliberately on mechanical ventilation.

The current constitution already behaves as if this is established discipline. F07 confirms that posture.

# 17. Provisional Ovid-delta hypotheses

F07 discovery leaves little conceptual novelty, which is a valid result.

## 17.1 Long-life reason for passive-first

Ovid's strongest project-specific reason is not simply `save energy`.

It is temporal:

- orientation, form and envelope can last for generations;
- plant and controls turn over faster;
- durable architecture that performs environmental work can reduce exposure to future equipment failure / obsolescence;
- passive fallback can reduce consequence when active systems fail.

This is a plausible **cross-family synthesis** of F01/F04/F07.

But Alex Gordon already joins long life and low energy, so the residual delta is likely narrow.

## 17.2 Explicit proportional anti-ideology

The current Ovid claim contains an unusually explicit veto:

> performance / health / resilience outrank simplicity.

Banham, Hawkes, Passivhaus and Historic England all demonstrate why this boundary matters.

Candidate delta:

- disciplined project **governance formulation**;
- not a new environmental principle.

## 17.3 Permanent geometry under future climate uncertainty

Ovid's constitution explicitly asks long-lived orientation, shading opportunity, purge paths, drainage and envelope choices to be tested against a reasonable range of future climate/hazard scenarios and allows architecture to rewind where a robust passive response cannot be achieved proportionately.

This may represent a project-specific coupling of:

- passive-first architecture;
- permanence;
- uncertainty;
- generative rewind.

F07 discovery does not yet establish whether that combination is materially distinctive.

Likely later disposition:

- cross-family operationalisation rather than C13 originality.

## 17.4 Technical dependence as secondary, not supreme, objective

Ovid does not seek autonomy for its own sake.

It prefers:

- understandable control;
- reduced unnecessary dependence;
- passive fallback where useful;

but accepts active systems when they are the better performance solution.

Candidate delta:

- **proportional narrowing** of autonomy / conviviality concerns.

# 18. Counterpoints that must survive

## 18.1 Banham — machines are not architectural failure

Mechanical environmental control can be a legitimate architectural mode.

## 18.2 Hawkes — selective does not mean uncontrolled

Selective buildings still use active systems where required; the issue is primary versus supplementary control and user relationship.

## 18.3 Passivhaus — low demand can require sophisticated active support

Mechanical ventilation can be integral to health / efficiency even in a building defined by passive performance.

## 18.4 Autonomous house — independence can create apparatus

Seeking complete self-sufficiency can increase technical complexity and maintenance burden.

## 18.5 Historic England — fabric-first can cause harm

A heuristic becomes ideology when it overrides building-specific physics, heritage, health or unintended-consequence analysis.

## 18.6 Fathy — environmental response is not only energy arithmetic

Climate, material, social practice and architecture can remain integrated; optimisation alone is not the whole architectural task.

# 21. Discovery-stage result

F07 discovery produces a restrained result:

> **C13 is overwhelmingly inherited architectural discipline.**

Victor Olgyay already gives an architectural-first environmental sequence ending in mechanical correction. Dean Hawkes later states the selective-building principle in terms extremely close to Ovid: building fabric / ambient sources primary, mechanical systems supplementary. Banham makes active environmental systems fully legitimate architectural tools. Gordon already binds low energy to long life / loose fit.

The relevant Ovid contribution, if any, is therefore unlikely to be the passive-first proposition itself.

The later audit should test only whether Ovid materially adds by coupling that established discipline to:

- lifespan hierarchy;
- service replacement / dependence;
- failure fallback;
- future-climate scenario testing;
- proportionality;
- explicit rewind / governance.

That is the correct adversarial question.
