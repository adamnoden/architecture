# 1. Question

F05 asks:

> **How should the physical work of servicing, maintenance, replacement and intergenerational understanding be given architectural geography and order?**

The family contains three Ovid claims that are tightly connected in practice but have different intellectual histories.

## ILA-C08 — Maintenance geography

Current Ovid proposition:

> **Organise maintenance-intensive systems, working spaces, access routes, supporting ground and replacement paths as a coherent spatial topology established during design.**

The stronger project claim is that maintenance is not adequately represented by a small `access zone` around a component.

Real work requires:

- approach;
- safe working position;
- tools;
- opening / manipulation space;
- lifting or support where necessary;
- withdrawal path;
- replacement-component route;
- waste route;
- service isolation;
- reinstatement.

## ILA-C09 — Service topology as architectural ordering

Current Ovid sequence asks:

> **In what order should the building create places for services so that later systems can be resolved without consuming the architecture that should have constrained them?**

The sequence runs approximately:

`service demand → utility entry → service hub → vertical distribution → high-service adjacency → horizontal routes → controlled crossings → failure / isolation → room-scale distribution → stewardship identity`

The stronger claim is procedural:

> consequential service geography is capable of forcing upstream changes in plan, section and structure rather than being routed residually through whatever architecture remains.

## ILA-C11 — Intergenerational legibility and stewardship

Current Ovid proposition:

> **Preserve enough information—in records and, for critical systems, in the building itself—that future owners and trades can understand, isolate, maintain and alter it safely.**

The distinctive current hierarchy is:

- physical discoverability / identification first for critical systems;
- records reinforcing that physical legibility;
- digital models / archives valuable but not the sole means by which the house can be understood.

F05 therefore has to separate four questions:

1. **maintenance work geography**;
2. **service topology / architectural ordering**;
3. **maintenance and repair as first-class intellectual objects**;
4. **stewardship / legibility across owners and trades**.

No one source is expected to own all four.

# 4. Stream A — services as architectural hierarchy and spatial order

## 4.1 Louis Kahn — served / servant architecture

Kahn is a materially stronger F05 source than a casual reference to `served and servant space` would suggest.

At the Richards Medical Research Laboratories, the distinction is physically architectural:

- laboratory towers form the principal `served` space;
- stairs, lifts, air intake and exhaust functions are given distinct `servant` towers;
- the service organisation materially shapes massing, structure and plan;
- services are not residual routes pushed through leftover space.

MoMA describes the servant spaces as stairs, elevators and air-handling towers placed to leave the laboratories as uninterrupted served space.

Sources:

- MoMA, Richards Medical Research Building: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/82346
- MoMA, service-tower drawings: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/397

### Relationship to Ovid

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT`.

No claim is made here that Kahn is the sole source of service hierarchy.

### Relevant overlap

Kahn directly anticipates the proposition that:

> **service needs can be important enough to deserve their own architectural geography rather than being treated as background technical routing.**

That materially narrows C09 novelty.

### Important non-overlap

Kahn's hierarchy is primarily architectural / programmatic / technical order.

It does not by itself establish Ovid's complete sequence of:

- utility entry;
- hub;
- branch hierarchy;
- isolation;
- replacement geography;
- physical system identity;
- weak-stewardship handover.

Those remain separate questions.

## 4.2 Salk Institute — service space as maintainable change infrastructure

The Salk Institute is even closer to C08/C09 at the level of physical maintainability.

The Institute's own architectural account states that Kahn separated laboratory floors from full-height interstitial utility levels containing:

- heating / cooling;
- plumbing;
- gas;
- electrical distribution;
- data / communications.

The official virtual tour states explicitly that contractors can work in these interstitial spaces and remodel mechanical/electrical systems **without disrupting the laboratories above or below**.

Sources:

- Salk Institute, `About Salk Architecture`: https://www.salk.edu/about/about-salk-architecture/
- Salk Institute, virtual architecture tour / interstitial space: https://www.salk.edu/about/visiting-salk/virtual-architecture-tour/

### Relationship to Ovid

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT + PRECEDENT / EXEMPLAR`.

### Relevant overlap

Salk directly anticipates:

- services given dedicated spatial volume;
- horizontal service access;
- future service modification;
- work performed without consuming occupied/served space;
- a durable architectural framework remaining intact while service arrangements change.

That is close prior art for both **maintenance geography** and **service topology**.

### Counterpoint / proportionality boundary

The Salk solution is spatially lavish: full-height interstitial service floors alternate with laboratory floors.

That is proportionate to a research laboratory with extraordinary service intensity and change frequency.

It is not evidence that a detached house should acquire whole-storey service voids.

F02 proportionality therefore remains essential.

## 4.3 Richards as a counterexample to topology being sufficient

The Richards Laboratories also supply a useful internal critique of Kahn's service order.

Later renovation accounts document:

- functional shortcomings from the original building;
- growing service additions over decades;
- piping, conduit and ductwork becoming tangled despite the original architectural ordering;
- later teams needing to re-establish explicit service zones during renovation.

Docomomo source:

- `Upgrading the Mechanical Systems in Louis Kahn's Richards Building`: https://stage.docomomo-us.org/news/upgrading-the-mechanical-systems-in-louis-kahns-richards-building

### Consequence

A strong original service topology does **not** guarantee long-term legibility or disciplined change.

This is important for Ovid C09/C11.

The building also needs:

- change control;
- persistent identifiers;
- rules of occupation within service zones;
- stewardship through successive interventions.

That distinction may become part of the Ovid delta.

# 5. Stream B — accessible service geography and subsystem disentanglement

## 5.1 Christopher Alexander et al. — `Duct Space` (Pattern 229)

`A Pattern Language` contains an unusually close architectural antecedent that should not be missed merely because Alexander is usually cited under pattern methodology.

Pattern 229, **Duct Space**, begins from the problem:

> services disappear into walls and become difficult to locate.

Its proposed response is spatial:

- create dedicated duct spaces for plumbing, gas, conduits and air;
- connect horizontal distribution through vertical ducts / chases;
- provide access panels / outlets along the route.

Bibliographic source:

- Christopher Alexander, Sara Ishikawa and Murray Silverstein, *A Pattern Language* (Oxford University Press, 1977), Pattern 229, `Duct Space`.

Accessible reference copy:

- https://patternlanguage.cc/Patterns/Duct-Space-%28229%29

### Relationship to Ovid

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT`.

### Relevant overlap

This is directly close to Ovid's propositions that:

- buried services become unknowable;
- horizontal and vertical distribution deserve deliberate geography;
- access should exist along the service route;
- service location should be predictable rather than discovered destructively.

### Consequence

Ovid cannot claim novelty for **accessible horizontal / vertical service geography** in architecture.

Alexander already articulated it explicitly in 1977.

### Remaining difference

Ovid's topology is broader and more procedural:

- utility entry and service hub precede distribution;
- high-service room adjacency can force plan changes;
- structural crossings are controlled / rewind-capable;
- failure / isolation are integrated;
- stewardship identity closes the sequence.

Whether that combination is materially distinct remains a discovery hypothesis.

## 5.2 Open Building — dedicated zones and rules of deployment

Open Building is again highly relevant, but F05 asks a different question from F02/F03.

Kendall and Teicher's *Residential Open Building* describes technical subsystem disentanglement in spatial terms:

- each system is given a **dedicated zone and rules of deployment**;
- piping, wiring and ductwork are prevented from chaotic interweaving through structure / other systems;
- building-level common infrastructure is located to maximise infill freedom;
- subsystem deployment is coordinated for later independent adjustment / replacement;
- known placement reduces guesswork in future renovation.

Source:

- Stephen Kendall and Jonathan Teicher, *Residential Open Building* (E & FN Spon, 2000).
- CIB W104 summary: https://cibworld.org/cib-commissions/w104-open-building-implementation/

### Relationship to Ovid

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT`.

### Relevant overlap

Open Building strongly anticipates:

- fixed service zones;
- distinct common / local service levels;
- rationalised horizontal / vertical distribution;
- subsystem independence;
- future renovation made easier by known service position;
- architecture treated as serviced support rather than one entangled bundle.

### Consequence

Ovid cannot claim to originate the proposition:

> **give each technical system a known spatial zone / deployment rule so future work does not require destructive discovery.**

That is established Open Building prior art.

### Boundary with F02/F03

F02 owns support/infill and allocation of permanence.

F03 owns interface interoperability and replacement.

F05 uses Open Building only for the **geography of technical distribution and future work**.

## 5.3 Professional building-services planning — service topology is established practice

Modern building-services guidance makes the same spatial logic explicit without architectural theory language.

### SCI service integration guidance

SCI P166 `Design of Steel Framed Buildings for Service Integration` gives concrete recommendations including:

- locate plant rooms close to vertical service routes;
- avoid restrictive distribution between plant and risers;
- provide continuous, uninterrupted vertical routes;
- coordinate the transition between vertical and horizontal distribution;
- separate routes for materially different services;
- limit horizontal distribution distances where practicable.

Source:

- SCI P166: https://www.steelconstruction.info/images/7/70/SCI_P166.pdf

### CIBSE maintainability guidance

CIBSE Guide M treats services designers as responsible for installations that can be maintained economically and safely over their life.

Source:

- https://www.cibse.org/guidem

### Current public-building pattern books / briefs

Contemporary institutional design standards routinely require:

- plant rooms;
- service spines;
- vertical risers;
- dedicated distribution zones;
- working space;
- direct access where replacement warrants it;
- maintenance / removal routes.

### Relationship to Ovid

`EVIDENTIAL SUPPORT / ESTABLISHED PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE` rather than one intellectual ancestor.

### Consequence

The **mechanics** of C09 are not new:

- service hubs;
- risers;
- horizontal distribution;
- routing hierarchy;
- plant adjacency;
- removal access.

The high-value ILA question is instead procedural:

> **does Ovid make that established services knowledge architecturally generative earlier and more forcefully than ordinary design workflows?**

That remains a candidate delta.

# 6. Stream C — maintenance and repair as first-class intellectual objects

This stream does not establish service geometry directly.

Its importance is conceptual: it challenges the cultural tendency to treat maintenance as secondary, invisible or uncreative work.

## 6.1 Stephen Graham and Nigel Thrift — `Out of Order` (2007)

Graham and Thrift argue that maintenance and repair are central to modern societies and infrastructure yet systematically pushed from intellectual attention.

Source:

- Stephen Graham and Nigel Thrift, `Out of Order: Understanding Repair and Maintenance`, *Theory, Culture & Society* 24(3), 2007: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0263276407075954

### Relationship to Ovid

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT / INTELLECTUAL CONTEXT`.

The project already cites this family in research.

### Relevant overlap

Their strongest F05 contribution is the proposition that:

> **maintenance is constitutive of infrastructure, not an embarrassing afterlife of design.**

This supports Ovid's decision to make maintenance a first-class design concern.

It does not prescribe riser dimensions or replacement routes.

## 6.2 Steven Jackson — `Rethinking Repair` (2014)

Jackson's `broken world thinking` asks scholarship to begin from fragility, breakdown, maintenance and repair rather than from novelty / production alone.

Source:

- Steven J. Jackson, `Rethinking Repair`, in *Media Technologies* (MIT Press, 2014): https://academic.oup.com/mit-press-scholarship-online/book/14976/chapter-abstract/169335302

### Relationship to Ovid

`EXPLICIT_PROJECT_SOURCE / INTELLECTUAL CONTEXT` at the current manuscript level.

### Relevant overlap

Jackson gives intellectual support to the Ovid stance that:

- breakdown is ordinary;
- repair is not peripheral;
- design should be judged partly by the long-run relationship between people and artefacts after initial production.

Again: this is conceptual posture, not architectural topology.

## 6.3 Shannon Mattern — `Maintenance and Care` (2018)

Mattern similarly argues for maintenance, care and repair as central to cities, infrastructures, homes and social relations rather than as residual activity.

Source:

- Shannon Mattern, `Maintenance and Care`, *Places Journal*, 2018: https://placesjournal.org/article/maintenance-and-care/

### Relationship to Ovid

`EXPLICIT_PROJECT_SOURCE / INTELLECTUAL CONTEXT`.

### Relevant overlap

Mattern strengthens the project stance that long-lived environments depend on ongoing care practices.

### Consequence across this stream

Ovid **does not originate the intellectual rehabilitation of maintenance / repair**.

That field is established and should be credited proportionately.

Ovid's architectural delta, if any, must be physical / procedural rather than merely saying `maintenance matters`.

# 7. Stream D — stewardship, information continuity and physical legibility

## 7.1 The `golden thread` — intergenerational building information is established policy

Following Dame Judith Hackitt's building-safety review, England's higher-risk-building regime requires a persistent `golden thread` of building information.

Government guidance defines the golden thread as both:

- information needed to understand the building and keep it / people safe; and
- information-management processes that keep that information accurate, accessible and current through the building lifecycle.

It explicitly addresses:

- changes in ownership;
- design / construction / refurbishment records;
- version control;
- handover;
- accessibility;
- durability / interoperability of information over the building life.

Sources:

- GOV.UK, `Keeping information about a higher-risk building: the golden thread`: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/keeping-information-about-a-higher-risk-building-the-golden-thread
- BRAC golden-thread report: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/building-regulations-advisory-committee-golden-thread-report/building-regulations-advisory-committee-golden-thread-report

### Relationship to Ovid

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT / CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL-REGULATORY PARALLEL` for C11's information-continuity problem.

### Consequence

Ovid cannot claim novelty for:

- information surviving owner / team changes;
- current / accurate building records;
- change control;
- lifecycle information handover;
- records being available to future maintainers.

Those are now explicit regulatory concepts.

### Important non-overlap

The golden-thread regime is fundamentally **information-system oriented** and, in scope, focused on higher-risk buildings / safety information.

Ovid makes a different additional claim:

> **critical systems should remain physically intelligible even when digital stewardship is imperfect.**

That needs separate antecedent testing.

## 7.2 Building logbooks / O&M information

Building logbooks and O&M records long predate the golden-thread regime.

They provide information on:

- installed services;
- operation;
- maintenance requirements;
- commissioning;
- later changes.

CIBSE TM31 / Part L traditions are direct professional precedents for C11's record side.

### Relationship to Ovid

`ESTABLISHED PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE / EVIDENTIAL SUPPORT`.

### Consequence

The project should not overstate the idea of giving future owners building records.

The real question is **what records cannot safely be allowed to carry alone**.

## 7.3 Physical service identification — labels, charts and record-drawing correspondence

Physical legibility is also far from novel.

Building-services practice routinely requires:

- permanent pipe identification;
- flow-direction markers;
- valve tags;
- unique plant identifiers;
- valve charts;
- equipment labels;
- correspondence between physical labels and record drawings.

Examples include:

- BS 1710 pipeline identification practice;
- public-sector M&E specifications requiring permanent labels and charts;
- water-service guidance requiring service valves to be labelled;
- plant-room standards linking each physical tag to record information.

Representative public guidance / specifications:

- Water Regs UK FAQ on pipe / valve identification: https://www.waterregsuk.co.uk/topics/all-faqs/
- Government / public M&E specifications for valve and plant identification.

### Relationship to Ovid

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT / ESTABLISHED PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE`.

### Consequence

Ovid cannot claim novelty for:

- physical labels;
- persistent system identifiers;
- valve charts;
- matching physical equipment to drawings.

## 7.4 The unresolved C11 question — physical discoverability outranks perfect records

The remaining candidate delta is more specific.

Ovid's current hierarchy is:

> **records should reinforce discoverability rather than being the only means by which the building can be understood.**

This combines two established traditions:

1. **physical service legibility** — labels, predictable zones, visible isolation, known route logic;
2. **information continuity** — O&M records, logbooks, golden thread, change control.

The discovery search found abundant prior art for each half.

It did **not yet find a stronger single architectural formulation** of the exact Ovid hierarchy:

> physical organisation and identifiers should leave the critical system intelligible under degraded information stewardship, while records add depth and history.

This therefore survives as a candidate **synthesis / weak-stewardship formulation**, not an originality claim.

# 8. The strongest existing prior art to Ovid's service-topology sequence

C09 is one of the highest-value ILA claims because the project currently treats the service-topology sequence as its strongest generative sequence.

Discovery materially narrows what is actually new.

## The following are established

### Utility / plant geography

Professional building-services design has long located:

- utility entries;
- plant rooms;
- service cores;
- distribution centres;
- risers.

### Vertical before horizontal coordination

Professional guidance explicitly coordinates:

- vertical risers;
- horizontal floor distribution;
- route lengths;
- structural depth;
- fire compartmentation.

### Dedicated zones

Open Building explicitly gives subsystems dedicated zones and rules of deployment.

### Accessible routes

Alexander explicitly proposes horizontal duct spaces linked to vertical chases with access points.

### Services as architecture

Kahn makes service hierarchy architectural enough to shape massing and structure.

### Maintainability / replacement

CIBSE / structural-maintenance guidance already requires working space, access and replacement routes.

### Identification / stewardship

Existing physical-label + logbook traditions already tie field systems to records.

## Therefore

Ovid cannot claim novelty for the **individual steps** in its service-topology sequence.

The surviving candidate delta is the **generative governance** of those steps:

> consequential service geography is resolved early enough that it may invalidate / rewind room layout, structure or section rather than being compromised downstream to preserve an already-frozen architectural plan.

That is more specific than `good services coordination`.

ILA-01C must attack it directly.

# 11. Counterpoints and limits

## 11.1 Salk — exemplary but spatially extravagant

Salk proves that maintenance geography can be architectural and extraordinarily effective.

It also proves why F02 proportionality matters.

Full-height service floors are rational for laboratory science but are not a domestic default.

## 11.2 Richards — strong topology can decay under unmanaged change

Richards demonstrates that:

- clear original service hierarchy;
- dedicated structural zones;
- careful routing;

can still accumulate later entanglement.

This strengthens C11 / governance and warns against assuming physical topology alone is self-preserving.

## 11.3 Open Building — rational zones can become technical system thinking

Open Building's technical discipline is highly relevant but can drift toward systematisation / industrial fit-out as an end in itself.

Ovid must retain C14/Repose constraints:

- ordinary rooms remain the goal;
- service geography need not advertise itself visually;
- maintainability should not turn the house into a laboratory / office floor.

## 11.4 Maintenance access can over-provision the building

Routes, lifting zones, removable floors, service voids and plant access all consume:

- area;
- volume;
- material;
- capital cost;
- embodied carbon;
- acoustic / fire complexity.

C03 proportionality therefore constrains every F05 claim.

## 11.5 Records can go stale

A golden thread or perfect BIM model is valuable only while:

- maintained;
- readable;
- accessible;
- transferred;
- updated after change.

Ovid is right to distrust record-only legibility, but physical labels can also go stale after undocumented change.

The correct hierarchy is likely **redundant correspondence**, not `physical good / digital bad`.

## 11.6 Labels are not topology

A badly routed system with perfect labels remains badly routed.

C11 must not become a documentation substitute for C08/C09.

## 11.7 Visible services are not required

F05 concerns physical discoverability / access, not permanent visual exposure.

Alexander's duct spaces, Kahn's servant spaces and Ovid's own doctrine all allow services to be spatially known without becoming the visual subject of the room.

# 13. Residual Ovid-delta hypotheses

Discovery leaves four hypotheses worth carrying into adversarial freeze.

## 13.1 Maintenance geography as a coherent cross-scale topology

The phrase may be project-authored, but the meaningful question is not naming.

Potential delta:

> **combine approach, work position, lifting / handling, isolation, withdrawal, replacement and support conditions into one architectural topology spanning site → envelope → plant → riser → horizontal route → room interface.**

The pieces are established.

The cross-scale architectural synthesis may be distinctive enough to matter.

## 13.2 Service topology as a generative sequence with rewind authority

The strongest C09 delta hypothesis is procedural.

Professional practice knows all the pieces.

Ovid may differ by granting service topology **explicit upstream authority**:

- plant / utility route can alter plan;
- riser can alter room relationships;
- horizontal distribution can alter section / floor build-up;
- structural crossing conflict can force rewind;
- failure / isolation geography can invalidate an otherwise attractive arrangement;
- service identity closes the sequence rather than being a handover afterthought.

Candidate formulation:

> **services are not merely coordinated with architecture; consequential service topology participates in generating it.**

This must be tested against Kahn, laboratory planning, Open Building and professional integrated-design practice before being credited to Ovid.

## 13.3 Weak-stewardship legibility through physical + informational redundancy

The exact C11 hierarchy may survive:

- predictable physical geography;
- durable in-situ identity;
- record correspondence;
- digital history / deeper information;
- safe degradation if the record layer becomes incomplete.

Potential delta:

> **critical building understanding should degrade gracefully when stewardship quality drops.**

The components are prior art; the weak-stewardship synthesis may be project-specific.

## 13.4 Integration of maintenance work, service topology and stewardship

Most existing traditions focus heavily on one of:

- technical distribution;
- maintenance operations;
- information management;
- repair theory.

Ovid may have a meaningful integrative claim in binding all three:

`where the system is → how a person reaches/works/replaces it → how a future person knows what it is`.

This three-part relationship is one of the strongest F05 Ovid-delta hypotheses.
