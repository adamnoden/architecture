## 1. Purpose

ILA-00B freezes the **small set of current Ovid’s House propositions for which significant prior articulation would materially affect the project’s intellectual positioning**.

This is not a sentence-by-sentence citation map, a bibliography index or a list of everything the project believes. The spine exists so later ILA work can begin from the project’s actual claims rather than from famous names.

Each record therefore binds:

- the current proposition;
- the authority that owns it;
- its role in the wider argument;
- material downstream architectural consequences;
- current evidence / precedent references already present in the repository;
- how the proposition currently **reads** to a careful reader before ILA performs new discovery.

The final field is descriptive only. `Inherited`, `established / conventional`, `project judgement / synthesis` and `apparently project-specific formulation` are **not lineage verdicts**. They record the current rhetorical posture of the project so ILA can later test it.

## 2. Inclusion threshold

A proposition enters this spine only if at least one of the following is true:

1. it materially structures the governing doctrine;
2. it organises several important patterns, strategies or Reference House decisions;
3. it is a substantial standing discipline that prevents the doctrine collapsing into a merely technical system;
4. the project currently presents a distinctive synthesis, term or operational consequence whose antecedents matter to intellectual honesty;
5. it defines how architectural knowledge is represented or generated in Part III.

Minor technical rules, statutory duties, isolated implementation choices and empirical claims remain outside the spine unless later ILA work proves that they conceal a major conceptual family.

# 4. Claim records

## ILA-C01 — Building as a temporal object

### Current proposition

> **Design the house as a building that will be maintained, repaired, altered and renewed repeatedly, not as an object frozen at practical completion.**

Part I opens with the corresponding argument that practical completion is the beginning of a long-lived building’s life rather than the end of the architectural story.

### Owning authority

- Governing Principle 1 — **Build for time**.
- Part I, Chapter 1 — **The house after completion**.

### Role in the wider argument

This is the temporal premise from which the rest of Ovid’s House follows. Maintenance, replacement, access, failure and adaptation become design inputs because the building is treated as an object with a continuing life rather than a finished state.

### Material downstream consequences

- service-life distinctions throughout Part I;
- maintenance and replacement sequences in patterns;
- Reference House rejection of arrangements that are coherent only at handover;
- Part V prototype, commissioning and whole-life evaluation methods;
- OCG and HFI treatment of future intervention / occupation as design inputs.

### Current references / precedents

The current manuscript explicitly treats service-life planning as established professional knowledge and cites ISO 15686, RICS whole-life assessment, CIBSE maintainability guidance and RIBA Plan of Work Stage 7. The general Sources page also includes Habraken, Brand and related long-life literature.

### Current reading posture

**Established field + project framing.** The text explicitly says Ovid’s House does not claim to have discovered that buildings change. What currently reads as project-owned is the architectural emphasis placed on consequences of change in the ordinary house.

## ILA-C02 — Differential rates of change and destructive coupling

### Current proposition

Part I states that building layers change at different rates and sharpens that into the test:

> **A component’s lifespan matters less than the lifespan of the things that must be destroyed to replace it.**

Principle 2 gives the architectural consequence:

> **Do not use long-lived fabric as the routine distribution or attachment zone for shorter-lived systems.**

The project names **coupling** as the important condition: lifespan difference is benign when faster layers can change independently and destructive when renewal consumes slower fabric.

### Owning authority

- Governing Principles 1 and 2.
- Part I, Chapter 2 — **Buildings change at different speeds**.

### Role in the wider argument

This is one of the central causal claims of Ovid’s House. It converts the general fact of differential service lives into an architectural problem about dependency between layers.

### Material downstream consequences

- protected permanent fabric;
- controlled attachment and designed penetrations;
- permanent opening / replaceable window and door patterns;
- service routing around primary structure;
- rejection of destructive renewal as an ordinary access method;
- Reference House separation of principal structure from shorter-lived service systems.

### Current references / precedents

Current text explicitly names:

- N. John Habraken / Open Building — support and infill;
- Frank Duffy — layered buildings changing at different rates;
- Stewart Brand — shearing / pace layers;
- Alex Gordon — long life, loose fit, low energy;
- ISO 20887 — disassembly / adaptability;
- Herbert Simon — nearly decomposable systems as a bounded systems parallel.

### Current reading posture

**Explicitly inherited lineage + project emphasis.** Differential rates and layer separation are presented as established lineage. The emphasis on **destructive coupling between lifespan layers** currently reads as the project’s sharper architectural framing, but ILA has not yet tested whether that framing has important earlier formulations.

## ILA-C03 — Proportional serviceability

### Current proposition

The governing constitution states:

> **Serviceability is pursued proportionately. Permanent provision should reflect the frequency, consequence and retrofit difficulty of the future event it is intended to support.**

Part V develops this into burden classes, the co-renewal rule and a rising burden of proof for permanent provisions made in anticipation of possible future change.

### Owning authority

- Governing constraints above the eleven principles.
- Part V, Chapter 19 — **Whole-life value and proportionality**.

### Role in the wider argument

This is the principal anti-maximalism control on Ovid’s House. It prevents maintainability, adaptability and reversibility from becoming universal goods that automatically justify extra area, material, mechanisms or complexity.

### Material downstream consequences

- B0–B3 burden classification;
- incumbent status for excellent conventional construction;
- rejection / holding of whole-house access floors, universal removable linings and speculative structural reserve;
- OCG proportionality framework;
- pattern-level `Proportionality` sections;
- Reference House use of targeted rather than universal access provisions.

### Current references / precedents

Current support is primarily whole-life / service-life practice and project research rather than one named conceptual ancestor: ISO 15686-5, RICS cost/carbon methods, ISO 20887’s contextual posture, OCG counter-evidence and Reference House option studies.

### Current reading posture

**Project judgement / synthesis.** The underlying economic and whole-life logic is conventional; the current architecture-specific decision structure — especially burden classes, co-renewal and “hold the provision” for high-burden uncertainty — reads as a project-owned synthesis pending ILA research.

## ILA-C04 — Selective permanence / permanence through transformation

### Current proposition

Part I rejects the inference that acknowledging change requires maximum flexibility:

> **Change should be given deliberate places to occur so that architecture can afford to remain permanent elsewhere.**

Principle 7 states:

> **Concentrate adaptability in layers that benefit from change; allow rooms, axes, stairs, structure and other defining architectural relationships to be deliberately enduring.**

The project summarises the position as **permanence through transformation**.

### Owning authority

- Governing Principle 7 — **Let permanence be architectural**.
- Part I, Chapter 3 — **Selective permanence**.
- `docs/research/preface-precedents.md` for the interpretive frame.

### Role in the wider argument

This claim prevents the long-life agenda becoming a neutral universal kit of parts. It establishes that durable architectural commitments may themselves be valuable and that adaptability belongs where change is probable and useful.

### Material downstream consequences

- conventional continuous principal-room floors remain admissible;
- permanent room / stair / facade order in the Reference House;
- targeted rather than maximum flexibility;
- support for room capacity / compatibility rather than literal physical transformability;
- persistence-condition reasoning around what architectural relationships deserve to endure.

### Current references / precedents

Current text names Open Building / Molenvliet / NEXT21, C. S. Holling’s resilience distinction, Ovid and Heraclitus as bounded interpretive analogies, and the persistence / identity research record.

### Current reading posture

**Project synthesis with explicit antecedents / analogies.** The project openly inherits adaptability and layer-separation traditions while presenting selective permanence and permanence-through-transformation as its own synthesis. ILA must later test how much earlier architectural theory already occupies that exact middle ground.

## ILA-C05 — Architectural platform

### Current proposition

Part I defines **architectural platform** as the relationship between long-lived architecture and systems expected to change within it. It has four principal parts:

1. permanent fabric;
2. replaceable systems;
3. designed interfaces;
4. maintenance geography;

joined by an assembly hierarchy and governed by workmanship robustness.

### Owning authority

- Part I, Chapter 4 — **The architectural platform**.
- Governing Principles 1–7 provide its constitutional basis.

### Role in the wider argument

This is the project’s principal integrative model. It turns several claims about time, interfaces, access and permanence into one architectural framework without requiring the house to look modular or technical.

### Material downstream consequences

- pattern hierarchy between doctrine, strategy, pattern, implementation family and occurrence;
- Reference House coordination across structure, services, openings, maintenance and environmental systems;
- the distinction between long-lived architectural order and faster technical layers;
- the project’s recurring phrase: **build the permanent house; assemble the changeable house inside it**.

### Current references / precedents

The ingredients currently reference Open Building, layer / pace thinking, maintainability, design for disassembly, workmanship and conventional architectural practice. No current source is presented as the origin of the four-part **architectural platform** synthesis itself.

### Current reading posture

**Apparently project-specific synthesis of established ingredients.** This is not an originality verdict. It is the current reader-facing impression and therefore a high-value target for ILA concept-family research.

## ILA-C06 — Designed interface

### Current proposition

Principle 3 states:

> **Treat junctions between systems, materials and lifespan layers as designed building elements.**

Part II adds the structural maxim:

> **Deterministic load paths; bounded freedom of movement.**

The interface is expected to separate or reconcile support, restraint, movement, tolerance, environmental boundaries, finish, access and replacement rather than allowing a final filler / sealant / plaster layer to absorb unresolved relationships.

### Owning authority

- Governing Principle 3 — **Design the interface**.
- Part II, Chapter 6 — **The designed interface**.

### Role in the wider argument

This is the physical mechanism by which slower and faster systems coexist without accidental coupling. It converts abstract lifespan separation into buildable junction logic.

### Material downstream consequences

- Movement / Slip Junction;
- Permanent Opening / Replaceable Window and Door;
- Designed Threshold;
- Controlled Attachment Plane;
- Designed Structural Penetration;
- structural strategy **Decompose Structural Interface Functions**;
- tolerance strategies, boundary ledgers and replacement sequences.

### Current references / precedents

Current support includes conventional movement-joint and bearing practice, British tolerance guidance, construction-tolerance research, David Pye on workmanship and Shigeo Shingo / mistake-proofing as a bounded transfer. Historic building / window practice provides recurring precedents for replaceable subcomponents in durable hosts.

### Current reading posture

**Mixed conventional practice + project formulation.** Many component ideas are established construction knowledge. Treating the interface itself as a first-class architectural object joining movement, lifespan, replacement and architectural finish reads as a stronger project synthesis that ILA should test.

## ILA-C07 — Failure architecture

### Current proposition

Principle 4 states:

> **Design credible failures to reveal themselves early, remain safely managed and locally containable where practical, and be reachable for repair before they consume long-lived fabric.**

Part II asks:

> **If the first defence fails, what happens next?**

The project combines prevention, passive damage limitation, detectability, isolation, access, drying and recovery into an architectural failure sequence.

### Owning authority

- Governing Principle 4.
- Part II, Chapter 7 — **Failure architecture**.

### Role in the wider argument

This extends long-life design beyond normal operation. A maintainable house is expected to manage credible abnormal states without turning every small defect into hidden whole-building damage.

### Material downstream consequences

- Fail-Safe Water Distribution strategy;
- Visible Leakage Path;
- Failure-Tolerant Wet Service Room;
- outward / observable roof-water failure logic;
- granular isolation;
- dry-side access and drainage / drying requirements;
- Reference House rejection of inward courtyard roof-failure geography.

### Current references / precedents

Current material cites Norwegian water-installation guidance, British hot-water / tundish practice, conservation moisture logic and Charles Perrow’s distinction about tightly coupled systems as a deliberately narrow cross-domain transfer.

### Current reading posture

**Project architectural synthesis with bounded cross-domain support.** Individual safety / drainage principles are established. The integrated architectural framing of visible, local, repairable failure currently reads as a project synthesis.

## ILA-C08 — Maintenance geography

### Current proposition

Principle 5 states:

> **Organise maintenance-intensive systems, working spaces, access routes, supporting ground and replacement paths as a coherent spatial topology established during design.**

Part II insists that accessibility is more than nominal reachability: the work requires approach, posture, tools, handling and withdrawal geometry.

### Owning authority

- Governing Principle 5.
- Part II, Chapter 8 — **Maintenance geography**.
- External-maintenance research and manuscript material.

### Role in the wider argument

This turns maintenance from a component-level detail into a spatial / architectural problem. It is one of the principal moves by which the project claims that maintenance belongs in the plan, section and site strategy.

### Material downstream consequences

- Plant Room as Service Hub;
- Accessible Vertical Service Zone;
- coherent horizontal routes;
- dry-side access to high-service rooms;
- Roof Maintenance Route;
- Ground-Supported Façade Access;
- replacement paths and withdrawal geometry in the Reference House.

### Current references / precedents

The manuscript explicitly invokes Steven Jackson and Shannon Mattern. The transformation research also cites Stephen Graham and Nigel Thrift. Professional support comes from CDM, CIBSE and circular / maintainability guidance.

### Current reading posture

**Project formulation with explicit maintenance lineage.** The fact that maintenance is ordinary and spatial is credited to a wider repair / maintenance literature. The phrase and prospective architectural topology **maintenance geography** presently read as project-specific formulation.

## ILA-C09 — Service topology as architectural ordering

### Current proposition

The service-topology sequence asks:

> **In what order should the building create places for services so that later systems can be resolved without consuming the architecture that should have constrained them?**

The sequence moves from service demand and utility entry through hub, vertical distribution, high-service adjacencies, horizontal routes, controlled crossings, water failure, room-scale distribution and stewardship identity — with explicit rewind conditions when topology exposes a weak plan.

### Owning authority

- `docs/patterns/service-topology-sequence.md`.
- Pattern system / Part III.
- Supported by Principles 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 10.

### Role in the wider argument

This is the project’s strongest demonstrated generative sequence. It claims that service distribution is not residual MEP routing after architecture is fixed; consequential service geography is an architectural ordering problem that can force upstream changes in rooms, structure and section.

### Material downstream consequences

- Controlled Utility Entry;
- Plant Room as Service Hub;
- Accessible Vertical Service Zone;
- High-Service-Room Service Wall;
- Coherent Horizontal Service Route;
- Compartmented Service Void;
- Designed Structural Penetration;
- fail-safe water patterns;
- Physical Service Index;
- Reference House service-side / gallery topology.

### Current references / precedents

Current project evidence is mainly internal design research, pattern development, professional maintainability requirements and the Reference House integration test. The manuscript does not presently provide a developed named intellectual lineage for the **architectural ordering of services** as a concept family.

### Current reading posture

**Apparently project-specific operational synthesis.** This is precisely the kind of impression ILA must test against architectural traditions of services, servant / served order, infrastructural hierarchy, open building and related work without preselecting a canon.

## ILA-C10 — Ordinary parts, stable interfaces and reproducible assembly

### Current proposition

Principle 6 states:

> **Prefer widely understood components, standard interfaces and ordinary fabrication methods; concentrate invention in the architectural arrangement and at interfaces that genuinely require it.**

Part V adds the manufacturing gradient:

> **Build the permanent house. Assemble the changeable house inside it.**

Commodity components should remain commodity products; bespoke architectural assemblies should be repeatable and remanufacturable rather than dependent on unique proprietary systems.

### Owning authority

- Governing Principle 6.
- Part V, Chapter 21 — **Standardisation, manufacture and reliable assembly**.

### Role in the wider argument

This is the delivery discipline preventing serviceability from creating a fragile custom machine. Long life depends not only on physical removability but also on future intelligibility, supply, ordinary tools and the ability to reproduce missing parts.

### Material downstream consequences

- standard fixings / commodity plant inside architectural enclosures;
- small families of repeated interface infrastructure;
- controlled manufacture of faster-changing assemblies where worthwhile;
- representative-installer trials;
- refusal of bespoke mechanisms that cannot be remade after supplier disappearance.

### Current references / precedents

Current reader-facing sources include David Pye, Ursula Franklin and Shigeo Shingo. The Preface uses containerisation / standardised interfaces and Paxton / Crystal Palace as narrative precedents for interoperability and controlled manufacture.

### Current reading posture

**Mixed established making theory + project judgement.** Standardisation, workmanship theory and mistake-proofing are explicitly inherited / transferred. The rule about where to concentrate invention within a century-scale domestic platform reads as project judgement.

## ILA-C11 — Intergenerational legibility and stewardship

### Current proposition

Principle 10 states:

> **Preserve enough information—in records and, for critical systems, in the building itself—that future owners and trades can understand, isolate, maintain and alter it safely.**

It explicitly ranks physical legibility above dependence on a perfect digital archive: records should reinforce discoverability rather than be the only means by which the house can be understood.

### Owning authority

- Governing Principle 10.
- Part V, Chapter 20 — governance / weak-stewardship floor.

### Role in the wider argument

Long life crosses owners, software systems, suppliers and professional teams. The project therefore treats transfer of understanding as part of durability rather than an administrative afterthought.

### Material downstream consequences

- Physical Service Index;
- durable system identifiers shared with drawings and isolation;
- building passport / digital model as enhanced stewardship rather than sole authority;
- discoverable service geography and ordinary fixing logic;
- change-control records for consequential boundaries and interfaces.

### Current references / precedents

Current Sources and further reading includes Ivan Illich, James C. Scott and Langdon Winner under agency / legibility / dependence, but ILA-00A found no corresponding named invocation in the main argument. DfD / maintenance guidance also supports documentation and physical legibility at an evidential level.

### Current reading posture

**Project synthesis / judgement.** The current reader sees a strong architectural position about weak stewardship and information durability without an explicit conceptual ancestry in the prose.

## ILA-C12 — Domestic repose / low vigilance

### Current proposition

Principle 8 states:

> **Treat the house as a place of repeated occupation and recovery. Its ordinary spaces should minimise unnecessary vigilance by being physically comfortable, spatially comprehensible, capable of privacy and retreat, locally controllable and perceptually settled.**

Part I summarises this as:

> **A good home should minimise unnecessary vigilance.**

The project explicitly refuses to turn this into a clinical or physiological claim and gives stronger evidential weight to physical comfort, privacy, local control and comprehensibility than to aesthetic claims about structural appearance.

### Owning authority

- Governing Principle 8.
- Part I, Chapter 3 — **Repose as a domestic requirement**.
- dedicated Repose research / evidence-audit records.

### Role in the wider argument

Repose is the occupation-side constraint preventing maintainable / technical architecture from making the home visually or cognitively demanding. It also gives the project a criterion for judging whether technical legibility has become technical theatre.

### Material downstream consequences

- privacy and acoustic planning;
- local environmental control;
- rejection of gratuitously exposed technical systems;
- bounded review of apparent structural precariousness;
- HFI concern with repeated bodily / cognitive compensation;
- demand that maintainability remain compatible with ordinary domestic rooms.

### Current references / precedents

Current evidence includes research on architectural coherence / fascination, perceived environmental control, privacy and intuitive physical perception. These are presented as evidence with explicit limits rather than as a single architectural genealogy.

### Current reading posture

**Project architectural proposition supported by bounded evidence.** The term and integration of these concerns under **Repose** read as project-authored; whether there is an important prior architectural / phenomenological lineage is not decided here.

## ILA-C13 — Passive architecture does the first work

### Current proposition

Principle 9 states:

> **Use form, envelope, orientation, shading, mass, controllable openings, drainage and gravity to reduce demands on active systems before adding mechanical or electronic correction.**

The constitution immediately limits the claim: passive-first does not mean passive-only; measured performance, health and resilience outrank ideological simplicity.

### Owning authority

- Governing Principle 9 — **Let passive architecture do the first work**.

### Role in the wider argument

This standing discipline prevents a serviceable house from becoming unnecessarily dependent on active technical systems and ensures that permanent geometry performs useful environmental work before faster-changing equipment is added.

### Material downstream consequences

- orientation / shading / opening strategy;
- geometry-first drainage;
- source-capture extraction;
- future-climate scenario testing;
- preference for passive fallback where proportionate;
- explicit acceptance of mechanical systems where performance requires them.

### Current references / precedents

The project treats this largely as established environmental design / building-physics practice. Alex Gordon’s “long life, loose fit, low energy” is already present in the wider temporal lineage, but the constitution does not claim passive-first design as an Ovid invention.

### Current reading posture

**Established architectural discipline, deliberately bounded.** ILA should only pursue deeper lineage if it materially affects how this standing discipline is framed relative to the rest of the project.

## ILA-C14 — Technical necessity must become architecture

### Current proposition

Principle 11 states:

> **Technical necessities encountered by occupants should be resolved through architectural composition, material and craft rather than treated as concealed accidents or gratuitous technical display.**

Part II expresses the related tectonic position: construction may be concealed, but it should not be falsified; joints, access, wear, movement and replacement should become part of the architectural resolution where they affect occupied space.

### Owning authority

- Governing Principle 11 — **Resolve technology as architecture**.
- Part II, Chapter 6 — tectonic honesty / architectural expression.

### Role in the wider argument

This is the architectural-quality constraint preventing the platform from degenerating into commercial access panels, exposed trunking or an aesthetic of maintenance for its own sake.

### Material downstream consequences

- architraves / skirtings / thresholds / panels may perform genuine interface work;
- access mechanisms can be concealed by real architectural elements;
- technical systems are not required to advertise themselves;
- counterfeit structural / material expression is rejected;
- ordinary rooms remain the desired experiential result.

### Current references / precedents

The current main argument does not anchor this position to one named tectonic theorist. The Preface’s Crystal Palace discussion and the DRW programme reinforce the broader demand that technical rationality become architecture, but DRW precedents remain representation authority rather than intellectual ancestry.

### Current reading posture

**Architectural judgement / tectonic position.** The stance is presented confidently but without a developed intellectual genealogy. Significant prior articulation would matter to positioning, so it remains in the spine.

## ILA-C15 — Pattern and generative-sequence knowledge

### Current proposition

The Part III model claims that reusable architectural knowledge can sit between broad doctrine and project-specific implementation as **patterns**: recurring architectural responses with context, forces, relationships, variation, consequences and scoped evidence.

A mature pattern language adds:

1. relationships among patterns; and
2. generative sequences that order consequential design decisions.

The current corpus explicitly refuses to claim one uniformly mature language; service topology is the strongest demonstrated generative sequence.

### Owning authority

- `docs/patterns/language-model.md`.
- `docs/patterns/README.md`.
- Part III publication layer.

### Role in the wider argument

This is the project’s epistemic / design-method bridge between principle and building. It determines how Ovid’s House turns broad claims into reusable architectural moves without collapsing them into universal rules or software constraints.

### Material downstream consequences

- distinction among constitution → strategy → pattern → implementation family → occurrence;
- typed pattern relationships;
- admission test and evidence / maturity separation;
- service-topology generative sequence;
- Reference House as an integration test rather than validation;
- explicit boundary between architectural pattern and compiler rule.

### Current references / precedents

Christopher Alexander, Sara Ishikawa and Murray Silverstein are present in Sources and further reading, and the pattern README explicitly describes the corpus as not yet a uniformly mature **Alexander-style pattern language**. The present authoring contract, evidence model and pattern/compiler separation are largely project-defined in current reader-facing material.

### Current reading posture

**Explicit pattern-language ancestry + project-specific contract.** The existence and ambition of pattern language are openly inherited; the exact Ovid’s House pattern ontology, evidence discipline and separation from computational rules read as project-specific method.

# 5. Claims deliberately kept outside the spine

ILA-00B intentionally does **not** promote the following into independent intellectual-lineage claims:

- individual Building Regulations, BS, CIBSE, RICS or ISO requirements;
- specific dimensions, clearances, loads, environmental targets or product constraints;
- one Reference House implementation such as the 900 × 700 mm riser or plant-room dimensions;
- individual patterns whose conceptual burden is already represented by a spine claim;
- G-01 Georgian grammar rules as a separate intellectual-history family at this stage — they are a selected architectural language / empirical grammar programme rather than a governing Ovid proposition;
- HFI anthropometric and accessibility findings except where they have materially entered Principles 1 or 8;
- OCG intervention-consequence machinery except where it supports proportional serviceability and bounded maintenance;
- drawing / typography precedent, which remains DRW / PLT / TYP authority;
- Ovid / Heraclitus / Ship of Theseus as independent architectural claims — they belong under ILA-C04 as interpretive frames unless later research demonstrates a stronger architectural role.

This exclusion is important. ILA is auditing intellectual position, not creating a provenance edge for every project fact.

## 8. Important baseline observations without verdict

Several areas now deserve particular care in later packets:

1. **The project already credits its most obvious time-layer lineage.** Habraken, Duffy, Brand and Gordon are not absent from the current argument. The audit question is therefore precision, depth, chronology and the actual Ovid delta — not simple name insertion.
2. **Several of the most distinctive-looking Ovid formulations are integrations rather than isolated inventions.** `architectural platform`, `proportional serviceability`, `maintenance geography`, `failure architecture` and the service-topology sequence combine multiple kinds of existing knowledge. Later ILA work must test the combination itself.
3. **The current project is much lighter on explicit architectural lineage for services, interfaces, stewardship and tectonic resolution than for time/layers.** This is a search priority, not yet an under-attribution finding.
4. **Repose needs special restraint.** It has substantial empirical support but should not be turned into a grand intellectual-history excursion unless a prior architectural concept materially changes how the claim should be framed.
5. **Pattern-language ancestry is acknowledged but not deeply worked through.** ILA should distinguish Alexander’s actual conceptual contribution from the project’s later evidence / maturity / compiler-boundary machinery.
