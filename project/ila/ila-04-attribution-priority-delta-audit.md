# 4. C02 — Differential rates and destructive coupling

## Final finding

`UNDER_ATTRIBUTED + SUBSTANTIVE_QUALIFICATION_REQUIRED`.

## Why

This is the single clear T5 result of ILA.

The present Part I sequence says, in effect:

1. Habraken/Duffy/Brand/Gordon establish temporal/layered change;
2. `But a diagram of layers does not by itself produce an architecture.`;
3. `The important condition is coupling.`

The audit proves that this transition assigns too much conceptual distance between the cited lineage and the coupling mechanism.

Brand already explicitly describes:

- layers needing sufficient slippage;
- differently paced systems being destructively embedded;
- fast change damaging or `tearing up` slower construction.

Earlier than Brand, Schulitz/SCAG states independent exchange without alteration/destruction of other elements, and Century Housing System formalises replacement dependency by damage to adjacent components.

Therefore Ovid cannot present **coupling itself** as the conceptual step beyond the named temporal lineage.

The manuscript's one-sentence test remains excellent:

> **A component's lifespan matters less than the lifespan of the things that must be destroyed to replace it.**

ILA finds that sentence to be a strong **project-authored compression / operational test** of inherited territory, not a priority claim that should be deleted.

## Final Ovid delta

`INHERITS + SYNTHESISES + OPERATIONALISES`.

Ovid inherits differential rates, slippage/destructive embedding and non-destructive replacement. It then binds them to:

- actual removal sequence;
- boundary reinstatement;
- co-renewal;
- failure geography;
- maintenance geography;
- proportionality;
- workmanship;
- evidence/pattern/compiler governance.

## Minimum treatment

**T5 — SUBSTANTIVE_CORRECTION.**

T5 here does not imply a large rewrite. It means the **logic of the transition** must change, not merely gain a footnote.

ILA-05C should:

- keep Duffy and Brand in the temporal lineage;
- make Brand's slippage/destructive-embedding contribution explicit enough that coupling is not presented as Ovid's post-Brand discovery;
- distinguish Habraken's support/infill control lineage from Duffy/Brand's rate-of-change lineage without turning the paragraph into historiography;
- retain the Ovid destruction-test sentence as the project's operative compression;
- use SCAG/CHS primarily in note/reference apparatus unless prose requires them for accuracy.

## Why no larger treatment

The correction is local. It does not undermine the doctrine or require a new chapter. The Ovid operationalisation remains materially useful after the historical claim ceiling is corrected.

# 6. C04 — Selective permanence / permanence through transformation

## Final finding

`UNDER_ATTRIBUTED`.

## Why

The current manuscript correctly cites Open Building as precedent for durable support plus changeable infill, but the audit establishes a closer lineage for the claim that **committed architectural form can retain identity while accepting changed use**.

By 1961–63:

- Van Eyck warns that universal flexibility can become **false neutrality**;
- Hertzberger's **polyvalence** gives the positive architectural alternative: form can remain itself while supporting different uses/interpretations.

This is materially close to current Ovid language that:

- the result need not be a neutral container;
- architecture can remain recognisably itself;
- capacity and compatibility may matter more than literal movement.

A second precision issue concerns Habraken: the original support/infill argument is governed substantially by independent dwelling / distributed control, not simply by component change rates. The current temporal paragraph is directionally correct but historically compressed.

## Final Ovid delta

`SYNTHESISES + OPERATIONALISES`.

Ovid combines committed/polyvalent form with:

- temporal/service-life separation;
- destructive-coupling discipline;
- proportional serviceability;
- maintenance/failure geography;
- explicit permanent/changeable allocation.

The project terms `selective permanence`, `permanence through transformation` and `persistence conditions` remain project-authored formulations.

## Minimum treatment

**T3 — LOCAL_PROSE_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT.**

ILA-05C should add one compact acknowledgement in or immediately around the selective-permanence passage that:

- recognises the older critique of neutral universal flexibility;
- names Hertzberger's polyvalence, with Van Eyck where the sentence remains readable;
- does not imply a proven Van Eyck→Hertzberger derivation;
- leaves Open Building to carry the support/infill precedent it actually owns.

## Why not T4

The book does not need a structural history passage. One well-placed sentence plus notes can restore the intellectual position without interrupting the architectural argument.

# 14. C12 — Domestic Repose / low vigilance

## Final finding

`UNDER_ATTRIBUTED`.

## Why

The present Repose text is epistemically strong. It carefully distinguishes:

- doctrine term from clinical construct;
- physical comfort/privacy/control evidence from weaker aesthetic hypotheses;
- architectural judgement from physiological claim.

ILA does **not** require weakening that work.

The attribution issue is historical.

The 1902 Wainwright account of W. H. Bidlake's teaching is exceptionally close:

- the house as a haven of rest;
- domestic rather than heroic virtues;
- suspicion of self-assertive architectural features;
- decoration should not seize or rivet the occupant's unwilling attention.

Wainwright labels the first of the reported principles the **`Lamp of Repose`**.

This creates a material antecedent to both the project term `Repose` and the underlying low-attention domestic posture.

Other established territory includes Rybczynski's compound comfort, Alexander's privacy/retreat spatial patterns, Aalto's humanisation of technical architecture, Altman's privacy-regulation model and Calm Technology's low-attention technical relationship.

The project still owns its exact contemporary synthesis and evidence contract.

### Attribution caution

ILA-05 must **not** write `Bidlake's Lamp of Repose` as if a direct Bidlake text using that phrase has been found.

The defensible form is equivalent to:

- Wainwright's 1902 account of Bidlake's teaching;
- Wainwright called the first principle the `Lamp of Repose`;
- Bidlake's reported posture concerned the home as a place of rest and avoidance of attention-seizing self-assertion.

## Final Ovid delta

`SYNTHESISES + OPERATIONALISES + NARROWS`.

Ovid combines domestic, privacy, humanising, environmental-control and attention traditions and places explicit evidence ceilings around the combined architectural criterion.

The sentence:

> **A good home should minimise unnecessary vigilance.**

remains a project-authored formulation and should be preserved.

## Minimum treatment
