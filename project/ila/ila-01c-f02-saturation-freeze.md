# 1. Decision

F02 is **FROZEN** at discovery stage.

The adversarial pass did not overturn the central ILA-01B finding that Ovid's position belongs inside a long architectural argument about **where change should occur, what should remain committed, and how much future capacity is worth buying now**.

It did, however, materially correct the discovery model in three places:

1. **ordinary British housing provides an earlier and more direct domestic capacity lineage than the F02 discovery register initially captured** — the 1961 Parker Morris report explicitly rejected room-by-room space prescriptions because they inhibit flexibility both at initial design and in later use;
2. **the infrastructure / changeable-user-realm proposition predates Habraken in parallel form** — Yona Friedman's mobile architecture / Spatial City work from the late 1950s already places user-controlled dwellings or components inside a collectively ordered `spatial infrastructure`; and
3. **modern professional adaptability guidance already contains a strong proportionality boundary** — ISO 20887:2020 explicitly rejects overbuilding for a vast number of unknowns and requires project-specific selection under cost, risk, obsolescence, technical complexity and life-cycle trade-offs.

These additions **narrow**, rather than broaden, the space in which Ovid can later claim a material contribution.

The strongest bounded conclusion is now:

> **Ovid's F02 propositions do not originate the architectural middle ground between fixity and universal flexibility. Long before this project, architects, housing researchers and professional guidance had distinguished stable support from changeable occupation, committed form from neutral flexibility, adaptability from physical transformability, and useful future capacity from speculative over-provision. Ovid's possible contribution lies only in how those inherited positions are synthesised, bounded and operationally enforced across the wider house system.**

That is enough to stop broad discovery.

# 2. What F02 is actually about

F02 should not be narrated as a history of `flexible architecture`.

The term is too broad and hides several different questions.

The discovery-saturated family separates at least five:

1. **control** — who is authorised to decide what changes and at what level;
2. **physical transformability** — whether walls, partitions, components or larger assemblies must physically move;
3. **capacity / polyvalence / loose fit** — whether a stable architectural form can support several uses without physical transformation;
4. **allocation of permanence and change** — which relationships are deliberately long-lived and which are expected to change;
5. **option value / proportional provision** — how much present space, material, cost, complexity and risk uncertain future change deserves.

Those questions overlap physically but have different intellectual histories.

The frozen F02 topology therefore uses **five streams**, not one heroic genealogy.

# 3. Stream A — user-controlled / open framework

This stream treats architecture as a durable collective or ordering framework within which more local states remain open to later decision.

It contains materially different traditions that should not be collapsed into one chain.

## 3.1 Yona Friedman — mobile architecture / Spatial City, 1956–58 onward

### Discovery correction

Friedman matters more to F02 than the initial discovery register allowed.

His `mobile architecture` proposition emerged in the CIAM 10 context in 1956 and was developed through his 1958 manifesto and Spatial City work.

The Spatial City establishes:

- a large collective structural grid / **spatial infrastructure**;
- user-controlled or user-determined dwelling components inserted into that infrastructure;
- a deliberate refusal to make the architect determine the final pattern of occupation;
- a stable superior order within which local elements remain mobile/changeable.

MoMA's account of the 1958 Spatial City explicitly describes the wide grid of pillars and slabs as the `spatial infrastructure`, designed for collective use, while structural elements connected to the individual user remain radically mobile.

Authoritative record:

- MoMA, Yona Friedman, `Spatial City`, 1958: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/800
- MoMA, `Spatial City Project`, 1958–59: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/104695

### Relationship to C05

Friedman is an important **earlier parallel** to:

`collective / long-lived enabling infrastructure → more local changeable user realm`.

This prevents any later F02 graph from treating Habraken as the sole conceptual origin of that general architectural relation.

### Relationship to Ovid

The overlap is structural but the architectural posture is very different.

Friedman maximises user freedom and deliberately avoids committing the local architecture to a fixed style or pattern of use. Ovid deliberately retains strong permanent spatial and architectural commitments.

The correct provisional type is therefore:

- `NO_DOCUMENTED_PROJECT_CONTACT`
- `EARLIER_ANTECEDENT` / `CONVERGENT_PARALLEL` for the enabling-infrastructure relation

—not a formative Ovid source.

### Dossier disposition

Retain in the F02 source map. Do not automatically promote to a full ILA-02 dossier unless the C05 infrastructure-priority question later requires it.

Habraken / Kendall are closer to the actual Ovid platform problem.

## 3.2 Oskar Hansen — Open Form, 1959/61

Hansen's Open Form similarly resists the closed finished architectural object and treats architecture as a background/framework within which later users and events can complete or alter the work.

Its importance is to show that **openness to future authorship** was an explicit architectural programme by the turn of the 1960s.

However:

- Ovid is not trying to maximise co-authorship;
- it does not reject strong permanent spatial order;
- the technical serviceability problem is more bounded than Hansen's social/artistic programme.

Retain as a contextual `EARLIER_ANTECEDENT` / `CONVERGENT_PARALLEL`, not a core F02 dossier unless later primary reading changes the claim matrix.

## 3.3 John Weeks — bounded generality / indeterminate architecture

Weeks remains one of the strongest F02 antecedents.

His 1960 `Planning for Growth and Change` / `duffle-coat` reasoning is important because it is **not** universal flexibility.

The design problem is studied across a known range of needs and resolved into a small set of useful general-purpose capacities rather than one bespoke room for every present task or one infinitely adjustable space.

That is a direct precursor to:

- bounded loose fit;
- purposeful dimensional generosity;
- room/department capacity without constant physical movement.

His later `Indeterminate Architecture` and Northwick Park work develops a second scale:

- stable routes / streets / geography;
- adaptable or replaceable subordinate units;
- future change that does not require the whole composition to be remade.

Weeks therefore bridges C03, C04 and C05 unusually well.

He also supplies an important counterpoint:

- the future does not arrive exactly as predicted;
- flexibility cannot be justified simply by purchasing redundant area;
- institutional change can invalidate original assumptions.

Discovery disposition:

- retain as a high-priority ILA-02 dossier candidate.

## 3.4 N. John Habraken / SAR / Open Building

Habraken remains central, but the freeze preserves the correction from ILA-01B:

> **support / infill is fundamentally a proposition about levels of control and responsibility, not merely a technical diagram of long-life and short-life parts.**

The original mass-housing problem asks how a durable collective order can coexist with meaningful household-level agency.

That produces an architectural distinction between:

- collective / longer-term support;

That transfer must be stated rather than allowing support/infill to become a generic technical permission for demountability.

# 4. Stream B — committed / polyvalent architecture

This stream is the strongest intellectual correction to any narrative in which adaptability naturally tends toward neutrality or maximum transformability.

## 4.1 Aldo van Eyck — critique of flexibility as false neutrality, 1962

Van Eyck's 1962 `Forum` argument treats flexibility as dangerous when promoted from a useful capacity into an architectural absolute.

The important proposition is:

> trying to be equally available to every unknown future can erase the architectural commitments through which a place becomes intelligible and inhabitable.

Ovid's selective-permanence argument is not identical, but it occupies the same fault line.

The project should not later imply that its rejection of `uncertain future → neutral universal shell` is historically novel.

Retain Van Eyck as an early `EARLIER_ANTECEDENT` / material counter-position to maximal flexibility.

---

## 4.2 Herman Hertzberger — flexibility versus polyvalence, 1962/63

Hertzberger remains the **closest early conceptual antecedent** located for a central part of C04.

His distinction is not simply fixed versus flexible.

**Polyvalence** proposes that a form can:

- retain a clear identity and architectural commitment;
- remain physically itself;
- support several interpretations / uses.

The neutral flexible alternative risks withholding architecture in anticipation of an unknowable future.

This materially anticipates Ovid's current position that:

- a room does not need to physically transform to be adaptable;
- useful dimensions, openings and relationships can support different patterns of occupation;
- **capacity and compatibility can matter more than literal movement**.

### Freeze consequence

The underlying middle ground is prior art.

Ovid must not later imply that it invented the proposition:

`strong / settled architecture can remain itself while supporting changed use`.

### Project contact

No pre-ILA durable contact was found.

Use:

- `NO_DOCUMENTED_PROJECT_CONTACT`
- strong candidate `EARLIER_ANTECEDENT`

### Dossier disposition

Highest priority for C04.

# 5. Stream C — ordinary domestic adaptability

The adversarial pass materially strengthens this stream.

Its importance is that future-use capacity is not confined to avant-garde flexible architecture or support/infill systems.

Ordinary housing standards and room planning already developed a quieter form of adaptability.

## 5.1 Parker Morris / _Homes for Today and Tomorrow_, 1961 — adversarial correction

### Identity

**Great Britain, Ministry of Housing and Local Government, _Homes for Today and Tomorrow: Report of a Sub-Committee of the Central Housing Advisory Committee_, HMSO, 1961.**

Chair: Sir Parker Morris.

Bibliographic record:

- Google Books: https://books.google.com/books/about/Homes_for_Today_and_Tomorrow.html?id=pkKu0AEACAAJ

Strong historical analyses quoting the report directly:

- `Homes for Today and Tomorrow: Britain's Parker Morris Standards and the West Ham Experimental Scheme`, 2023: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20507828.2022.2198299
- `Space standards and housing design: typological experimentation in England and Chile`, 2022: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13602365.2022.2045340

### Relevant proposition

The report rejects the earlier practice of setting space standards by **individual named rooms** because doing so assumes both:

- a conventional arrangement of the dwelling; and
- a particular use for each room.

The report explicitly says that this inhibits flexibility in both initial design and later dwelling use.

Its response was to emphasise:

- overall dwelling capacity / space;
- less prescriptive allocation to named room functions;
- later flexibility in household use.

This is highly relevant because it provides a **British domestic** antecedent to the current Ovid argument that room usefulness should not depend on one tightly prescribed programme.

### Important nuance

Parker Morris also explored moveable partitions and `openable` rooms, so it should not be reduced to a pure polyvalence doctrine.

The stronger F02 transfer is narrower:

> fixed functional labels and minimum room-by-room prescriptions can make a dwelling less adaptable than a sufficiently generous, less predetermined spatial envelope.

### C04 relationship

Strong candidate `EARLIER_ANTECEDENT` to:

- low functional predetermination;
- useful domestic capacity;
- changed use without total redesign.

### C03 relationship / counterpoint

Parker Morris also exposes the cost boundary.

The resulting larger spatial standards and adaptable-house experiments came under increasing cost pressure during the late 1960s; practical schemes compromised some of the original flexibility aspirations.

This is useful evidence that:

- future capacity is not free;
- spatial generosity itself has a permanent burden;
- adaptability goals can be eroded when the cost model is not robust.

### Project contact

Repository search found no pre-ILA Parker Morris reference.

Use:

- `NO_DOCUMENTED_PROJECT_CONTACT`

### Dossier disposition

Promote to a bounded F02 dossier or paired domestic-capacity dossier with Rabeneck/Sheppard/Town.

It is too directly relevant to the British domestic argument to remain a footnote.

## 5.2 Rabeneck / Sheppard / Town — adaptable housing without flexibility machinery, 1973–74

These papers remain the strongest explicit housing-theory antecedent to Ovid's current domestic language.

They attack both:

- tight-fit minimum functionalism; and
- technical flexibility that substitutes mechanisms for genuine user freedom.

Their adaptable-housing response relies substantially on ordinary planning:

- useful room-size variation;
- relationships between rooms;
- slightly generous openings;
- low overt predetermination of room function.

This is extremely close to Ovid's preference for:

- well-proportioned rooms;
- useful dimensions;
- sensible doors/openings;
- multiple occupation patterns without physical transformation.

### Freeze consequence

The broad proposition `ordinary room capacity can be more valuable than moving architecture` is deeply inherited.

### Dossier disposition

Highest priority domestic F02 dossier, with Parker Morris as an earlier public-housing antecedent.

---

## 5.3 Steven Groák — adaptability versus flexibility, 1992

Groák's vocabulary remains useful:

- adaptability concerns capacity for different social uses;
- flexibility concerns capacity for different physical arrangements.

It clarifies the distinction but does not displace the earlier antecedents.

Retain as `SUPPORTING` later vocabulary unless ILA-02 finds a stronger reason to promote it.

## 5.4 Maccreanor / ordinary robust type

Later work on adaptable building stock is useful because it shows a non-mechanical route to long-term usefulness:

- canal houses;
- Georgian / terraced housing;
- warehouses;
- other ordinary robust building types

can accommodate changing uses because of proportion, depth, openings, access, structure and general spatial capacity rather than visible flexibility apparatus.

This supports Ovid's direction, but it does not alter the historical model after Parker Morris, Hertzberger and Rabeneck.

Retain as `EVIDENTIAL_SUPPORT` / later `CONVERGENT_PARALLEL`, not a core dossier.

---

## 5.5 Schneider & Till — critical synthesis, 2005

Schneider and Till remain a high-value later source because they organise and criticise the field rather than merely repeating it.

Their useful distinctions include:

- adaptability in use versus physical flexibility;
- `soft` versus `hard` flexibility;
- user agency versus architect-controlled mechanisms;
- ordinary/background adaptability versus visible technical apparatus.

Their counterpoints remain material:

- flexibility mechanisms can become obsolete;
- mechanisms can go unused;
- technical systems intended as freedom can extend designer control into occupation;
- adaptable housing often succeeds precisely because the flexibility recedes into ordinary architecture.

Use as a later critical synthesis / counterpoint, not as the origin of C04.

### Dossier disposition

Retain a bounded ILA-02 dossier or strong secondary-source map because it can prevent the primary-source dossiers from becoming a list of celebrated examples without critical structure.

# 6. Stream D — proportional / selective future provision

This stream now substantially closes the historical space around C03.

The general proposition that future adaptability must earn its present burden is clearly inherited.

## 6.1 Weeks — bounded range rather than infinite adjustability

Weeks's `duffle-coat` analogy remains an early architectural form of proportional future capacity:

- study the plausible range;
- choose a small set of useful general capacities;
- do not tailor every room to one present task;
- do not make everything infinitely adjustable.

This is one of the clearest early ancestors to Ovid's **bounded** serviceability posture.

---

## 6.2 SCAG — homogeneous adaptability is uneconomical

F01 already established SCAG as a strong early C02 source.

For C03 its important proposition is different:

> different parts require different amounts and frequencies of change; making everything equally adaptable is economically wasteful.

This is direct prior art for **selective allocation of adaptability**.

SCAG therefore serves F01 and F02 through one shared later dossier rather than being duplicated.

---

## 6.3 Alex Gordon — long life / loose fit / low energy, 1971

The freeze retains the ILA-01B chronology correction.

The earliest exact primary statement located in the bounded search is Gordon's RIBA Presidential Address:

- delivered **26 October 1971**;
- published in the **December 1971 RIBA Journal**;
- includes the formula `long life, loose fit, low energy`.

The address treats the three qualities as linked to long-term building value and recognises that improved long-term performance / adaptability can require greater first cost.

### Authorship caution

The primary address itself describes the wider concept as already having an increasing number of supporters.

Therefore:

- Gordon is safely the source of the exact early formula located here;
- Gordon is a major British advocate of the linked position;
- ILA must **not** make a universal invention/coinage claim without a dedicated priority study.

Weeks's relevant work predates the address by more than a decade, which further warns against reading the formula as the origin of all three concepts.

### Dossier disposition

Promote as a high-priority current-project source.

---

## 6.4 Parker Morris — spatial generosity has cost

Parker Morris adds a useful domestic C03 counterpoint.

Its adaptability strategy relied partly on **more space** and less fixed room-size prescription.

Later implementation faced strong cost pressure.

This is a direct reminder that `ordinary capacity` can still be a permanent burden:

- area has capital cost;
- area has embodied/operational consequences;
- an adaptable plan cannot be justified merely by saying `more space is better`.

That reinforces the need for Ovid's proportionality machinery while also showing the problem predates it.

## 6.5 ISO 20887:2020 — professional proportionality boundary

### Identity

**ISO 20887:2020, _Sustainability in buildings and civil engineering works — Design for disassembly and adaptability — Principles, requirements and guidance_.**

Relevant accessible copy used during this packet:

- https://www.steelconstruct.com/wp-content/uploads/ISO-20887_2020_01.pdf

### Discovery significance

This standard materially narrows the remaining C03 delta.

Its introduction explicitly says that design for adaptability/disassembly:

- **does not suggest overbuilding to meet a vast number of unknowns**;
- should be considered in relation to investment return and risk;
- requires compromises and trade-offs;
- is constrained by technical complexity, resources, time, obsolescence risk and uncertainty about cost/environmental burden.

Its general adaptability clause further distinguishes:

- **specific** known/expected adaptation;
- **general** unknown potential future adaptation;

and gives specific adaptation precedence where it can be defined.

The wider decision framework asks the project to consider, among other things:

- expected future uses;
- staged development;
- ownership and operation;
- foreseeable economic/market risks;
- likelihood of obsolescence;
- project-specific objectives.

### C03 consequence

Ovid cannot claim as distinct merely that:

- adaptability should not mean speculative overbuilding;
- not every adaptability principle belongs in every project;
- future flexibility must be weighed against cost/risk/obsolescence;
- known changes deserve stronger treatment than highly abstract unknowns;
- trade-offs should be explicit.

Those are now professional-standard prior art.

### Relationship

ISO 20887 is not primarily an intellectual ancestor of Ovid.

Use:

- `EVIDENTIAL_SUPPORT`
- contemporary professional codification of a proportionality boundary

### Project contact

Already cited in the current manuscript, so this is a current acknowledged/adopted source at least. The exact formative role for C03 remains unresolved.

### Dossier disposition

Do not create a full thinker-style intellectual dossier.

Retain a strong source card / claim-matrix role because it is decisive for what Ovid **cannot** present as a project-specific general principle.

## 6.6 Real-options methods — bounded analytical parallel

Real-options reasoning remains useful only as a method parallel:

- a future option can have value;
- creating the option has a cost;
- uncertainty alone does not justify every option;
- options should be embedded where the expected value of later choices can justify present expenditure.

This mechanism aligns strongly with C03.

But Ovid is not a financial stochastic model, and architectural value is broader than monetary expected value.

Retain as `CONVERGENT_PARALLEL` / `METHOD_ANTECEDENT` if later useful; do not expand the family into engineering economics.

# 7. Stream E — architecture as infrastructure / platform

This stream determines the safe intellectual posture for C05.

## 7.1 Generic platform relation is inherited

The broad relation:

`long-lived enabling architectural framework → shorter-term fit-out / systems / occupation`

is unquestionably established prior art.

It appears in materially different forms through:

- Friedman — spatial infrastructure + mobile/user realm;
- Habraken — support + infill / levels of control;
- Weeks — stable routes/geography + adaptable units;
- Japanese KEP / Skeleton-Infill;
- later Open Building base-building / fit-out practice;
- Kendall's explicit **architecture as infrastructure** terminology.

Ovid must not imply that the generic idea of an enabling long-life architectural `platform` for changing shorter-term parts is new merely because the project uses the word `platform`.

---

## 7.2 Stephen Kendall / Open Building — architecture as infrastructure

Kendall's later Open Building work is the most direct terminological / conceptual comparison to C05.

The infrastructure model treats:

- base building as a long-term project / investment;
- fit-out and unit-level decisions as shorter-term / independently managed;
- uncertainty as something to handle through levels and deferred decisions;
- responsibilities and supply chains as distributed across decision levels.

This is materially close to the generic C05 structure.

### Difference to Ovid

The current Ovid four-part platform adds a specific maintenance-oriented composition:

1. permanent fabric;
2. replaceable systems;
3. designed interfaces;
4. maintenance geography;

and binds that to:

- workmanship robustness;
- failure/isolation;
- withdrawal paths;
- patterns/compiler/evidence verification;
- domestic architectural repose rather than a visible technical system.

Whether that combination is a material synthesis rather than a more detailed application cannot be judged inside F02 alone.

F03, F04 and F05 own several of those allegedly differentiating ingredients.

### Freeze consequence

The only honest C05 position after F02 is:

> **the generic platform/infrastructure relation is inherited; the possible Ovid contribution is the particular cross-family domestic synthesis and enforcement architecture.**

### Dossier disposition

High priority F02 dossier / comparison source.

# 8. Frozen F02 topology

F02 discovery should now be represented as **several convergent traditions**, not a linear genealogy.

## A — User-controlled / open framework

- Friedman — mobile architecture / spatial infrastructure
- Hansen — Open Form
- Weeks — bounded generality / indeterminate architecture
- Habraken — support/infill / control levels

These share openness to later state but differ sharply in social programme, scale, physical method and amount of architectural commitment.

## B — Committed / polyvalent form

- Van Eyck — critique of false neutrality / flexibility as absolute
- Hertzberger — polyvalence

This is the closest early conceptual field to Ovid C04.

## C — Ordinary domestic adaptability

- Parker Morris — overall dwelling capacity and reduced room-function predetermination
- Rabeneck / Sheppard / Town — adaptable housing through ordinary planning rather than flexibility machinery
- later Groák / Maccreanor / Schneider & Till — vocabulary, robust type and critical synthesis

This is the closest domestic lineage to Ovid's `capacity and compatibility > literal movement` argument.

- Weeks — bounded general-purpose range
- SCAG — homogeneous adaptability is wasteful

- Parker Morris implementation — spatial capacity has cost
- ISO 20887 — no overbuilding for unknowns; project-specific cost/risk/obsolescence/trade-off framework

This closes the general conceptual space around C03.

## E — Architecture as infrastructure / platform

- Friedman — earlier spatial-infrastructure parallel

- Weeks — stable geography + adaptable subordinate units
- KEP / Skeleton-Infill / NEXT21 — built/technical precedents

This closes the generic conceptual space around C05.

# 9. Falsification tests and results

## Test A — Are Weeks / SCAG / Gordon sufficient to prove that proportional future capacity predates Ovid?

**Yes, and ISO 20887 makes the case stronger.**

The general proposition is clearly prior art:

- future capacity should be bounded;
- different parts need different amounts of adaptability;
- future-readiness has present cost;
- adaptability should be project-specific rather than universal;
- unknown futures do not justify indiscriminate overbuilding.

C03's potential Ovid delta therefore cannot be the general principle of proportionality.

---

## Test B — Is there an earlier domestic capacity tradition than Rabeneck/Sheppard/Town?

**Yes. Parker Morris materially corrects the discovery shortlist.**

The 1961 report explicitly rejects prescriptive individual-room standards because they inhibit flexibility in both initial design and later use.

This is a British domestic antecedent directly relevant to Ovid's room-capacity argument.

Rabeneck/Sheppard/Town remain important because they later provide a more explicit theory of `adaptability` through ordinary planning and attack technical flexibility/control.

---

## Test C — Are Van Eyck / Hertzberger still the strongest early antecedents to committed form capable of changed use?

**Yes.**

The adversarial search found many open/flexible precedents but no earlier source that more directly states the specific proposition:

`strong / non-neutral architectural form can remain itself while supporting several uses`.

Hertzberger's polyvalence remains the clearest early match to the current Ovid C04 middle ground.

Parker Morris strengthens the domestic-capacity branch but does not replace the polyvalence argument.

---

## Test D — Does Habraken uniquely originate the platform relation?

**No.**

Friedman provides an earlier parallel for collective infrastructure + user-controlled changing realm.

Weeks also separates stable geography from adaptable subordinate units.

Habraken remains more directly relevant to Ovid because support/infill became an architectural building-level system of control and later Open Building / base-building practice.

Therefore the correct graph is branching / convergent, not `Habraken → everyone else`.

---

## Test E — Does Friedman materially change Ovid C05 rather than merely broaden the canon?

**It changes priority understanding, but not the final Ovid delta.**

Friedman proves that the infrastructure/changeable-user-realm relation is older and more plural than a Habraken-only history.

But Friedman's maximally open user-controlled megastructure is less directly analogous to Ovid's deliberately committed domestic architecture than Habraken/Kendall.

Retain, but do not promote merely for prestige.

---

## Test F — Do Price / Archigram / Metabolism change the F02 judgement?

**No.**

They are real parts of the wider flexibility/indeterminacy field, but additional reading now mostly reinforces already-understood poles:

- temporal / replaceable architecture;
- user-responsive or plug-in systems;
- framework + changeable subordinate parts.

They do not displace the retained sources for C03–C05.

This is decision saturation, not ignorance of the canon.

---

## Test G — Is Alex Gordon safely the origin of `long life, loose fit, low energy`?

**The exact early formula is safely located in Gordon's 1971 primary address; universal coinage remains unproved.**

The primary text itself recognises a wider body of supporters around the concept, and Weeks had already developed closely related loose-fit / growth-change thinking.

Use Gordon as the exact formula source and major advocate, not as an unsupported solitary inventor.

---

## Test H — Does current professional guidance leave C03's generic proportionality claim available as an Ovid-specific idea?

**No.**

ISO 20887 explicitly:

- rejects overbuilding for a vast number of unknowns;
- distinguishes specific from general adaptation;
- states that not all principles apply in all situations;
- considers investment return and risk;
- requires trade-offs under technical complexity, resources, time, obsolescence and life-cycle burden;
- asks projects to consider likelihood of obsolescence and future-use scenarios.

That is too close to the generic Ovid C03 posture for the project to imply conceptual novelty at that level.

---

## Test I — Does `capacity and compatibility can matter more than literal movement` remain safely project-owned as an underlying idea?

**No.**

Hertzberger, Parker Morris and Rabeneck/Sheppard/Town collectively establish a deep antecedent field:

- strong form can support several uses;
- dwelling flexibility can be improved by reducing fixed functional predetermination;
- room dimensions/relationships can matter more than transformation machinery.

The Ovid sentence may remain a project-authored formulation. The mechanism it compresses is inherited.

---

## Test J — Does `architectural platform` remain safely project-owned as an underlying concept?

**Only in the narrow sense of the project's own four-part formulation.**

The generic relation is inherited.

Later ILA may accurately say:

- `Ovid's House uses architectural platform to mean ...`
