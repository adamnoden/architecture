**State:** family structure for discovery; no new source discovery or lineage verdicts

## 1. Purpose

ILA-00B froze fifteen Ovid’s House claims worth tracing. ILA-00C froze the relationship and source contract. ILA-01A now groups those claims by the **underlying intellectual problem** that later source discovery must investigate.

The family map is not a chapter outline and not a list of schools, movements or thinkers. It exists to stop ILA-01B from doing either of two bad things:

1. searching separately for a precedent for every Ovid sentence; or
2. starting from famous names and looking for places to attach them.

The correct discovery unit is the coherent intellectual question beneath several project claims.

> **A family is defined by a problem, not by a person.**

## 2. Mapping rules

The map obeys the following rules.

1. Every ILA-C01…C15 claim has **exactly one primary family**.
2. A claim may have secondary family links only where another family asks a genuinely different question about the same proposition.
3. Secondary links do not authorise duplicate research. They are handoff points between family packets.
4. Families may overlap in architecture while remaining distinct in intellectual history.
5. Technical standards, empirical evidence, built precedents, philosophical analogies and representation precedents are not promoted into concept families merely because they support one.
6. Family names describe the question Ovid needs to understand, not the terminology any eventual source is expected to use.
7. No source earns inclusion merely because it appears relevant to a family name.

# 4. ILA-F01 — Time, rates of change and coupling

## Primary claims

- **ILA-C01 — Building as a temporal object**
- **ILA-C02 — Differential rates of change and destructive coupling**

## Secondary links

- C04 — selective permanence / persistence through transformation
- C05 — architectural platform
- C06 — designed interface

## Intellectual question

> **What follows architecturally from treating a building not as a finished object but as a long-lived system in which components, uses and institutions change at different rates?**

The family must distinguish the simple observation that buildings change from the stronger claim that **rate differences create dependency problems when faster-changing things are physically captive to slower-changing things**.

## Why these claims belong together

C01 supplies the temporal ontology: the building continues after completion and must be understood through maintenance, alteration and renewal. C02 supplies the causal mechanism: different rates of change matter because replacement can propagate destruction across coupled layers.

The family therefore asks about **time structure before adaptability policy**.

## Discovery must test

- whether architectural traditions distinguish buildings by component/service/change cycles;
- whether differing rates are treated descriptively or as a design obligation;
- whether prior work identifies **coupling between rate layers** as the relevant failure mechanism;
- whether prior theory treats slow layers as constraints, supports, authorities or simply longer-lived objects;
- whether cross-disciplinary rate-hierarchy concepts materially precede or explain architectural formulations;
- whether Ovid’s “destructive coupling” framing materially sharpens an existing architectural proposition or merely renames one.

## Important tensions

- **change as fact vs change as design problem**;
- **different lifespans vs dependent lifespans**;
- **hierarchy vs interaction** — slow layers may constrain fast ones, but fast layers can also damage or force changes in slow ones;
- **description vs prescription** — a model of how buildings change does not automatically say how they should be built;
- **general systems transfer vs architectural specificity**.

## Explicit boundaries

Do not let F01 become:

- a general history of time in architecture;
- a complete service-life planning literature review;
- a history of ecological or systems hierarchy for its own sake;
- a flexibility/adaptability survey — F02 owns the normative question of what should be allowed to change;
- a maintenance-practice survey — F05 owns the geography of intervention;
- a technical durability evidence packet.

F01 closes discovery when the project understands the strongest intellectual formulations of **temporal building + differential rates + coupling**, not when every theory of architectural change has been catalogued.

# 5. ILA-F02 — Adaptability, selective permanence and architectural platform

## Primary claims

- **ILA-C03 — Proportional serviceability**
- **ILA-C04 — Selective permanence / permanence through transformation**
- **ILA-C05 — Architectural platform**

## Secondary links

- C02 — differential rates and destructive coupling
- C08 — maintenance geography
- C09 — service topology as architectural ordering

## Intellectual question

> **Given that buildings change, how should architecture decide what is stable, what is adaptable, where future capacity belongs, and how much present burden uncertain future change can justify?**

This family owns the normative move from “buildings change” to **selective architecture of change**.

## Why these claims belong together

C03 prevents future-readiness becoming maximalism. C04 asserts that changeable layers can coexist with deliberately enduring architectural relationships. C05 integrates those decisions into a platform of permanent fabric, replaceable systems, designed interfaces and maintenance geography.

Together they define Ovid’s answer to the flexibility problem: **not maximum changeability, but deliberately located changeability**.

## Discovery must test

- traditions separating enduring framework from changeable occupation / infill;
- theories of loose fit, flexibility, adaptability, indeterminacy and capacity;
- arguments for permanent spatial or architectural order within adaptable systems;
- critiques of universal flexibility and over-provision;
- earlier formulations of targeted, proportional or probabilistic future capacity;
- whether “platform” is a materially established architectural concept, a cross-domain transfer, or an Ovid synthesis;
- distinctions among physical transformability, functional capacity, compatibility and replaceability.

## Important tensions

- **flexibility vs architectural identity**;
- **future capacity vs present burden**;
- **support/infill freedom vs domestic spatial order**;
- **loose fit vs purposeful fit**;
- **option value vs speculative infrastructure**;
- **adaptability as social agency vs adaptability as technical mechanism**;
- **platform neutrality vs architecture with strong permanent commitments**.

## Explicit boundaries

Do not let F02 become:

- a complete history of flexible architecture;
- a catalogue of movable walls, plug-in megastructures or transformable houses;
- a technical cost-benefit study of every adaptable provision;
- service routing research — F05 owns service geography;
- interface detailing research — F03 owns the joint between long-life and short-life systems;
- philosophical identity-over-time research for its own sake.

Interpretive material about persistence belongs here only where it clarifies the **architectural choice between permanence and change**.

# 6. ILA-F03 — Interfaces, assembly and tectonic resolution

## Primary claims

- **ILA-C06 — Designed interface**
- **ILA-C10 — Ordinary parts, stable interfaces and reproducible assembly**
- **ILA-C14 — Technical necessity must become architecture**

## Secondary links

- C02 — destructive coupling occurs at interfaces
- C05 — the architectural platform depends on designed interfaces
- C07 — failure often propagates through interfaces
- C11 — future legibility depends partly on readable assembly

## Intellectual question

> **How should architecture design the boundary between things that move, age, fail, are made differently or are replaced at different times?**

The family covers the interface as a real architectural object: joint, fixing plane, threshold, separation layer, removable connection, standard interface and tectonic resolution.

## Why these claims belong together

C06 makes the junction itself a design responsibility. C10 asks how ordinary parts and reproducible assembly can remain viable over long life. C14 prevents technical resolution from becoming either hidden accident or gratuitous technical display.

Together they ask how **separation and connection become architecture**.

## Discovery must test

- traditions that treat joints/interfaces as primary architectural or tectonic elements;
- theories of separation, movement accommodation and reversible connection where they materially concern long-lived buildings;
- the relation between standard interfaces and heterogeneous replaceable components;
- workmanship traditions distinguishing designed certainty from skilled adjustment;
- architectural positions on legibility, honesty, concealment and expression of construction;
- whether differential replacement cycles have previously been conceptualised as an interface problem rather than only a layer problem.

## Important tensions

- **separability vs additional parts and complexity**;
- **standardisation vs craft**;
- **repeatability vs tolerance of real construction variation**;
- **legibility vs visual technicality**;
- **concealment vs falsification**;
- **replaceability vs structural/material continuity**;
- **ordinary components vs bespoke architectural interface**.

## Explicit boundaries

Do not let F03 become:

- a general history of tectonic theory;
- a catalogue of expressive joints;
- a materials science literature review;
- a design-for-disassembly standards survey;
- a construction-detail precedent library;
- DRW/PLT visual-representation research.

A source matters only if it changes understanding of **how architectural interfaces manage difference across systems, lifespans, movement or assembly**.

# 7. ILA-F04 — Failure, robustness and recoverability

## Primary claims

- **ILA-C07 — Failure architecture**

## Secondary links

- C06 — interfaces can arrest or propagate failure
- C08 — repair requires maintenance access
- C09 — service topology shapes propagation and isolation
- C13 — passive fallback may affect resilience

## Intellectual question

> **How can architecture assume that components and systems will sometimes fail, then shape where the failure goes, how it becomes visible, how it is isolated and how ordinary repair can restore the building?**

F04 is intentionally a single-primary-claim family because C07 contains a substantial independent conceptual structure that would be distorted if submerged inside maintenance or interfaces.

## Why this remains independent

Failure is not identical to maintenance. A system can be maintainable yet fail catastrophically before intervention. Nor is failure simply an interface question: propagation, detection, redundancy, safe state and recovery can be system-level properties.

## Discovery must test

- architectural traditions that explicitly design for foreseeable failure rather than only prevent it;
- relevant concepts of loose/tight coupling, fault containment, graceful degradation, safe failure and recoverability;
- distinctions between robustness, resilience, redundancy, repairability and maintainability;
- whether prior building theory treats visible failure paths and sacrificial/replaceable elements as architectural principles;
- where engineering concepts transfer legitimately into domestic architecture and where they do not.

## Important tensions

- **prevention vs accommodation**;
- **redundancy vs simplicity**;
- **fail-safe vs continued operation**;
- **containment vs interconnected performance**;
- **visible consequence vs domestic repose**;
- **monitoring/automation vs passive observability**;
- **rare-event protection vs proportional burden**.

## Explicit boundaries

Do not let F04 become:

- general structural/fire/safety engineering history;
- disaster-resilience literature at urban scale;
- cybersecurity or software reliability analogy collecting;
- code compliance research;
- technical hazard analysis of the Reference House.

Only concepts that materially sharpen **architectural handling of ordinary foreseeable failure and recovery** belong here.

# 8. ILA-F05 — Maintenance, services and stewardship geography

## Primary claims

- **ILA-C08 — Maintenance geography**
- **ILA-C09 — Service topology as architectural ordering**
- **ILA-C11 — Intergenerational legibility and stewardship**

## Secondary links

- C05 — architectural platform
- C06 — designed interface
- C07 — repair / recovery after failure
- C10 — ordinary parts and future reproducibility

## Intellectual question

> **How should architecture make the recurring work of servicing, inspection, repair, replacement and handover physically possible without forcing that work to destroy or dominate ordinary domestic space?**

This is the family in which **maintenance becomes plan and section**, services become an architectural ordering problem, and future understanding becomes part of durability.

## Why these claims belong together

C08 says access has geography. C09 says service distribution is an upstream architectural ordering problem rather than residual routing. C11 says the resulting building must remain intelligible to future owners and trades even when records, suppliers and personnel change.

All three concern the **spatial and informational infrastructure of care over time**.

## Discovery must test

- architectural traditions in which services and maintenance materially order plan, section or circulation;
- distinctions between served/servicing relationships, service cores/zones and ordinary occupied space where relevant to the actual mechanism;
- traditions of maintainability, repair, care and stewardship that treat maintenance as ordinary rather than exceptional;
- precedents/theories of access, replacement paths and working space as architectural requirements;
- the history of making building services inspectable, separable or legible;
- physical versus documentary legibility across generations;
- whether prior work addresses **occupied change** and the disruption caused when maintenance/renovation is forced through finished domestic fabric.

## Important tensions

- **centralised services vs distributed convenience**;
- **accessibility vs spatial/visual intrusion**;
- **architectural order shaping services vs services dominating architecture**;
- **physical legibility vs documentary/digital records**;
- **specialist efficiency vs future ordinary-trade intelligibility**;
- **maintainability vs domestic calm**;
- **planned access vs the real geometry of tools, bodies, withdrawal and reinstatement**;
- **repairability vs replacement culture**.

## Explicit boundaries

Do not let F05 become:

- a general MEP design history;
- a catalogue of service cores and plant rooms;
- facilities-management scholarship without architectural consequence;
- technical access-dimension research already owned by HFI / standards work;
- building-information-management research unless it changes the physical/documentary stewardship question;
- construction logistics in general.

The family is specifically about **the architecture of recurring intervention and service order**.

# 9. ILA-F06 — Domestic repose, comprehensibility and local agency

## Primary claims

- **ILA-C12 — Domestic repose / low vigilance**

## Secondary links

- C11 — legibility across generations
- C13 — passive/environmental control
- C14 — technical necessity resolved as architecture

## Intellectual question

> **What architectural conditions let a dwelling feel settled enough for ordinary life while remaining comprehensible, locally controllable, private and physically comfortable?**

The family exists to test whether Ovid’s **Repose** proposition has a meaningful architectural lineage beyond its current empirical evidence base.

## Why this remains independent

Repose is not simply comfort, ergonomics, beauty or passive design. The project combines several conditions — privacy, control, comprehensibility, physical comfort and absence of gratuitous vigilance — into one domestic architectural judgement.

That synthesis deserves a bounded lineage search, but not a universal theory of human response to architecture.

## Discovery must test

- architectural traditions linking domesticity with retreat, privacy, ease, control or comprehensible order;
- serious prior formulations of environmental/architectural control as a condition of comfort or agency;
- whether “low vigilance” has a meaningful architectural analogue or is chiefly an Ovid framing;
- traditions linking legible structure/construction with psychological ease, with strict attention to evidential limits;
- counter-traditions that value stimulation, ambiguity, exposure or technical display in domestic architecture.

## Important tensions

- **clarity vs monotony**;
- **legibility vs visual technical display**;
- **local control vs automation**;
- **privacy/retreat vs openness and social connection**;
- **settled order vs adaptability**;
- **architectural judgement vs physiological overclaim**.

## Explicit boundaries

Do not let F06 become:

- a neuroscience-of-architecture survey;
- a phenomenology canon survey;
- general wellbeing architecture;
- HFI evidence re-review;
- a style history of cosy/traditional houses;
- an aesthetic defence of Georgian architecture.

A source belongs only if it materially sharpens the bounded proposition **a home should minimise unnecessary vigilance through architectural conditions we can responsibly describe**.

# 10. ILA-F07 — Passive-first environmental architecture and technical dependence

## Primary claims

- **ILA-C13 — Passive architecture does the first work**

## Secondary links

- C12 — local control / physical comfort
- C05 — permanent platform versus faster active systems
- C07 — passive fallback / failure consequences

## Intellectual question

> **What work should durable architectural form, fabric and geometry perform before shorter-lived active systems are asked to correct the environment?**

The important Ovid boundary is equally part of the question: passive-first is not passive-only, and simplicity is not allowed to outrank health, measured performance or resilience.

## Why this remains independent

Passive-first design has its own architectural lineage and can easily dominate a wider long-life discussion if left unbounded. It is related to Repose through comfort and control, and to temporal architecture through equipment lifespans, but neither of those is its central question.

## Discovery must test

- architectural traditions prioritising form, orientation, envelope, mass, openings, shading, drainage or gravity before mechanical correction;
- arguments connecting passive provision to durability, autonomy, robustness or reduced technical dependence;
- counterpositions showing where passive-only approaches fail performance, health or resilience requirements;
- whether Ovid’s specific **passive-first but evidence-governed** posture materially differs from established environmental-design positions.

## Important tensions

- **passive-first vs passive-only**;
- **simplicity vs measured performance**;
- **occupant control vs automatic control**;
- **permanent fabric optimisation vs future climate uncertainty**;
- **low technical dependence vs systems needed for health and resilience**;
- **architectural form as environmental machine vs domestic repose**.

## Explicit boundaries

Do not let F07 become:

- a history of sustainable architecture;
- an energy-efficiency evidence review;
- a survey of vernacular climate architecture;
- Passivhaus or any other standard-history packet unless a source materially changes the intellectual claim;
- a ventilation-mechanism or building-physics research packet;
- a carbon accounting review.

This is an intellectual-lineage audit of the **passive-first ordering principle**, not environmental design in general.

# 11. ILA-F08 — Patterns, generative sequences and architectural knowledge

## Primary claims

- **ILA-C15 — Pattern and generative-sequence knowledge**

## Secondary links

- C09 — service topology as the strongest current generative sequence

## Intellectual question

> **How can architecture encode recurring knowledge between abstract principle and one-off design, and how can sequences order decisions without becoming deterministic recipes?**

This family concerns the project’s method for representing reusable architectural knowledge.

## Why this remains independent

The pattern system is epistemic/methodological rather than another physical building doctrine. Its ancestry and Ovid delta therefore need to be judged against traditions of patterns, generative processes and rule-based design, while preserving the project’s explicit boundary between architectural patterns and compiler rules.

## Discovery must test

- what earlier pattern-language traditions actually claimed patterns were;
- the role of forces/context/relationships/consequences in a pattern;
- the difference between a catalogue of patterns and a genuinely generative language;
- prior concepts of sequence, unfolding or ordered design decisions;
- critiques of pattern languages, universalisation and apparent evidence;
- later adaptations that clarify the boundary between human-readable design knowledge and computational rule systems;
- whether Ovid’s evidence/maturity/authority distinctions materially extend or simply adapt established pattern practice.

## Important tensions

- **pattern as observation vs prescription**;
- **language vs catalogue**;
- **sequence vs recipe**;
- **general reusable knowledge vs local project judgement**;
- **designer agency vs generative constraint**;
- **evidence vs intuition / accumulated craft knowledge**;
- **architectural pattern vs software/compiler rule**.

## Explicit boundaries

Do not let F08 become:

- a general history of design methods;
- a software design-pattern survey;
- parametric/generative-design history unless it materially addresses the same knowledge problem;
- AI design research;
- DRW/PLT representation precedent;
- compiler implementation research.

The family closes around **architectural knowledge representation and sequencing**, not computation as such.

# 13. What changed from the provisional ten-family seed list

The programme issue deliberately treated its initial families as provisional. ILA-00B makes a tighter map possible.

## Merged into F01 / F02

The provisional families:

- time / differential rates / shearing;
- support-infill / long-life-short-life separation;
- loose fit / flexibility / indeterminacy;
- persistence through transformation;

are now divided by the actual intellectual question rather than historical vocabulary:

- **F01** owns the descriptive/causal temporal model;
- **F02** owns the normative allocation of permanence, adaptability and future capacity.

This prevents the same sources being searched four times while preserving the critical distinction between **how change occurs** and **how architecture should respond**.

## Merged into F05

The provisional:

- services / infrastructural hierarchy / serviceable geography;
- maintenance / repair / stewardship;

become one family because the project’s distinctive claim is precisely that maintenance and services acquire **physical architectural geography**. Separating them would artificially divide service ordering from the work required to inspect, repair and hand it on.

## Kept independent

- failure remains F04 because propagation/recovery is not reducible to maintenance;
- interfaces remain F03 because the joint is a distinct architectural problem across several families;
- Repose remains F06;
- passive-first environmental architecture becomes F07 rather than being hidden inside Repose;
- pattern/generative knowledge remains F08.

# 14. Cross-family structure

The eight families are not a flat list.

## Foundation layer

### F01 — Time, rates of change and coupling

This establishes the temporal problem all change-oriented families depend on.

### F02 — Adaptability, selective permanence and platform

This establishes the project’s normative response to temporal change.

F02 should be researched after enough of F01 is known to avoid confusing a model of change with a theory of flexibility.

## Physical consequence layer

### F03 — Interfaces, assembly and tectonic resolution
### F05 — Maintenance, services and stewardship geography

These can proceed substantially in parallel after the F01/F02 baseline because they ask how the time/change proposition becomes physical architecture.

### F04 — Failure, robustness and recoverability

F04 can begin independently, but its final discovery freeze should follow the first-pass F03/F05 work because interface and service-topology traditions may contain important failure concepts that should not be rediscovered separately.

## Independent standing-discipline layer

### F06 — Domestic repose
### F07 — Passive-first environmental architecture
### F08 — Pattern / generative knowledge

These do not depend on the temporal lineage strongly enough to block discovery. They may proceed in parallel once the common ILA contract is being applied consistently.

Their search should remain especially bounded because each opens onto enormous external literatures.

# 17. Family-level anti-sprawl tests

Before adding a candidate to any discovery packet, ask:

1. Which **primary claim** in this family could this source materially change our understanding of?
2. What is the exact proposition or counter-position we expect to find?
3. Is this an intellectual-lineage question, or actually technical evidence / precedent / representation research?
4. Is another family the true owner?
5. If the source proves relevant, could it change relationship type, chronology, Ovid delta or publication treatment?
6. If not, is it merely interesting?

“Interesting” is insufficient.

# 18. Important absences are intentional

ILA-01A does not create separate families for:

- **building regulations / standards** — evidence and constraint, not a single intellectual lineage;
- **Georgian grammar** — selected architectural language, not a governing Ovid proposition;
- **human factors / accessibility** — HFI already owns the empirical and normative evidence; only Repose’s architectural synthesis is in ILA;
- **occupied renovation disruption** — currently a consequence/search question within F05, not a separate intellectual doctrine;
- **sustainability / circular economy** — too broad; only concepts that change an ILA family enter;
- **digital twins / building passports** — possible stewardship mechanisms inside F05, not a family;
- **modularity** — a possible mechanism in F02/F03, not an assumed virtue;
- **prefabrication** — a possible assembly mechanism in F03, not a doctrine;
- **phenomenology** — a possible source tradition for F06, not a family in itself;
- **systems theory** — a possible source domain across F01/F04, not a family;
- **philosophy of identity** — interpretive material relevant to F02 only where it changes the architectural permanence/change question;
- **architectural representation** — DRW/PLT/TYP authority;
- **compiler / parametric architecture** — computation is downstream unless a source materially addresses F08’s knowledge problem.

This prevents familiar disciplines from becoming research workstreams merely because they touch the project.
