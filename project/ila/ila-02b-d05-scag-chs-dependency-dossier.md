# 1. Executive decision

D05 removes the last plausible broad originality reading of Ovid's **replacement-destruction** mechanism.

The result is unusually strong because the two traditions in this comparison cluster arrive at the same architectural problem through different routes and at different times.

## SCAG / Helmut C. Schulitz — 1968 project, published 1970–71

By 1971, Schulitz explicitly argues that:

- a fixed building package becomes obsolete according to its shortest-lived components;
- components with different useful lives should be allowed different replacement cycles;
- components in an indeterminate assemblage can be replaced independently;
- disassembly and reuse should not require alteration or destruction of other elements;
- short-cycle units should be easy to replace;
- achieving that requires **autonomous components with as little physical dependence on other components as possible**;
- independently exchangeable components should not be stacked in ways that make one support or trap another;
- the longest-lived assemblage structure should remain general enough to host changing shorter-cycle components;
- uniform flexibility everywhere is economically wasteful, so indeterminate and determinate assemblies should be combined selectively.

This is not merely a general adaptability precedent.

It is a direct earlier architectural articulation of:

> **different lives → independent replacement → minimise physical dependency → do not destroy other elements during change.**

That mechanism is materially equivalent to the core causal content behind Ovid's replacement-destruction heuristic.

## Century Housing System / Yositika Utida and collaborators — 1980s Japan

CHS goes further in formalising the same issue.

Its programme:

- classifies components by expected `durable years`;
- makes construction / installation order respond to those durability differences;
- treats the interface between component groups as an explicit design object;
- requires shorter-lived components to be replaceable without damaging longer-lived or neighbouring components;
- classifies dependencies according to damage caused when one component is removed or changed;
- uses interface maps / matrices to expose those dependencies;
- joins component compatibility to maintenance and renewal;
- ultimately becomes a housing certification / implementation system rather than merely a theoretical diagram.

Later CHS accreditation criteria state the mechanism directly: when a less durable part is renewed while more durable parts remain, the structure and fixing method should prevent damage to those remaining parts.

The 1997 CHS guidebook, as reproduced in later peer-reviewed research, also requires repairs, renewals, replacements and relocations to be possible without affecting other parts of the building.

Again, this is not merely different service lives.

It is explicit **damage-sensitive interface design for future replacement**.

# 2. D05 consequence for Ovid's C02

After D03 and D04, Ovid could no longer claim:

- discovery of temporal layers;
- discovery of generic independent change;
- discovery of fast/slow destructive coupling;
- discovery that differently paced systems need physical slippage.

D05 now removes the remaining broad claim that Ovid newly converts that principle into a **replacement-destruction test**.

Schulitz already states that replacement / disassembly should not alter or destroy another element and that components should minimise physical dependence.

CHS later formalises interfaces precisely in terms of damage caused to other components during replacement.

Therefore the underlying proposition:

> **Judge a replacement arrangement partly by whether changing the shorter-lived component damages longer-lived or neighbouring construction.**

is established architectural prior art.

The Ovid sentence:

> **A component's lifespan matters less than the lifespan of the things that must be destroyed to replace it.**

may remain a strong **project-authored compression** of that mechanism.

It may not be used to imply that the mechanism itself originated in Ovid's House.

The surviving project contribution is narrower:

1. a memorable authored formulation;
2. a house-wide synthesis of dependency with maintenance geography, failure, access, workmanship, stewardship and proportionality;
3. explicit intervention / withdrawal / reinstatement review;
4. typed boundary-debt accounting;
5. project governance preventing pattern, evidence, Reference House occurrence and compiler checks from upgrading one another's authority.

Even those are synthesis / method hypotheses rather than historical-priority findings.

# 10. SCAG already rejects homogeneous maximum flexibility

The article explicitly says homogeneous flexibility throughout a structure is expensive because different locations / components need different degrees of adaptability.

Uniform adaptability can be:

- inadequate for rapidly changing components;
- wasted where change may never occur during the component's life.

SCAG therefore combines:

- **indeterminate assemblage** where change justifies it;
- **determinate assemblage** where a stable, bounded function and shorter product life make a more fixed unit economically sensible.

It describes the overall aim as the greatest possible adaptability at the lowest possible investment.

### C03 consequence

This materially narrows Ovid's apparent novelty around proportional serviceability.

Ovid cannot claim to have discovered that:

- flexibility has present cost;
- adaptability should vary by credible need;
- universal flexibility is wasteful;
- fixed / conventional solutions remain valid where change does not justify the burden.

Those propositions are explicit in SCAG.

### Residual Ovid C03 field

Ovid's specific project governance may still add:

- B0–B3 burden classes;
- co-renewal rule;
- explicit incumbent status for excellent conventional construction;
- `hold` decisions when future benefit is uncertain;
- integration with carbon / area / workmanship / occupation burden.

That is a more formal proportionality apparatus.

D05 does not establish priority for it.

# 11. Determinate short-cycle units complicate a simplistic permanence story

SCAG is not simply `everything should be loose and demountable`.

It permits determinate assemblies for complex functions expected to remain stable for a bounded period.

Those assemblies can be:

- efficient;
- more fixed internally;
- planned for a life no longer than the function they serve;
- replaced as whole short-cycle units when their needs change.

Examples include kitchens, bathrooms, sleeping-room and stair units in the proposal.

### C04 significance

This anticipates an important Ovid posture:

> selective permanence and selective replaceability are more credible than maximum reversible construction everywhere.

The exact architecture differs, but the anti-maximalist principle is prior.

# 14. CHS origin and domain

CHS begins as a Japanese government-led housing research initiative around **1980–82** and develops into a formal housing certification system in the mid-1980s.

Its purpose is to extend housing longevity while allowing components to change during the building's life.

Its domain is therefore especially relevant to Ovid:

- housing;
- component renewal;
- maintenance;
- long-lived structure;
- shorter-lived interiors / equipment;
- future renovation.

Unlike Duffy's office context, CHS requires much less cross-building-type transfer.

It is one of the closest direct domestic antecedents in ILA-F01.

# 16. CHS formalises installation order by durability

Open Building sources describing CHS state that components with fewer `durable years` are installed **after** components with longer durable years.

The reason is architectural rather than merely logistical:

- the short-lived part should be reachable / replaceable;
- its renewal should not require dismantling the longer-lived part that encloses or traps it.

Official CHS criteria reproduced in CASBEE likewise link interface / construction method to durability difference.

### Ovid consequence

The generic rule:

> do not bury the shorter-lived component behind / inside a longer-lived component in a way that forces destruction during renewal

is established prior art.

Ovid's golden-rule phrasing is powerful, but the underlying lifecycle-ordering mechanism is not new.

# 17. CHS interface rules explicitly prevent damage to remaining components

The CHS accreditation criteria are particularly close to Ovid.

They require interfaces and construction methods to correspond to differences in durability.

For replacement of a less durable part while more durable parts remain, the fixing / construction arrangement should prevent damage to those existing parts.

Later CHS technical material expands the logic across cases:

- different durability groups;
- similar durability groups;
- components inside one group;
- consumable / shortest-life elements attached to longer-lived elements.

The repeated invariant is:

> replacement of one part should not damage what is supposed to remain.

### C02 relationship

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT` — `DOCUMENTED` at official-program level.

### C06 relationship

`EARLIER_ANTECEDENT` — strong.

CHS does not merely say `make things replaceable`.

It makes the **interface** and fixing method respond to the different temporal roles of the two components.

That is directly relevant to Ovid's Designed Interface claim.

# 18. CHS dependency typing is uncannily close to Ovid's replacement-destruction audit

Strong technical reconstruction of the CHS method shows that interface relations were classified by what happened when a component was removed or changed.

The relationship could distinguish cases such as:

- both components damaged;
- one component damaged;
- no damaging dependency.

The matrix focused on the residual effect on components that were **not** being changed.

This is effectively a formal dependency representation of the question:

> **what else gets damaged when this component changes?**

### D05 consequence

The Ovid replacement-destruction heuristic is not only conceptually anticipated.

CHS also demonstrates an earlier attempt to **represent the dependency systematically**.

Therefore Ovid cannot claim novelty merely for turning the concept into an explicit audit question or dependency map.

### Residual difference

Ovid's current representation is broader in scope:

- damage / destruction;
- access;
- isolation;
- withdrawal geometry;
- reinstatement;
- environmental / fire / acoustic boundary debt;
- failure / drying;
- occupied-work burden.

That **combined audit surface** may still be project-specific.

Its ingredients need to be judged across F03/F05 and the eventual ILA-03 matrix.

# 20. CHS was implemented, not merely theorised

Later longitudinal research on CHS housing is evidential rather than genealogical, but it matters to source credibility.

A 2021/22 long-term study of a 1987 CHS housing complex reports that:

- many components designed for 12–25 year replacement were in fact replaced;
- a large majority of surveyed households had renovated;
- some residents / renovators found CHS partition construction easier to dismantle;
- reduced dismantling scope lowered work and waste in some renovations;
- the building used measures such as double floors / ceilings and avoided driving equipment pipes into the structural frame in relevant areas.

The same study reproduces CHS certification criteria including:

> repairs, renewals, replacements and relocations should be possible without affecting other building parts.

### ILA type

This later research is:

`EVIDENTIAL_SUPPORT`.

It does not create the original CHS proposition, but it shows that the programme crossed into built housing and real renovation.

# 21. CHS counterpoint — system sophistication can destroy proportionality

D05 must preserve CHS's failure mode as strongly as its conceptual closeness.

Later histories report that CHS became highly complex and administratively burdensome.

The detailed rules and certification apparatus weakened economic incentives and the field later gravitated toward simpler support / infill models.

This is a major counterpoint to Ovid.

### C03 consequence

A system can be intellectually rigorous about lifecycle interfaces and still fail if its governance burden is too high.

That directly supports Ovid's proportionality concern:

- do not make every interface special;
- do not create permanent complexity merely because future change is imaginable;
- prefer excellent conventional construction where it wins;
- systematisation itself spends cost, coordination and stewardship capacity.

### But again: no originality rescue

The existence of a known over-systematisation failure does not make Ovid's proportionality principle historically unique.

SCAG already states the economic argument against homogeneous flexibility.

CHS supplies a built / institutional warning about excessive rule complexity.

Ovid's contribution may be the particular governance apparatus it uses to enforce restraint.

# 35. Frozen D05 disposition

**D05 is complete.**

The strongest safe judgement is:

> **By 1971 Schulitz's SCAG had already connected unequal component lives to whole-package obsolescence, required independent replacement and non-destructive disassembly, and explicitly sought autonomous components with minimal physical dependence. The Japanese Century Housing System later formalised the same architectural problem through durable-year bands, construction order, damage-sensitive interface rules and dependency matrices designed around whether replacing one component harms another. These are convergent earlier architectural traditions, not documented ancestors of one another and not documented formative sources for Ovid's House. Together they establish that Ovid's replacement-destruction mechanism is inherited prior art rather than a historical invention.**

The Ovid sentence:

> **A component's lifespan matters less than the lifespan of the things that must be destroyed to replace it.**

remains useful and plausibly project-authored as **wording / compression / decision heuristic**.

Its underlying mechanism does not.

The defensible project position after D05 is therefore:

> **Ovid inherits temporal decomposition, physical decoupling, non-destructive replacement and damage-sensitive interface design, then combines them with its particular domestic doctrines, maintenance/failure/workmanship constraints and explicit evidence/computation governance.**

Whether that combined system constitutes a materially distinctive synthesis is an ILA-03/04 judgement, not a D05 priority claim.

No manuscript, doctrine, pattern, Reference House, compiler or reader-facing publication content changes in D05.
