# BCR-PROPORTIONALITY — conventional controls and owner burden

**Result:** early comparison complete. The evidence supports ordinary finishes/openings with local access at real tasks, while unusual water, floor and service provisions must earn their additional burden. This is not a new design selection or a quantified whole-life saving.

**Owner:** [BCR #944](https://github.com/adamnoden/architecture/issues/944), under the [early/final scope](buildability-construction-resolution-programme.md). Final convergence must update this appraisal for adopted selections.

## What is actually being compared

The excellent conventional control is a properly supported, weathered and serviceable house: ordinary direct dry finishes, a conventional supported floor, mechanically fixed renewable frames/doorsets, independently supported services at reachable positions, and an explicitly designed gravity water route. It is not cheap workmanship, inaccessible concealed joints, cosmetic flush thresholds or a claim that ordinary products automatically perform.

Current IAS already selects ordinary dry finishes and local mechanical release, rejects a house-wide backplane/floor grid and retains wet/service adoption holds. OAS selects the conventional renewable frame/doorset family, not a continuous cassette. Those owning decisions are the starting point; this appraisal does not reopen them by treating every earlier challenger as the current house.

The courtyard and 12 × 12 m shell remain the owner's accepted reference. A solid rectangular gable roof is a useful reference for ordinary construction, but eliminating the courtyard would not be a like-for-like substitute. The outward-only ring is a closer geometric challenger already studied by ROF, with real height and appearance consequences. RWT's low twin channels and later higher relief are unadopted responses to a real court problem, not mandatory components of the house.

### Roof trade-off without a false complexity score

The nominal current ring has 48 m exterior low edge, 14 m inner low edge, 31 m ridge ring, about 12.02 m plan-length outer hips and 12.02 m inner valleys. These categories have different weathering, water concentration and repair duties; adding their lengths into one price or reliability score would be misleading. Actual coverings, laps, joints and overhangs remain separate quantities.

At the same 25° pitch and +6800 exterior eaves, an outward-only ring removes the inner valleys and 14 m of inner collection edge but adds a high court termination of that same 14 m. Its outer hips extend to about 24.04 m in plan. The court edge rises to about +8781.8, **1981.8 above current court eaves and 990.9 above the current ridge**. It transfers roof collection outward but still leaves direct court rain and its escape obligation. ROF/HAD must assess enclosure/daylight/appearance, support and exterior receiving load; this is not a free simplification or a roof change by BCR.

Ordinary work may require skilled installation, test equipment and occasional specialist intervention. The comparison does not label it effortless. Conversely, “ordinary tools” in a drawing is not a verified release method: name the actual driver, lifting/support device, clearance and material compatibility when a component is selected. Keep consumables and replaceable parts documented in DEL; do not require the owner to preserve a private manufacturer's undocumented ecosystem to maintain the house.

## Uncertainty, money and whole-life claims

There are no matched installed quotations, intervention frequencies, demonstrated service lives, material takeoffs with applicable EPDs, or a chosen analysis period. Therefore no percentage saving, payback year, lifecycle carbon advantage or universal maintenance interval is claimed. Minimum stock-sheet mass is not a whole-wall weight; occupied package volume is not embodied carbon. A future calculation needs equivalent functional duty and a common boundary for transport, installation waste, repairs, replacement, temporary protection and end of life.

The useful decision rule is conditional: an added interface earns itself only if its demonstrated avoided intervention burden exceeds its extra installation, space, maintenance and eventual replacement burden under the owner's plausible use. The number and nature of interventions must be supplied rather than chosen to make a preferred system win. When that evidence is absent, retain the simpler satisfactory conventional control while keeping a bounded local challenger available. This does not waive water, fire, access, structural or comfort duties in pursuit of fewer parts.

Owner burden includes knowing where isolation/release points are, noticing meaningful faults, arranging safe access, moving furniture, retaining records and restoring usable rooms. Sensors and reminder schedules do not replace passive safe water routing. The RWT heavy-rain/missed-cleaning scenario and IAS wet-failure/drying tasks remain obligations even if a choice looks economical.
