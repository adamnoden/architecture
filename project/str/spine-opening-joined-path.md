## A load path cannot pass through the opening

The service-spine core is x9230–9370; its ground opening is y2750–3650. HAD identifies it as the PLANT protected-hall boundary door; plant hazards and the actual required door rating remain to be established. The current protected-route candidate limits the rectangular assembly to 2250 mm height and requires a separately qualified closure above it. The east-strip floor field sends a **123.633 kN ULS** support-edge action to x9300 over y332.5–8290. The upper wet-spine aligns with this ground wall and crosses the opening. The previous opening screen assigned **10.546 kN** of gross upper-wall challenge across the gap, with 5.273 kN at each end; it did not include the floor edge. The west-side gallery edge begins at y3917.5 and contributes no load over this opening under the same line model. Roof, local wall, construction and other forces are not thereby zero.

For an explicit sensitivity, distribute the east-strip edge uniformly along its stated 7.9575 m support: 15.537 kN/m, or **13.983 kN** over the 900 mm opening. Superpose the already recorded gross upper-wall challenge, producing an **adverse sensitivity of 24.529 kN**. A symmetrical simply supported clear-span idealisation sends **12.265 kN to each end**, before head self-weight and the other loads above. The floor edge distribution is an assumption, not a proven slab line traction, and the upper-wall figure intentionally uses gross full-height weight rather than an actual spandrel. The 7.5 kPa floor G budget contains 5.0 kPa slab and a 2.5 kPa maximum for other permanent layers, services and ceiling; its actual constituents are not resolved. The parent source warns some upper-wall loads may share a floor allowance. Until the actual layer and wall takeoff shows otherwise, this superposition can overlap and is **not a disjoint design load case**. It is also not an upper bound on the whole head, because other actions are omitted. These are transferred existing challenges, not new loads to add to the building inventory again.

## A finite trial and its rejection conditions

A candidate opening head occupies the 140 mm core and bears on **200 mm long patches**, y2550–2750 and y3650–3850. Their axes are y2650 and 3750, 1.1 m apart. These patches fit in the unbroken plan wall in the source; that plan fact establishes no sound masonry, contact length, edge distance or usable construction tolerance. Treat this potentially overlapping 24.529 kN sensitivity as uniform over the central 0.9 m between those centres. Elementary equilibrium gives **3.986 kNm** maximum bending moment and **12.265 kN** each reaction. Ideal uniform contact on a full 140 × 200 mm patch gives **0.438 MPa** average, with no local eccentricity or other actions. This is an action/contact sensitivity, not a masonry compression or head capacity check.

A genuine arch challenger needs a horizontal thrust path. For a *trial* 300 mm rise and symmetric three-hinge crown equilibrium, the same bending ordinate gives **13.287 kN horizontal thrust** at each support. The calculation does not assume that a masonry ring has a particular shape, thickness or strength, and does not select a tie. The head and its actual bearing must fit the protected door, separately qualified upper closure, clear height, slab soffit and services in a joined section. The 300 mm rise alone supplies no spring or crown level and proves none of those fits. An RC or steel head is the comparison if a genuine arch and its horizontal reactions cannot fit. A slab spanning the opening directly would change the sharing and must be analysed as a joined alternative; both mechanisms cannot claim the entire load independently.

The physical route is therefore **upper wall and floor → head or verified slab bridge → two masonry/RC bearing zones → continuous warm receiving grid → compression-qualified mineral/AAC/DPC interface → cold grid → site-dependent support group**. The ground spine continues beyond each patch, but the floor's other line reactions, lower-wall self-weight, wind reversal, arch thrust, differential movement and actual openings elsewhere must be combined at the same receiving lengths. The old 850 kN/100 kNm node and rejected 400 mm joint do not prove this path. A head tie, if used, must be anchored through real masonry and its ends, with clearance for the door and services; a steel starter through the mineral band is not silently selected. The floor/roof diaphragm and selected wall lines must separately transfer lateral actions into a qualified shear/tension crossing at the warm/cold boundary. Vertical contact alone cannot do so.

Reject or redesign this trial if the real floor and wall sharing overloads the head or piers, the thrust cannot be contained, bearing or door/section fit fails, or the warm/cold crossing cannot transfer the combined signed actions without defeating moisture, movement and thermal functions. The actual section, wall openings and support sharing are internal design work. Product qualification, real ground/wind, competent engineering and construction acceptance remain distinct later obligations. The eight-opening screen exposes a connected family of transfers and one failed patch, not a claim that their actual heads, roof corners, diaphragm or foundation are solved.
