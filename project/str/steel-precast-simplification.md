# Ordinary steel and precast: development choice

## Decision — 28 November 2026

**Develop ordinary rolled steel with precast floor units as the preferred replacement for the unresolved monolithic floor/portal joint. Allow a 700 mm intermediate floor zone.** Put the loggia's wall-carrying beams beside the parallel precast units, within their depth. Retain genuine, separately supported masonry arches below. Stop treating the inherited 350 mm floor zone as fixed.

This is a reasoned development choice under the owner's steel permission, prefab preference and latest simplicity priority. It is not adoption of the higher house, a complete framing layout or closure of STR-FLOOR-PORTAL. The actual coupled structure, occupied house and owner-facing architectural review still have to agree. The earlier RC and separated-bearing investigations remain evidence; their forces and passes do not transfer to this scheme.

The first checkpoint allowed 750 mm and a single long stair trimmer. Resolving the ordinary stair post and splitting that trimmer makes a shallower rolled section viable, reducing the selected zone to 700 mm. The published 750 mm checkpoint remains history; 700 mm is the current development case.

## The stair now has an explicit ordinary support route

Retain the two 1094 mm flight bodies, 2925 mm bearing spans, 200 mm central well and source post position. Use two precast flights and a separately supported 200 mm landing. A straight 150×150×6.3 hot-finished SHS continues from ground support to the first-floor trimmer, splitting that trimmer into 4.6075 m and 1.595 m spans. Its 200×200 mm provisional fire envelope fits inside the old 250×250 mm RC-post territory. The header and midlanding beam both use the same wide-flange 254×254×73 UC as the ordinary floor beams: each receives precast on opposed sides, so the narrower trimmer flange is insufficient for the selected seat-and-tolerance budget. A top header connects into the south trimmer; the half-landing beam meets the post at its own level and a narrow west sill beam at the other end. No flight hangs from an assumed midpoint force.

Applying the 170 mm normal waist and average solid-step triangles across the full bearing span gives the conservative gravity comparison of **8.306 kN/m² permanent flight action**, plus 3 kN/m² imposed load. The ordinary reaction at each flight end is 13.289 kN permanent plus 4.800 kN variable. A 300 mm long thickened top end adds 0.810 kN permanent weight; that weight is distributed to its actual two supports. This is a specified candidate geometry for the precaster to develop, not an accepted reinforcement or lifting design.

Every beam-to-beam transfer keeps permanent and variable reactions separate. The subtree includes the adjacent floor tributaries and steel self-weight and balances to 114.968 kN permanent plus 43.421 kN variable at its eight external receivers, including the separate west upper-window lintel. The post base takes **74.05 kN factored gravity**. Gross stress and a pinned Euler comparison are reported, but neither substitutes for column restraint, eccentric joints, fire protection, cap/base details or an actual ground receiver.

The upper stair end must be 300 mm deep at its bearing, compared with a 201.23 mm vertical waist. The ground toe requires a different 145 mm end profile against the retained ground support. Actual end reinforcement, bearing lengths, tolerances and assembly remain explicit design work. The independent precast landing also changes the midlanding joint; the source monolithic lap/cage cannot be copied across.

The half-landing rises from 1725 to **1900 mm**. Its supporting steel top is **1665 mm**, below its 25 mm finish, 200 mm slab and 10 mm bed. Keep the window head at 2850 mm and raise its sill from 1750 to **1925 mm**, losing 175 mm of window height. The guard base rises to 1900 mm and needs **235 mm upstands above the new steel**. The old guard attachment directly onto RC no longer applies. The 138.7 mm sill-steel/fire envelope fits the 140 mm core nominally, with only 1.3 mm spare: this is not an installed tolerance or accepted fire assembly.

The limited first-nose/header and upper-arrival/raised-roof headrooms are 2995.9 and 2994 mm. Full three-dimensional headroom and the window/guard/rail maintenance arrangement remain unjoined. These measured consequences belong in the architectural review before adopting the new floor level.

## Two actual openings need ordinary lintels above their arches

The bathroom transfer at Y6500 lands above the actual east water opening Y6273–7527. The first checkpoint's “solid pier” description was wrong. A 1.800 m **203×102×23 UB** above the genuine masonry arch transfers the 45.36 kN factored bath reaction to the source side-pier/ground-node axes Y6000 and Y7800. Its gravity challenge also includes upper-core masonry, adjacent floor and roof. The resulting factored pier reactions are **69.55 and 49.38 kN**. The 300 mm pads clear the actual opening and neighbouring windows.

The upper stair header likewise meets the west wall above the Y4100–5100 window. A second beam of the **same standard section**, spanning 1.400 m between Y3900 and Y5300, receives the header's 15.09 kN factored reaction plus upper wall/floor/roof challenges. Its pier reactions are **39.89 and 34.12 kN**. The stair header terminates on this beam, not an imaginary uninterrupted wall.

Both lintels move 5.9 mm roomward so their 25 mm provisional side-fire envelopes stay within the source wall/finish territory without entering the cold-side layers. The eastern bath beam and western header shorten accordingly, and their loads and reactions are recomputed. Their local fire envelopes clear the arch outer crowns by **120.9 and 220.9 mm** respectively. These are geometric fire-envelope checks; full thermal continuity, arch loading, torsion, bolted connections, bearing spread, pier capacity and ground response remain to be designed. The genuine arches remain separate masonry structures; no lintel is concealed inside a decorative arch shape. The previous full-wall-through-arch load-path assumption must be reconciled with these new inner-leaf transfers before adoption.

The south stair trimmer is only 125 mm from its receiving wall's end. Centreing a 300 mm pad on the beam would overhang the wall by 25 mm. Shift the pad inward by 25 mm instead: its X2817.5–3117.5 footprint fits, with 25 mm load eccentricity. The linear full-contact comparison gives **0.423–1.268 MPa** under the current gravity demand. This reports demand and fit, not padstone or masonry resistance.

## Why the portal arrangement matters

The loggia floor spans east–west between its side supports. The two portal wall beams also run east–west, so they can lie beside those units. They carry the wall above without requiring the units to sit on top of them. At the selected floor level this leaves space for the retained 280 mm insulation/lining envelope above the masonry arch.

The same calculation rejects placing the portal beam below the units: its insulated underside falls 89.6 mm below the arch's outer crown. The 350 mm inherited floor zone also fails the ordinary beam-and-unit stack by 314.6 mm. These failures are retained in the generated receipt. No shallow fabricated beam, hidden shelf, reduced hollowcore end or special sliding joint is introduced to rescue those arrangements.

The arches still need real abutments, ties, their own gravity/thrust checks and erection support. The source portal includes RC pier/skewback details; this comparison has not proved that replacing those piers with masonry works. Steel above the arch does not erase that negative evidence. Prefer ordinary factory-made bearing/post details where the source wall cannot receive the new loads; do not restart the rejected curved cage merely because it exists in the old model.

## Precast and site work

The retained sixteen RC field rectangles cover 90.57855 m². The source additionally has a 2.447425 m² entrance bridge and 7.634225 m² of residual strips, closures and wall-head territory, totalling 100.6602 m². **Sixteen rectangles are not a precast panel schedule.** Actual unit widths, end bearings, trimmed openings, wall-head closures and service holes must be located before quantities or installation completeness are claimed.

Use grouted longitudinal keys, necessary tied end/core closures and local bearing beds as explicit site operations. A full structural topping is not selected by this comparison. Its necessity must be decided from the actual diaphragm, ties, load distribution and floor finish. Factory precast is preferred, but an unqualified untopped diaphragm is not a simplification. Installed materials, fire protection, seals and insulation remain subject to the whole-assembly brief.

### Reproduction and change propagation

Authored inputs: [settings](studies/steel-precast-inputs.json) and [primary sources / assumptions](studies/steel-precast-sources.json). The pure `steel-precast-kernel.ts` computes simple-beam equilibrium and elastic deflection, floor/portal envelopes, stair dimensions, entrance fit and combined rear-bearing demand. The current-source adapter authenticates the occupied and structural sources. The public CLI and saved `steelPrecast` engineering stage call the same methods; old stages are not edited into apparent compatibility.

```sh
node --import tsx project/str/studies/steel-precast-comparison.ts --check
node --import tsx --test compiler/reference-house/current-design-steel-precast.test.ts
```

CLI entry points are `--read-steel-precast`, `--steel-precast`, and `--read-steel-precast-document`; the saved document uses the existing `--bundle` route. Changing the floor datum changes section, stair mass, transfer reactions, landing window and roof-height consequences. The independent non-stair member results remain an unchanged control. Changing floor actions changes affected beam forces and deflections while the parallel portal wall-beam actions stay unchanged. Too-shallow floors, a stacked portal, excessive floor actions, an undersized common pad, missing/unknown settings and stale sources are tested as failures or explicit withheld evidence.

Residual computational work is the complete floor-panel/support network, signed structural response and joined occupied/roof/service geometry. The stair subtree is now explicit, but its external receivers and connections remain to be joined. STR-FLOOR-PORTAL owns that integration before the coherent provisional scheme can close. No current native RC analysis is relabelled unavailable or deleted.
