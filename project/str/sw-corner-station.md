# Southwest corner: joined stability and hold-down candidate

**STR-HOUSE continuation, 2 November 2026. Conditional reference design; not released for construction.** This tests one actual outer corner at **(332.5, 332.5)**, from the roof into the connected ground concept. The [source-bound calculation](studies/sw-corner-station.py), [data](studies/sw-corner-station.json) and [schematic](studies/sw-corner-station.svg) pin the selected HAD, STR and MAT records. This is a concrete alternative for joint STR/MAT/HAD development, not a selected pier, tie, roof or foundation. The actual corner hip/jacks and seat are still missing; the source's roof forces are collapsed-corner **action challenges**, not reactions from a completed corner assembly.

## One constructive route

The leading trial uses a **300 × 300 mm reinforced-concrete corner pier**, x/y262.5–562.5, from warm grid top −80 to a cap elevation +6460. It replaces the intersecting masonry core inside this footprint; it is not laid on top of an unchanged wall. Four straight **Ø20 longitudinal ties**, at x/y322.5 or 502.5, continue from a proposed cold-receiver embed ending −990, through the entire thermal/DPC band and pier, to the underside of a **20 mm roof cap plate** at +6460. Closed Ø8 links around those four bars are a conventional cage direction; with 60 mm bar-centre edge setback their nominal outside cover is 42 mm. Their pitch, hooks, anchorage, bar grade, plate welds, joints and sequencing are **not designed**. The bars are not assumed to provide reinforcement merely because they are drawn; a compatible concrete and steel design is required. A conventional tied/anchored corner and a larger or differently located receiver remain alternatives if the cage, roof join or moisture boundary cannot be made to work.

The candidate separates the two directions of force:

- **Compression:** roof bearing plate → pier → monolithic warm RC floor/girder → local compression-qualified AAC/mineral bearing → cold RC grid → site-dependent support group. Other wall and floor actions join at their actual locations; the roof point is not a whole-node load.
- **Uplift:** roof-to-cap connection → four continuous tension ties → cold-receiver anchorage → the support group. No favourable dead load or tensile strength of AAC, mortar or DPC is assumed. All four ties must have real top and bottom load transfer; 225 mm nominal cold embed is a geometric reservation, not a breakout/development pass.
- **Horizontal reversal:** roof collector and floor diaphragm must connect to the pier; the pier must transfer combined H/N/M to the cold grid through a deliberately designed crossing. The ties can be challenged for bending and shear, but their 385 mm unbraced interval, actual restraints, cyclic slip and concrete anchor actions are unresolved. No friction or masonry shear strength is silently credited.

The earlier +6640 pier-top trial has been **rejected** by the finite roof join below: it intersects a lowered hip. The revised +6460 top and 20 mm cap are a geometric starting point, not a selected roof seat or qualified anchor.

### Actual outer-face actions and their limit

The calculation clips the actual **x/y0..2125 outer triangular face** into five 500/125 mm strips, plus the separately retained **0.020 m²** outside corner tip. Each strip is assigned to its actual x500/1000/1500/2000/2125 jack; its part behind the hip face goes directly to the diagonal. The model carries the resulting force **and the two relocation couples**, rather than moving a distributed load to the jack/hip without its first moments. Under the declared downward 6.3 kPa, the five strip loads are 1.418, 2.993, 4.568, 6.143 and 1.782 kN. The x1000 jack requires **−0.240 kN at its hip seat even in that downward case** because of its eave cantilever: the seat needs a real signed hold-down. The x500 jack is fixed at its hip root, with its eave contact omitted as above.

## Footprint, floor and material consequences

The ordinary outer section is 102.5 brick + 50 drained gap + 110 glass + 140 structural core + 27 finish = 429.5 mm. The pier's outside faces are at the core's exterior faces, **262.5**, so the nominal glass and 50 mm drainage gap remain outside it in plan. Its inside faces reach 562.5, projecting **133 mm into the room** beyond the 429.5 finish on each axis. It is a visible 133 × 133 mm room-corner toe and a structural substitution, not a harmless hidden thickening. A centred 300 mm pad would instead occupy 80 mm of the 110 mm glass and leave only 30 mm before the nominal drained gap; it does not by geometry alone block that 50 mm slot. Actual corner bond, retention, insulation continuity and three-dimensional moisture/thermal effects belong to MAT's joined study.

The pier has **0.5886 m³ gross RC** or **14.715 kN G** at the trial 25 kN/m³. This is *not* an incremental building weight. Its plan overlaps **0.0644 m²** of the two 140 mm core strips and **0.0529 m²** of the first-floor inner-axis slab; the replaced masonry, existing slab concrete and actual ground-floor intersections must be removed before any total is updated. The four full-height nominal bars also replace concrete and must be included once in a real takeoff. The pier and its reinforcement must pass through the actual first-floor slab and its 80 mm upper-layer transition with a continuous cast and force path; their cage, concrete cover, penetrations, fire and movement interfaces are not resolved by this gross volume.

The rectangle does not intersect any recorded ground- or upper-furniture rectangle. Its nearest recorded proxy is the study desk, with **99.8 mm diagonal rectangle gap**. The closest retained HAD round-body route has **1727.6 mm reserve** from this new solid under the existing witness model. These are source-bound proxy checks only: they do not test skirting, every operation, physical furniture, human use or the changed wall/finish junction. HAD must reconcile the occupied plan and affected views before adoption.

## Forces and the four-anchor choice

The parent gives the SW outer roof challenge **+68.111 kN downward** and **−24.325 kN uplift** with no favourable G. They are separate authored ULS sensitivities, not concurrent site loads or a full load combination. Using right-handed axes x east, y north, z up and a positive-down load, the roof point lies 80 mm west and south of the pier centroid. Its roof-only signed moments about that centroid are **(+5.449, −5.449) kNm** down and **(−1.946, +1.946) kNm** uplift. At the cold grid's (300, 300) axes, the point is only 32.5 mm away: the corresponding moments are **(−2.214, +2.214)** and **(+0.791, −0.791) kNm**. Transporting the force from the pier centroid alone gives ±7.662 kNm down, but the opposing ±5.449 kNm couple must travel with it; adding both magnitudes would invent moment.

A two-tie tension-only control at (337.5,337.5) and (487.5,487.5) does not enclose the roof point. For the uplift point alone, equilibrium demands **25.136 kN tension and 0.811 kN compression**, so that *tension-only two-point model* fails. This does not prohibit induced local compression: a real pad/plate, AAC/DPC bearing area and anchored ties may develop it, subject to unilateral contact, local crushing, seal, stiffness and combined-action checks. Two collinear ties also cannot independently establish arbitrary biaxial moment resistance.

The four trial ties at x/y **322.5 or 502.5** surround the roof point. The Ø60 band bores are 30 mm from the pad edge and 120 mm apart clear; four circular bores remove **12.57%** of the nominal 300 × 300 mm contact plan. A nonnegative *static witness* for the roof uplift alone gives tie tensions **21.698, 1.276, 1.276 and 0.075 kN**, in the order southwest, northwest, southeast, northeast. It conserves both moments, but is not a predicted stiffness distribution. A linear equal-stiffness calculation instead asks the northeast tie to push **4.730 kN**, exposing the need for actual unilateral contact or redistribution. Actual plate stiffness, tension-only response, bearing and minimum/maximum G must be analysed together; the bilinear witness is not a design allocation.

For a separate **1 kN total station shear unit**, four equal Ø20 shanks treated optimistically as fixed-ended elastic members over 385 mm and E200 GPa give **61.3 MPa** nominal end bending stress and **0.757 mm** elastic lateral movement before anchorage/concrete slip. The largest tension-only roof witness gives **69.1 MPa** using smooth gross area. These values are not resistances, thread-section checks, an interaction design or actual wind. At the inherited primary centre +6746.721, a 1 kN horizontal action would have a **7.512 kNm** lever to cold-grid top −765, before floor-diaphragm restraint or load sharing. Both signs, biaxial load, wind, slab/roof chords, rod bending and combined N/M/H must be resolved together; there is no basis for attributing all global wind to this one point or assuming an equal four-corner split.

## The thermal and damp break is a real structural joint

The four Ø60 holes, centred on the ties, cross the AAC/mortar course and full mineral DPC from warm RC bottom −380 to cold RC top −765. Their **20 mm nominal radial annuli** reserve a possible mineral collar/grout and seal route; no product, capillary resistance, gas/air seal, movement capacity or non-plastic material is selected. A bare bar through those layers creates a metal and interface bypass. A solid 300 × 300 mm RC bridge would give a more direct structural path but also bridge the thermal and capillary barriers; it is a deliberate adverse comparator, not an automatic cure. MAT is comparing the actual 429.5 mm ordinary corner, local floor/glass discontinuity, four penetrations and separate weather-leaf support. Its 519.5 mm loggia-sidewall 2D thermal result is not transferable.

The four long bars imply a real construction sequence: anchor and survey them in the cold receiver, form/lay qualified local AAC and DPC details around them, build the warm grid and pier with their links and reinforcement, and connect the cap and roof without damaging the boundaries. This is a sequence to design, not evidence that a 7.45 m bar assembly can be positioned, threaded through stock blocks, cast, protected and inspected in the actual site. The anchor ends, bar/cage congestion, plate welds, roof erection, joint tolerances, weather, fire, retained ventilation and renewal access remain internal constructive work. If the required seal, force transfer or occupied corner cannot be achieved together, redesign the station or return to a competing structure; do not accept an unsealed shortcut.

This develops a **single candidate station**, not the whole-house lateral scheme, actual roof corner, selected foundation or STR-HOUSE exit. The 400 mm failed joint and low-tie/cage defects remain rejected. The complete signed support group requires the real roof/diaphragm reactions, wall and floor actions, ground movement and actual wind/soil before design and external acceptance.
