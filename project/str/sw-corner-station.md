# Southwest corner: joined stability and hold-down candidate

**STR-HOUSE continuation, 2 November 2026. Conditional reference design; not released for construction.** This tests one actual outer corner at **(332.5, 332.5)**, from the roof into the connected ground concept. The [source-bound calculation](studies/sw-corner-station.py), [data](studies/sw-corner-station.json) and [station](studies/sw-corner-station.svg), [roof](studies/sw-corner-roof.svg) and [cold-cage](studies/sw-corner-cage.svg) schematics pin the selected HAD, STR and MAT records. This is a concrete alternative for joint STR/MAT/HAD development, not a selected pier, tie, roof or foundation. The actual corner hip/jacks and seat are not adopted; the source's roof forces are collapsed-corner **action challenges**, not reactions from a completed corner assembly.

## One constructive route

The leading trial uses a **300 × 300 mm reinforced-concrete corner pier**, x/y262.5–562.5, from warm grid top −80 to a cap elevation +6410. It replaces the intersecting masonry core inside this footprint; it is not laid on top of an unchanged wall. Four straight **Ø20 longitudinal ties**, at x/y322.5 or 502.5, continue from a proposed cold-receiver embed ending −990, through the entire thermal/DPC band and pier, to the underside of a **20 mm roof cap plate** at +6410. Closed Ø8 links around those four bars are a conventional cage direction; with 60 mm bar-centre edge setback their nominal outside cover is 42 mm. Their pitch, hooks, anchorage, bar grade, plate welds, joints and sequencing are **not designed**. The bars are not assumed to provide reinforcement merely because they are drawn; a compatible concrete and steel design is required. A conventional tied/anchored corner and a larger or differently located receiver remain alternatives if the cage, roof join or moisture boundary cannot be made to work.

The candidate separates the two directions of force:

- **Compression:** roof bearing plate → pier → monolithic warm RC floor/girder → local compression-qualified AAC/mineral bearing → cold RC grid → site-dependent support group. Other wall and floor actions join at their actual locations; the roof point is not a whole-node load.
- **Uplift:** roof-to-cap connection → four continuous tension ties → cold-receiver anchorage → the support group. No favourable dead load or tensile strength of AAC, mortar or DPC is assumed. All four ties must have real top and bottom load transfer; 225 mm nominal cold embed is a geometric reservation, not a breakout/development pass.
- **Horizontal reversal:** roof collector and floor diaphragm must connect to the pier; the pier must transfer combined H/N/M to the cold grid through a deliberately designed crossing. The ties can be challenged for bending and shear, but their 385 mm unbraced interval, actual restraints, cyclic slip and concrete anchor actions are unresolved. No friction or masonry shear strength is silently credited.

The earlier +6640 pier-top trial intersects the lowered hip. A first 165 mm hip drop also **fails the q450 purlin crossover** below. The revised +6410 pier top and 20 mm cap are geometric starting points, not a selected roof seat or qualified anchor.

## Footprint, floor and material consequences

The ordinary outer section is 102.5 brick + 50 drained gap + 110 glass + 140 structural core + 27 finish = 429.5 mm. The pier's outside faces are at the core's exterior faces, **262.5**, so the nominal glass and 50 mm drainage gap remain outside it in plan. Its inside faces reach 562.5, projecting **133 mm into the room** beyond the 429.5 finish on each axis. It is a visible 133 × 133 mm room-corner toe and a structural substitution, not a harmless hidden thickening. A centred 300 mm pad would instead occupy 80 mm of the 110 mm glass and leave only 30 mm before the nominal drained gap; it does not by geometry alone block that 50 mm slot. Actual corner bond, retention, insulation continuity and three-dimensional moisture/thermal effects belong to MAT's joined study.

The pier has **0.5841 m³ gross RC** or **14.603 kN G** at the trial 25 kN/m³. This is *not* an incremental building weight. Its plan overlaps **0.0644 m²** of the two 140 mm core strips and **0.0529 m²** of the first-floor inner-axis slab; the replaced masonry, existing slab concrete and actual ground-floor intersections must be removed before any total is updated. The four full-height nominal bars also replace concrete and must be included once in a real takeoff. The pier and its reinforcement must pass through the actual first-floor slab and its 80 mm upper-layer transition with a continuous cast and force path; their cage, concrete cover, penetrations, fire and movement interfaces are not resolved by this gross volume.

The rectangle does not intersect any recorded ground- or upper-furniture rectangle. Its nearest recorded proxy is the study desk, with **99.8 mm diagonal rectangle gap**. The closest retained HAD round-body route has **1727.6 mm reserve** from this new solid under the existing witness model. These are source-bound proxy checks only: they do not test skirting, every operation, physical furniture, human use or the changed wall/finish junction. HAD must reconcile the occupied plan and affected views before adoption.

## Forces and the four-anchor choice

The parent gives the SW outer roof challenge **+68.111 kN downward** and **−24.325 kN uplift** with no favourable G. They are separate authored ULS sensitivities, not concurrent site loads or a full load combination. Using right-handed axes x east, y north, z up and a positive-down load, the roof point lies 80 mm west and south of the pier centroid. Its roof-only signed moments about that centroid are **(+5.449, −5.449) kNm** down and **(−1.946, +1.946) kNm** uplift. At the cold grid's (300, 300) axes, the point is only 32.5 mm away: the corresponding moments are **(−2.214, +2.214)** and **(+0.791, −0.791) kNm**. Transporting the force from the pier centroid alone gives ±7.662 kNm down, but the opposing ±5.449 kNm couple must travel with it; adding both magnitudes would invent moment.

A two-tie tension-only control at (337.5,337.5) and (487.5,487.5) does not enclose the roof point. For the uplift point alone, equilibrium demands **25.136 kN tension and 0.811 kN compression**, so that *tension-only two-point model* fails. This does not prohibit induced local compression: a real pad/plate, AAC/DPC bearing area and anchored ties may develop it, subject to unilateral contact, local crushing, seal, stiffness and combined-action checks. Two collinear ties also cannot independently establish arbitrary biaxial moment resistance.

The four trial ties at x/y **322.5 or 502.5** surround the roof point. The Ø60 band bores are 30 mm from the pad edge and 120 mm apart clear; four circular bores remove **12.57%** of the nominal 300 × 300 mm contact plan. A nonnegative *static witness* for the roof uplift alone gives tie tensions **21.698, 1.276, 1.276 and 0.075 kN**, in the order southwest, northwest, southeast, northeast. It conserves both moments, but is not a predicted stiffness distribution. A linear equal-stiffness calculation instead asks the northeast tie to push **4.730 kN**, exposing the need for actual unilateral contact or redistribution. Actual plate stiffness, tension-only response, bearing and minimum/maximum G must be analysed together; the bilinear witness is not a design allocation.

For a separate **1 kN total station shear unit**, four equal Ø20 shanks treated optimistically as fixed-ended elastic members over 385 mm and E200 GPa give **61.3 MPa** nominal end bending stress and **0.757 mm** elastic lateral movement before anchorage/concrete slip. The largest tension-only roof witness gives **69.1 MPa** using smooth gross area. These values are not resistances, thread-section checks, an interaction design or actual wind. At the inherited primary centre +6746.721, a 1 kN horizontal action would have a **7.512 kNm** lever to cold-grid top −765, before floor-diaphragm restraint or load sharing. Both signs, biaxial load, wind, slab/roof chords, rod bending and combined N/M/H must be resolved together; there is no basis for attributing all global wind to this one point or assuming an equal four-corner split.

## A finite cold-node cage challenger

The old SW-regular 600 mm node is not adopted at the new elevation. As an adverse geometry test, translating its eight **bent H22 starter routes** down 585 mm into the −765..−1215 cold member brings P7 within **10.358 mm clear** of a proposed Ø20 hold-down, after the 0.1 mm computational allowance. This fails the retained 22 mm clearance comparator. The old starter bends cannot simply be shifted into the new node.

A conventional **headed straight-bar challenger** is laid out explicitly for the same 600 × 600 cold footprint, not selected as a qualified product. It retains the eight source pile-bar axes but substitutes straight H22 bars with **Ø120 × 12 mm circular heads**, alternating head centre elevations −1030/−1075. The four full-height Ø20 hold-downs terminate at −990 on separate **Ø80 × 12 mm trial heads**. The alternating pile heads have 32.9 mm solid-to-solid clearance after the 0.1 mm allowance despite overlapping in plan; their positions, links and bar paths are in the calculation. This is a genuine change from hooked starter development: no bending shape or head resistance is inherited.

The trial cold mat has five H20 lines per direction at 60/110/300/490/540, with EW/NS centres at −1165/−1114. Three H20 top lines per direction are at 60/270/550, with EW/NS centres at −823/−866. Six closed Ø8 **horizontal perimeter hoops** at −809/−903/−950/−1030/−1100/−1151 confine the vertical-bar group. Their intended tangencies to the top EW and bottom NS/EW mats are explicit; they do not establish joint shear capacity or substitute for the arm links. This arrangement replaces an earlier vertical-link sketch after it was found to intersect a pile bar. The exact 3D segment/disk check, including the 0.1 mm computational allowance, gives **22.9 mm minimum** for a top-mat crossing, an unrelated hoop/mat crossing and a pile-head/bottom-mat crossing; hold-head/hoop clearance is **32.482 mm**, and hold-down/top-bar clearance is **27.4 mm**. The smallest 22.9 leaves only **0.9 mm** over this idealised 22 mm comparator: placement tolerance can remove it. This is a narrow nominal layout, not permission to build without a tolerance and aggregate/placement design. Rod, link and head coordinates are recorded so an actual joint redesign can be checked rather than hidden behind a rectangle.

For scale only, the prior **165.275 kN** H22 full-yield challenge divided by the net face area of the Ø120 head gives **15.122 MPa** against the old authored 17 MPa concrete stress comparator. The largest 21.698 kN roof uplift witness on a Ø80 hold-down head gives 4.604 MPa. Neither average proves a head: actual head bending/weld or forged detail, anchorage length, local punching, edge/splitting, interacting struts, confinement, load cycling, concrete and pile transfer must be calculated with the complete N/M/H actions and the real head geometry. In particular, two staggered head layers and the four penetrations change the node's concrete load paths. The close fit, real cold-grid arm steel, joint assembly and independent materials/site acceptance remain open. If the headed configuration or its reinforcement cannot meet those conditions, redesign the support rather than calling this clearance check a node pass.

## The thermal and damp break is a real structural joint

The four Ø60 holes, centred on the ties, cross the AAC/mortar course and full mineral DPC from warm RC bottom −380 to cold RC top −765. Their **20 mm nominal radial annuli** reserve a possible mineral collar/grout and seal route; no product, capillary resistance, gas/air seal, movement capacity or non-plastic material is selected. A bare bar through those layers creates a metal and interface bypass. A solid 300 × 300 mm RC bridge would give a more direct structural path but also bridge the thermal and capillary barriers; it is a deliberate adverse comparator, not an automatic cure. MAT is comparing the actual 429.5 mm ordinary corner, local floor/glass discontinuity, four penetrations and separate weather-leaf support. Its 519.5 mm loggia-sidewall 2D thermal result is not transferable.

The four long bars imply a real construction sequence: anchor and survey them in the cold receiver, form/lay qualified local AAC and DPC details around them, build the warm grid and pier with their links and reinforcement, and connect the cap and roof without damaging the boundaries. This is a sequence to design, not evidence that a 7.40 m bar assembly can be positioned, threaded through stock blocks, cast, protected and inspected in the actual site. The anchor ends, bar/cage congestion, plate welds, roof erection, joint tolerances, weather, fire, retained ventilation and renewal access remain internal constructive work. If the required seal, force transfer or occupied corner cannot be achieved together, redesign the station or return to a competing structure; do not accept an unsealed shortcut.

This develops a **single candidate station**, not the whole-house lateral scheme, actual roof corner, selected foundation or STR-HOUSE exit. The 400 mm failed joint and low-tie/cage defects remain rejected. The complete signed support group requires the real roof/diaphragm reactions, wall and floor actions, ground movement and actual wind/soil before design and external acceptance.
