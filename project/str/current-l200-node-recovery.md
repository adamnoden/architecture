## Do not refine the monolithic cage by inertia

The tested straight-ended arrangement fails its declared short-end anchorage. The first actual conventional90-degree vertical-tail arrangement has enough nominal bend/bond length but fails particular bar-clearance and wall-end-cover checks. These are rejected layouts. They do not establish that all conventional vertical tails or all200 mm monolithic joints are impossible.

The current ordinary16H12 section has substantial demand margin. More longitudinal steel is therefore not the first response to the unresolved node. Compare these two complete-system alternatives before escalating cage complexity:

| Relationship | Revised monolithic alternative | Direct-bearing/slip alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corner wall | Continuous RC L with a fully composed three-dimensional slab node | Separately continuous RC lateral/roof wall through the floor level |
| Floor gravity | Slab/node action transferred into the RC L and existing bearings | Floor end reactions carried in compression on actual authored bearing-wall lines |
| Floor rotation | Resisted through the composed node | Permitted at explicit bearing/slip interfaces without an assumed wall bending connection |
| Diaphragm forces | Monolithic source transfer | Separately detailed, anchored in-plane ties/shear interfaces to the continuous lateral system |
| Main new risk | Reinforcement, links and anchorage cannot occupy the same physical body reliably | Bearings open or rock, the diaphragm loses a valid lateral route, or redistributed floor/foundation demands become unacceptable |
| Evidence required | Exact bar paths, local node equilibrium, actual finite-solid/plate demands and adequacy | A fresh full-house physical model of the changed interfaces and support geometry, then actual96 source programmes |

For the direct-bearing alternative, keep the corner-wall footprints and lateral/roof continuity as the explicit starting topology. End the slab against the vertical wall faces at a real separation joint. Support the floor directly on actual lower bearing-wall territory; do not grant a corner cantilever because the old monolithic node disappeared. If the present wall footprint cannot support the floor end, author the necessary conventional bearing-wall/setback geometry and its architectural consequences first. A load-bearing ledge, corbel or added support cannot be represented as a zero-volume constraint or receive uncharged stiffness.

The interface must separate three jobs physically: compression-only gravity bearing, allowed floor rotation/slip, and anchored diaphragm shear/tie transfer. Its normal/shear/rotational laws, force origins, own weight, bearing lengths and movement allowances must be declared. No perfect pin, permanent closed contact, rigid diaphragm tie, zero-friction support or material resistance is inferred from the word bearing. Material constituents must also respect the house's no-plastic direction.

Keep the lateral-resisting wall continuous independently of the floor's vertical support mechanism. Merely releasing rotations on the existing wall/slab node while leaving overlapping concrete, stiffness or gravity unchanged is not this alternative. Simply deleting the corner-wall moment without providing a complete in-plane wind route is not this alternative either.
