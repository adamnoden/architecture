# STR-GROUND — foundation typology and moving-ground architecture

**1 November 2026 · P0, owner-directed comparative engineering result.** This **narrows the pre-site structural options**, but is **not** an adopted foundation, drawn-to-scale construction section, site geotechnical investigation or STR-GROUND packet closeout. The [P0 brief](ground-movement-and-substructure-brief.md), [12 × 12 m Reference House](../../docs/reference-house/current-house-basis.md), [solid masonry and arches](str-masonry-result.md), [floor/roof systems](str-spans-result.md), [ground-water policy](../../docs/reference-house/ground-water-policy.md), [material programme](../mat/material-integrity-programme.md) and [Boundary Service Lodge/raceway](../had/boundary-service-lodge-and-raceway-brief.md) retain their authority.

## Preliminary decision

**Prefer developing a structurally continuous, deep-supported pile/ground-beam grid and a truly suspended mineral ground floor with a deliberately open heave/movement void.** It best expresses the owner's requirement that permanent solid masonry should not depend on seasonal volume stability of shallow clay, without pretending that the pile heads themselves never move. It is a **preferred research typology only**. A real plot may demand a different answer, including a pile group or raft variant. The correct foundation is selected *from* geology, tree/root evidence, loads and construction access; depth or concrete mass alone is not durability.

**No default shallow strip footing on a tree-heavy shrinkable-clay plot**, and **no polymer void-former silently built in** to deliver the preferred option. Both are explicit comparison challenges, not universal bans. No active pump or tanked subterranean inhabited volume is accepted as a substitute for an independent, dry, inspectable house/ground interface.

## Ranked options and disqualifying conditions

| Family | Load path / why investigate | What must disqualify or force reconsideration | Relative buildability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G1 — engineered piles / piers, reinforced ground-beam grillage, independently supported suspended mineral floor** | Outer and court masonry, continuous internal bearing walls, genuine arch abutments and roof/floor reactions → designed reinforced beam grid → located pile heads → adequately proven receiving strata/shaft action. A real gap separates active near-surface ground from beams and floor. **Leading pre-site candidate.** | Unfounded assumptions about load-bearing stratum; negative skin friction, shaft heave/uplift, pile group movement, pilecap reactions, group stiffness, tree-root disturbance, adjacent foundations, groundwater/soil aggressivity and gas. A nominally continuous beam cannot hide changed internal wall loads or pier rotation. | Highest early design/test/rig cost and complexity; buys the ability to create designed isolation where warranted, not immunity to movement |
| **G2 — deepened trench-fill / strip with raised suspended mineral floor** | Continuous direct compression support and familiar trade; potential if ground and tree assessment actually permit a sound deep founding plane | Deep continuous excavations sever roots and may disturb boundaries/utilities; the full trench side can suffer swell/uplift; exposed permanent fabric can still move with clay; void and internal floor no substitute for footing movement assessment | Conventional trade, but excavation/disposal and tree conflicts rise; comparator rather than P0 default |
| **G3 — stiff raft / piled raft** | Can redistribute concentrated loads, transfer moments and respond to variable bearing; a design challenger for specific ground profiles | A concrete plate resting on active shrink/swell soil is not “ground decoupling”; uplift/rotation and soil contact are central; piled raft load-share/movement are site-specific; high mass, embedded services and repair implications remain | Geotechnically sophisticated and material-intensive; cannot inherit a heave void merely by being thick |
| **G4 — shallow strips, stepped mineral foundations and traditional masonry footings** | Honest minimum-complexity comparator on benign and proven ground, potentially lower embodied material/labour in a right-site case | Tree-driven active-zone movement, inconsistent bearing, ground-water changes, differential settlement, capillarity and inaccessible buried service crossings; wider/deeper brickwork by instinct does not cure soil deformation | Easier to procure, but **reject as default** where movement evidence contradicts the owner's durable-solid-wall objective |
| **G5 — raised pier/arcade with accessible undercroft** | Structural separation and genuine access possible; legible compressive geometry can exploit a slope or generous plot | More concentrated pier/arching reactions, lateral stability, fire/thermal/rodent/gas/air interfaces, exposed foundation/void, approach/access and garden/courtyard level conflict; no site is yet demonstrated | Significant architectural and cost consequence; retain only where site/owner composition justifies it |

**Selection rule:** the cheapest shallow option may legitimately win if a selected plot's actual strata, trees, moisture regime and life-cycle movement warrant it. The strongest long-life answer is not automatically the one with the most concrete, piles or reinforcement. G1 is a **starting structural hypothesis**, not a site-independent constitutional rule or a plotted pile count.

## Ground movement has three separate frames

1. **Unloaded or shallow-ground motion `G(t)`**: clay shrinks in drought, swells after rehydration/tree loss, and may shift near the surface and at trenches, paths and chambers. A pile grillage may be isolated from much of this, but foundations and adjacent works still interact with it.
2. **House structural support motion `S_i(t)`**: each pile/pier/footing can settle, rotate, uplift or move laterally. Its location and actual load may differ across the outer masonry, court ring, internal bearing walls and arch abutments. Beam stiffness and reinforcement carry resulting bending/tension; compression-led architecture **does not remove** the need for reinforcement, lateral stability and an uplift/negative-skin-friction assessment.
3. **Interface differential `D_i(t)=S_i(t)-G(t)`**: the facade threshold, ventilated floor-edge, exterior service raceway, surface paving, court drain and utility lodge experience **relative** motion. No rigid concealed pipe or cast-in fixture may be assumed to survive arbitrary 3D offsets. Water paths need continuing fall and predictable release.

**A free heave void addresses only some `G → floor/beam` contacts. It is not a repair for `S_i-S_j` differential support movement.** Solid masonry and load-bearing arches are particularly unforgiving of misaligned supports and concentrated tensile stress, so the final grid must be coordinated with the inhabited design rather than added after the plan is frozen.

### Source-bounded void comparator — *not* an adopted dimension

The public NHBC **2025** Chapter 4.2, clause 4.2.10.2 Table 7, gives these historic **reference-guide void dimensions** where clay heave applies:

| Soil volume-change potential | Foundation/ground beam side | Under ground beam or suspended in-situ concrete floor | Under suspended precast-concrete or timber floor |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| High | 35 mm | 150 mm | 300 mm |
| Medium | 25 mm | 100 mm | 250 mm |
| Low | 0 mm | 50 mm | 200 mm |

The **suspended precast/timber column includes 150 mm ventilation allowance**. This distinction matters: treating a 150 mm heave-only space as interchangeable with a 300 mm precast subfloor would quietly consume ventilation, working or movement territory. NHBC 2025 notes proprietary void formers and compressible materials with their *residual compressed dimensions*; heave load before collapse can matter. These are **2025 guidance comparisons**, not an approved source for the future plot: the **2026 edition** took effect for relevant NHBC homes from 1 January 2026, and the **2027 edition is announced for 1 January 2027**. Confirm the applicable detailed chapter and full build specification at site commission; do not silently freeze old numbers as a 1,000-year rule.

The owner's plastic-avoidance preference makes **a genuinely clear, persistent void with removable temporary construction support** the first alternative to permanently concealed expanded-polymer void formers. The void must exist after formwork removal, concreting, debris, soil slough, paths, roots and decades of maintenance. A compressible substitute cannot earn a “no-plastic” material pass merely because it is hidden. A true open void is not self-certifying: demonstrate bearing/grillage stability **during** construction, floor-gas/air/fire/thermal/cold-bridge continuity, external ventilation if applicable, moisture and flooding routes, rodent control and inspection access.

**Three simple sensitivity cases** (arithmetic diagnostics, not a site profile or acceptable foundation sizing):

- If the chosen model requires **150 mm** clear movement and only **100 mm** remains after construction/infilling, **50 mm of motion may force contact**. A nominal drawing gap is not the maintained free void.
- A hypothetical **5 mm differential** support displacement across the 3.5 m court-pier spacing gives a geometric slope `5/3500 ≈ 1/700`; **15 mm** gives `15/3500 ≈ 1/233`. Across the nominal 4.25 m centred court-to-outer-wall annulus width the same 15 mm gives `1/283`. These are support-point gradients, **not** masonry crack acceptance, arch thrust results or foundation tolerances.
- If a designed ground void stays clear through a **150 mm** local soil heave but one pile head itself rises relative to its peers, the gap can still perform while masonry/beam stability **fails**. Thus nominal gap margin and pile/beam differential checks are independent failure gates.

## Reference House load-grid obligations — structural support before pier count

The accepted pre-site shell is **12 × 12 m** with a centred **3.5 × 3.5 m** courtyard; nominal courtyard-to-outer sidewall annulus width is **4.25 m**. This is **not** an effective structural span: wall thickness, bearing lines, stairwell, rooms, openings and service-zone trim change actual support positions.

The next house/foundation plan must locate and assign separately:

- **outer and court ring bearing**: both cavity leaves, corners, roof eaves/hips, roof ring loads, court valley concentrations and the dedicated courtyard water escape;
- **internal solid masonry**: each actual vertical supporting wall and its upper-wall alignment; no assumed 125 mm lightweight partition or unrecorded floating mass;
- **arches and openings**: impost/pier thrusts, entry/stair void and bridge reaction, side restraint and construction sequencing; do not put a pile merely at a pier label without checking the resulting beam group;
- **load sharing**: pile/ground-beam stiffness, outer/court/main internal junctions, beam torsion, changed bearings and accidental loss/stiffness reduction of one support;
- **lateral and uplift load paths**: soil/earth and wind actions, arch horizontal thrust, accidental settlement, slab diaphragm/ties and ground-beam reinforcement;
- **service/court crossings**: intentional voided, inspectable structural passages *before* final pile placement; never cut a pile cap or a permanently embedded tie to reach a drain afterwards.

In G1 the room plan determines the minimum credible load-bearing grid. Conversely the grid’s true reactions and floor depth can force a **source-controlled house redesign** through STR-HOUSE and HAD, with all drawing/evidence applicability assessed. No new pile coordinate, depth, count, capacity or grade is adopted in this study.

## Connected interfaces: the foundation is not just the concrete

**Outer wall / plinth / floor / active soil:** weathered brick facade → controlled mineral wall-base/capillary break with tested compressive/shear continuity → supported inner masonry/solid wall lines → independently supported mineral floor with true dry/wet/gas/thermal boundary → mechanically verified ground beams/piles → **open motion void** over active soil. Keep external ground slopes away from walls, and cavity-base drainage plus subfloor ventilation/air barrier deliberately resolved. Never let a planted/moving path, granular trench, landscape bridge, downpipe receiver or high court paving fill the void and reconnect the clay to a vulnerable wall/floor. A thicker slate course is **not** an answer to soil heave or wall-base water pressure; MAT/GWT/MRA own compatible capillary and moisture physics.

**Courtyard:** its floor and threshold have their own water level and relative settlement frames. A piled foundation does not make a failed court drain safe. RWT's normal and emergency through-ring exits must remain hydraulically continuous and renewable across house/soil motion; court water must not discharge into the heave void, foundation footing, basement-like dry moat or inaccessible mineral rubble.

**Boundary Service Lodge / moving-service raceway:** arrange a **house-fixed structural port** and a separate **moving-site segment**. For each service locate the fixed/ground endpoints, 3D movement envelope, coupling/separation, permitted supports, replaceable lengths, first shut-off, leak indication and safe opening. Do not cast live services into permanent beam fabric, rigidly bridge a moving trench to a stable slab or automatically install polymer corrugations. A **designed opening** alone proves neither movement tolerance nor gas/water/fire continuity.

**Groundwater and flooding:** a ventilated heave void is not a guaranteed dry accessible tunnel. Keep inhabited accommodation above ground where practically possible; no hidden membrane/pump-dependent basement. If the void can become wet, its protected structure, foundations, ground-gas barrier, drainage/escape and accessible safe maintenance must be defined before acceptance. No unproved “zero groundwater” site is assumed.

## Fault / construction assurance matrix

| Challenge | Evidence or rejection condition |
|---|---|
| Drought under mature trees, then prolonged wet winter or tree removal | Geo-specific moisture and volume-change model and expected movement direction/range; active-soil rise must not bear on floor/beam or capture services; no invented time to equilibrium |
| Growth, root pruning and retained tree roots | Real arboricultural constraints, construction apparatus/trench/boring footprint, tree survival and settlement; “bored pile” is not automatically root-safe |
| One pile/ground bearing anomaly | Installation logs, integrity and load testing where justified, structural redistribution and local deflection/rotation at actual masonry lines; do not assume a grid transfers load without calculation |
| Positive heave along beam side/underside and pile shafts | Independent side/underbeam void and uplift/shaft-action checks, pilecap tension, no accidental concrete bridge, no hidden polymer default |
| High water/blocked external gully + court rain | No water path into dry accommodation or structural void; distinct RWT, MRA and DPL receiving/overflow duties |
| Heavy masonry opening/arch next to uneven support | Impost reactions, ties and serviceability gradient; no geometric brick-arch preference overrides equilibrium |
| Moving site trench at foundation penetration | Full 3D displacement plus retained falls/seals, inspection and replacement; a tightly grouted pipe through an arch/beam is a failed interface |
| Difficult concrete installation/curing and pile construction in wet season | Hold points for strata, roots, installation logs, integrity testing, steel position/cover, free void after construction, mix/exposure class, no casual added water, curing, as-built survey |
| Long-term paths/hedges/planted receiver | Verify landscaping and root growth cannot close the intended heave/inspection gap; mineral impact bed not a disguised foundation French drain |
| Gas/radon or flooding hazard | Site ground-gas classification and actual ventilation/barrier performance; a no-plastic preference does not abolish gas/air/fire physics |

**Material integrity** must be checked end to end: reinforcement, bearing pads, sacrificial supports, spacers, gas/radon barrier, expansion/movement joint, ventilation mesh, insulation, DPC, pipe collars and any hidden forms. It is incorrect to claim this candidate “plastic free” before each is named. Reinforced concrete under permanently buried/wet exposure requires an actual ground/chemical durability class and inspection; there is no scientific basis for “double the cement strength” as a longevity guarantee.

## Remaining acceptance work; why STR-GROUND is still planned

This **P0 first option reduction** has earned a preferred *family* and strong adverse checks, not the **full packet closeout**. The [P0 brief](ground-movement-and-substructure-brief.md) also requires a **source-bound pile/grid plan and representative 1:10 outer-wall and court/internal-bearing sections**, a longitudinal moving-service crossing, a real tree-constrained approach trial, and a candidate-by-candidate movement/settlement sensitivity supported by explicit model geometry. Those are **not** supplied by prose or the historical 2025 void table. No as-built, design load or geology is available; do not fabricate it. A next step should produce exactly those bounded source-labelled schematic layouts and test them against the latest STR-HOUSE openings, not another stand-alone geotechnical essay. Once they are credible, STR-GROUND may be completed as a *pre-site design option study* with actual-site acceptance retained under DPL/BCR/DEL/EPR; until then **lifecycle remains `planned`**.

This record is the authoritative **partial** under the existing canonical STR-GROUND packet, not a new workstream or duplicate task graph. It gives STR-HOUSE a constrained reference family now, and forbids STR-RECONCILE from treating that preference as an adopted structural foundation.
