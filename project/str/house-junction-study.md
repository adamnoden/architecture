## Rear-west: close the room before calling the jamb solved

The earlier rear-west wall ended at x 4250 on y 8852.5, with a door x 3100–4000. That left a 250 mm free-ended strip. It also allowed a route around the wall end: the court’s north inside face is y 8167.5, while the wall’s south finished face is y 8767.5. A closed door did not by itself close the bedroom.

The revised comparison is precise:

- Extend the rear-west wall to **x 4450**, retaining its 170 mm finished depth and door x **3100–4000**.
- Add a **245 mm finished return centred on x 4450**, faces **4327.5–4572.5**, from **y 8012.5 to 8852.5**, on both storeys.
- Its south end reaches the actual courtyard structural-inner-leaf region, rather than merely touching the room-side plaster at y 8167.5. The junction must be bonded or otherwise connected as actual structure; the planning overlap is not a connection design.
- The return fits between x 4250 and the upper left court-window start x 4650, with **77.5 mm nominal margin on each side**. It remains **227.5 mm** clear of the lower court-north doorway starting at x 4800. These are plan margins, not bearing/anchorage capacities.

This produces a **450 mm nominal right jamb** and closes the privacy bypass without moving the rear wet cell, bed, stair or existing doorway. The paired-plan owner is rerunning access and closed-door separation; this memo does not replace that check. The return’s 245 mm reservation does not choose its masonry material or joint with a 140 mm concrete wall.

### Explicit horizontal-force mechanism

The return is transverse to the east/west thrust of this doorway arch. Its presence cannot be promoted into adequate in-plane buttressing. For the first complete force-path comparison, nominate a **permanent non-timber tie at springing level**, connected to the actual structural arch/abutment region and independent of the renewable door assembly. The ring still carries its assigned gravity loads; the tie closes the horizontal reaction locally. Anchorage, fire/durability and access remain design duties.

That tie is an architectural consequence: it acts as an enduring transom across the opening and affects the fanlight and usable opening. It is not described as an invisible benefit. If that expression is unacceptable, the alternative is a deliberately enlarged/replanned masonry abutment and its foundation, with actual three-dimensional resistance demonstrated. The present 450 mm strip plus return is not evidence that the tie can be omitted.

The longer stair-trimmer fallback still has its finite front L-junction issue. The revised rear door leaves **82.5 mm nominal separation** from the existing 200 mm trial patch ending at x 3017.5. That does not establish the final member, bearing spread or tolerance. Developing the short trimmer/header route remains the first enquiry.

## A credible 140 mm arch family — and its precise limit

Solid concrete units of **440 × 215 × 140 mm** are real published products [1, 2]. Primary industry guidance addresses genuine concrete-masonry arches, including solid units, filled joints, centering and abutment behaviour [3]. Manufacturer sitework guidance also describes cutting concrete blocks, including accurate bench-saw work [4]. Together these establish a credible **custom solid-concrete voussoir enquiry**.

They do **not** establish a supplier-approved 140 mm arch for this house. A possible blank retains the 140 mm through-wall dimension while its face is cut/shaped into the required wedge. The 215 mm stock height is not automatically an adequate radial depth, and an ordinary block’s declared compression direction cannot silently be transferred to the different loading direction in a voussoir. Confirm unit configuration, cutting limits, actual orientation/strength, taper, mortar joints, bond, ring analysis, skewbacks and construction sequence. Obtain a named construction proposition before treating the family as selected.

This enquiry can produce a genuine plastered inner structural arch while a separate 102.5 mm clay weathering-leaf arch supplies the exterior expression. It does not require pretending that a 215 mm bonded-clay wall fits inside a 140 mm core. Where exposed clay is itself the architectural requirement, use a real clay family and its actual through-wall depth.

## Alternative B: a supported outward bay, not a stair intrusion

For a concrete geometric fallback, take the west bay **y 2650–6550**, encompassing the window y 4100–5100 and nominal 1450 mm wall territories on each side. This bay lies between the neighbouring window openings rather than ending at their jambs.

Hold the room-side construction in place and change the layers to:

| Layer | West x interval | Thickness |
|---|---|---:|
| Weathering leaf | −75 to 27.5 | 102.5 mm |
| Cavity | 27.5 to 187.5 | 160 mm |
| Structural inner leaf | 187.5 to 402.5 | 215 mm |
| Interior finish | 402.5 to 417.5 | 15 mm |

The **75 × 3900 mm** additional footprint is **0.2925 m²**. All of the thicker bay must have a real supporting route from the foundation; do not start it on a corbel or suspend the outer leaf from an unexplained shelf. Develop it through the upper storey and to a coherent eaves termination. Its new facade steps, cavity transitions, ground clearance, wall-base barrier, thermal effects, window reveals, roof edge and site/setback availability require explicit reconciliation.

Moving the extra masonry outward also changes the core’s centre and therefore the eccentricity of a given room-side bearing. It does not automatically improve that bearing. Recalculate the actual header/slab end patch and restraint against the new core; do not inherit the old 75 mm trial patch or represented projecting seat.

This is more architectural change than A, but it preserves the 160 mm cavity, the four-bedroom/two-en-suite room allocations and the checked stair width. No full-height outward bay or foundation is adopted by drawing its coordinates.

## Court-south: 1800 mm is the stronger first geometric comparison

Reduce the proposed court-south opening from **x 4800–7200** to **x 5100–6900**. Nominal side territories grow from 550 to **850 mm**. Use the SPANS reviewer's serious **200 mm** member comparison wholly outside the B void: **x 4685–4885 / 7115–7315**, with centres 4785/7215. It leaves **215 mm nominal clearance** to the 1800 opening; each patch overlaps the old 2400 opening by 85 mm. A member centred on the void edge would instead intrude 100 mm into the void; its 115 mm clearance is retained only as an intrusion comparison in the JSON. Actual member widths, bearing lengths, tolerances, loads and through-leaf eccentricity must replace these trials.

Keep the genuine arch enquiry: **2250 springing + 600 rise + 215 radial ring** still gives an intrados crown of 2850 and extrados crown of 3065. For the narrower span, however, the circular intrados radius becomes **975 mm**, and the outer skewbacks extend about **198.46 mm** beyond the opening edges at elevation **2332.69 mm**. Reusing the old 1500 mm radius or 172 mm extension would be wrong. The serious outside-void patch ends only **16.54 mm horizontally before the ring's outer extreme**; that geometric separation is not an adequate bearing, load-spreading or tolerance allowance.

This change moves the illustrated floor reactions toward a direct pier route; it does not eliminate arch thrust. Compare a properly assessed 850 mm abutment/connected-foundation route with a permanent springing tie. If a tie is the provisional mechanism, locate and disclose it in the opening section. A collector 920 mm higher at the old framing datum is not an equivalent tie without a designed intervening moment/force path.

The narrower opening changes the door leaves, permanent aperture, fanlight and courtyard elevation. Preserve the 2400 mm garden opening as a **separate wider arch duty**, rather than claiming that the narrower court result proves it. The court-north doorway remains at its stated geometry unless separately changed. The garden doorway also has different adjacent masonry: its ground-floor neighbours leave nominal strips x 4400–4800 and 7200–7600, **400 mm each**, before the next windows. Do not transfer the court's 850 mm-abutment enquiry to those strips or assume neighbouring arch thrusts cancel.

Nominate the 140 mm structural family separately from the 102.5 mm outer weathering leaf. Each leaf has its own supported loads, out-of-plane restraint, joints and environmental interfaces. The outer arch receives no floor reaction by visual association.

These shapes require **actual custom frames/fanlights or shaped openings**. A full-width rectangular top at 2850 cannot remain under a curve whose springing is 2250 or 2550. Nor can a 2250 mm door leaf plus frame simply be inserted under a 2250 mm masonry springing: leaf, frame, threshold, seals and tolerances must be budgeted. Rated closures and replacement routes remain OAS/IAS work on the actual geometry.

For mechanism comparison only, the ideal parabolic UDL ratios `H/V = L/(4f)` are 0.75 for the 1800/600 case and 1.0 for the 2400/600 case, where V is one vertical end reaction. They do not analyse the circular rings, actual wall/floor loading or finite bearings. Wider openings retain distinct thrust and abutment duties even when their crown heights match.
