# STR-HOUSE — the east spine's actual shared-bearing alternatives

**Result: retain the 140 mm core as the first supplier-detail enquiry at the east spine; reject a 215 mm wall as a drop-in repair.** A real tied narrow-wall detail exists. It is a different construction from two independent 100 mm seats, and its connection, temporary works and upper-wall load path must be designed as that system. The wider wall remains an explicit replanning alternative if the narrow detail cannot satisfy this house's actions and erection conditions.

This closes the previously generic choice of which local alternative to investigate first. It does **not** select the floor or prove the junction. STR-HOUSE remains incomplete; the accepted house and the joined, unadopted B geometry are unchanged.

## 1. Starting authority and exact question

The [joined plan](house-plan-study.md) places the aligned lower service wall and upper wet spine at **x9300**, with **140 mm core plus two 15 mm finishes**. The [support study](house-support-study.md) identified a real incompatibility: two opposed independent 100 mm slab ends cannot fit on that core. Rotating the gallery floor north/south avoids that local condition but creates a **5462.5 mm structural-face field** and can transfer the opposed-end problem to the front wall. Rotation is therefore an alternative, not an automatic solution.

The [A3 comparison](studies/house-spine-bearing.svg) and [exact record](studies/house-spine-bearing.json) investigate the supplier-detail route and three explicit widenings. The [generator](studies/house-spine-bearing.py) reads the existing plan/support records, verifies their controlling-source fingerprints, and changes neither them nor their existing route witnesses.

## 2. What the primary supplier details actually establish

The [Ibstock/Coltman engineer's page](https://www.ibstock.co.uk/products/flooring-and-lintels/hollowcore/engineers-section) publishes both a [dual-bearing 140 wall detail](https://images.ctfassets.net/eta2vegx3yuv/xDkkn2b15F9r0FS4iHie7/e089626385026a81229e47190e38e4f8/Hollowcore_-_Dual_Bearing_on_140_Wall.png) and an [ordinary 215 internal-wall detail](https://images.ctfassets.net/eta2vegx3yuv/4MYlz58h6CRqbs9VqR2Ke5/bb7a762b18eb7ebaa875ad167fc05a76/Hollowcore_-_Bearing_on_Internal_Wall.png), visually inspected on 30 October 2026. The narrow detail shows a minimum 140 wall, H12 bars in opened/grouted cores at 400 mm centres, a full bearing at the other slab end, and purpose-designed temporary support retained until the infill reaches its design strength. The ordinary 215 drawing labels 100 mm minimum bearings on both sides. Neither drawing supplies this house's actions, slab selection or reinforcement anchorage design.

[Forterra's current technical page](https://www.forterra.co.uk/bison/precast-hollowcore-solid-floors/) separately describes tied opened cores for shared walls below 190 mm, with opposing cores aligned; its typical arrangement opens two cores in a 1200-wide unit. **Do not combine that core count with Ibstock's 400 mm pitch into a fictitious standard detail.** Supplier-specific unit profiles, openings and reinforcement must agree.

The dated [MPA/PFF *Hollowcore Floor Systems*, August 2019](https://www.mpaprecast.org/MPAPrecast/media/root/Publications/Flooring/p2.pdf), page 3, recommends 215 mm for opposed support, a tied alternative no narrower than 140, and the narrow condition at only one slab end. Its general nominal bearings are not project-specific minima or permission to omit tolerances. The manufacturer's actual system controls the eventual comparison; this dated guide is corroboration, not a new 2026 statutory rule.

These sources change the architectural enquiry. A narrow shared support is an established detail family worth locating, rather than an impossible arithmetic arrangement to be rescued by a generic engineer referral. It introduces deliberate steel tension and wet infill, already compatible with the structural brief's exception for necessary tension. It does not make the prestressed floor a compression-only or demountable structure.

The band is a panel-grid trial. Side joints, cuts, core axes, aligned opening positions, tie phase and actual unit lengths are unselected. It does not resolve the riser trim, plant doorway, family doorway or every other spine segment. The retained 150 mm plank and 350 mm total floor remain dimensional enquiries; the published narrow detail does not establish their compatibility with this particular load case.

Two independent 100 mm seats exceed 140 by **60 mm before any joint**. On 215 they leave **15 mm total**. That remainder cannot simultaneously be a 15 mm joint and a separate tolerance allowance. A 20 mm authored joint plus two 100 mm seats would require 220 mm; that arithmetic does not select a 220 mm product or justify reducing a manufacturer's minimum. The narrow detail's actual end dimensions remain unknown rather than being invented by halving 140.

## 5. Decision, load path and finite next evidence

**First develop the supplier-bound tied 140 junction at this intact bay.** It preserves the established plan reservation and offers much shorter east/west floor fields than the north/south gallery alternative. Its merit is avoidance of demonstrated spatial damage, not proven strength, lower cost or easy erection. Keep 215 widening as a real fallback with the listed replanning duties; reject all three widenings as unchanged-layout substitutions.

The actual upper-wall path must be shown separately: upper masonry/contact → designed solid infill or end-closure region → lower spine. The existence of a tie does not prove that path, and a nonstructural core plug cannot be credited as structural infill. The final connection package needs actual wall line loads, slab-end reactions, material strengths, contact/bearing/eccentricity checks, infill geometry, reinforcement development, core alignment and a matched manufacturing/erection tolerance schedule. It must show how the lower wall and planks remain stable before infill cures and when upper-wall loading is permitted.

The slab tie is not automatically an arch-thrust tie, diaphragm collector, roof restraint or foundation/DPC shear connection. Those force paths retain their own locations and actions. The upper-floor joint is also not the ground-level damp barrier. Concrete/grout constituents, any pads and closures remain subject to MAT; no concealed polymer exception is approved here. Fire, acoustic and movement duties must survive the actual closed joint and adjoining finish assemblies.

This is sufficient to choose a **specific next engineering detail**, not to approve construction or close STR-HOUSE. A supplier rejection of the actual HC150 end/profile, inability to align and anchor the connection, inadequate upper-wall transmission, an unworkable erection sequence or failed remote bearing invalidates the preferred enquiry and returns the wider-wall or floor-direction alternatives. The remaining stair/bridge, riser, thrust, roof and occupied-room questions are unchanged.
