# P0 — ground movement and foundation architecture

**Owner direction:** 31 October 2026. **Owning programme:** [STR — Structural Architecture](structural-architecture-programme.md), [#1103](https://github.com/adamnoden/architecture/issues/1103). **Status:** binding design brief and investigation mandate, **not** a selected foundation, a PCO closeout or site-specific structural adequacy.

## Architectural aim

**The house must not rely on the dimensional stability of shallow soil for the integrity of its permanent masonry.** This applies to a tree-heavy London plot and to the external courtyard, internal solid wall lines, genuine arches and long-lived floor/roof structure. Significant clay movement, differential settlement and heave are priority failure modes, not peripheral specialist details.

The owner wants to select a plot for its life, setting and trees rather than merely because the geology makes foundations cheap. **Design the strongest feasible house-to-ground solution for the chosen land.** Geological investigation remains indispensable to identify the actual bearing stratum, shrink/swell zone, water regime, former and existing trees, adjacent foundations, contamination and feasible construction methods. The mandate does **not** assert that any ground can be made suitable, that piles are immune to movement, or that a quantified zero-settlement target has been proved.

This brief supersedes **presumptively selecting shallow concrete strips** as the reference-house solution. Shallow and traditional footings remain meaningful comparators. It does not change the accepted 12 × 12 m house or authorise construction.

## Preferred initial hypothesis

**Deep load transfer + a sufficiently stiff, continuous, verified supporting grid + an independent suspended mineral floor + deliberate movement clearance between the building and active shallow ground.**

Develop appropriate piles/piers and ground beams as a leading comparison. Track gravity and lateral reactions from outer and courtyard structural leaves, aligned internal masonry, arch abutments, corners, stair/bridge frames and roof to each footing/pile and ultimately the evidenced ground. Provide verifiable load paths and restraint. A pile cap or ground beam may need steel reinforcement; compression-led architecture does not mean omitting the necessary tension system.

Importantly, distinguish:

- **Soil heave clearance:** ground may move into a designed gap without touching the structure. The gap must stay open after pouring, landscaping and maintenance.
- **Actual support movement:** piles can settle, rotate, heave or move differentially; deep supports are not automatically stationary. Size the grid for the movement and stiffness of the whole system, not a magic pile depth.
- **Masonry serviceability:** establish a calculated project displacement, distortion, crack-width and differential-rotation brief for the actual openings, solid walls and arches. A hypothetical 15 mm differential is a warning illustration, **not** an accepted tolerance.
- **Boundary movement:** garden ground, trenches, inspection chambers, paths and highway infrastructure may move relative to the house. No rigid buried pipe or permanent plinth detail may quietly bridge those frames.

## Alternatives to compare against the leader

| Family | Why retain it | Potential disqualifying mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional shallow mass-concrete strip / trench-fill and masonry plinth | Simple, familiar, direct support; honest control for benign ground | Soil shrink/heave, differential movements and root influence not solved by width or high-strength concrete |
| Deep trench-fill / strip with a raised mineral floor | Fewer discrete pile heads where strata and tree constraints support it | Deep excavation through roots; heave on sides and base; construction volume and poor access |
| Piles / piers + reinforced ground beams + suspended mineral floor | Isolates more of the building from the active shallow stratum; supports a continuous masonry grid | Pile head settlement, negative friction, swelling/uplift, group effects, soil chemistry, pile integrity and difficult interfaces |
| Piled raft or stiff structural raft | May redistribute reactions on some ground profiles | Large monolith, changing ground contact, peripheral thermal/moisture/detail burden, reinforcement and ground-movement response |
| Traditional stone/stepped brick/rubble footings | Historical material and load-path comparator; potential architectural plinth vocabulary | Mortared capillary paths, heterogeneous strength, workmanship, higher excavation/labour and movement compatibility |
| Raised pier/arcade/plinth | Potential inspectable space and separation of floor from soil | More lateral stability, access, thermal, moisture, landscape and public-approach complexity |

No material or option is selected by this table; where the site requires another class, add it as a reasoned response rather than an undeclared fallback.

## Tree-heavy plot challenge

Retain mature trees where lawful and practicable. The design must evaluate root-protection areas, existing and projected roots, drought, heavy rain, rising temperatures, maturing/dying/removed trees and differential clay rehydration. Consider root-preserving pile locations, installation rigs, limited-dig or engineered bridging, but **do not** label trenchless work automatically root-safe. The root protection assessment and foundation-movement assessment are different, simultaneous duties.

Root barriers may protect particular paths or pipes; they are not the primary building foundation against clay movement and may violate the polymer-minimisation brief. Avoid concentrating groundwater or foul-water leakage beside foundations, including by constructing an apparently helpful gravel channel.

## Water, floor, material and ground-wall interface

Separate durable, wet-tolerant buried structural materials from the dry inhabited house via a properly designed **mineral wall-base / DPC / suspended floor** assembly. No pump-dependent groundwater suppression; no presumed sealed below-ground occupied enclosure. Resolve both leaves of a cavity wall, cavity base and drainage, external grade and court thresholds, internal bearing walls, floors and services. The DPC must carry actual compressive/shear/restraint loads without encouraging sliding or bridged capillary routes. The floor must close moisture, gas, air, thermal, frost and fire boundaries, not merely rest above a void.

The [ground-water policy](../../docs/reference-house/ground-water-policy.md) and [MAT-ENVELOPE](../mat/envelope-ground-boundary.md) govern materials and damp-barrier questions. MRA attacks compound water/ground failure; RWT/DPL own receiving-water and site outfall.

## Concrete workmanship: spend effort on verifiable construction

The owner prefers **conservative, verified construction** over arbitrary 'double-strength' concrete. Specify site-ground evidence and applicable concrete durability/exposure class first. Compare larger sections where actually beneficial, not because doubling width, depth or compressive strength promises twice the reliability.

Establish hold points and records for:

1. Excavation bearing ground, groundwater, root protection and adjacent assets before concealment;
2. Pile type, placement and depth, installation logs and spoil/volume anomalies;
3. Pile integrity and proportionate load testing against design assumptions;
4. Reinforcement positioning, cover, supports, openings and designed heave clearance before concrete;
5. Concrete specification, arrival traceability, no casual added water, placement, consolidation, curing and independent sampling where appropriate;
6. Survey benchmarks, initial as-built levels and long-term inspection/monitoring strategy.

Do not claim cube strength alone proves soil capacity, pile continuity, movement performance or waterproofing.

## Moving-site service connections

The adjacent [Boundary Service Lodge and accessible raceway brief](../had/boundary-service-lodge-and-raceway-brief.md) owns the architectural *candidate*, not the footing engineering. STR must locate supports and intentional service crossings, distinguish the house-fixed from the soil-fixed portions, account for three-dimensional relative movement and prevent pipes/ducts from being cast tightly through primary beams. A designed oversized opening, sleeved penetration or movement-compatible accessible joint is a candidate only after the actual load path, joint envelope, gas/water seals, service gradient and replacement route are checked.

The existing controlled foul stack has a **below-ground drop within the service suite** and an exit through the service-side foundation/wall territory ([DPL-08C](../dpl/dpl-08c-foul-surface-site-water-interface.md)); the reference-house service/court route must be rechecked against the movement-isolated proposal, not silently overwritten.

## Acceptance questions and outputs

Develop a bounded, source-traceable option reduction with enough geometry to expose failure:

- plan showing loadbearing outer/court/internal wall lines, openings, pile/foundation grid, tree constraints and protected service passages;
- a representative **1:10 outer wall / floor / ground / footings** candidate section and corresponding **courtyard / internal bearing** section, with actual cavity-base, plinth, mineral capillary break, insulation, air/ground gas, void and drainage topology;
- a longitudinal house-to-moving-ground service crossing and one tree-constrained approach/raceway trial;
- measured/computed settlement, differential rotation and heave sensitivity cases, including one pile anomaly, restrained beam uplift, a blocked drain, mature tree loss, tree growth, long drought and prolonged wet winter;
- costs and material/construction complexity as secondary evaluation, and explicit reasons for retaining or rejecting each option.

Never invent pile depth, number, soil bearing, frost/groundwater margins, ground beam sizes, tree positions, sewer level or a settled 1,000-year performance claim. A real-site design requires real-site evidence and appropriate independent calculation/inspection. The pre-site study may earn a **preferred typology with site-specific selection rules**, not a universal as-built foundation.
