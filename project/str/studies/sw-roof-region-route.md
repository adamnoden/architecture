# Southwest roof region: joined route under development

The source square is 4,650 mm per side, from (−200, −200) to (4,450, 4,450). Its 21.6225 m² includes two previously represented outer triangles (5.405625 m² together), two outer rectangles (10.81125 m²) and two inner valley triangles (5.405625 m²). The two 0.02 m² outer tips are already within the triangular pair. The region calculation keeps each piece once and conserves both horizontal first moments; it is one signed static witness, not a stiffness or capacity analysis.

The constructive challenger routes the outer and inner surfaces to eave beams, ridge members, a mitred hip/valley crown and the existing SW and court axis seats. Ridge line support trials coincide with actual upper wall cores at (4800, 2125) and (2125, 3247.5). Both lie over ground-floor openings. A proposed 140 × 250 mm RC head bridges each opening to actual flanking wall extents, with conditional roof actions joined to the separately bounded first-floor wall/floor comparison. The right cloak bearing is 150 mm, not the previously assumed 200 mm. The four widened jamb alternatives carry the intended uplift route through warm pads and sleeved tie clusters to wall-aligned cold rails and their source node axes. Their 20 mm cap plates and crossing geometries are proposals; actual anchorage, reinforcement, distribution and continuity are not demonstrated.

A 300 mm-wide study jamb fails two actual GROUND body witnesses by 2.5 mm. A 260 mm-wide alternative changes each nominal reserve to +17.5 mm; it still needs construction allowances and door-leaf, protected-route and fire checks. The court endpoint is a geometric core intersection, not an established pier. Mirroring the inward 300 mm SW pier there fails a protected-route witness by 97.5 mm. A core-contained SHS120 with an illustrative 15 mm casing gives +57.5 mm nominal against the nearest GROUND witness, but crosses the first-floor RC and has no proven connection, casing or fire detail. Its warm pad and metal-boundary crossing remain unresolved.

The two sloped diagonal members meet at a ridge, so a straight A–B member is geometrically wrong. The section comparison provides a 71.82 mm vertical bounding gap for a shaped crown saddle; it does not establish a continuous bearing face. A simple two-force pin control exposes horizontal thrust, but is not the total roof or wind demand. A horizontal SHS60 tie at +6500 clashes with the sloped diagonal at the two end seats. An SHS50 tie trial at +6459 leaves only 4.00 and 4.54 mm nominal gaps. A revised RHS80-horizontal ×40-vertical tie at +6459.27 leaves about 9.27 mm at each boundary and crosses the actual bed2-L-return wall core; it needs an explicit framed opening, protection and force path. The west eave trial extends 1202.5 mm beyond its existing support to the regional cut; the original 532.5 mm eave-axis-to-weather-edge projection exceeds the prior 500 mm cantilever comparator by 32.5 mm. A q295 tapered jack-seat trial reduces reach to 495 mm, with 5 mm comparator reserve, a 7.5 mm nominal shoe margin to the ring edge and an 8.10 mm nominal assembly gap; its 37.5 mm offset couple is retained in the first-moment ledger. Steel, glass, air/vapour and tolerance interfaces remain open.

The roof surface accounting and signed reactions must not be presented as a completed building. The saddle and finite members, roof/deck and eave edge, openings and their head cages, post/slab intersections, hold-down plates, metal-field penetrations, weather, fire, wall and floor transfers, adjacent roof areas, diaphragm and site-supported foundation remain open. The accompanying Python/JSON/SVG studies are reproducible candidate geometry and adverse controls, not selected members or construction instructions.
