## Clearance and renewal sequence to prove

1. Observe from safe positions; record primary/relief behavior separately. Arrange the work for safe weather and manage court occupation. Rainfall is not isolated by shutting a domestic valve.
2. Lift each actual court inlet/guard independently, remove debris from both sides and inspect its connection. The primary and relief must not require dismantling one another to clear them.
3. Expose the carrier from the gallery/pantry removable strips. The gallery's1270 mm clear width and pantry's2020 mm width are the actual horizontal working territories; covers, tools, hands, supports and temporary barriers must fit within them without pretending residents can pass through the work area.
4. Rod/inspect the straight carrier from accessible ends, with controlled release of any retained water. No bend or hidden intermediate joint is inserted to avoid a conflict. Reach and tool withdrawal must be verified with the actual equipment.
5. Disconnect exposed joints on both sides of each wall sleeve, support the component and withdraw the short joint-free wall piece into the adjacent open strip. The longest boundary crossing is417.5 mm, not the full4250 mm route. Product socket/coupling, tool and withdrawal lengths remain explicit inputs; the available gallery/pantry spans must be checked against them.
6. Renew exposed carrier lengths in the open strips, reinstate supports and connections, inspect the independent channel/weather boundaries, then test discharge and leakage before replacing lids. The removable lid must retain its floor load, thermal, moisture, air and finish duties; a loose board is not a complete floor.
7. If the sleeve or permanent support itself fails, acknowledge the larger repair extent. Replaceability of the carrier does not establish replaceability of the surrounding structure.

This sequence avoids assuming that a4250 mm rigid pipe can be withdrawn into the3500 mm court. A full-length pull-through alternative would instead require an actual exterior extraction/handling envelope or a documented flexible product system; neither is silently credited.

## High-level receivers and exterior drops

Ground observation/remote cleaning and high-level joint/support/receiver replacement are different tasks. The eaves are at+6800; a telescopic tool does not install a replacement gutter or prove the roof safe to stand on. ROF-06 and RWT-06 require one shared actual access comparator: component transport through actual clear openings, court footprint/stability, working position/reach, lifting/withdrawal and reinstatement. No roof hatch, usable loft, tower, anchorage or platform is selected by this draft.

Outer drops need a demounting direction and a safely managed ground transition; an open shoe/gully can improve observation but still creates splash, obstruction and backwater questions. The same duties apply inside the court with the added threshold/crossing consequence. A decorative cornice must not hide the outlet fasteners or prevent independent receiver removal.
