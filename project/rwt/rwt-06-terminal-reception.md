# RWT-06 — terminal receiving geometry and first-harm ledger

**3 November 2026. Source-bound negative result; no adopted drainage change and no RWT-06 closeout.** The [reproducer](studies/court-terminal-reception.mjs) and [adverse tests](studies/court-terminal-reception.test.mjs) read the selected HAD-B open-loggia object (Git blob `25faad2c4d40df2b7da7212f73808843a88d2f52`) and the joined MAT terminal/threshold object (Git blob `0ec54ba2420b9337ce81b1867eef4c83d661e69b`). This answers a more basic question than chute capacity: **where does the water actually land after leaving either outfall?** The earlier [second-daylight study](rwt-06-second-daylight-exit.md) rules out treating a second north grate as another through-ring exit; it does not prove that the two drawn ground receivers catch either terminal.

## The two proposed outlets miss their own receiving areas in plan

| Source element | N/W normal copper route | E broad surface relief |
|---|---|---|
| In-house route | 250 mm clear copper, covered; straight primary | 1800 mm clear open walking-surface path |
| Outer termination | 350 mm further covered extension from y13200 to **y13550**, invert **−271.75**; then proposed **closed** copper downbody to **−600** | Surface exits at y13200/crest **−152.5**; proposed 600-wide, 100-wall chute runs **350 mm** and drops to **−600** |
| Named receiving rectangle | x4250–5750 / **y13650–15150** | x6050–7550 / **y13650–15150** |
| Farthest north outlet location justified by the source | **y13550** | **at most y13550**, *only if the full 350 mm chute run goes north* |
| Gap to first receiver plan point | **100 mm** | **at least 100 mm** |
| Authored nominal receiver water | −800 | −800 |
| Nominal outlet-to-water vertical distance | 200 mm | 200 mm |

The receiver rectangles are **300 mm apart east–west**, but no plan line from either authored discharge lip falls within its own receiver. The 200 mm nominal vertical separation is real source arithmetic; it is **not an air-gap/capture pass**. The relief run has no specified bearing, so crediting its *maximum possible* northward extension is already a generous geometric interpretation. Without an engineered outward throw, extended outlet or south-shifted receiver, a vertical jet would arrive outside the named footprints. For a zero-downward-velocity, still-air point departing an exactly 200 mm vertical drop, falling takes about **0.202 s**; moving 100 mm north in that idealisation requires at least **0.495 m/s** horizontal launch. No actual exit direction, nozzle, velocity profile, splash, spray, wind, debris, outlet-to-receiver connection or independent ground support is supplied. That arithmetic is **not** a jet design rule.

This gap may look small relative to a 1500 mm receiving bed, but it is a category error: a rectangle *near* an outlet is not the inlet actually receiving it. Positive flow continuity and harmless first impact must be drawn through the named exit and accepted head, not assumed from the words “independent receiver”.

## A deliberate, costed architectural rework rather than a fake pass

A **300 mm internal landing overlap** is an *authored comparison target*, not a selected splash buffer or performance requirement. To place each lip that far inside its existing rectangular receiver, the best-case present model needs **400 mm additional northern outlet reach** (to y13950), or the matching receiver's south edge moved **400 mm south** (to y13250). The latter leaves **50 mm** between its front edge and the authored guard/exterior-deck control at y13200, without any real supports, passage, guard opening or person/maintenance separation. An extended copper spout or chute would instead add external reach, wind/impact/support, thermal movement, maintenance, first-shedding and protection requirements. A different bounded receiving-body geometry could be better. **Neither change is selected.** Replacing 300 with a guessed larger factor is not evidence; obtain the actual hydraulic jet/splash envelope and constructible supported collector first.

The two nominal 1500 × 1500 receiver territories and their equal assumed −800 planes are *not evidence of independent site destinations*. A common stormwater level, outlet submergence, splash onto the deck or failure of the unsupported transition could affect both. At **−600** water level the nominal 200 mm air gap is exhausted; whether any raised water travels backwards through the normal **closed** body or across the exposed chute requires real path/joint/head design. At the source's −800 plane it remains only an unverified **200 mm separation**, not a demonstrated receiving and lawful disposal system. DPL must supply site grade, receiver and overflow elevation/rights/soil/backwater evidence; it cannot manufacture those facts pre-plot.

## Match the same event to the first vulnerable fabric

Under the *existing inward-draining* roof, combined wall-line roof plus courtyard runoff is **60.0625 m²**. The existing illustrative 216 mm/h stress sends **3.60375 L/s** into the court if inner collection fails; direct court rain alone is **0.735 L/s**. The following are **steady uniform-flow rectangular section probes only**, using MAT's 1% falls and roughness assumptions, with full combined event assigned to each surviving route separately.

| Necessary reference | Normal copper primary | Open relief |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mouth invert / walking level | −213.75 mm | −110 mm |
| Uniform depth at full 3.60375 L/s | 32.84 mm | 10.51 mm |
| Hypothetical water surface at mouth | −180.91 mm | −99.49 mm |
| Relevant local nominal clearance | **42.16 mm** below 75 mm copper side wall | **69.49 mm** below the **unqualified −30 mm pan/DPC comparison** |
| Actual receiving/operating head | **Unknown** | **Unknown** |

These positive ideal gaps are not containment, freeboard or safety margins. The normal path's covered U joints and open-to-closed terminal have untested seals and failure leakage; the relief's actual inlet capture, mouth contraction/debris, hydraulic jump, terminal, backwater and wall-base wetting remain unqualified. A site-level rise can invalidate either ideal uniform comparison. **The pan/DPC join at −30 is not a proven first-leak elevation or acceptable wetting stage.** At a continuous 80 mm mouth dam starting from −110, the resulting −30 crest already reaches its level *before* energy to pass flow over it. The separate +15 door crest cannot protect that lower joint.

The relief's authored **447.5 mm drop over 350 mm plan run** has a geometric drop/run ratio of **1.279**. It is a short steep cascade, not a continuation of the 1% gently sloping walking route. Do **not** apply an ordinary shallow, steady, gradually varied 1D open-channel profile across that steep drop. [USACE HEC-RAS limitations](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/ras1dtechref/6.4/theoretical-basis-for-one-dimensional-and-two-dimensional-hydrodynamic-calculations/1d-steady-flow-water-surface-profiles/1d-steady-flow-program-limitations) require a different hydraulic treatment at rapidly varied controls and caution beyond slopes of 1:10. A real drop, collector, free-fall/impact surface and first harmful wetting envelope must be demonstrated together.

## Design disposition and handoff

**Keep the architectural north loggia as the sole selected through-ring passage; do not credit either candidate terminal as a positively captured exit.** The next RWT construction of a *single joined normal/relief receiving section* must, at minimum, locate each fully supported and maintainable lip, its downstream receiver mouth and harmless exceedance route, full-event inlet/turn/cascade/jet/capture behavior, actual first susceptible wetting, and the normal/relief/common backwater levels. The high-level roof/court receiver renewal task must not be bypassed by this easier low-level calculation. Do not erase the full-body temporary rain route or an actual site interface with a favourable source rectangle.

The first design choice is **extend supported copper terminals or move/rebuild the external receivers** with a verified impact/overflow territory and access route. If neither fits without occupying protected exits, footing, a real plot boundary or vulnerable fabric, route an actual HAD/STR/RWT architecture change rather than hiding the gap in a future installer detail. Even a corrected terminal will not cure a complete blanket across the *shared north mouth*: the [separate-passage rejection](rwt-06-second-daylight-exit.md) remains live.

This is a durable **negative topology and level control** only. It neither selects a roof, receiver nor material exception, and it cannot unblock RWT-07. The canonical RWT-06 lifecycle remains `planned`; full receiving/head, failure safety, physical tests and site acceptance are unearned.
