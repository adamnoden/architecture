# Copper assemblies: renewal modules, divided gutters and genuine brass

**30 October 2026 — bounded RWT-METALS development, not adopted construction.** Starting main `886c9a3fe85e0edaf33701872c076b757cd72c4c`, after the material-policy integration. The [whole-house material policy](../../docs/reference-house/material-policy.md) governs; the [rainwater rebrief](rainwater-elegance-brief.md) owns scope. The [shared roof/water compatibility record](../../docs/research/roof-rainwater-material-compatibility.md) remains the single compatibility authority.

## Decisions earned

Develop **fabricated, naturally finished copper collector modules** ahead of a gasketed or silicone-sealed copper kit. This is not approval of every solder, flux or mounting accessory. Some all-metal movement joints divide the gutter hydraulically: a pipe beyond that division cannot back up the blocked compartment. Give each actual compartment its own assessed escape path, or redesign the joint/collector. Do not let removal of an elastomer silently remove hydraulic continuity.

For a genuinely brass ground channel, investigate a **specified low-zinc copper-zinc alloy**, not generic yellow brass or a brass-coloured cover. For a concealed carrier, distinguish pressure-capable copper tube and matched joints from thin rainwater downpipe. Neither a tube catalogue nor this comparison resolves the court crossing.

## C-G1 — exposed copper collector module

The development assembly has a formed copper trough with folded/stiffened edges, soldered end plates and a separately fabricated outlet. A removable metal cap covers each required expansion division. Investigate a roof apron that is independently secured, so gutter renewal need not pull its fasteners from permanent fabric. Compatible metal cradles carry the trough through an accessible replaceable bracket interface; the gutter wall is not peppered with new mounting holes during every renewal. The bracket's permanent anchorage and its load path still require design.

**An adverse exposure limit is retained:** CM1 detail 10.2A rejects its two-piece gutter/apron arrangement for severe snow/ice or high winds; its 10.2B integral arrangement is a different comparator. Therefore the separable apron is conditional, not a prescription for every elevation. If the actual exposure defeats the separable interface, compare a larger integrated renewable roof-edge unit or another verified detail and acknowledge the wider repair extent. Do not waive weather resistance to protect the desired disassembly boundary.

Locate a defined fixed point and sliding seats **before** choosing joint gaps. Do not rigidly restrain both ends then call a solder seam an expansion joint. Any anti-wear pad, isolation layer, coating or fixing plug must be disclosed under MAT; no invisible rubber liner or plastic wall plug is presumed. Unlined metal-to-metal sliding is a candidate needing wear, restraint and noise assessment, not permission to delete a necessary separator.

Provide an independently accessible primary outlet and a separately routed relief from the same water compartment. Investigate a front-side spill lip or weir whose operating level stays below the lowest vulnerable rear seam/apron/interface. Its full flow, splash, destination and pedestrian consequences must be calculated; “spill outward” is not by itself safe. Where outward discharge is harmful, use a separately supported metal relief route, not an unassessed waterfall. Place neither relief inlet nor its destination behind the same debris guard or common blocked gully as the primary.

**Renewal task:** manage incoming rain independently; access and support the actual module; disconnect the exposed lower transition; release the cap and bracket retainers; withdraw the trough without disturbing a retained independent apron or adjoining module, where that separation has been admitted; reinstate and test. A soldered module is not an endlessly demountable joint: repair may consume a local copper seam. State that bounded repair length rather than promise zero material loss. Actual module mass, lifting path, bracket pitch and high-level access remain open.

**Disposition:** this is the preferred *development architecture*, not a selected profile, sheet thickness, solder/flux product or proven dry-jointed system. Off-the-shelf rubber/silicone systems remain technical comparators only; no polymer exception is granted.

## C-G2 — the expansion/backup conflict, checked explicitly

The [A3 mechanism sheet](studies/copper-compartments.svg), [source](studies/copper-compartments.mjs), [tests](studies/copper-compartments.test.mjs) and [values](studies/copper-compartments.json) examine two **authored 3000 mm compartments**, separated by a **12 mm installation gap**. This is a 6012 mm fixture, not an adopted house eave or new cornice dimension. Each outer end is fixed; adjacent ends slide toward the joint when heated. A cap attached to A must remain free at B.

Using CM1's coefficient, converted to **17.64 × 10^-6/K**, and an explicitly assumed metal-temperature range **−10 to +70°C**, installed at **20°C**, combined hot closure is **5.292 mm** and cold opening **3.1752 mm**. A **2 mm total adverse positioning allowance** leaves **4.708 mm** hot gap and gives **17.1752 mm** maximum cold gap. These temperatures and allowance are not climate evidence or allowed construction tolerances. The calculation proves neither cap overlap nor weather resistance. Reducing the installed gap to 7 mm fails even this limited contact screen. Other restraint arrangements require their own calculation.

The retained route checks demonstrate a necessary condition, not hydraulics: with P-A blocked, P-B on the far side of a solid divider is unreachable. Adding A's own relief restores a path in the graph; sharing the primary's blocked receiver defeats it again. Both compartments are checked with either local outlet unavailable. The graph contains no outlet capacity, crest height or real receiving level; it deliberately cannot label a reachable path safe. RWT-ROBUSTNESS must supply those physical results. Do not add four downpipes merely to satisfy this drawing: a safe local weir or fewer justified compartments may be simpler.

## C-B1 — genuine low-zinc brass receiving trough

Investigate a formed C22000/CuZn10-family trough with accessible metal-supported joints and removable debris/impact pieces. **Independent wet-section lift-out is a question to prove, not an established property:** the [outer-west construction comparison](outer-west-receiver-renewal.md) distinguishes a retained soldered wet body from separate open-transfer trays and identifies their different repair and level duties. Exact cross-standard equivalence, temper and supplied composition must be checked. A structural stone support/receiver keeps walking and hedge loads off thin sheet; neither soil nor roots bear on a solder seam. Preserve an inspectable route to an actual outlet, a replaceable wear surface where falling water/stone could abrade it, and an independent overflow. Do not seal it to the masonry with an undisclosed polymer or bury it beneath inaccessible gravel.

CM7/8 justify testing a lower-zinc alloy but do not prove its patina, corrosion rate, slip resistance, thickness or cost in this location. Demand a real wetting/cleaning sample and fabricator joint detail; avoid acidic cleaning/de-icing exposure by design, not an assumed perfect maintenance regime. A copper hydraulic trough with a solid removable brass grate is a **different** alternative, to be presented honestly rather than called a brass channel. A polymer lacquer is not the default means of preserving colour. The no-hedge drainage case remains governing.

## C-P1 — copper carrier, not a downpipe relabelled

CM5 establishes a real bare tube section and warns against borrowing small-tube solder instructions at this diameter. CM6 provides another real long-stock family; neither proves the complete hidden route. Retain separate identities for tube, sleeve/service void, supports, terminal unions, leak observation and pressure closure. Swaged rainwater joints from CM3 cannot inherit tube pressure duty or the former −50 court-water target.

First compare an accessible service route with the under-floor sleeved route. If the latter survives, develop non-plastic supports, permanent protection and independently removable end joints; preserve outward leak observation. A proposed metal union must disclose any fibre, rubber or polymer seal. A brazed fixed end may frustrate repeated withdrawal; a pressure seal cannot also serve as an unanalysed structural support. No existing 8/11-ring schedule, diameter clearance, withdrawal length or stiffness passes by substituting “copper” into the old model.

**Hold:** no matched polymer-free, pressure-qualified and repeatedly renewable court-end assembly is established here. This specific missing assembly, rather than the availability of copper tube, remains the selection barrier. No fictitious metal gasket product, supplier guarantee or universal buried-copper approval is supplied.
