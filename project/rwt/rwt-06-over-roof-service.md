# RWT-06: complete temporary over-roof challenger

**Development only, 2 November 2026.** The lead comparison is a complete, supported, removable Layher Keder Roof XL and compatible access scaffold over the whole house. This study does not select an operating method, supersede the selected roof or receiver, authorise opening the weather boundary, prove emergency egress or accept any discharge. Its attraction is to keep roof and court under one weather enclosure while the original receiver is accessible, rather than forcing a second gutter through the court-edge withdrawal sweep. It also moves the weather and water obligations to a much larger temporary installation and site footprint. The earlier one-piece and local interception failures remain valid; see [high-eave comparison](rwt-06-high-eave-service-comparison.md) and [manufacturer access comparison](rwt-06-manufacturer-access-comparison.md).

## Manufacturer evidence and honest scope

The [Layher protective systems catalogue](https://www.layher.it/cataloghi/EN_Protective_System_Catalogue.pdf) describes the XL aluminium lattice beam, 750 mm high, with integrated Keder section, standard 18° double-pitch roof, 2.57 m bay and compatibility with Allround/SpeedyScaf, and illustrates a nominal 15.4 × 12.86 m roof. The [XL instructions](https://layherna.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Keder-Roof-XL-Manual-PDF.pdf) and [manufacturer roof guide](https://www.layher.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/Layher-Keder-Roof-XL.pdf) must be read together with the selected revision, bracing and temporary-works design. A seven-bay 17.99 m extension is an **arithmetic candidate**, not an identified approved kit. Total sheet outline, structural support span and standard axes are different quantities; do not use one as the other in a load table. Ridge ties and the manufacturer's 750 mm beam depth are not a complete lower envelope. The actual anchors, foundations, stability, eaves, gables, end bracing, lateral and erection-stage restraint, tie positions, scaffold and hoist require a joined design. A manufacturer's example on another site establishes neither capacity nor available ground here.

Manufacturer calls the uninsulated roof rainproof in normal conditions and warns that condensation can drip uncontrolled. That is a **mechanism to design for**, not a demonstration that every engineered over-roof must fail. The roof's primary Keder cover is PVC ([manufacturer description](https://www.layher.co.nz/protection-roofing-weather/layher-keder-roof/)). The complete OEM sheets, rails, aluminium/steel structural components, ties, couplers, anchors, cladding, seals, drainage, platforms, guardrails, PPE and consumables must be identified by constituent and removed after service. The [whole-house material policy](../../docs/reference-house/material-policy.md) applies to installed house fabric and apparatus; naming a permanent membrane temporary cannot create an installed-polymer exception. This external removable kit is proposed as equipment, not an amendment to the installed house. No protective element may be omitted to change the material ledger; actual admission and residues/repair of fixings remain decisions for the owners.

The steel [cassette roof](https://www.layher.co.uk/steel-cassette/) is a real alternative with steel cassettes and trusses; the [manual](https://layherna.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Layher-Cassette-Roof-PDF.pdf) gives 11° geometry and warns that its uninsulated, unsealed joints can admit wind-driven rain and condensation. Manufacturer sealing, component finishes, safety equipment, crane handling, full footprint and loading must be assessed. Metal primary panels do not establish a polymer-free complete kit or an automatically dry work zone. Keder remains the lead for development; neither system is accepted.

## Finite cover and conserved water challenge

[Generated plan and figures](studies/over-roof-service.svg) and [machine-readable result](studies/over-roof-service.json) pin the actual HAD selected house source. A trial cover centered on the 12 m square runs x −1700…13700, y −2995…14995 mm: 15.4 × 17.99 m, seven nominal 2.57 m bays, before any scaffold, gutter, bracing or support extension. If an ideal straight 18° pitch ran across the entire half-width from an illustrative eave +10050, its ridge would be +12552 mm; the OEM eave is curved, so this is not the actual manufacturer ridge datum. If the entire beam and fittings lay within an assumed 750 mm vertical envelope, its +9300 underside would be about 1209 mm above the source +8090.9 house ridge; this assumption does not represent actual ties, fixings, movement or construction access and is not a clearance pass. The side/end margins are 1700/2995 mm to the house shell, not a claim of accessible scaffold or available land.

At the existing **216 mm/h stress**, horizontal roof projection 277.046 m² produces **16.62276 L/s** before allowances, storage, losses or wind. This includes the intercepted house/court and **133.046 m² of additional previously exposed ground**. The existing local court-plus-roof 60.0625 m² / 3.60375 L/s is part of the intercepted area; it must not be added a second time. Rain outside the trial cover and any bypass, splash and diverted flow remain separate site loads. The full temporary collection and discharge capacity cannot be inferred from the old local receiver, nominal court outfalls or their nominal water levels. Derive effective exposed area, both slopes and outlet/overflow allocations from the selected system, survey and actual receiving head; 16.62276 is an ideal demand sensitivity, not an actual flow prediction or sized design.

Both sloping cover faces require positively connected eaves gutters and independent, accessible downpipes to named external reception. A blocked gutter or pipe must spill to a visible, positively graded safe external overflow, before water reaches house, access, escape, scaffold bases or occupied openings; provide isolation and inspection without depending on the blocked branch. A common receiving-head failure can defeat both branches: the wet stop state must keep the **restored permanent weather/receiver path** functioning, and the temporary overflow must have an independently demonstrated safe route; otherwise defer exposed work and dismantle only in suitable weather. No existing court storage, unsurveyed underground route or exterior ground is assumed to be that route. Site design must establish a boundary encompassing the illustrated roof plus every foundation, anchor, gutter, overflow, erection exclusion, crane/outrigger, service and exit corridor, and identify its own surveyed safe receiving and escape destinations. The drawing does not assert a plot size or right of use.

## Dry-work and occupied-house states

1. **Before opening the roof:** survey and approve the entire temporary works, manufacturer details, ground and ties, loading and staging. Keep the original roof, receiver, ground routes and main final exit live. Erect the outside support scaffold with full guards and fall protection; assemble and brace roof in the engineered OEM sequence, including temporary bracing for each incomplete state. Lift/insert individual roof components using the specified access/lifting arrangements, not an improvised elevated exit. An adverse forecast or unrestrained incomplete structure prevents proceeding. Preserve the occupied front exit, worker escape and rescue during every lift; segregate the drop zone and provide a tested accessible alternative if any normal route is obstructed.
2. **Enclosed and tested, still intact:** install compatible gable/side rain screens, water collection, sealed/lapped connections and inspections; verify that their real frames, anchorage, doors, gutter falls and bracing coexist. Preserve normal front door opening and egress through an outward-operable escape gate with release accessible from the occupied side, or an independently verified alternative route and controlled occupation policy. A gate shown open in a plan is not first-motion proof. Avoid enclosure-induced flooding at doors and foundations. Inspect sheet joints, drain both eaves with an applied-water test, and separately challenge blocked branch and high/common tailwater. The result must show actual safe discharge, not just nominal pipe diameter.
3. **Dry-work permit:** before exposing either roof weather layer, demonstrate the actual protective assembly handles wind-driven side rain, localized leaks and condensate, including underside drips and end/corner splashes, with a continuous compatible secondary collection/protection arrangement over the affected roof, down to a positively drained visible collection point. A putative liner has its own cold underside, seams, fixings and overflow; treating a second sheet as automatically dry is invalid. Use manufacturer's compatible screened/clad provisions and a designed local drip catch and drainage detail where needed, including materials and support, with site measurements and inspection to verify the environmental state. Active ventilation/dehumidification or alarms can help but do not replace a passive protected stop state when power fails. **No compatible full detail is authored here: until demonstrated, opening the weather boundary is prohibited.**
4. **Receiver work:** after the dry-work permit, make and support the proposed receiver/gantry/stair system in the actual joined court plan, keeping guards, rescue access, the water paths and both receiver ends serviceable. This study does not approve the prior narrow ladder/hoist or a new apparatus fit. Isolate only a receiver whose replacement water and weather protection has been demonstrated; validate seams, support, maintenance movement and full stress/blocked paths. Close the work at a defined weather-tight pause state whenever protection, wind, power, water discharge, access or egress falls outside its accepted conditions. Protect occupants throughout; do not count a closed ordinary door as a watertight seal.
5. **Reversal:** reinstate and test the original or separately approved permanent receiver, roof layers, ventilation, drainage, guards and interfaces while the temporary cover still works. Restore an independently weather-tight and drained house before disconnecting temporary gutter routes, opening cladding or removing the roof. Remove sheets, roof, braces and supports in engineered reverse order with an intact safe exit and controlled loads. Remove all temporary constituents and reinstate any anchor penetrations and site surfaces; verify no obstruction of the permanent drainage and escape. If weather or a common outfall stops safe reversal, retain the complete stable system until an approved safe state exists.

## Remaining decision boundary

The trial establishes a checkable cover footprint and rainfall consequence and identifies physical mechanisms and ordered stops. It does **not** yet furnish a complete compatible condensate/side-rain assembly, joined scaffold/hoist, occupied-house gate, support, weather-stage or water-failure design. In particular neither the candidate plan nor an ideal dry local gap proves any of those. The next work must obtain actual OEM sections, bay/end/support and drainage provisions, then author their finite geometry and load/sequence against the maintained house, access and selected site interfaces. Reject or revise any explicit collision; do not relabel an unproved test as a pass.

## Eaves collection: a first failure-sensitive reservation

For this same trial only, reserve a 300 mm wide external trough at each eave: west x −1900…−1600 and east x 13600…13900, both running y −2995…14995 mm. Their open top is assumed +9950; centre invert at y6000 is +9800, falling 1:200 toward both ends to +9755.025. Place four nominal 100 mm internal-diameter outlets at (x,y) = (−1750,−2800), (−1750,14800), (13750,−2800), (13750,14800). These coordinates and levels are a design reservation, **not** published XL gutter hardware, a supported assembly, a surveyed site, or an actual termination. Roof edge and trough overlap in plan by 100 mm, but wind, splash, sag, clips, gable closures and the curved OEM eave can defeat interception; no catch performance is claimed.

For ideal uniform roof rainfall, each entire slope contributes 8.31138 L/s at the stress. With one end outlet blocked and the other receiving the whole slope, water must rise at least **44.975 mm above the lower end invert** simply to reach the central high point; losses and the head needed to carry flow are additional. At +9950 top, the static centre depth reservation is 150 mm, but that is **not** proven freeboard. In a completely full ideal 100 mm bore the remaining branch's average velocity would be approximately 1.06 m/s; this is an arithmetic continuity check, not capacity: entrance, air, turns, screens, slope, blockage, overflow, structural deflection and tailwater are not modelled. Provide overflow before the lowest harm level and verify all water safely reaches independent approved external ground. If both outlets or their common receiving route fail, neither branch may be credited. Actual downpipe geometry, accessible isolation, collection from cladding and condensate, scaffold fit, egress, soil, outfall and connection remain unadmitted. The calculation is intended to expose a possible full-side failure demand early, not to make the nominal sizes a specification.

## Source-based roof support axis trial

The XL manual's double-pitch, no-tie table associates roof width **B = 15.40 m** with structural span **L = 13.40 m**, with roof supports and conditions stated in that table. The figure distinguishes this span from the frame axis widths: inner width L − a and outer width L + a, with a = 0.73 or 1.09 m. This is a geometric source, not evidence that its tabulated loading covers the site. Under a centered 0.73 m frame trial, the west longitudinal standard axes are x −1065 and −335, the east axes x 12335 and 13065; eight transverse axes run y −2995 + 2570n, n = 0…7. The generated plan shows these **axes only**, not fabricated full frames, braces, plates, ties or bearing. With an ideal 205 mm diameter nut-wing projection aligned toward the wall, the nearest projected plan gap to the shell is 335 − 102.5 = **232.5 mm**. The wider 1.09 m frame instead leaves only 155 − 102.5 = **52.5 mm**. These do not establish a foundation, usable assembly workspace, completed roof-support connection, or clearance of a sole board, bracket, brace, gutter, wall detail or anchor. Actual foot, spindle, nut rotation, board extent, load distribution, wall openings and weather damage all need joined elevation and ground checks.

The western trial gutter's closest inside edge x −1600 is 535 mm from the outer standard axis x −1065 (432.5 mm from the same 205 mm wing projection). Its downpipes are separately positioned outside those axes, but their fittings, workers, brackets and overflow remain untested. The source's standard roof support is a 0.73 m swivelling unit (ref. 5975.073), not a license to omit its connections, ties, braces or the selected manufacturer's erection requirements. The manufacturer's published 15.4 × 12.86 m five-bay material example weighs 1,942.3 kg **without supporting scaffold**; do not extrapolate that as the seven-bay assembled mass or anchor force. The source's calculated truss table excludes a site-specific ground/support design and the additional weight and wind area of the candidate's gutters, side cladding, condensate provisions and access. A structural engineer must establish the actual complete load path and all partial-assembly states; if needed support, tie or board geometry occupies the shell, openings, ground drains or assumed egress, revise or reject the arrangement.
