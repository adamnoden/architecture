# Passive court escape: receiving levels and the building section

**30 October 2026 · bounded RWT-06 comparison · RWT-06 remains planned.** Starting main: `68d0273f928681aed7222bba2927dc8a89ae5ead`. This result tests the historical north crossing against the later [passive-dryness policy](../../docs/reference-house/ground-water-policy.md). It selects no house datum, carrier, groundwater limit, flood allowance or actual site.

## Decision

Make the receiving-water relationship an early selection test. Compare a daylighted receiver and raised building section with the existing sunken-terminal proposition before committing to more concealed crossing detail. A daylighted receiver has an exposed, inspectable gravity continuation; excavation alone does not create that continuation or lower the receiving water. Useful pre-site comparison can proceed with explicit conditional limits; an actual destination is required for site acceptance, not invented here to permit the study.

This is a design priority, not adoption. Raising the building may impose approach, garden, height and structural costs. The useful question is whether that section removes inaccessible wet boundaries and difficult renewal duties while retaining the owner's inhabited plan. Neither a deeper pipe nor extra outlet labels can compensate for an unsuitable receiving condition.

## Necessary receiving condition and three counterexamples

Let `F` be absolute house FFL and `R` the maximum receiving-water elevation for a named event and failure case, both on one datum. Retaining the historical comparison requires:

`R ≤ F − 585 mm`, equivalently `F − R ≥ 585 mm`.

This preserves an authored interface condition; it does not establish hydraulic safety. In a **synthetic at-grade receiving-water case**, set `R` equal to the old schematic ground, `F₀ − 150 mm`. The shortfall is `585 − 150 = 435 mm`. Raising the whole building by 435 mm relative to that **fixed external receiving event**, while retaining its internal relative geometry, would only meet the historical target. It would earn no additional allowance or overall pass.

Three sensitivities prevent misleading fixes:

- If the receiving event rises a further `p` mm above that fixed ground, the required relative increase becomes `435 + p` mm.
- If building, surrounding site and receiving-water level all rise together, `F − R` is unchanged: there is **no gain**.
- If the whole historical carrier/sleeve arrangement is lowered 100 mm while its nominal terminal separations are retained, its receiver target becomes −685 mm. Against the same fixed event, the shortfall increases from 435 to **535 mm**. Deepening worsens this screen.

These are transparent subtractions, not selected construction heights. The original trial is **rejected for the synthetic at-grade receiving event**; a 435 mm relative raise is **not ruled out by this level comparison alone**; actual site applicability is **unknown**. Actual groundwater, flood levels, lawful discharge and approach geometry remain unknown.

An outlet submerged by receiving water can still discharge under sufficient upstream head. Therefore failing the −585 condition does **not** prove gravity flow impossible. It invalidates this particular receiving arrangement; any alternative must calculate tailwater and losses, then demonstrate acceptable court water levels. Allowing greater court head can simply move failure to a lower sill drain or concealed joint.

## Five connected feasibility ledgers

| Duty | Inputs and necessary demonstration | Present disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Primary conveyance | Actual inlet, carrier bore/invert, losses, flow allocation and receiving-water envelope; name normal and obstructed conditions | Unselected assembly and hydraulic result; historical slope or outside diameter proves neither |
| Independent relief | Activation crest, head needed to convey the assigned failure flow, complete downstream path and receiving condition with the primary unavailable | No shared blocked throat or flooded destination credited as independence; −75 activation and −50 operating targets remain uncomputed |
| Annular leak escape | Actual lowest sleeve/terminal outlet, retained water boundary, receiving backwater, blockage and visible inspection | Historical −555 and nominal 30 mm do not admit a product; a sealed invisible reservoir is rejected |
| Usable service access | Actual working-space floor, entry, susceptible materials, groundwater and surface/flood exposure during operation and intervention | No claim that a sunken terminal bay stays dry; raising the section requires its own complete access comparison |
| Court and threshold protection | Each susceptible joint's actual elevation, local water exposure, splash/transients and justified construction/movement allowance | The [HAD first-failure register](../had/had-03-court-threshold-first-failure.md) remains controlling; −45 underside is not an established first-failure level |

For each susceptible interface, require the assessed local water exposure plus its justified allowance to remain below that interface's demonstrated failure boundary. A nominal relief crest is insufficient: operating head and downstream backwater matter. No allowance is supplied here. Pan depth, paving level and the height of an upstand label do not identify the lowest vulnerable joint.

The enclosed court has no assumed harmless route across occupied rooms. Doors, open or closed, are not emergency weirs. Raised paving does not remove direct rainfall; removing roof inflow would not remove that duty either. Preserve the [combined roof-spill/direct-rain challenge](../bcr/bcr-water-result.md) until an alternative topology earns its own failure result.

## Groundwater is a separate exposure

Record groundwater, including relevant seasonal/perched conditions, separately from receiving flood/backwater. They may interact, but a favourable outfall does not establish a dry service bay. Conversely, wet ground around a durable buried component does not automatically prohibit it: the owner policy permits appropriately designed ground-contact construction.

The rejection condition is dependency on active dewatering or inaccessible groundwater tanking to keep accommodation or essential service space usable. Do not invent a universal groundwater ceiling at the sleeve's −565 outside bottom. If a component or void is intended to tolerate wetting, demonstrate adjacent-fabric protection, leakage observation and credible intervention without silently restoring the forbidden dependency.
