# RWT-06 — stop detailing the losing crossing; reserve courtyard egress in the architecture

**1 November 2026 — owner-directed engineering direction after twenty RWT-06 lease generations.** This is a source-backed **negative feasibility decision and redesign order**, not a new drain proposal, source-geometry change, hydraulic certification, selected site datum or RWT-06 lifecycle closeout. The accepted [12 × 12 m courtyard house](../../docs/reference-house/current-house-basis.md), [RWT-06 current trial](rwt-06-access-court-transition.md), [historical north crossing](north-court-carrier.md), [receiving-level screen](passive-court-site-interface.md), [raised terminal](raised-terminal-section.md), [roomward replacement alternative](threshold-renewal-access.md), [HAD court-first-failure study](../had/had-03-court-threshold-first-failure.md) and [rainwater failure attack](../../docs/research/rainwater-failure-and-exceedance.md) are the record.

## The missing thing is not a product

The current court is **enclosed by an occupied, load-bearing house ring**. Its floor receives direct rain, even if every inward roof drop is diverted elsewhere. With the present inward roof it may also receive failed-gutter and valley spill. A court outlet that appears in plan does not explain where its water goes **through the building ring**, nor how it remains accessible after a joint fails or its lower discharge is blocked.

Every feasible scheme has to supply one or more **physical and level-coordinated crossings of the structural/thermal/moisture boundary**. Calling an inaccessible buried tube “service-zone infrastructure” is no solution. Nor is a second court grate or pipe full redundancy if both feed the same blocked lower throat, backwater level or concealed receiver. A high-level roof bypass does not remove direct rainfall from the open court. Where a continuous low route can be proved only by sinking a service well below the surrounding groundwater/flood regime, the owner policy forbids making the house depend on a pump or tanked accommodation to maintain that well.

**Decision: stop treating the old narrow/deep north-axis carrier as the baseline awaiting a better fitting.** It is a **rejected reference configuration for pre-site selection**, retained as a falsification comparator. Product-matching and ever-deeper carrier iterations are not a reason to defer the architectural decision. A new study must begin with the **exterior receiving level, first susceptible court/wall/threshold boundary, gravity escape topology and an accessible structural passage**, then fit a product inside that earned envelope. The actual alternative remains unselected.

This does **not** prove that gravity drainage of a courtyard is impossible. It says that *this particular proposed crossing and its emergency disposition have not passed*, and its remaining unknowns cannot be made harmless by repeated catalogue search.

## Why the existing nominal numbers defeat a shortcut

These are explicitly historical authored dimensions on separate trial sections, not an installed coordinate stack.

| Evidenced check | What it rules out; what remains unknown |
|---|---|
| Raised court walking datum `z=0`, proposed waterproofing `z=-180` at perimeter / `-215` low | Water can be below the walking surface while already challenging a concealed wall joint. **Walking dryness is not fabric dryness.** |
| Nominal relief activation `z=-75`, nominal operating-water target `z=-50`, hypothetical sill-support underside `z=-45` | The nominal vertical separation is only **5 mm** before splash, moving parts, joint position, construction tolerance and flowing head. `-45` has **not** been established as the actual first vulnerable interface. No safety margin is earned. |
| Historical carrier outside bottom `z=-535`, sleeve outside bottom `z=-565`, receiving comparator `z=-585` | The named comparator is not a selected drainage invert, terminal or flood datum. Against a **synthetic** receiving event at old external `z=-150`, matching `-585` requires a **435 mm relative change** before added margin. Raising both building and receiving level together gains nothing. |
| Raised-terminal trial: 220 mm flange interferes with fixed ground; two 220 mm gross envelopes on 400 mm centres need 620 mm in a 650 mm passage | Leaves only 15 mm nominally at either side. The older 35 mm allowances imply 690 mm **before sleeve walls and actual removal travel**. A 220 mm intact flange does not pass a 160 mm bore. Raising a nominal FFL cannot cure transverse or upper structural clashes. |
| Current roof + open court projected 60.0625 m² inward catchment; illustrative 144 mm/h uniform event | If combined discharge is blocked and no water escapes, source is **2.4025 L/s** and **1,441.5 L in ten minutes**. Those are mass-balance input sensitivities, not a guaranteed ponding depth or a safe response window. |

A receiving body may discharge when submerged if sufficient upstream head exists; the historical `-585` screen is therefore *not* a proof of no gravity flow at higher receiving water. But the alternative cannot silently increase court head past the first susceptible interface to recover discharge. A level inequality must include **flowing** head, losses, outfall submergence, movement and a justified reserve. None is earned for the current trial.

## Reframe the design as two separate egress obligations

**Normal court-water duty — N.** A continuous, accessible gravity path from actual court collector/inlet through the house ring to a named `SITE-WATER-HANDOFF`. Its topological, geometric and maintenance interfaces must be explicit before choosing a bore. Trace the unselected inlet, branch, actual passage through masonry/floor, separate support, intentional movement, outlet, inspection/cleaning reach, first dismantling, whole-unit withdrawal, reinstatement and returning water to service. Preserve house structure, dry wall/cavity base, gas/thermal/fire closure and replaceable floor surfaces. A drawing showing a carrier centreline but hiding its joints in an inaccessible beam is an **N failure**.

**Degraded/emergency court duty — E.** A physically independent passive path from the **same court accumulation territory** to a **harmless external destination**, with the nominated N inlet, section, throat or site receiving point blocked or surcharged. It must start conducting at a **computed operating water level below the actual lowest susceptible court/threshold/wall interface**, accounting for flowing head and margin; a painted weir crest is not the operating level. It may need an architectural daylight opening through the ring, but no room or doorway is admitted as a flood channel merely because it connects to the garden. If its outlet shares the flooded N receiver, the pair fails the named common-mode event. The house must be safe in a bare, maintenance-lapsed, power-off condition; no sensor, emergency occupant action, hedge, buried infiltration next to foundations or pump receives essential credit.

The two paths may converge **only after** the failure and high-water scenarios being claimed have been examined. Whether this can be accomplished on a selected London plot is an explicit constraint, not a guessed answer.

## Ranked next *architectural* trials, not more nominal pipe catalogues

1. **One designed, accessible gravity service passage** between court and suitable exterior, coordinated with STR's continuous masonry/arch/pile-grid concept and IAS's maintainable replaceable floor. Compare a permanently supported formed metal carrier under fully removable independent access/cover construction and a controlled thermal/gas/moisture boundary; no embedded disposable service, no rigid pipe cast into a moving foundation and no unidentified ring seal. Locate its actual internal/external termination, access and *outward* renewal route. Design the passage first; avoid selecting a pipe to justify an impossible opening. This is the **N candidate** only.
2. **A separately daylighted passive E route** that can operate when the N passage is blocked, without crossing occupied doors or an inaccessible below-grade well. Explore a dedicated structural portal, rearranged court/landscape geometry and relative court-to-exterior level. Its aperture, loss/head, setback, wall-base and exterior consequence are design questions. If a separate E is geometrically impossible through the intact ring, **architectural redesign is mandatory**. A roof high-level transfer reduces load but is not a court emergency outlet.
3. **Raise/recompose the court, building and external receiving landscape as a coupled section** only if the *actual surveyed* receiving-event elevation remains relatively lower and there is a complete step-free entry, planted edge, door, foundation-heave and services strategy. The old 435 mm sensitivity is a failure boundary, **not** an adopted raise or a target to engineer toward.
4. **Reconsider whether the ring must remain fully enclosed at ground level.** A designed open-ground escape through a genuinely open passage/arch, or another explicit architecture, is a legitimate alternative to making underground rainwater machinery increasingly complex. This would alter HAD/owner-approved composition and therefore needs an overt design decision, not a hidden pipe amendment.

Study A and E as **two coupled failure states**: a design that looks wonderful for ordinary light rain while sending its worst overflow across permanent fabric cannot proceed as a selectable house option. Had an authorised site existed, site datum and permissible outfall would immediately constrain these trials; no plot is presently supplied.

## One explicit site-water handoff; no implied sewer

The **building-side interface record** for each candidate must say:

| Field | Required fact / uncertainty |
|---|---|
| Physical exit | Wall face, plan location, level datum, actual opening, carrier and terminal withdrawal envelope, structural support and whether the boundary is house-fixed or soil-fixed |
| Normal discharge | Inlet/exit invert, hydraulic grade at nominated events, final flow, removable-unit split and receiving-system dependence |
| Emergency escape | Origin/crest, discharge head at full-duty flow, first external wetted area, common-cause closure/flooding, safe splash/wetting and adjacent boundary consequences |
| Material and repair | Installed constituents including concealed joints/coatings/spacers, first release, rodding reach, retained-water control and replacement without consuming permanent structure |
| Site query | Maximum receiving level per event and failure, independent outfall(s), lawful access/discharge, groundwater/perched conditions, movement/root/construction corridor and known utility constraints |
| Status | `measured`, `source-defined`, `authored trial`, `unknown`, `rejected`; **no unknown may be quietly recast as an accepted site fact** |

It is valid now to publish the **data schema** and a rejected, source-bounded historic trial. It is *not* valid to fill in real sewage/storm drains, roadside invert, groundwater or landscape width with assumptions. DPL owns actual site queries; STR/HAD/OAS/IAS own redesigned space and structure; MAT owns complete non-plastic constituents; RWT owns the physical water paths and failed state; RWT-ROBUSTNESS compares compound failures, and RWT-07 rejects absent systems rather than saying “later engineer”.

## Execution disposition

**RWT-06 remains `planned`**. This result does not fabricate the actual N/E route, installed joints, rodding and replacement operations, dry working section, floor/threshold/sill product, external level or a safe hydraulic escape. Therefore it neither satisfies the packet's full exit nor clears RWT-07.

What it **does** resolve is the programme-level *choice of next question*: abandon the repeated historical carrier's product search as the default research loop. The next useful action is a **coupled architectural section** with a new N service passage and independent E gravity escape, and direct rejection of any unchanged plan lacking them. If no such section survives the owner’s requirements, formally ask HAD to rewind the house rather than embed a pump or concealed failure zone to preserve a neat diagram.

This is an explicit project design constraint, not an extra workstream or another editable task graph. It protects the correctness of the later TRIAD/ROF-RWT-GATE and the owner’s no-service-in-permanent-fabric, passive-dryness principles.
