# Threshold carrier renewal: move towards the room

**Result: develop inward withdrawal of a short threshold carrier before enlarging its blind passage.** The end that leads into the opened room and the end that must pass through the threshold impose different constraints. An enlargement on the leading end need never enter the passage; an enlargement on the trailing end does. This changes the next assembly trial, but does not select a carrier, joint, floor, water boundary or maintenance method. RWT-06 remains planned.

The [dimensioned comparison](studies/threshold-renewal-access.svg) and [calculation](studies/threshold-renewal-access.json) extend the [segmented route](segmented-copper-access.md). They do not replace its unresolved floor depths, the [raised-terminal constraints](raised-terminal-section.md), or RWT-METALS' component and constituent work.

## 1. Which piece moves, and where?

The current north-axis comparison crosses a 417.5 mm court threshold/support band, 3415 mm of family-room territory and a 417.5 mm garden threshold/support band. Those are plan reservations, not removable pipe lengths. The [historical north carrier](north-court-carrier.md) supplies the unadopted 160 mm bore, 60 mm fall over 4250 mm, tube/bore axis offset and 100 mm end outstands used below. None selects a plastic sleeve, an installed copper section or a working allowance.

For this maintenance comparison, remove the relevant adjoining room module and open the necessary room-floor territory. Pull the court crossing northwards into the room and the garden crossing southwards into it, along each retained sloping axis. The short piece is supported independently; the threshold and doorset remain supported throughout.

| Arrangement during withdrawal | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Intact 220 mm enlargement on the trailing end | It must traverse the 160 mm bore and fails that passage comparison. Reversing the label on the drawing cannot remove the obstruction. |
| Intact enlargement wholly outside the band on the leading, room-side end | It moves away from the band. Its complete sweep, joint release, room receiving space and later lift still need demonstration. |
| Both enlarged end assemblies removed; measured residual fits the passage | A straight inward translation becomes a legitimate comparison. The numerical witness below uses only an explicitly bare 108 mm tube. |
| Residual collar, fixing or seal envelope unknown | Passage remains unassessed. Neither removing a backing ring nor cutting nearby automatically leaves a bare 108 mm envelope. |

The leading-enlargement case is a directional alternative, not the numerical bare-tube witness. No catalogue flange thickness is promoted to a complete installed end projection. A fixed outlet or water-barrier body must not be treated as a freely removable trailing fitting.

## 2. A finite body, not merely an endpoint

Let the band length be `B`, the axis-end outstand away from the room be `p`, and the outstand into the room be `q`. For a square-cut circular tube at angle `theta = atan(60/4250)`, its end plane contributes longitudinal body projection `e = radius × sin(theta)`. Then:

- Complete projected body length: `L = B + p + q + 2e`.
- Translation to first complete-body exit from the band: `u = B + p + e`.
- With an actual positive post-exit gap `g`, the receiving interval consumes `L + g` of the cleared room length. The required `g` is not known here.

For the historical 100 mm outstands transplanted solely as a sensitivity, the bare 108 mm tube has an axis-end span of 617.5 mm, but a complete projected body length of approximately **619.0 mm**. First complete-body exit requires approximately **518.3 mm** of longitudinal travel. The simpler 517.5 mm axis-tip calculation still leaves approximately 0.76 mm of the inclined end plane across the band. These are mathematical consequences of the declared ideal tube, not measured installation tolerances.

The generator also retains a finite demonstration: translate by one complete projected body length, then lift vertically only after the whole body has cleared the band. The court piece moves down approximately 8.7 mm during that axial translation; the garden piece moves up by the same amount. Both projected receiving intervals lie inside the 3415 mm room band in this deliberately cleared model. The chosen stroke and lift endpoint are arbitrary geometric witnesses, not prescribed working clearances, handling heights or an occupied-room access pass.

The model preserves the historical tube/bore offset instead of reporting the centred diametral difference as available clearance. Supports, seals, deformation, debris, a real sleeve end and handling equipment still require their own envelopes. A positive bore result establishes only this ideal passage comparison.

This removes one supposed necessity: an intact enlarged terminal need not be dragged through a band merely because it remains attached to the end pulled into the room. It does **not** establish a shorter garden working area or permission to interrupt the structural floor. The connected floor build-up and its opening, furniture relocation, traffic closure, handling and set-down remain design duties.

## 3. Cutting access and reinstatement access are separate

Primary manufacturer evidence recovered on 31 October 2026 supplies a real cutting comparator. [RIDGID model 154, catalogue 31652](https://www.ridgid.eu/gb/en/quick-acting-tubing-cutters), lists 48–116 mm capacity and the E-2558 wheel. The [official 151–156 operating sheet](https://cdn2.ridgid.com/resources/media?key=710f7e4f-6d75-47de-978f-88374e012058&languageCode=en&type=document), dated 10/96, identifies that wheel for copper and describes full revolutions around the tube. This supports a 108 mm cold-cutting comparison; it supplies no verified full tool-and-hand orbit or chamber dimension. It is not the 154-P plastic-wheel model.

The [Endex brochure](https://aalberts-ips.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Endex-09969088_56035915_Brochure_2023.pdf), printed pp.56–57, requires square cutting, internal/external deburring, clean mating surfaces and sound tube shape. Its brazing procedure heats the joint on all sides to 700–750°C, then specifies undisturbed cooling, cleaning and flushing, and recommends system testing. Its permitted fine-toothed hacksaw alternative needs a different demonstrated stroke and preparation space; rotary clearance is not a universal requirement for every cutting method. The retained 108 mm Endbraze comparison remains brazing-only. An exact replacement joint, consumables and their material-policy admission are still missing.

Consequently, reaching a cut plane from above does not establish enough access to prepare, insert, heat and inspect a new joint beside the retained water barrier. Prefabricating the removable module elsewhere can move some joining work, but cannot silently eliminate its final connections. No generic safe heat setback, reusable stub length or number of future renewals is assigned. Sacrificing a replaceable service piece differs from repeatedly shortening a fixed penetration or damaging permanent fabric.

## 4. Keep the water boundary and support intact

The [Wade roof-outlet installation guide](https://wadedrainage.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/wade/downloads/installation-guides/wade-ig-roof-outlets.pdf) provides a useful assembly distinction: a securely fixed body, a watertight spigot connection, accessible pipe connection and separately clamped waterproofing. It is evidence for different connection duties, not selection of Wade, its membrane or sealant for this house. The retrieved source text supports those duties; no dimensioned product section is inferred from it.

The architectural inference is conditional: W1 service work must be possible without moving, twisting, cutting or thermally damaging the retained W2 penetration and adjacent threshold support. If the proposed removable crossing is itself the fixed W2 penetration, this sequence is inapplicable until the actual boundary and its renewal route are redesigned. Relabelling an inaccessible service part as permanent fabric does not meet the brief. The fixed penetration also needs its own eventual replacement strategy.

| Maintenance state | Evidence needed before proceeding |
|---|---|
| Establish the work state | Independently control incoming rainwater and the receiving-water condition; close the affected occupied route. A favourable forecast or an unproved second pipe is insufficient. |
| Open and support | Expose the specified joints, cut planes and receiving floor territory; support the retained connections and removable part without borrowing the doorset support. |
| Release the ends | Demonstrate actual fastener/seal disengagement or the chosen cutting operation, including preparation access and the measured residual envelope. |
| Translate and lift | Follow the complete finite-body path, with real tools, temporary supports and adjacent pipework included. Clear the band before the later lift. |
| Reinstate | Show the replacement insertion and joining sequence, usable retained tails and material constituents; protect W2 and permanent construction from loads and heat. |
| Return to service | Inspect and test renewed W1 and retained W2 separately, then reinstate the complete floor and occupied route. Test conditions remain assembly-specific. |

Keeping W2's seal intact is not the same as safely containing water while W1 is open. The [court threshold first-failure register](../had/had-03-court-threshold-first-failure.md), [passive site interface](passive-court-site-interface.md) and actual lowest susceptible construction still control that consequence. No low pumped service well, extra groundwater tanking, safe ponding depth or receiving-water level is introduced here.

## 5. Decision and verification

**Next trial:** locate the actual two joint/release zones and a threshold-only carrier that can withdraw towards the room, while its independent water-boundary and support assembly remains in place. Compare detachable trailing-joint release with accessible cut-and-renew. Reject either if its complete release, reinstatement or retained-boundary duty cannot be demonstrated; do not enlarge the blind passage merely to preserve the original pulling direction.
