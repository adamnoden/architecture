# Copper terminal: right-angle release and an access-first route

**30 October 2026 · bounded RWT-METALS continuation · not for construction.** Baseline main `90cbc5fe9526d2c30712b90394738afa06b5c009`. This develops the [terminal first-release finding](copper-terminal-renewal.md), under the [material policy](../../docs/reference-house/material-policy.md), [passive-dryness requirement](../../docs/reference-house/ground-water-policy.md) and [rainwater brief](rainwater-elegance-brief.md). It selects a next design mechanism, not a house route, component schedule or hydraulic size.

## Decision

**Develop a separately supported, removable right-angle terminal at an already-needed change of direction, and compare it first in an accessible service route. Do not enlarge the old blind sleeves merely to accommodate a flange.** Its two non-interlocking face joints need not oppose one another. Real copper bend/flange families give the proposal a product-bound starting point; assembled faces, seals and space remain unresolved. Do not add otherwise unnecessary bends merely to obtain this removal geometry.

A listed 220 mm intact flange cannot travel through the original 160 mm sleeve bore. Removing the elbow is not the same as withdrawing the straight carrier: a flange left on that carrier still matters. This rejects one combination, not every flange or union.

## A defined first-removal mechanism

Develop on the **service side** of a fixed collector/pan penetration:

```text
        court collector and retained water boundary W2
                            |
                     fixed outlet stub
                            |
                     Jv: face joint
                            |
                   removable 90° terminal -- Jh -- supported carrier
                          ↙                         to accessible route
                  down and away
```

Jv and Jh are at right angles. The permanent stub, carrier and terminal have independent support; temporary handling supports the terminal before release. Any factory-joined tails belong to the replaceable module, not permanent masonry. W2 remains intact. These demountable joints contain conveyed water W1; they are not also the court's external water barrier.

Take rightwards/upwards as positive. Normals away from the fixed faces are `(-1, 0)` and `(0, -1)`. Translation `(-a, -b)`, for positive a and b, opens both normal gaps. Two opposed normals `(-1, 0)` and `(1, 0)` cannot both gain positive gap from one rigid translation. This is an **ideal separating-direction witness**, not a physical removal test.

**Important counterexample:** an opposed flat-flange spool may nevertheless slide sideways after suitable gasket release. Positive normal separation at both joints is not necessary for every removal method. The earlier stopped-socket trap and this flat-face comparison are different mechanisms. Do not ban straight flanged spools using the normal-vector result.

For the proposed diagonal movement, fasteners must be removed, gaskets released without damaging retained faces, and mating faces free of locating spigots/recesses that obstruct tangential movement. Interlocking faces can defeat the motion even when both normal gaps grow. The full elbow, flange, bolt and tool swept envelopes must clear adjacent pipework, supports and construction. The [calculation's unit movement](studies/copper-access-geometry.mjs) is **not a one-millimetre working clearance**; required travel stays unknown until exact sections and release methods exist.

Demonstrate this sequence: independently divert incoming water and depressurise; support retained connections and the terminal; expose/remove both sets of fasteners; release sealing faces; move the terminal through the verified down-away sweep and out through the service opening. Inspect the retained stub/pan interface without dismantling it. Only then undertake the separate carrier task. Reinstate specified new or demonstrably reusable consumables, controlled bolt loading, and separate W1/W2 leakage and flow checks. A favourable forecast is not isolation.

## Quantified cost of retaining the blind route

The [original north comparison](north-court-carrier.md) supplies unadopted 160 mm bores, 400 mm centres, 35 mm side allowances and a 650 mm clear opening. None is a selected plastic sleeve or engineered structural dimension.

| Check | Result | Limit |
|---|---:|---|
| Original bore minus intact listed flange | **160 − 220 = −60 mm** | Diametral interference before running clearance. The bare tube's apparent 52 mm spare diameter is irrelevant if the larger part travels with it |
| Residual envelope after hypothetical backing-ring removal | **Unknown** | Identify and demonstrate removal, then measure retained collar/hardware. Do not substitute 108 mm, cut a ring or invent a split-flange product |
| Two passages, retained centres and side allowances, zero sleeve wall/clearance | **400 + 220 + 2 × 35 = 690 mm** | Already 40 mm beyond the old opening under those assumptions; real walls/clearances add more. This is not a structural minimum: different centres/margins change it |
| One listed bend plus two listed adaptors | **1.34 + 2 × 4.05 = 9.44 kg** | Catalogue sum only; excludes tails, bolts, gaskets, retained water and equipment. Fixed mating adaptors are separate items; no lifting limit is inferred |

Do not downsize the carrier to obtain an easier union: 108 mm is itself a comparison size, **not a hydraulic selection**. Inlet, debris, normal flow, blockage and surcharge must determine the eventual size.

## Access-first disposition

| Arrangement | Next design disposition | Burden that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| Original blind sleeve carrying the intact 220 mm flange | **Reject this combination** | First terminal release does not fix passage interference |
| Larger sleeve or verified separately removable backing ring | **Conditional alternative, not the next default** | Structure, retained collar, supports, water boundaries, tell-tale, receiving levels and full-length external withdrawal; no inherited 5.25/5.45 m pass |
| Accessible route with supported removable modules and a terminal at the needed turn | **Priority for a connected section** | Access along the actual removal path, lifting/side movement, additional joints, floor/fire/thermal/moisture interfaces, indoor disruption and set-down |
| Fixed service joints renewed by cutting in accessible territory | **Honest conventional comparator, not selected** | Sacrifices replaceable pipe/joint material; differs from repeatedly damaging permanent fabric and must not be described as infinitely reusable |

An accessible route cannot be another inaccessible box. Where protection is needed, compare an openable enclosure before a larger blind passage. Do not create a low service well requiring pumps or concealed groundwater tanking. Retain W2 and the building's dry section at both ends; access covers cannot silently replace structural floor, insulation or threshold support.

**Stop optimising the old sleeve's ring count and diameter until RWT-06/HAD/STR compare a located accessible route against the complete sleeved alternative.** This is a change of development priority, not permission to relocate house geometry. Access may impose worse spatial or structural burdens and be rejected; serviceability does not excuse spoiling the inhabited house.

## Seal choices remain constituent-bound

[DPL's ILC union](https://www.dpluk.co.uk/product/hygienic-tubes-fittings/unions-ilc/) has a metal-to-metal seat **for controlled compression** but lists Nitrile, EPDM, Viton and PTFE seals. That is not an all-metal fluid-seal claim or authority to omit the seal.

[SGL SIGRAFLEX UNIVERSAL/UNIVERSAL PRO](https://www.sglcarbon.com/en/markets-solutions/component/sigraflex-universal-and-universal-pro/) is adhesive-free/PFAS-free graphite reinforced with tanged stainless steel, but is also **impregnated**. The impregnant remains a composition question; those labels do not establish whole-product compliance. This does not classify all graphite gaskets as plastic.

A matched metal-seated union or fully disclosed inorganic/metallic gasket remains legitimate to develop, subject to face, load, surface, water and maintenance compatibility. Do not improvise bare metal faces into a pressure seal or infer graphite's galvanic compatibility from its being non-plastic. The [shared roof/water compatibility record](../../docs/research/roof-rainwater-material-compatibility.md) remains the compatibility authority. No new pairing or polymer exception is approved.
