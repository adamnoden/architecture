# Copper terminal: right-angle release and an access-first route

**30 October 2026 · bounded RWT-METALS continuation · not for construction.** Baseline main `90cbc5fe9526d2c30712b90394738afa06b5c009`. This develops the [terminal first-release finding](copper-terminal-renewal.md), under the [material policy](../../docs/reference-house/material-policy.md), [passive-dryness requirement](../../docs/reference-house/ground-water-policy.md) and [rainwater brief](rainwater-elegance-brief.md). It selects a next design mechanism, not a house route, component schedule or hydraulic size.

## Decision

**Develop a separately supported, removable right-angle terminal with two non-interlocking face joints, and compare it first in an accessible service route. Do not enlarge the old blind sleeves merely to accommodate a flange.** The bend changes the first-removal problem: its two mating planes need not oppose one another. A real copper bend and flange family now give this proposal a product-bound starting point, while their assembled faces, seals and space remain unresolved.

The intact flange comparison also defeats a specific shortcut: a listed 220 mm flange cannot travel through the original 160 mm sleeve bore. Removing the elbow is not the same operation as withdrawing the straight carrier. A flange left on that carrier still matters. No claim is made that every flange or union is unsuitable, or that no compact solution exists.

## A first removal that does not ask an opposite stop to move

Develop this arrangement on the **service side** of a fixed collector/pan penetration:

```text
        court collector and its retained water boundary W2
                            |
                     fixed outlet stub
                            |
                     Jv: face joint
                            |
                   removable 90° terminal -- Jh -- supported carrier
                          ↙                         to accessible route
                  down and away
```

Jv and Jh are deliberately at right angles. The permanent stub and carrier have independent supports; the elbow has its own removable/temporary support. Any factory-brazed tails belong to the replaceable module, not to permanent masonry. W2 is retained; the demountable joints contain conveyed water W1 and do not double as the court's external water barrier.

For the idealised section, take rightwards and upwards as positive. Normals pointing away from the fixed mating faces are `(-1, 0)` and `(0, -1)`. A down-left translation `(-a, -b)`, with positive a and b, increases both normal gaps. By contrast, two opposed normals `(-1, 0)` and `(1, 0)` cannot both gain positive gap from the same rigid translation. This is a **kinematic existence argument**, not an assembly test.

It applies only after bolts/retainers are removed, gaskets are released without damaging retained faces, and the chosen mating faces have no locating spigot, recess or other interlock that obstructs tangential movement. An interlocking flange can defeat the diagonal motion even when both normal gaps increase. Full elbow, flange, bolt and tool swept envelopes must clear the actual stub, carrier, supports and surrounding construction. A normal-vector test deliberately cannot prove those collisions absent.

The unit movement in the [executable comparison](studies/copper-access-geometry.mjs) is a mathematical witness, **not a one-millimetre working clearance**. The exact required movement remains unknown until the face sections and gasket-release method are established. Do not give a fabricator the diagram as a dimensioned assembly.

**Renewal sequence to demonstrate:** independently divert incoming water and depressurise; support both retained connections and the elbow; expose and remove fasteners at both joints; release the sealing faces; move the elbow through the verified down-away sweep and remove it through the service opening. Inspect the retained stub/pan junction without taking it apart. Only then undertake the separate carrier-removal task. Reinstall with the specified new or demonstrably reusable consumables, controlled fastener loading, and separate W1/W2 leakage and flow checks. A favourable weather forecast is not isolation.

## The flange changes the route, not just the coupling symbol

Using the published **intact** 220 mm envelope against the [north study's original](north-court-carrier.md) 160 mm bore gives **160 − 220 = −60 mm**. That is diametral interference with no running allowance. The bare 108 mm tube's apparent 52 mm spare diameter is irrelevant when the larger part remains attached.

If a backing ring can genuinely be removed first, the residual collar/attachment needs a new measured envelope. It is presently **unknown**, not 108 mm. Do not cut a backing ring, machine away a stop, omit a collar or credit a split-flange product that has not been identified and qualified.

Preserving the historical two-passage **400 mm centres** and **35 mm side allowances**, a carried 220 mm envelope gives a zero-wall, zero-running-clearance lower bound of **400 + 220 + 2 × 35 = 690 mm**, already 40 mm beyond the old 650 mm clear opening. A real surrounding sleeve would require more space under those retained assumptions. This is **not a structural minimum**: changing centres or allowances changes the calculation and must be authored as a new coordinated proposal. It does not prove that every possible 650 mm arrangement fails. The previous 180/160 sleeve and structural reservations remain unadopted historical dimensions.

One listed bend plus two listed adaptors sums to **9.44 kg** before added tails, bolts, gaskets, retained water or handling equipment. This is catalogue bookkeeping, not the final module mass or a lifting limit. The two mating adaptors on fixed pipework are separate items. The terminal and its tool access deserve a real support and handling design rather than a tiny union symbol.

The 108 mm tube itself is a comparison size, **not a hydraulic selection**. Do not downsize to obtain a convenient small union without re-establishing inlet, debris, normal-flow, blockage and surcharge duties.

## Accessible carrier versus blind sleeve

| Arrangement | Earned disposition | Cost that must remain in the comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Original blind sleeve with the complete 220 mm flange travelling through it | **Reject this combination** | Intact envelope does not fit; the first terminal release alone does not fix it |
| Larger blind sleeve, or a separately removable backing ring | **Hold as a conditional alternative**, not the next default | Re-resolve structure, retained collar, supports, seals, tell-tale, receiving levels and the full-length external extraction route; no inherited 5.25/5.45 m pass |
| Accessible route with separately supported flanged modules and the right-angle terminal | **Priority for the next connected section** | Access along the actual removal path, whole-body lift/side movement, joints, indoor disruption, floor/fire/thermal/moisture boundaries, and final set-down space |
| Fixed joined service pipe renewed by cutting in accessible service territory | **Honest conventional comparator** | Sacrifices replaceable pipe/joint material; it is not infinitely reusable, but is different from repeatedly damaging permanent fabric. Not selected by this result |

An accessible route is not permission to put a pipe in another inaccessible box. Where a protective casing is actually necessary, compare an accessible openable enclosure before inventing a larger blind passage. A drainage service zone must not become a new low well requiring pumps or concealed groundwater tanking. The courtyard's W2 water boundary and the building's dry section still need continuity at both ends. Nor may access strip covers silently replace the load-bearing floor, insulation or threshold support.

**The change in priority is real:** stop optimising the old sleeve's ring count and diameter until RWT-06/HAD/STR have compared a located accessible route against the complete sleeved alternative. This result does not relocate a wall, floor, door, collector or support. If the accessible route imposes worse occupied-room or structural burdens, the comparison may reject it; retain that possibility rather than making serviceability an excuse to spoil the house.

## Seals: useful alternatives, no hidden approval

The [DPL ILC union](https://www.dpluk.co.uk/product/hygienic-tubes-fittings/unions-ilc/) advertises a metal-to-metal seat **for controlled compression**, while explicitly listing Nitrile, EPDM, Viton and PTFE seals. That wording does not establish an all-metal fluid seal. It is a counterexample, not a proposed substitution or authority to remove the seal.

[SGL's SIGRAFLEX UNIVERSAL/UNIVERSAL PRO](https://www.sglcarbon.com/en/markets-solutions/component/sigraflex-universal-and-universal-pro/) is adhesive-free and PFAS-free graphite reinforced with tanged stainless steel, but the manufacturer also describes **impregnation**. Its undisclosed impregnant remains a constituent question. Neither those two marketing properties nor a graphite headline proves compliance with this house's policy. This does not classify all graphite gaskets as plastic.

A matched metal-seated union or a fully disclosed inorganic/metallic gasket remains a legitimate development route. It must be suitable for the actual face, bolt load, surface condition, water exposure and repeated maintenance. Bare metal faces must not be improvised into a pressure seal, and graphite must not inherit galvanic compatibility merely by being non-plastic. The [existing shared compatibility record](../../docs/research/roof-rainwater-material-compatibility.md) remains the compatibility authority; this result grants no new approved pairing or polymer exception.

These tests verify arithmetic and an ideal plane-gap argument; they cannot verify catalogue accuracy, physical separation, full collision clearance or an installed seal. Earlier study generators, drawings and dimensions are unchanged. Full repository CI and fresh publication preflight belong to the integrating PR.

**Earned:** a specific right-angle first-removal mechanism, a real catalogue-envelope rejection, quantified costs of preserving the blind route, and an access-first design priority. **Not earned:** an approved polymer-free terminal, a dimensioned dry service bay, hydraulic sizing, support adequacy, physical renewal or RWT-METALS completion.

The next useful decision is the **connected access and water-boundary section**, with an exact mating-face/gasket specification inside it. RWT-06/HAD/STR own that spatial reconciliation; MAT controls residual materials. Further isolated fitting searches or another synthetic clearance drawing do not substitute for it.
