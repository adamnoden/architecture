## Decision

**Do not describe the retained court tail as a local maintenance simplification until its separate leak route reaches a demonstrably usable destination.** In the stated falling-tail comparison, a freely draining return whose outlet lip is at or above the tail's court-side outside bottom fails the elevation screen. Carrying leakage northwards instead requires a further **3832.5 mm** through the room and garden threshold before reaching the exterior face. That is an additional maintained water route, with its own joints, access and receiving-water condition.

The existing 300 mm floor screen still fails at its far 220 mm joint slice before a lower collector is added. Moving the first service break into the room does not remove that conflict. Keep the separately renewable threshold comparator in play; neither it nor the retained-tail alternative has a complete admitted assembly. This result rejects a particular high return and an unchanged shallow flanged section, not every retained copper outlet or every secondary-collection arrangement.

## The courtward return has a level test

This is a **continuous no-ponding gravity-return comparator**, not a pressurised drain or a capacity calculation. Retain only the historical straight W1 fall of 60 mm over the 4250 mm north span. Its outside-bottom elevation drops by:

`d = 417.5 × 60 / 4250 = 5.894 mm`

across the court band. These are the existing comparison dimensions; the actual outlet/tail may differ. No newer court-pan elevation is spliced into the old pipe section.

Let `z0` be the W1 outside-bottom elevation at the court face, with elevations increasing upwards. At the room face that bottom is `z0 − d`. Let `a ≥ 0` be the required vertical separation down to the secondary collector's flow line at this controlling low point. Let `f ≥ 0` be the further total fall from that point to a proposed courtward outlet. Then a necessary outlet condition is:

**`z_out ≤ z0 − d − a − f`.**

An outlet lip at or above `z0` fails even the zero-separation/zero-further-fall limit by at least 5.894 mm. Ponding, pressure or a pump cannot be silently substituted for the stated freely draining arrangement. Conversely, an outlet below the bound only passes this elevation screen: actual falls, flow depth/losses, complete capture, blockage, surface tension at small leakage and receiving backwater still need evidence. The formula establishes no minimum drainage gradient or detection time.

A courtward collector cannot simply stay a constant distance below this roomward-falling tail: if its room-face flow-line separation is `a` and its total return fall across the band is `f`, its court-face flow line must lie at least `a + d + f` below the local tube bottom, before adding the collector body.

For a freely discharging visible outlet, let `R` be its maximum receiving-water elevation for the assessed case and `δ ≥ 0` the unselected discharge clearance. The comparison also requires **`R + δ < z_out ≤ z0 − d − a − f`**. A submerged outlet may still convey water under sufficient head, but does not pass this free-outlet comparison; its different hydraulic and observation duties would need assessment. No value of `R` or `δ` is supplied here.

Returning leakage to the wetted court pan also shares that pan's receiving-water condition. Its actual water surface, not merely the outlet lip, may drown the route. A hidden return into the normal wet collector supplies no demonstrated observation of a concealed tail fault. A genuinely visible, separately qualified lower discharge remains a possible alternative; this study has not located one. The [passive site-interface ledgers](passive-court-site-interface.md) still control the receiver, groundwater and court first-failure questions. Neither the historical −555 leak reference nor −585 receiving target transfers to this assembly.

## Do not count a flange's depth twice—or give its space away

Retain the earlier authored **50 mm cover, centred 220 mm joint envelope, 108 mm tube envelope and 60 mm fall** solely to screen the unresolved floor section. They are vertical-envelope assumptions, not a measured installed fitting orientation. At a joint slice, the flange reaches 110 mm below the axis and the bare tube 54 mm. Define `k ≥ 0` as the **complete secondary-collector projection below the tube outside-bottom envelope**, including its separation and body. This differs from the flow-line separation `a` above. `k` is local to each slice; applying the same arithmetic probe at both slices does not specify a constant-depth collector profile, particularly for a courtward return.

The combined lower extent from the axis is `max(110, 54 + k)`. The incremental depth beyond the flange is therefore **`max(0, k − 56)`**, not automatically `k`. This corrects double counting, but it does not prove that the two parts can occupy the same space: **the flange occupies those 56 mm**. A collector passing beneath it needs real clearance and its own construction below the flange. A side bypass needs its own width, connection and containment detail.

A first service joint must lie far enough roomward for its complete body, tools and operation to clear threshold construction; its actual position is unknown. No 220 mm flange is assumed along the whole fixed tail. A smaller joint, relocated axis, staggered access or changed floor needs a new connected section. Floor support, insulation, lower containment, movement and the independent ground damp/gas boundary cannot be omitted to secure a numerical fit.

## Support and maintenance cannot borrow the pan joint

"Fixed water boundary" describes what stays intact during the proposed operation; it does not establish a structural anchor or zero thermal movement. Aurubis's separate fixed-zone and opening guidance, printed pp84 and 88, requires movement to be addressed at connections and penetrations. Its roof details do not size supports for this carrier. Show the pan's support/movement, the retained tail's restraint and the removable module's temporary support together; neither the secondary collector nor the pan seam is presumed to carry the module or release forces. No fixed-point location, load or movement allowance is selected here.

| Water state | Required consequence for this alternative |
|---|---|
| W1 closed; tail or service joint leaks | Capture the applicable leakage over the whole vulnerable extent and convey it to an observable safe destination. A favourable drip-level calculation does not establish capture or capacity for a larger defect. |
| First room W1 joint open for service | Control incoming rain and retained water independently. The open tail can discharge conveyed rain into the work area; a leak tell-tale is not assumed to carry that full duty. Keeping the circumferential pan seam intact does not close the bore. |
| Secondary route blocked or receiver backed up | Trace the resulting water level to the first susceptible floor/threshold interface. Do not credit a secondary path terminating in the same flooded destination as independent escape. |
| Tail or secondary collector itself fails | Demonstrate the larger intervention, including access through threshold construction, temporary rain management, reinstatement and separate checks of W1, court W2 and the ground boundary. Room-carrier replacement alone does not perform this repair. |
