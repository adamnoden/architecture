# RWT-ROBUSTNESS — rainwater fault independence, head and harmless escape

**1 November 2026.** Bounded **pre-site adversarial comparison**, downstream of [RWT-SHED](rwt-shed-result.md), [RWT-METALS](rwt-metals-option-disposition.md) and [RWT-05](rwt-05-blockage-failure-exceedance.md). It consumes the [current house basis](../../docs/reference-house/current-house-basis.md), [catchment ledger](../../docs/research/roof-shedding-and-ground-receivers.md), [courtyard trial](rwt-06-access-court-transition.md), [passive receiving-level comparison](passive-court-site-interface.md), and [courtyard threshold first-failure investigation](../had/had-03-court-threshold-first-failure.md). This result tests **whether a claim is earned by the represented topology and evidence**; it does not choose actual outlets, promise water-level safety or replace a named physical design.

## Decision

**No current Reference House court network demonstrates an independent harmless failed-state escape.** The controlling danger is a **shared downstream cut** in an enclosed basin, not merely too few downpipes. A second grate, parallel branch or copper pipe earns zero redundancy against a blockage *after* its convergence. If an inner collector overtops to the court, the additional roof inflow makes the unsolved court escape harder; it does not create a safe roof overflow. The historic low north crossing also has no accepted support/maintenance section, first susceptible threshold elevation or dependable exterior receiving-water condition. Accordingly **none of the existing court/crossing candidates passes at the current evidence state**.

**Outer exposed copper collection** remains the preferred comparison where an independently harmless exterior spill is geometrically possible. **Outboard free-shedding copper ground collection** remains a conditional challenger on selected garden edges, not a default or a proof from a neat hedge. Neither has an installed section, actual capacity, complete no-plastic assembly or site outlet. The preferred next network comparison is one coherent copper collection family and a *bounded* outer-west shedding challenger, not a proliferation of imaginary redundant pipes.

This is an actual adverse **topology and evidence disposition**: retain only candidates with a named independent failed-state receiving route; reject current shared-throat and court-only overflow topologies; require a consciously redesigned court passage/escape rather than letting the final house gate inherit a hidden failure.

## Two distinct promises — do not confuse them

1. **Full-duty survival (N+1 for a named fault):** with one nominated primary inlet, downpipe, branch or section unavailable, **the remaining intact hydraulic route(s)** must take the *whole assigned event inflow* to the destination, at a hydraulically established operating head, without crossing vulnerable fabric. This means `Q_surviving(h) ≥ Q_assigned` for the nominated event, supported by actual inlet, fall, loss, tailwater and routing data. Two nominally 50%-capacity pipes are not full-duty redundancy. A second pipe whose wet foot joins the first blocked throat is not independent. “Two” is a count, not a performance claim.
2. **Passive harmless exceedance:** when nominal controlled collection is blocked or overwhelmed, water may intentionally spill **clear of the house**, but only if the excess path has a demonstrated *full failed-state discharge/consequence* and a receiving destination that is not the same blocked channel. An outer eaves outward overtopping path may earn this instead of duplicate downpipes. In a fully enclosed court it cannot simply be labelled “spill into the court”; unless a safe through-ring escape is shown, that describes accumulation and likely vulnerability, not a solution.

For a high-consequence court, if the entire controlled system is incapacitated under the nominated event, the escape must pass the **entire residual inflow**, which may be close to the whole roof-plus-court contribution. A small warning spout or an overflow crest at a favourable elevation with insufficient capacity is an **alarm**, not a full-duty path. An alarm, leaf guard, motorised valve, pump, resident intervention or favourable weather forecast never receives passive-safety credit.

### Four tests before the word “independent”

For **each** source and named failure `F`:

- **Topology:** remove the failed upstream component and all common downstream components of the failed route. Is there a *continuously connected* gravity route from the still-wet source to an actually harmless exterior receiving territory? An alternative that rejoins a failed section has failed this test.
- **Hydraulic duty:** at the nominated rainfall concentration, does the surviving route physically pass its assigned `Q` with actual head losses, inlet capture, backing-up water and downstream receiving level? A nominal bore, overflow invert or manufacturer capacity for a different installation earns nothing.
- **Fabric and people:** at its **operating** water level plus splash, wind, structural movement and justified margins, does discharge remain below/outboard of every *actual* first susceptible roof, sill, door, wall, DPC, floor and foundation boundary? A still-unmatched threshold connection is **unknown**, not safe.
- **Maintenance and material:** can a person safely discover, isolate/divert inflow, reach, clear, release and renew the failed component without consuming permanent fabric, with each installed constituent disclosed? A route that only works while a blocked hidden branch is being instantly repaired is not passively resilient.

A route can be *topologically independent* and still **fail capacity or receiving-level safety**. Status labels below distinguish **FAIL** for a demonstrable common cut, **UNPROVEN** for required unbound physical inputs, and **CONDITIONAL CHALLENGER** where a plausible distinct fault path exists but requires a sourced section. No candidate is labelled PASS.

## Source conservation — the court cannot disappear

The current source has a 12 m square shell, 3.5 m square open court and inward roof projection. In the accepted wall-line-area comparison:

| Catchment | Projected area | Illustrative flow at 144 mm/h |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inward roof, including its four local valley concentrations | 47.8125 m² | 1.9125 L/s |
| Direct exposed court | 12.2500 m² | 0.4900 L/s |
| **Combined trapped input** when roof spill reaches court | **60.0625 m²** | **2.4025 L/s** |

All figures use the RWT source's **illustrative** `i=144 mm/h`, a unit runoff coefficient and `Q=iA/3600` L/s. These are not a London design storm or a routed gutter, and corner concentrations prohibit equal eight-way allocations. The corresponding **ten-minute unescaped volume is 1,441.5 L**; neither that volume nor a court plan area establishes safe depth, storage capacity or a time before a hidden threshold joint gets wet. No available data identify the lowest damage threshold.

If a qualified high-level roof transfer carried *all* inward roof water to independent outside destinations, the **direct court input remains 0.4900 L/s and 294.0 L per ten blocked minutes** under that same illustrative intensity. Such a transfer has **not** been selected or tested, and its own blocked/ruptured state may send the full 2.4025 L/s back to the court. **Roof redistribution alone cannot settle court drainage.**

## Compound degraded states are the design examination

| Fault pair / common cause | What to observe or calculate | Automatic rejection trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Leaf mat blocking the primary inlet + high rain intensity | Residual full `Q`, alternate inlet exposure, actual head and freeboard | Overflow is a token vent, reuses blocked collector or overtops inward into court/wall |
| Frozen/full ground receiver + lost normal outlet | Outboard spill from actual rim at flowing capacity, no-plant wetting, ice/splash/footpath exposure | New water returns beneath the foundation, across a threshold or into an inaccessible hidden tray |
| Roof valley concentration + one obstructed downpipe | Distinct inlet path at the corner, unaffected remaining fall/bore and usable access | Equal-area source assumption conceals a concentrated overload |
| Downpipe displaced/leaking + saturated site gully | Observe actual first water-to-fabric path and available safe daylight escape | Both normal and supposed backup wet permanent wall/cavity/DPC before leaving |
| Court drain blocked + high exterior tailwater | Flowing court surface and *actual* first susceptible sill/frame/pan/cavity joint with tolerance | Escape needs higher court head than the first vulnerable level, even if a second pipe exists |
| Primary unavailable during maintenance + rain resumes | Safe isolation/temporary diversion before any cover/liner is lifted; withdrawal and post-repair verification | Service requires opening a wet boundary or damaging permanent structure with no safe bypass |
| Soil/trees move differently from fixed service passage + blockage | Relative three-axis movement, opening/pipe restraints, joint integrity, backwater | Rigid crossing or cover becomes uninspectable, cracks, or carries loads into permanent masonry |
| Two outlets share a downstream manhole that floods during the event | Compare their common cause with the **failure actually claimed** | Independent-outlet label is retained despite identical flooded destination |
| Emergency relief is beneath a hedge or planted channel | Full operation when foliage absent, buried in clippings or root-displaced | Life-safety or dryness duty depends on mature plants or attentive gardening |

**A single observed dry section under nominal rain is not evidence for these rows**. The graph is evaluated under the *faulted* scenario, including continuing rainfall and high tailwater. Where high-water, head or first-fabric levels are unknown, record **UNPROVEN**, never grant safety by default.

## The exact hydraulic/elevation handoff

For a nominated source `s`, event `e` and failed component `f`, the following ledger must precede any N+1 claim:

`(A_s, i_e, Q_s, source/concentration map, f, remaining inlet(s), hydraulic grade under f, Q_remaining(h), high receiving water, first susceptible interface and margin, terminal and open spill, access/renewal)`.

A sufficient necessary form is:

`Q_remaining(H_operating, tailwater, damage state) ≥ Q_assigned(e)`

and, for every relevant susceptible interface `k`,

`H_operating + splash/wave/movement/tolerance allowance_k < H_first-harm,k`

with the entire discharge physically directed to a harmless exterior receiver **without the failed shared component**. The actual values and allowable margins are **not** yet known. A downpipe's bore alone cannot establish the first inequality; a weir crest alone cannot establish the second. For a site flood that disables all possible gravity receivers, no unlimited discharge guarantee is asserted; the house/site architecture must be screened, and such an unresolved site may simply be unsuitable.

This distinguishes **two goals** that are often confused: *hydraulic operational survival* and *harmless failure consequence*. A visible first symptom is a third, independent observation goal. The owner’s no-pump, accessible and maintainable house can meet the latter even when a collector is intentionally sacrificial/renewable; it cannot accept flooding of permanent fabric as the price.

## Disposition to RWT-07 and shared gate

**Earned by RWT-ROBUSTNESS:** the finite candidate fault tree has now been attacked across the nominated single obstruction, common branch, outlet, debris/ice, backwater, courtyard, leak, bare-hedge, movement and rain-during-maintenance states. C1/C2/C3/C4/C8 are rejected at the stated claims; C5/C6 are the only current bounded external challenges; C7 is the architectural **redesign demand**, not a fictitious selected house detail. The extant court system cannot claim full-duty redundancy or harmless complete blockage. This is a **completed comparative robustness assessment**, **not a passing resilience certificate**.

**RWT-07 must now** produce the smallest real edge-by-edge candidate network, with every source to an explicit primary and faulted destination, preservation of controlled collection at court/occupied edges, actual geometry and first-susceptible-interface levels. It shall **reject or rewind** any candidate that still has a shared fatal court cut. Do not promote a single copper baseline into selection merely because RWT-METALS or this analysis finished its bounded research scope. **RWT-06 remains unfinished** until the normal/relief crossing, access and dry boundary are physically established. This document does not close RWT-07, ROF-RWT-GATE, STR, HAD, MAT, MRA, DPL or DEL.

**RWT-METALS / MAT** must provide complete installed copper/brass and concealed accessory constituents; **ROF/HAD/OAS/STR** must prove eaves, roof, doors, structure and gravity opening; **DPL** provides genuine site receiving levels and lawful destination(s); **MRA** attacks the selected house. Where a site/design makes the failure inequalities impossible, **rework geometry or choose a different plot**, not a pump/hidden liner to rescue a preferred drawing.

No physical test, single product, standard-derived capacity, environmental claim, site drainage approval or maturity promotion is asserted here. Earlier RWT-05/RWT-SHED negative evidence stays in force and is not overwritten.
