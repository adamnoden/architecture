## Question and result

Which realistic rainwater material/joint families can be carried into house candidates without approving incompatible runoff, hiding movement or treating replacement as a label?

The shared record compares cast iron, copper, zinc, aluminium, stainless steel and suitable PVC-U systems. It distinguishes the actual joint/support/renewal duties and establishes directional candidate interfaces. It excludes copper-to-zinc runoff and copper-to-unprotected-aluminium runoff on the cited primary guidance; other cases remain conditional on named product, exposure and detail evidence. It supplies no generic metal compatibility grid or selected supplier.

The important house consequence is that a covering choice constrains downstream valleys, collectors, outlets and pipes even where metals do not touch. Conversely, a strong local material cannot make the inward court route maintainable or physically continuous. The ordinary external metal/polymer comparison remains available; neither a premium metal nor concealed collection receives preference by appearance alone.

## Evidence and adversarial review

Primary manufacturer/industry records cover the six families, jointing, movement, coating, contamination, supports and renewal. Source-specific instructions are bounded to their products. US copper details supply mechanisms, not UK dimensions or approval. Uginox's product-specific compatibility statements are not extended to every stainless alloy or to the adjoining metal. Supplier lifespan, guarantee and environmental marketing are not promoted to project evidence.

The review rejected: physical separation as a cure for incompatible runoff; same-metal identity as a complete assembly pass; fewer seams as automatic reliability; a painted barrier without edge/damage evidence; all soldering as movement accommodation; one generic sealant or bracket spacing; accessible-looking pipes without a removal task; and component material durability as whole-network assurance.

ROF-02/RWT-02/03 peer-reviewed draft inputs were inspected in integration PR #1031 before its adoption: four inner valleys/court concentration, exposed versus trapped collection, and the unresolved low-level/high-transfer court route. They inform interface questions, not a selected network. Source geometry is unchanged. The shared roof-water contract points to this one record; ROF-03/05 and RWT-07 must extend the same cases with actual candidates rather than create contradictory schedules.

Independent peer review confirmed the collection-interface limits and MGMA runoff restriction, and added guard/strainer materials plus the source-specific nonmetal-to-zinc exposure case to the shared inventory. Roof-side review independently checked MGMA and Uginox restrictions. No guard obstruction allowance, product sizing or siphonic claim was imported. Access limitations for the large iron guide and copper section are stated explicitly; their reviewed primary indexed excerpts are not presented as a full manual read.
