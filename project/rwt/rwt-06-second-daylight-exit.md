# RWT-06 — what a genuinely second daylight exit costs the selected house

**3 November 2026 — architectural collision screen, not a redesign or RWT-06 closeout.** This compares three **specific** 1200 mm-wide *straight, outdoor* through-ring passage reservations against the selected compact B/open north loggia, using its [source-pinned geometry](studies/court-second-passages.mjs), [regression tests](studies/court-second-passages.test.mjs) and existing [RWT fault-independence obligations](rwt-robustness-result.md). No selected wall, roof, room, window or drainage route is altered.

## The controlling distinction

The selected **2400 mm clear north outdoor loggia** makes one real surface path from the court to the garden. It can provide an *architecturally located* relief path while the separate 400 mm-gross normal copper collection remains in its selected west edge, subject to actual inlet, head, separation and receiving evidence. It does **not** prove full-duty discharge, harmless water levels, independent external receivers or a second path when the *entire north mouth/loggia or its receiving ground* is disabled. A second grate or concealed pipe feeding the same north cut does not fix that fault.

A second **independent open-to-sky escape** needs to reach a genuinely different exterior boundary without entering inhabited/controlled fabric. Test the straightforward orthogonal alternatives first, rather than hiding a flood route under a floor. The candidates are illustrative **1200 mm *gross* strip reservations** (not verified finished clear widths) crossing the 4250 mm house ring. Each removes **5.10 m²** of ground footprint before enclosing weather walls, insulation, required piers, flanking openings, replacement circulation or exterior receiving territory.

| Separate route trial | Exact plan strip (mm) | Current-source collisions | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **West, near court midline** | x0–4250, y5400–6600 | **2.88 m²** of the selected stair body's rectangular reservation; cuts the protected-stair east wall; overlaps **250 mm** of each of the two selected west-court windows | Not a drainage-only incision: stair/guarded exit and court-window architecture must be redesigned |
| **East, through plant** | x7750–12000, y5400–6600 | Cuts **1200 mm** of the solid service-spine wall (its only authored opening is at y2750–3650), crosses the plant-north partition, intersects the selected **600 ×600 mm** buffer and plant-controls territory, and overlaps **250 mm** of each east-court window | Not a service tunnel: destroys selected equipment/service access and needs STR/MAT/HAD replanning |
| **South, central axis** | x5400–6600, y0–4250 | Runs directly through the existing **front door and court-south door**, each covering its full 1200 mm band, and the explicitly protected entrance/hall | Already a **protected indoor entry**, not an independent external wet route; calling its open door a second outlet would abandon the house's protected route |

The two selected court-window intervals on either side are y4650–5650 and y6350–7350. They leave **700 mm of uninterrupted wall between them**, before structural jambs or weathering; a 1200 mm midline passage cannot retain both windows untouched. This is a *local screen*, not proof that no window can ever move or that all alternative bends/locations are impossible. A genuinely separate passage may exist after a material house-layout rewrite, but none is present or earned by this comparison.

## Fault and elevation ledger

| Fault | Current selected north path and proposed second path | What can be said now |
|---|---|---|
| Normal copper inlet/carrier alone blocked | The north 1800 mm-clear surface relief is geometrically distinct in the open loggia | **Candidate single-component bypass only**; full event, local interception, received head and first harm all unproved |
| Relief surface obstructed, normal clear | Normal copper branch may carry rain through its own terminal | Not independent unless it stays clear and its complete receiving route/head is verified |
| Blanket debris cuts **both** north mouths | All three studied second outlets would require a substantial house rewrite | **No present second surface outlet.** Ordinary north loggia cannot be counted twice |
| High tailwater submerges both north receiving routes | Two differently drawn downpipes or channels still face the common flooded receiving territory | **Fails the claimed independent destination** unless a demonstrably separate harmless exterior receiver exists |
| Complete north obstruction while rain continues | The sloped courtyard is an outdoor surface; the MAT pan-end/DPC is nevertheless **unqualified at −30 mm**, against mouth floor **−110 mm** | No trapped-water safety, storage allowance or door threshold is claimed; an 80 mm dam reaches the adverse −30 comparison before discharge head |

Neither site flood risk nor a credible debris scenario can be assessed from a generic London plot assumption. These are named fault cases for later physical/event/site screening; do not call every imaginable obstruction a universal safety-design requirement, but do not claim independent protection against any case still sharing the north cut.

## Decision

**Do not develop a buried cross-ring "backup" or a second north drain as though it resolved complete-mouth failure.** The existing open north loggia is the only accepted *architectural* surface passage. The RWT-06 design must proceed on one of two honest routes:

1. **Keep one north open loggia** and establish a complete wetting-tolerant/passively safe first-harm and external receiving proposition, explicitly constraining what blockage and tailwater states it can survive. If common whole-mouth blockage is an acceptance requirement, this branch cannot claim it without new geometry/evidence.
2. **Author a genuine second outdoor through-ring passage** under HAD/STR/IAS/OAS/ROF coordination, with its room, stair, protected-route, opening, support, thermal and land costs made explicit before RWT attempts product or capacity design.

The owner-house objective gives no standing permission to erase the protected entrance, main stair or ground plant just to add a drainage line. The second route needs a **revised house design**, not another copper fitting. A later real plot must then supply two independently usable water destinations and the applicable event/ground level; even two openings cannot beat common regional inundation automatically.

**RWT-06 remains planned.** The next constructive RWT step is a full normal/relief water-height and first-harm section in the selected north loggia with named operating/failure cases; if that cannot earn the required harmless bypass, route a *specific* HAD architectural revision rather than adding further nominal pipes. No RWT-07, ROF-RWT-GATE or construction admission follows.
