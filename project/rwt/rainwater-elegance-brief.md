# Rainwater elegance and failure tolerance

**Owner-directed rebrief, 30 October 2026.** This develops the existing [RWT programme](rainwater-systems-programme.md) and [roof programme](../rof/roof-systems-programme.md); it does not create a second drainage workstream. The [whole-house material policy](../../docs/reference-house/material-policy.md) applies to visible and hidden parts. Earlier drainage studies remain evidence, not adopted construction.

## The architectural direction

**Shed rain freely into an intentionally designed ground receiver where that is demonstrably simple and forgiving. Where control is needed, collect it in copper. Make blockage safe by geometry and genuinely independent escape, not by asking the occupant to notice in time.**

The owner's desired landscape is a composed English garden: clipped hedges, stone, deliberate metal water details and little visible technical clutter. Investigate genuine brass or bronze channels and grates, not a metal-coloured cover over a plastic body. Beauty and the material preference are design requirements; they do not supply missing hydraulic evidence. A quiet conventional copper system is preferable to a complicated device whose extra maintenance exists only to make gutters disappear.

The house still needs drainage even when it has no eaves gutter: collection and inspection have moved to the ground. A free-shedding edge is not an uncontrolled discharge or an excuse to soak the foundations. Gutter-free and drain-free are different propositions.

## Which edges earn which approach?

| Condition | Direction to investigate | What can defeat it |
|---|---|---|
| Outer garden-facing edge with real receiving and maintenance space | Generous but proportionate eave, positive drip, ground impact/capture bed, inspectable channel and planted edge | Wind-driven return, excessive splash/noise, paths, roots, insufficient stand-off, inaccessible receiver, boundary or ground-water consequences |
| Entrance, occupied route, public/adjacent boundary, valley concentration or an edge without receiving space | Controlled copper gutter/hopper/downpipe and a visible safe ground transition | Aesthetic concealment of critical joints, inaccessible high-level cleaning, a common blocked destination, water reaching doors or neighbours |
| Enclosed court | Controlled collection is the starting comparator; free shedding must earn a stricter case against nearby doors, wall bases, usable space and wind/splash | Treating the court as harmless storage, a hedge as a flood barrier, or additional roof spill as safe merely because a ground inlet exists |
| Concealed crossing below occupied fabric | First reconsider the route and whether it is necessary; if retained, develop the complete non-plastic carrier, support, joint, sleeve/void and renewal arrangement | Retaining PE/PVC sleeves, rings, hidden linings or seals by default; transferring old clearances or stiffness to a metal tube by changing its label |

This table is a research disposition, not selection of bigger eaves, a new roof or a real plot. ROF/HAD must reconcile cornice proportions, structure, daylight, views, roof access and the accepted house geometry before any overhang change. The current narrow court and high eaves cannot inherit a successful low garden-roof test. If gutterless collection needs an excessive trench, protective canopy or elaborate cleaning regime, the copper comparator may win.

## Ground receiver and English hedge

Study a connected section from the wall to the garden: **dry wall-base stand-off → stable impact/capture territory → inspectable conveyance → deliberately placed hedge/root territory → harmless exceedance route**. These are functional zones, not a fixed linear arrangement or an invented dimension. A hedge may partially occupy a removable impact bed only where access, roots and independent drainage remain viable.

The mineral or metallic receiving construction must control impact, erosion and the path to an actual outlet; compare open stone rills, stable gravel/stone beds over an accessible collector, and appropriately formed brass/copper channels. Gravel and root soil are not presumed watertight, maintenance-free or suitable for infiltration next to the house. A concealed geotextile, polymer liner or plastic drain below attractive gravel remains subject to MAT. Do not propose a French drain against the foundation merely because its top looks natural.

Hedges may soften the visual edge and might reduce some impact or noise. **No essential drainage capacity, splash protection or flood safety is credited to living foliage at selection.** The receiver must work when the hedge is absent, newly planted, leafless, dead, pruned, removed for renewal or locally flattened. Evaluate leaves and clippings blocking the collector, roots displacing its joints, waterlogging, drought, frost, safe pruning/cleaning and access to masonry. Species selection follows actual light, soil, wetting, climate and owner preferences; a miniature box hedge is not an engineered splash arrestor by name.

A real test should compare bare receiver, planted receiver and adverse/blocked states under concentrated roof discharge. Ordinary rainfall-interception evidence does not demonstrate resistance to a sheet or stream falling from an eave. Keep any measured benefit of planting separate from the independent no-plant safety case.

## Calculation contract: no magic overhang dimension

There are useful calculations, but **overhang, roof pitch and covering material alone do not determine a safe landing strip**. Inputs must include catchment geometry, selected rainfall event and duration, discharge concentration, eave height, exit behaviour, wind exposure, receiver shape, splash, drainage capacity, blockage and the destination's water level. State uncertainty and the supported range. Do not compensate for missing inputs by adding decimal places.

### 1. Catchment and flow

For rainfall intensity `i` in mm/h and effective contributing area `A` in m², a runoff-coefficient-one screening flow is:

`Q = i × A / 3600` litres/second.

Where a justified runoff coefficient `C` is used, the expression becomes `C × i × A / 3600`; do not invent losses to reduce the roof design duty. Identify each catchment once, including the adopted method's pitch/wind/effective-area treatment. Do not count the same roof projection and court area twice. If a roof discharges uniformly along receiving length `L`, `q = Q/L` is a first linear-flow screen; valleys, seams, blocked edges and uneven falls require local concentration cases rather than that uniform assumption.

Use current applicable rainfall evidence and climate allowances at the adopted site/event, with source date, jurisdiction and design purpose. A whole-site flood event and a roof-gutter sizing event are not interchangeable labels. Historical handbook rainfall maps, a generic London number or a summer garden rule do not establish the final design storm. Before a plot exists, report a sensitivity range and required site inputs, not a claimed site pass.

### 2. Detachment, fall and wind

A simple no-air-resistance particle model can help check arithmetic. With eave height `H`, prescribed downward exit velocity `v_d`, outward velocity `v_x`, overhang `e` and gravity `g`, the fall time is `(sqrt(v_d² + 2gH) − v_d)/g`, and the outward landing coordinate from the wall is `e + v_x × time`.

**This is not a capture-width design rule.** It prescribes rather than derives the exit velocity, ignores wind, sheet breakup, droplet size, surface tension, underside wetting and splash, and cannot turn pitch or roof smoothness into a reliable velocity without separate evidence. Low flow may cling to an edge; high flow may launch or concentrate differently. Wind may move rain back towards the wall. Use an appropriate tested/validated discharge envelope or a representative mock-up over the selected range, not one favourable ballistic trajectory.

The required receiving territory must capture the credible landing envelope plus impact/splash spread and construction/movement uncertainty, while keeping the first susceptible fabric and occupied routes outside the harmful-wetting envelope. Assess noise and slipperiness in the actual architectural setting. A nominal dry gap in a section does not prove this separation.

### 3. Receiver, channel and destination

Calculate inlet capacity, flow depth, freeboard, transitions and outlet capacity using the selected geometry and appropriate method. Check debris accumulation and diminished opening area rather than only a clean channel. Ground infiltration, storage, outfall legality, groundwater and downstream backwater remain actual site questions. A lined receiver is a collector, not proof of lawful discharge; an unlined bed is not proof that water safely disappears.

Keep a continuous elevation ledger from catchment through collector and every overflow crest to the site handoff and the **lowest vulnerable wall/threshold/joint**. Compare calculated water levels, not just pipe inverts. The existing −75/−50 court water targets and historical receiving levels remain uncomputed comparisons. No new free-shed or copper choice validates them.

## Guttering: reserve capacity that still has a route

The requirement is **a safe water path with one credible primary element unavailable**, not simply two downpipes on a drawing.

For each trapped or high-consequence catchment, define the primary-failure design flow and show that the surviving independent route can carry that flow without reaching vulnerable fabric. Where two outlets are claimed as a redundant pair, evaluate **each outlet blocked in turn**, with actual gutter crossflow, falls, stop-ends, low points, approach lengths and outlet head. Two outlets each sized only for half the flow are not full-duty redundancy. Likewise, separate pipes terminating in the same blocked gully or backing-up chamber are not independent escape routes.

Compare two full-duty low-level outlets against a primary collector with a deliberately separate, visible emergency overflow. The latter may provide clearer failure indication with fewer normal-flow components. The right answer is condition-specific. A second high-level opening counts only if water can reach it and pass through its full path before a lower susceptible joint is wetted. Normal symmetry or a central high point may prevent transfer between nominally paired outlets; show the actual hydraulic route.

Beyond one blocked element, attack common causes: a leaf mat covering both openings; frost; a neglected guard; displaced gutter/support; valley concentration; a leaking joint; blocked surface receiver; and exterior water simultaneously submerging both destinations. Define a **passive harmless final spill route** where practicable, and name any case that still requires architectural/site redesign. Two paths do not provide infinite-failure immunity. Do not credit check valves, sensors, emergency occupant action or a favourable maintenance forecast as removing new rainfall.

Size accessible collectors with deliberate reserve and freeboard, using an explicit design case rather than an arbitrary oversizing factor. Check full-water/debris load, supports, movement and cleaning/replacement. Prefer fewer accessible, independently functioning components over excessive joints and guards. Ordinary maintenance plus one neglected element and heavy rain is a design case, not a reason to presume perfect maintenance.

## Copper, brass and the complete non-plastic assembly

Copper is the intended language for controlled visible rainwater goods. Investigate folded/soldered sheet gutters, accessible hoppers, downpipes and replaceable fixings with actual joint and movement details. A soldered joint is not necessarily a removable one; define the repair length and access. A metal system with synthetic gaskets, anti-condensation liners, lacquer, isolation washers or hidden pipework still requires constituent review. Do not omit a necessary isolation/sealing duty merely to avoid its usual polymer solution.

Brass channels deserve a real alloy and fabrication study, not generic 'brass' specification. Assess wetting/stagnation, alloy-dependent dezincification, cleaning chemicals, solder/braze compatibility, joints, thickness, support, wear, slip/trip edges, impact noise and replacement. Natural patination and staining are deliberate appearance questions; an undisclosed clearcoat is not the default answer. Bronze, copper, suitable stainless steel or mineral channels are explicit comparison alternatives, not permission to ignore the brass preference.

Map runoff as well as physical contact between metals. Copper-bearing runoff can affect susceptible downstream metals even without direct metal-to-metal contact. Check ground-receiver water quality and planted-soil exposure; do not assert either harmlessness or inevitable plant injury without applicable evidence. Keep the existing ROF/RWT compatibility record as the single compatibility owner and route amendments there through RWT-METALS.

## Primary evidence and scope, accessed 30 October 2026

- [Historic England: Buildings Without Rainwater Goods, 2024](https://historicengland.org.uk/advice/technical-advice/retrofit-and-energy-efficiency-in-historic-buildings/resilient-rainwater-systems/buildings-without-rainwater-goods/) supports investigating deep eaves and appropriate ground receivers, but warns that near-wall French drains can concentrate water at foundations. Historic precedent is not a new-house permission or quantified splash proof.
- [Historic England: Upgrades or Alterations to Rainwater Goods, 2024](https://historicengland.org.uk/advice/technical-advice/retrofit-and-energy-efficiency-in-historic-buildings/resilient-rainwater-systems/upgrades-or-alterations-to-rainwater-goods/) explains that extra outlets need compatible gutter falls and downstream capacity, and that rainfall evidence needs current climate consideration. Two nominal pipes are not an independence demonstration.
- [England Approved Document H, 2015 edition](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/drainage-and-waste-disposal-approved-document-h), H3 guidance and printed pp.40–41, provides roof/ground drainage and effective-area/gutter guidance. It is guidance with a defined scope, not whole-building compliance or a licence to reuse its sample sizes on this house. The actual H3 duty is adequate rainwater removal.
- [Defra national SuDS standards, 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-standards-for-sustainable-drainage-systems/national-standards-for-sustainable-drainage-systems-suds) are **non-statutory national standards**. Their performance, water-quality, failure, maintenance and structural provisions inform the site interface; they do not replace the roof-specific design method or provide missing site evidence.
- [RHS: Rain gardens](https://www.rhs.org.uk/garden-features/rain-gardens) supports deliberate planting/overflow/maintenance and site suitability, not arbitrary infiltration at a wall or safety credit for a hedge. The 2026 study [Blanusa et al., rainfall attenuation by model hedges](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3298/13/5/252) is a useful research lead: its indexed primary abstract describes containerised seasonal simulated-rainfall work, **not concentrated high-eave impact**. Full methods were not accessible in this session, so no numerical interception or splash performance is adopted.
- [Copper Development Association: hung gutters](https://live.copper.org/applications/architecture/arch_dhb/arch-details/gutters_downspouts/hung_gutters.html) and [gutter/downpipe specifications](https://archive.copper.org/applications/architecture/arch_dhb/specifications/arch_specs/gutters_downspouts.php) establish actual fabricated copper/movement/joint families. Their US details are not a selected UK assembly and some accessories require a separate material audit.
- [Canadian Conservation Institute: dezincification of brass](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/conservation-preservation-publications/canadian-conservation-institute-notes/dezincification-brass.html) identifies alloy/environment dependence. [AMPP: galvanic and deposition corrosion, 2024](https://www.ampp.org/blogs/webmasternaceorg/2024/05/29/examining-galvanic-and-deposition-corrosion) supplies a relevant copper-runoff/downstream-metal failure mechanism. Neither selects this project's channel alloy or receiving-water treatment.

**Current result:** a stronger owner brief, researched failure model and executable programme. No hedge, overhang, channel width, gutter size, material assembly, court relief level or site outfall is yet accepted by this rebrief. The next drawings and calculations must earn those choices rather than merely illustrate them.
