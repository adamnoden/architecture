## Decision and design price

**Keep a limited in-basket clearing comparator, and develop a purpose-designed court scaffold/independent handling system for receiver renewal.** A Genie AWP-25S standard-base CE machine with outdoor outriggers can in principle reach the eave height. Its published 3000 × 2800 deployment, however, cannot occupy the 3500 court wholly south of the full-width y7000–7350 inlet: that dry-to-inlet depth is 2750. Even with its short side along the court axis and zero wall clearance, at least **50 mm** of the setup envelope reaches the inlet; the historical centred x4500–7500/y4600–7400 trial reaches it for **350 mm**. Rotation makes the minimum overlap 250. A full machine setup cannot be declared to stand on intact continuous floor, and the source does not identify the four jack-pad coordinates within that rectangle. Its delivered wheels must cross the inlet. Design an independent load-rated water-live wheel bridge, then prove whether all four pad/contact/sweep envelopes can straddle the inlet or need separate designed support over it; reject this machine if neither fits. This is a specific floor/support redesign, not an averaged 8.4 m² load.

The proposed **receiver-renewal** alternative costs a scaffold with separately located bases, ties, working and loading bay, guarded access and rescue, plus a separate hoist/receiver cradle and **two independently discharged temporary roof-water branches**. A single lift basket is not a receiver hoist or a path onto the roof. The temporary branches are spatially drawn to the existing normal N/W and broad E receiving systems, respectively. Their inlets, supports, joints and full-event capacities are yet to be designed and witnessed. The scaffold and temporary catchment cannot load an unqualified zinc hem, roof underlay, south-door head, cellular-glass face, outlet body or disabled receiver. If these contacts and wet boundaries cannot fit at the source, change the collector/component division or court/eave access architecture before RWT-07.

## Release, export, reinstatement and occupation

1. While the original high receiver, normal liner and full 1800 ground relief are live, build and inspect independent supports for two temporary roof openings and both long downroutes. Check full-debris access and release/replacement of each temporary joint. The normal +7100 eave and upper protected water layers are not anchors. Locate scaffold bases and ties around the two service lanes, south door, upper windows and both inlet/receiver territories. Provide a guarded path and rescue while every installed module is added; do not bridge an open drain with an unsupported jack.
2. Install both temporary captures from a guarded scaffold position with the permanent receiver still functioning, then test the actual roof arrival and the combined direct-court event, one outlet blocked at a time and with appropriate receiving levels. Observe leakage into the fixed wall/threshold construction and establish the actual first susceptible boundary with MAT/OAS. Only after a functioning alternate full-duty route is witnessed may the permanent receiver inlet be isolated and its retained water controlled.
3. Expose actual roof laps, guard and brackets in the real release order; independently cradle the **whole** receiver body and any residual water. A hoist supported by designed scaffold/structure (not by the AWP basket or detached zinc/collector) translates it away from x5900 and lowers it into an excluded court handling bay. Protect the south door below the suspended load. The old receiver's length, mass, fittings and withdrawal direction are **not authored**, so no nominal 500 module or one-person basket load is assumed. The replacement body must travel inward through its *actual* 2400 finished opening or a separately designed exterior route, be lifted and reinstated intact, and restore roof, air, thermal, support and water interfaces; a cut-up outgoing body is not a replacement proof.
4. During a stopped lift, power loss, gust, rain, worker evacuation or delayed repair, the two supported temporary water paths must continue by gravity with both ground receivers observed. No operator moves a suspended receiver to rescue drainage. Keep roof work, the mouth and downpipe splash zones closed to ordinary occupation; retain a verified south exit and north lowered/recovery route outside falling-load/wet-flow territories. Demonstrate ground controls, manual/auxiliary lowering and recovery from a blocked access bay; the machine manual's rescue control is not a scaffold/hoist rescue plan.
5. Reinstall and test the original roof receiver and both ground paths, then remove the temporary catcher and each branch while permanent collection remains live; inspect disturbed supports, roof laps, floor bridge, court threshold and water marks. Normal 1800 relief and ordinary occupation return only after load-rated flush floor/covers and dry/wet boundaries are reinstated. The temporary system cannot become an inaccessible permanent third collector.

**Disposition:** this is a finite geometry and failure comparator with a *specific dual-destination roof-water development alternative*. It does not qualify an operating receiver-replacement method. Before RWT-06 exit, ROF must define the actual collection/receiver body and upstream water/lap interface; RWT must produce a coupled intercept/head/joint/event result, including both complete destinations and first harm; HAD/MAT/STR/IAS must resolve supported pad/scaffold/hoist/water boundaries and protected movement; DPL must bind both site receiving levels. BCR/EPR/PHY/DEL then test the actual temporary works and installed operation. Those later admissions cannot repair an internally missing receiver, route or worker escape. If the two routes, support and renewal cannot physically coexist, reject this candidate and revise the roof/collector access architecture rather than mark the node complete.
