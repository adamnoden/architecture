# RWT-06 — protected ground reception challenger

**2 November 2026. A simpler alternative to high temporary B is worth developing; neither unshielded free fall nor this enclosure is accepted.** This compares a closed-to-ordinary-occupation service state in the selected open court, while the south high receiver is renewed. It leaves the [rejected one-piece B](rwt-06-live-receiver-renewal.md) and the [unfinished split B](rwt-06-split-receiver-trial.md) as distinct evidence. It does not change the ordinary roof-water system, adopt a site outfall, accept a maintenance operation or close RWT-06. It may avoid a high-level transfer and the corner B-removal problem **if** the complete protected state can be made and reversed while both roof layers and the court remain safe.

The [source-pinned sensitivity](studies/ground-catcher-challenge.mjs), [ledger](studies/ground-catcher-challenge.json) and [sheet](studies/ground-catcher-challenge.svg) use selected HAD and MAT edge values plus the [open-loggia event](rwt-06-open-loggia-event.md). The previously examined accessible shallow, top-renewable impact collector and open apron remain a [conditional ground-receiver family](../../docs/research/roof-shedding-and-ground-receivers.md), not an ordinary free-shedding default. Fully removable service screens, gates, catches, deck drains, supports and equipment are temporary apparatus; their actual selected constituents, fire/wind performance and removal/restoration need qualification. No permanent material change or automatic exemption is assumed.

## Unshielded fall is not the protected state

At the selected south edge the primary hem is y4450/+7006.738 and the transferred secondary drip y4470/+6913.887; the façade is y4250. In an intentionally idealised gravity-only probe, a secondary droplet with a constant **chosen**, wallward 0.2 m/s horizontal velocity reaches that wall after 1.1 s at **+978.84**, within the selected south leaf's +19…+2119 height and, at x6000, its meeting joint. At 0.3 m/s it reaches **+4276.09**, within the first-floor rough window height +4200…+6300 at x5150. These velocities are not measured wind or a frequency estimate, and the model has no drag, splash or actual water-resistance data. They show why a vertical-fall line and nominally closed door/window are not enough to establish a dry boundary.

A primary-drop ray cannot simply be continued to the door: at the same ideal 0.2 m/s it intersects the transferred secondary slope around y4425.73/+6934.53 before the wall. Where the actual secondary is continuous and intercepts it, the primary is redirected. This is a source geometry check, not proof of the real sheet's continuity, joints or response. Conversely, a primary drop with chosen **courtward** 0.7 m/s reaches the trial +5540 platform at y4832.8, inside its y4780–6150 deck. This is an ideal trajectory, not a platform wetting prediction, but shows that clearing the wall alone does not keep the working deck dry.

## Route and failure conditions

The proposed wet path is **screen/deck → protected court surface → full-width y7000–7350 intake / separate surface relief → normal N/W or broad E route → their respective external receiving territories**. Direct court rain continues on the same court. At the 216 mm/h probe, physical roof **50.4525 m²** plus exposed court **9.61 m²** remains **60.0625 m² / 3.60375 L/s**. This is exactly the existing combined roof-plus-court event, not another roof contribution added to it. Concentrated jets, wind-driven wall rain, deck/screen interception, timing and actual inlet capture can affect local head and wetting without authorising double-counting the area.

The selected surface falls 0 at y4250 to −30 at5750 and −110 at the mouth. The pan-end/masonry/DPC junction at **−30 is an unqualified wetting comparison**, not a demonstrated first-leak level. An 80 mm continuous dam at the −110 mouth reaches −30 before positive overflow head; the +15 south door seam is too late to be the safety control. Do not assume the court is a watertight storage basin. The normal route's 250 clear copper section and 1800 broad relief have only idealised section screens; their actual entrance capture, cover/transition leakage, losses, blockage, operating water levels and downstream heads are not proved. Authored N/W and E outlet −600 / nominal receiver −800 geometries are **not** surveyed site outfalls or proven independent receiving water. A common exterior water level +30 is an explicit failure case in the joined MAT evidence, not redundancy.

## Installation and reversal test

1. With A, the roof layers, court inlet and both receiving routes live, provide the independently supported guarded scaffold, screen frames, protected base apron and closable screen gates without blocking the full-width inlet. Install the screen in segments from a protected position; control rain behind every unfinished edge. Close and verify the temporary screen gates with the building exit still openable by the declared sequential escape operation. Prove continuous base, corners, screen-to-roof top, side terminations and the screen's own drainage before taking credit for it.
2. Independently establish the deck drainage and each ground path, test them at the assigned full combined event with the other named route isolated and the actual receiving heads, and inspect the actual first susceptible door/wall/pan joints. Test the blocked/common cases and safe wet access. This is an internal design requirement before any site or physical qualification, not an assertion that the current source supplies it.
3. Only after those tests, remove one A module with a separate supported handling rig while the roof layers remain intact. The first bolt, 170-down/400-out sweep, retained supports and actual joints must coexist with the screen at y4300/x4300/7700, the deck and workers. An actual member or tool penetrating a screen, roof layer or drip route needs its own sealed detail; the nominal 26 edge gap does not demonstrate it.
4. Restore and test every A module, seal and outlet at its actual full duty. Remove deck drains, then screen panels/gates and base apron in a staged order which maintains the restored roof path and court drainage through the last removable wet piece. Reinspect the roof, façade, door and the inlet; remove all temporary equipment and restore their original conditions. A screen that can be installed only by cutting the live weather boundary, or which cannot be removed without spilling behind it, fails this sequence.

This ground-led option replaces neither the source's safety limits nor the need for a finite apparatus. It **does** avoid asserting that a near-ground gutter can catch all wind and splash or that free shedding is harmless. The source-backed calculation exposes the issue, the screen and gate are a located design proposition, and actual upper/base seals, three-dimensional support/handling, coupled flow, first harm and fault operation remain unproved. RWT-06 stays planned; RWT-07 receives no accepted system from this comparison.
