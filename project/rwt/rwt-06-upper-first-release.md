### Carry the track between the receiver banks

Remove the X13500 crossarm and its posts from the 2,000 mm-wide east receiver in this comparison. Each rail spans from X12250 to X14950. A 100 mm-long bearing head centred on each of those axes ends 200 mm west or 400 mm east of the receiver. The new heads must bear on the underside of the deeper rail and retain uplift. The existing 100 × 50 × 5 mm rail, even crediting full composite action with its cap, deflects **5.14 mm under one 8.5 kN midspan wheel**. That exceeds the 5 mm movement allowance before dead load.

A **160 × 50 × 5 mm RHS** carries the same 70 × 12 mm cap at the existing running level. At most two translated wheels, spaced 1,550 mm apart, can occupy the 2,700 mm bank span on either rail. Assigning their full individual compression bounds to that rail gives at most 3.666 mm elastic deflection from moving wheels. Steel self-weight plus water filling the entire hollow section adds 0.168 mm. The combined **3.834 mm** leaves **1.166 mm** of the movement allowance for support displacement and other omitted effects. The two nominal steel rails with caps weigh 122.7 kg across this crossing. Cap stiffness supplies zero credit.

For a wheel at distance *a* from its nearer simple support, the maximum displacement anywhere along the span is proportional to `a(L²−a²)^(3/2)`. This function is concave up to midspan. When two wheels occupy this span their nearest-support distances sum to the span minus the wheel pitch, so the sum of the two individual peaks is greatest at equal end distances. This gives a continuous upper bound for both directions of travel. The signed wheel caps are deliberately applied independently for each influence envelope. A single bank and rail may need up to **12.501 kN downward or 5.704 kN uplift** before loads from an adjacent span. The upper bound on gross bending stress is 83.8 MPa. Local wheel contact, keeper torsion, bank seats, joint steps, support continuity and delivered section stiffness remain open.
