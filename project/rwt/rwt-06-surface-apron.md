# RWT-06 — can a shallow daylight apron replace the steep north relief chute?

**3 November 2026. Bounded selected-source rejection and site-dependent challenger, not a new roof, ground or terminal selection.** The [source-linked comparison](studies/court-surface-apron.mjs) and [tests](studies/court-surface-apron.test.mjs) consume the exact selected HAD-B/9300 open-north-loggia and joined MAT terminal source, via the existing [terminal receiving audit](rwt-06-terminal-reception.md). The prior audit identified a 100 mm northward miss for each receiver and a 447.5 mm drop over only 350 mm for the eastern relief chute. The question here is **whether avoiding that steep chute by continuing the whole broad relief directly out into a planted landscape is the simpler and safer architectural move**.

## A continuous surface is not free space for people

| Selected cross-section, x (mm) | Gross width | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| 4800–4825 | 25 mm | Left unused edge |
| 4825–5225 | 400 mm | Covered normal carrier reservation; **not** an established walking bridge |
| 5225–5300 | 75 mm | Separation between water routes |
| 5300–7100 | **1800 mm** | Broad open emergency surface relief |
| 7100–7200 | 100 mm | Right unused edge |

All positions come from the *selected* 2400 mm loggia (x4800–7200). The largest contiguous gross strip outside the normal carrier and broad relief is **100 mm**, before walls, guard, supports and people. A wide open daylight continuation cannot be advertised as a *dry pedestrian, rescue or repair escape* simply because it drains by gravity. A protected high-level footbridge, a dry route outside the 2400 mm aperture, or a different architectural section must be separately designed. This is a **plan-area counterexample**, not a claim that the entire 2400 mm loggia is a watercourse in ordinary rain.

The authored eastern terminal narrows that **1800 mm** relief to a **600 mm** visible chute (x6500–7100), a 1200 mm reduction. It could preserve a much larger nominal area beside the outfall, but this is bought with a contraction and a steep terminal, not a demonstrated full-duty surviving path. At 216 mm/h, with the whole original 60.0625 m² wall-line inner-roof-plus-court event assigned to relief, the full **3.60375 L/s** gives ideal *constant 1%* rectangular Manning depths **10.51 mm** for 1800 width versus **20.78 mm** for 600 width. This comparison is intentionally **not** applied to the actual 447.5/350 steep chute: approach contraction, energy change, turbulence, spray, impact, debris and downstream head need a different joined model. Neither ideal depth is freeboard or a discharge pass.

## The full-width surface-apron trial has two real costs

Reuse the predecessor's explicit **300 mm in-receiver overlap** as a geometric comparison target, not a selected splash/capture allowance. The eastern receiver begins at **y13650**; a trial continuous 1800 mm-wide apron must therefore reach **y13950**, or **750 mm beyond** the existing guard/terrace terminal datum y13200. This is **400 mm farther** than even the old 350 mm chute placed wholly northward. Its plan rectangle occupies **1.35 m²**, before edge support, dry access, turning, planting and guard redesign.

The second cost is elevation. As a *deliberately hypothetical* 1% outdoor continuation from the authored **−152.5 mm** terminal crest at y13200, the 750 mm apron ends at **−160 mm**. The still-authored eastern receiver-water plane is **−800 mm**, a further **640 mm lower**. Thus a 1%-fall apron followed by the old receiver is **still a waterfall/impact-transfer design**; it has not become a continuous shallow landscape drain. Its terminal must be positively supported, must actually deliver to a non-harmful receiver at all event and fault heads, and must not strike permanent fabric or obstruct independent movement/maintenance. Alternatively, **regrade/recompose the actual external receiver** into a suitable continuous surface system, which changes MAT/DPL/HAD/STR inputs and is conditional on a *real plot*. The quoted −800 is a notional receiver plane, not proof a London plot supplies it.

The normal copper route is **not repaired by the eastern apron**: its separate discharge still misses N/W by 100 mm. The two receivers also still share an unqualified external flood environment. A 300 mm overlap and two different x footprints neither prove jet capture nor independence when the entire north garden floods.

## Disposition

1. **Reject** a full-width 1800 mm apron as a simple no-change fix for the current north mouth: it occupies the only apparent dry passage, still needs a supported 750 mm projection/receiving area, and leaves 640 mm to the present unverified water plane.
2. **Retain** an open surface-to-landscape drainage family as a *site-conditional architectural challenger*, but only paired with a separately operable dry/guarded route, actual foundation/exterior grade, supported weathering, continuous receiver, full-event stage and harmless common-fault disposition. A site that cannot supply that geometry is unsuitable for this particular family.
3. **Do not approve** the alternative 600 mm steep chute merely because it frees lateral area. Its 1800-to-600 transition, actual rapidly varied water, first vulnerable wetting, debris and blocked receiver must be addressed together with the independent N/W terminal and full-mouth failure.
4. **Return to the owning architectural source** for a joined house/exterior section if neither family can meet both walking/maintenance and water escape. RWT-06 remains **planned**; RWT-07 is not unblocked. No flood-protection, supplier, roof, site-level or construction claim is earned.
