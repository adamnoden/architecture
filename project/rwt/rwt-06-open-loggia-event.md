## Head must be compared with the unqualified −30 join

The mouth floor is −110. Consequently a continuous **80 mm** dam has crest −30 and puts a connected pool at the elevation of the pan-end/DPC join **before any positive head needed to discharge over it**. The actual connection from that pool through wall-base/gap/pan details remains to be qualified; no shielding or leak resistance is credited. The inherited 110 mm dam to FFL0 and 125 mm dam to +15 are too late to protect that join. This does not assert that −30 is safe below or watertight above: actual leakage, splash and lower interfaces are unqualified.

The primary [US Bureau of Reclamation Water Measurement Manual, chapter 2 §12](https://www.usbr.gov/tsc/techreferences/mands/wmm/chap02_12.html) defines the critical-energy minimum; [§13](https://www.usbr.gov/tsc/techreferences/mands/wmm/chap02_13.html) distinguishes ideal rectangular-control equations from friction/curvature and approach-velocity corrections (retrieved 2 November 2026). The following algebra applies those relationships to an explicitly hypothetical control, not the actual loggia.

For a hypothetical unobstructed 1800-wide broad crest and negligible approach velocity, critical depth `yc=(q²/g)^(1/3)` gives minimum specific energy `1.5yc`: **5.35 / 8.49 / 11.13 mm** at the three combined inflows. Subtracting these from the geometric 80 mm difference gives **74.65 / 71.51 / 68.87 mm** dam-height comparisons with **zero** tolerance, splash or safety margin. These are idealised reservoir/control energy screens, not actual loggia stage/discharge results or permitted debris heights. Real contraction, blocked width and losses can require more energy; approach velocity changes the stage relation. A 60 mm dam leaves only 20 nominal to the unqualified join and receives no safe-operation pass.

A high tailwater at −30 independently reaches that same wetting comparison; it does not need a debris dam. The normal cover/metal joint is not a qualified pressure envelope. Do not use the depressed invert or deep solum to infer that backed-up water remains safely contained. A check valve closing the normal route cannot discharge the incoming court rain.
