## Bounded result

Compared separate external drops, accessible lower convergence, high-level headers/transfers, accessible enclosed service-zone routes and inaccessible embedded routes. Separate drops are the ordinary comparator; lower convergence remains conditional. The study refuses both minimum-pipe-count optimisation and automatic redundancy. The generic graph distinguishes a local blocked drop from a shared downstream obstruction.

The principal house-facing constraint is the courtyard crossing. A court-side downpipe does not reach an external handoff merely by reaching the ground. Any low-level crossing needs a real section through structure/service territory, levels, fall/head, joints, access and removal; high-level transfer needs its own viable route and does not remove direct court rainfall. No current service corridor, undercroft, outfall or gravity verdict is invented. An essential missing crossing must cause a design rewind or remain unselected, not be relabelled later technical detail.

Primary Historic England and manufacturer guidance grounds inspection, route simplification and system-specific joint/support/removal requirements. Product spacings, sealants and hydraulic numbers are not imported into general doctrine. The licensed standard remains unaccessed as recorded in RWT-01.
