# Copper crossing: what an openable floor route actually changes

**30 October 2026 · bounded RWT-METALS comparison · not for construction.** Starting authority: main `5a28e4d5d0f367cd8c3cda10788966b23c93f1a1`. This develops the [access-first direction](copper-access-first-result.md) alongside the [raised-terminal section](raised-terminal-section.md). It changes no house geometry, threshold, floor specification, water level or material selection. RWT-METALS remains planned.

## Decision

**Carry an openable, segmented north crossing forward as a conditional comparator, with its two threshold crossings treated as distinct renewal tasks. Do not describe a removable family-room cover as access to the entire route.** Segmentation can remove the need to pull one uninterrupted carrier through the whole house band. That advantage is not earned until the first release, threshold pieces and room-side lifting sequence are shown together.

The located comparison identifies a **3415 mm family-room run between two 417.5 mm threshold/support bands**. Those bands remain even when the interior floor is openable. A doorway supplies passage above its threshold; it does not make the construction below that threshold removable. A short crossing with an intact 220 mm flange still cannot pass through a 160 mm bore.

| Territory | Coordinates, mm | Proposed renewal task and retained construction |
|---|---|---|

| Family-room floor | y8167.5–11582.5 | Open discrete floor components; release supported pipe modules and lift them into a separately reserved handling/set-down area |
| Garden threshold | y11582.5–12000 | Independently release its crossing piece; retain threshold support/weather boundary and a usable exterior approach |

These are plan bands from the house, not engineered clear passages. The old 650 mm opening is an unadopted comparison. Its proposed continuation through the room would interrupt floor finish, support, insulation and occupation; it is not an existing service hatch. Furniture relocation and temporary loss of the court/garden route are maintenance states for HAD to assess, not permanent furniture-free territory assumed here.

The historical continuous carrier required a 4450 mm body and an authored 5250 × 1600 mm exterior extraction reservation. With accessible modules, the longest **unopened plan band** could be 417.5 mm, but the corresponding pipe piece must also include its actual tails, engagement and release needs. **No 417.5 mm removable component, reduced garden corridor or maximum module length is selected.** A piece trapped by its flanges can remain unrenewable despite a short wall crossing. Staggered joints, different supports or a different route may alter the comparison.

## The floor reserve does not contain an unallocated service slot

The house source has a nominal **300 mm ground-floor reserve**, not a resolved load-bearing, insulated ground-floor assembly. The earlier ACO shallow-cover comparison supplies a **50 mm cover/frame-depth comparator**; it did not establish a selected non-plastic, insulated or watertight hatch. Use it here only to expose depth consumption.

For a centred 220 mm flange envelope on a horizontal pipe, let c be cover depth, i additional insulation above the flange, g the remaining gap above it, and f the retained fall between the two comparison slices. A necessary depth below FFL is:

`near depth = c + i + g + 220; far depth = near depth + f`.

This deliberately excludes the lower support, enclosure, water boundary, bolt/tool projections and removal sweep. It is a lower-bound layer comparison, not an assembled elbow section. The flange is placed at the historical crossing end slices to screen the same envelope at both elevations; actual joint-face locations are unknown.

| Conditional arrangement | Near bottom | Far bottom with retained 60 mm fall | Comparison with 300 mm reserve |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| c=50, i=0, g=0 | −270 | −330 | 30 mm remains near; far exceeds reserve by 30 mm, before all excluded duties |
| c=50, i=100, g=10 | −380 | −440 | Exceeds reserve by 80/140 mm |

The second row borrows the earlier study's 100 mm insulation and 10 mm gap as **sensitivity inputs**, not thermal or working-clearance requirements. Neither row is an approved floor build-up. Counting only the bare 108 mm tube would omit **112 mm** of diameter at the intact flange. Using smaller fittings, moving the joints, staggering their stations or changing the floor arrangement requires its own connected comparison; this result does not reject every openable route.

At one common longitudinal station, two 220 mm envelopes on the retained 400 mm centres occupy 620 mm. In the old 650 mm opening they leave only 15 mm at each outside edge before support, hardware and tools. Retaining the old 35 mm side allowances gives 690 mm before sleeve wall or running clearance. These bounds concern the **same-station paired arrangement**; they are not universal minimum widths for staggered or separately accessed modules.

## W1 and W2 must survive different operations

**W1** is water conveyed inside the carrier and its service joints. **W2** is the retained court pan/collector boundary and fixed penetrations protecting adjacent construction. The [HAD court trial](../had/had-03-court-levels.md) places paving at 0, pan top at −180 to −215 and a trial threshold reserve down to −45; the [first-failure register](../had/had-03-court-threshold-first-failure.md) explicitly withholds an actual lowest susceptible joint. Those drawn levels do not locate a selected outlet or admit a flange behind the threshold.

Before locating the first demountable W1 joint, provide an actual fixed outlet/stub section through W2 and the supported threshold. Show which side of W2 is accessible, how leaked or flood water behaves there, and which boundaries remain intact while the joint is open. An openable interior lid cannot supply the missing fixed penetration or make a low well dry. The [passive site-interface comparison](passive-court-site-interface.md) and raised-terminal result remain controlling: no deeper local trench, pump dependence or inaccessible groundwater tanking is introduced to rescue the fit.

Develop this falsifiable sequence on an actual assembly: independently divert the contributing rain; support retained pieces and the removable module; open its floor access without disturbing threshold support; remove fasteners and release the specified seals; perform the complete elbow/module sweep; renew each short threshold crossing by its demonstrated path; reinstate and test W1 separately from retained W2. The first right-angle terminal release does not prove the later straight-module lift. Opposed flat faces may permit a lateral release, but actual locating features, gasket adhesion and tools determine that result.

## Local cutting is not yet a complete renewal method

**31 October 2026 continuation, from main `6c3c7c6d4c5fabd1112b13b86d309edfa1db4df3`.** Keep conventional cut-and-replace copper as a serious comparator to the flanged modules. It can consume a replaceable pipe length without consuming permanent fabric. The missing operation is **reinstatement between retained ends**, not merely cutting out the failed piece. This is an assembly constraint; it does not establish that local copper repair is impossible.

The [shared compatibility record](../../docs/research/roof-rainwater-material-compatibility.md#local-copper-repair-evidence--31-october-2026) now distinguishes an actual small coupling envelope, published small-bore slip ranges and a separate 108 mm slip-coupling listing. Aalberts N1 item 89601 is published at 60 × 114 × 114 mm, much smaller than the 220 mm flange. Its ordinary coupling description does not establish full travel over a retained tube, required engagement or a demonstrated replacement procedure. Do not turn its envelope, generic solder metadata or absence of a supplied gasket into an installed repair approval.

For a straight replacement between two fixed, coaxial tails, a stopped socket needs axial insertion movement. If neither retained end can move and the opening supplies no demonstrated alternative insertion path, cutting out the old piece does not explain how the new piece engages both ends. This is a conditional geometric deduction, not a claim about the unverified internal stop detail of item 89601. A correctly specified stopless sleeve may supply the missing assembly movement: park it wholly clear of the cut, place the replacement, then move it to its qualified joint position. Its travel, usable straight tail, engagement, tolerances and tools must all fit **before** joining. The manufacturer's small-bore repair explanation supports that mechanism, not extrapolation to an unlisted size.

Show both end joints together, including whether the sleeves can be parked on the replacement length before it is lowered. Parking need not occur on the retained tails if the actual replacement and sleeve geometry permits that alternative.

There is positive counterevidence to any assertion that a 108 mm end-feed slip coupling does not exist: WRAS listing 251015004 names Linhai Hengye 5270S108. That is a recoverable lead, not a matched substitute for Endex. The retrieved listing supplies no installed drawing, sleeve parking length, material/consumable schedule or qualified local repair procedure. Obtain those for the actual product before using it to remove the flange from the crossing design. No supplier contact, purchase or availability assurance is represented here.

### Apply the renewal test to all three territories

| Territory | What cutting could change | What reinstatement must still demonstrate |
|---|---|---|
| Open family-room run | Remove a damaged section upward after its supported ends are cut; avoid requiring every service break to carry a large flange | Room to park and translate actual sleeves or move a released end; full joining, cleaning and inspection access; controlled cut locations and enough sound straight tube for the proposed repair |
| Court threshold band | A smaller, plain-ended crossing piece could be considered separately from the room module | Exposure of both necessary joint positions, an actual withdrawal/insertion path, and protection of the fixed W2 outlet, threshold weathering and bearing while W1 is repaired |
| Garden threshold band | Local replacement could reduce the length needing external handling | The same end-release and reinstatement proof, plus usable exterior access and the existing receiving-water/groundwater constraints |

These are proposed intervention tasks, not selected cut stations or accepted removable lengths. The 3415 mm room band is not automatically the replacement length; the 417.5 mm threshold band is not automatically a removable pipe component. Retaining an immovable sleeve or outlet can leave a short piece trapped. Success on the open room length does not prove either threshold operation.

For the studied 108 mm Endbraze family, the existing brazing-only boundary remains. The manufacturer's instruction calls for heating around the joint, then cooling undisturbed, cleaning and flushing. As a **design consequence**, access for viewing or cutting from above is insufficient evidence of access for that whole operation. Show the actual hot-work position and protection of retained W2, adjoining joints, insulation, finishes and support. A metal/mineral material label does not establish tolerance of the joining operation. If the necessary protection or working space requires dismantling the fixed water or load-bearing boundary, this local repair variant fails the stated renewal goal; relocate the service break or change the route.

The comparison therefore remains two honest alternatives: accessible mechanical release with its actual seal/hardware burden, or consumptive local pipe repair with its actual insertion and hot-work burden. Neither receives a lower floor depth or narrower opening from catalogue diameter alone. Record the replaced extent, retained sound tube and finite repair allowances for the chosen method; do not promise indefinitely repeatable cutting or remaking of a brazed joint. Reinstatement must restore and test W1 while keeping W2 independently intact.

**Earned by this continuation:** a separate reinstatement failure test, a positive 108 mm slip-product lead, and a sharper connected renewal comparison. **Still unearned:** a selected repair assembly, constituent pass, clearance, thermal/support solution, physical demonstration or hydraulic acceptance. The generated comparison sheet and its 220 mm flange scenarios are unchanged.

## A retained court tail changes the maintenance boundary

**31 October 2026 continuation, from main `7e1ec1c76ac1bda454a6710baa085f0eb4b52e0f`.** Compare the separately renewable court-threshold piece above with a second allocation: **retain the pan attachment and a continuous outlet tail through the court threshold, and put the first service break in the accessible room-side run.** This is a design hypothesis about which fabric each repair consumes. It earns no installed-fit, constituent, hydraulic or renewal pass.

There is now a more specific construction starting point for the fixed attachment. The [shared evidence record](../../docs/research/roof-rainwater-material-compatibility.md#fixed-copper-outlet-evidence--31-october-2026) binds CDA detail 10.6B: copper pan, continuous soldered attachment to a supported sheet flange, and a separately identified brazed flange-to-pipe joint. Aurubis also describes rigid copper roof-outlet connections. These are roof-detail precedents, with their stated scope and support limitations; neither is an approved court outlet or a complete pressure-rated carrier assembly. The sheet flange is a fixed construction interface, distinct from the demountable plumbing flange previously screened at 220 mm.

Locate the alternative using the existing bands, without inventing a component length. If the fixed pan connection is at or courtward of y7750 and the first service joint is roomward of y8167.5, its continuous retained assembly must span the entire **417.5 mm** threshold band plus the actual end positions. The assembly includes any required turn, joints and supports; moving a service break cannot erase their depth. This does not select a horizontal side outlet, a 417.5 mm tail, a fitting size or a new outlet level. The first room joint must have actual access clear of threshold bearing and weathering, rather than merely lie one drawing coordinate beyond the band.

| Intervention | Construction retained by this alternative | Consequence to demonstrate |
|---|---|---|
| Clean the inlet/strainer | Pan attachment, outlet and threshold tail | Inlet access independent of room-floor opening; debris removal without damaging the fixed joints |
| Replace the accessible room carrier | Pan attachment and the complete threshold tail | A proved first release at the room joint, full module sweep or local repair/reinstatement, and independent support; the earlier stopped-socket trap remains applicable |
| Replace a failed threshold tail or its pan attachment | Only the construction outside the defined larger repair package | Plan access to its fixed connections and threshold interfaces, temporary water management, reconstruction and separate W1/W2 reinstatement checks; ordinary room-carrier renewal does not perform this task |

The alternative moves the service-break burden into the room and leaves a larger permanent repair package. Compare that tradeoff explicitly against a separately removable crossing. **The tail still conveys W1.** Assigning its eventual replacement to the pan/threshold intervention does not make leakage from its concealed bore safe or contained by W2. Provide a continuous passive route by which such leakage becomes observable without wetting vulnerable floor or threshold fabric. A retained tail with no credible leak-observation and eventual-repair route fails this comparison; presumed exceptional copper life cannot rescue it.

During room-carrier renewal, retaining the circumferential pan attachment also leaves the outlet bore open toward the service area unless a separate measure closes or diverts it. Show independent incoming-rain management, the actual temporary closure/diversion where needed, and a surviving safe escape route before opening the W1 service joint. Do not count the retained sheet joint as isolation of the bore, or call the service area dry because its cover opens indoors. No unspecified cap, valve or seal receives material approval here.

Keep support and movement equally explicit. A rigid attachment can receive forces from a restrained tail, carrier or handling operation. The actual fixed/sliding supports and movement provision must protect the pan joint; the roof precedent does not demonstrate that separation. The room joint still needs its complete seal, fastener, tool and removal envelope. The existing 300 mm reserve and 220 mm flange screens remain unchanged, as do the passive-groundwater and receiving-level constraints.

**Earned:** a construction-based outlet starting point and a second, explicit allocation of renewal work. **Still open:** the connected section that chooses between these allocations, actual constituent disclosure, supported outlet/tail geometry, pressure duty, passive leak observation and demonstrated renewal of both the room carrier and the larger fixed package. The current diagram continues to show the separately renewable-threshold comparator; it is not a drawing of this retained-tail alternative.
