# Raised court route: copper terminal and release space

**30 October 2026 · bounded RWT-06 section comparison · not for construction.** Starting main: `bb8e94adbbbe705b5609ba5d1d0e98727a20a4e1`. This joins the [passive receiving-level comparison](passive-court-site-interface.md) to the newer [copper access-first direction](copper-access-first-result.md). It retains historical dimensions only to test a proposition; it adopts no floor, terminal, house height or site.

## Decision

**Do not carry the earlier 435 mm relative house raise forward as a ready-made accessible copper-terminal solution.** That raise only met the historical receiving-level comparison. A centred 220 mm flange envelope at the historical exterior-interface pipe axis already reaches **5 mm below** the fixed comparison ground plane, before downward release, supports, tools or a complete elbow are included.

Develop the actual terminal and its release space together with the raised or openable floor section. Keep the accessible-route direction; this result does not restore the blind sleeve as the default. Nor does it select a crawlspace, prescribe a working height or solve the court threshold. STR owns the connected support/floor proposal; RWT supplies these dimensional interface constraints and must re-derive water levels once the real arrangement exists.

## What is being placed, and where

The historical north study supplies axes x5800/6200, a 4250 mm crossing from y7750 to y12000, near carrier centre −420 and a 60 mm fall, giving **−480 at the exterior wall interface**. Its floor/bridge underside is −285. These remain unadopted envelopes; the historical polymer carrier and sleeve are not reinstated by using their coordinates.

The [Aalberts item 89604 page](https://aalberts-ips.co.uk/products/item/89604/) and [N1FMGI assortment](https://aalberts-ips.co.uk/products/detail/n1fmgi/), checked 30 October 2026, list the 108 mm adaptor as **19 × 220 × 220 mm**. This comparison places the 220 transverse envelope symmetrically around a horizontal pipe axis. That is an explicit geometric placement, not a measured installed assembly. Only the transverse extent is used; the apparent width of the rectangle on the sheet is **not** an axial fitting dimension. Exact face, collar, gasket, backing ring, bolts and orientation remain unresolved under RWT-METALS. Neither the catalogue's metal-sealing field nor its pressure category supplies a gasketless drainage-joint approval.

The near/far slices are at **y7750/y12000**, not claimed terminal-face locations. A joint lying farther outside along the falling pipe is lower. An actual fitting turned, offset or relocated requires its own envelope. The elbow, second flange, tails and support can extend beyond the single flange considered here, so a passing flange comparison cannot pass the terminal.

## The necessary raise, including release travel

Use one fixed datum. Let the old house FFL be zero, old schematic exterior grade be −150, and the new FFL be raised by **Δ**. Retain the old internal geometry. At the exterior-interface slice, the flange bottom is then `Δ − 590`. Let **b** be downward release travel, **g** the specified remaining gap below the swept flange, and **s** any rise of the comparison access plane above the old ground. Then:

`Δ − 590 − b ≥ −150 + s + g`

or **`Δ ≥ 440 + b + g + s`**.

This is a **necessary flange nonpenetration condition for the stated overlapping plane**, not a safe-access height. The plane must remain beneath the projected flange throughout the tested sweep. A real platform edge, falling ground or open void can remove that obstruction; its complete removal path and safe working arrangement then need a different check. At equality with g=0 the flange touches the plane. No actual release travel or required gap has been established. The access plane is deliberately held at the synthetic ground datum for the first comparisons; no dry working platform or surveyed ground under the house is thereby asserted.

The earlier hydraulic-interface sensitivity required Δ≥435 against receiving water fixed at the old grade. If that receiving event is raised by **p**, preserving the **historical** condition and this flange bound together gives:

**`Δ ≥ max(435 + p, 440 + b + g + s)`**.

The first term remains a historical comparison, not an admitted receiving-water limit for the copper arrangement. The actual bore, elbow, leak outlet, inlet/relief operating head, downstream backwater and threshold first-failure boundary must all be re-derived. The access-plane rise s and receiving-event rise p are separate inputs; favourable water at one location does not establish dry access elsewhere.

All dimensions are millimetres; s=0 in the table. The **50/50/550** values are sensitivity inputs, not selected engineering allowances or a recommended house height. If the last case also raises the access plane by 550, its actual gap becomes **−490**: moving building and ground together gives no relative benefit. Raising the receiving event with them likewise provides no improvement to the earlier water-level difference.

The old floor underside would stand only **300 mm above** a plane at that fixed ground datum when Δ=435. This is merely the vertical difference between two planes; it is not proof of an undercroft, an entry route, a working position or person-access suitability. A floor that opens from above and an accessible external terminal are different arrangements with different disturbance, thermal, moisture, handling and support duties.

## Changes that require a new section

Continuing the historical fall beyond y12000 shifts the flange-contact bound from 440 to **441.412 mm at a 100 mm extension**, or **442.824 mm at 200 mm**. These are slope sensitivities, not claims about where a fitting ends. A flat extension or an elbow has different geometry. The historical carrier-end projection cannot silently supply the actual flange face.

Moving the whole carrier down 100 mm changes the contact bound to **540 mm**, while increasing the near upper gap from 25 to 125. Moving it up 50 changes the contact bound to390 but leaves the near upper gap at **−25**. This exposes the conflict between making ground clearance and occupying the floor above; no shifted case inherits the old hydraulic condition. A deeper floor or lower service well may restore one fit while worsening receiving and passive-dryness requirements.

Raising the house also does not remove the [existing transverse conflicts](copper-access-first-result.md#quantified-cost-of-retaining-the-blind-route). Two 220 envelopes at 400 centres occupy **620 mm**, leaving just **15 mm each side** in the old 650 opening. Keeping the earlier 35 mm side allowances needs **690 mm before sleeve walls and running clearance**. A 220 intact flange still cannot pass through a 160 bore. None of these arithmetic spaces is a tool or structural clearance.

## Handoff and verification

The next connected section must locate the actual joint faces, support feet, complete terminal envelope, required release direction/travel and its exit/set-down route. Retain independent supports and independent temporary rain diversion; freeing a terminal is not the same as withdrawing a carrier. Match the floor footprint and accessible cover/approach to that operation, then establish the real receiving-water, groundwater and annular-leak limits. The [HAD first-failure register](../had/had-03-court-threshold-first-failure.md) still governs susceptible threshold interfaces. No amount of geometric clearance substitutes for that water-boundary result.

The source pins the historical geometry JSON, RWT-METALS catalogue-input module and passive-interface result to their Git blobs and guards the numerical inputs. A change requires reassessment. Run `node project/rwt/studies/raised-terminal-section.mjs --check` to verify the generated JSON and SVG. Independent arithmetic/architectural review and rendered-sheet inspection are separate from repository CI; neither establishes physical access, load capacity or hydraulic safety.

**Earned result:** the minimal historical raise fails even the located flange-envelope comparison; the additional release/ground relationship is explicit; raising cannot fix the upper, lateral or blind-passage conflicts. **RWT-06 remains planned.** No house geometry, support, material exception, safe working height, outfall or RWT-07 selection is adopted.
