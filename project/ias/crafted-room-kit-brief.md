# Crafted Room Kit — beauty, gravity and workshop-made interiors

**Owner direction:** 15 November 2026, recorded in [#1555](https://github.com/adamnoden/architecture/issues/1555). This is a critical part of the owner's original inspiration for Ovid's House, not an optional access-panel accessory. It refines the [workable-interior plan](workable-interior-design-plan.md); the [owner-house charter](../owner-house-charter.md) governs priority. Georgian/Victorian character belongs to this house's aesthetic brief, not universal doctrine.

> A room can slot into place in an hour and be as beautiful as if weeks was spent on it.

This is the owner's ambition, not an achieved installation time. Design a complete, exceptionally crafted room that happens to come apart. Move the craftsmanship into the workshop; do not remove it. No assembly, manufacturer, material exception or owner design verdict is selected by this brief.

## 1. Beauty is an acceptance condition

The room must aim to match or surpass the owner's finest Georgian/Victorian and contemporary fixed-interior references **on architectural appearance alone**. Convenience is not compensation for an inferior room. HAD must present fair, equally resolved furnished comparisons at the same viewing conditions and detail level; the owner supplies the aesthetic verdict at the existing design gates. A renderer, score or technical pass cannot pronounce the room more beautiful.

Compose panel proportions, timber grain, tile patterns, borders, mouldings, skirting, cornice, doors, window reveals, lighting, sockets and grilles together. Standardise hidden interfaces without forcing the visible room into a manufacturing grid. Module boundaries should coincide with deliberate architectural joints, not look like office access flooring. Conceal frames wherever practicable; investigate metal for strength and stability where it earns its cost. Use restrained brass or a separately specified brass-coloured finish to signal selected interfaces, not a gold outline around everything. Record actual substrate, alloy, surface treatment and hidden constituents under MAT; appearance is not material identity.

Develop timber-finished and tile-finished floor variants and timber-panelled and tiled wall variants. One shared interface language does not require identical substrates, fixings or dimensions in every exposure. Do not assume timber or a dry modular assembly is suitable in every wet or protected zone. No blanket final floor pitch, panel size or finish thickness is authorised; earlier 400 mm studies remain comparisons.

## 2. Mechanism hierarchy: borrow first, then simplify

**Gravity carries the weight. Geometry locates the part. Minimum necessary restraint prevents unsafe movement.**

Start with hung wall panels and seated floor modules. Compare concealed cleats/Z-profiles, simple bearing rails/ledges, locating stops and accessible screws or other non-actuated retainers. Prefer no mechanisms in ordinary use. A safety restraint earns its place; omit neither anti-lift restraint nor lateral stability to advertise zero moving parts. Hinges, spring catches, interlocking release chains and special tools require a demonstrated advantage over the simple comparator.

Research existing work before custom hardware. The precedent study must contain primary-source drawings/instructions, dates, assembly constituents, removal clearance, load/support assumptions, failure cases, material conflicts and a **borrow / adapt / reject** disposition. Research leads from the owner's discussion include traditional panelled joinery and floor borders, French cleats/metal Z-clips, Monarch and Armstrong wall mounting, Lindner timber/ceramic access floors, Howe Green infill covers, Junckers dry timber systems and DryTile. These are **leads to verify, not adopted products, proven performance or endorsements**. Commercial installation speed or component ratings must not be inherited by a different whole assembly.

Reject an ordinary domestic finish system whose default removal needs a bespoke lifting bridge. First reduce module mass/size, improve the grip or change the lifting direction and first rest. Distinguish a reusable ordinary handling tool from a mechanism built into every panel. Large or unusual pieces need a justified separate handling case, not a concealed exception spread across the room.

## 3. What genuinely removable means

Specify the full operation: isolate any affected service; expose simple retainers; grip; release; support; move to a safe first rest; work; refit; verify seating and restore any disturbed boundary. Show the room with its furniture, radiators, doors and standing space.

A normal module must not require destructive cutting, breaking adjoining grout, stripping neighbouring finish panels, draining unrelated services or dismantling a radiator. Any small releasable trim/stop must be named, accessible and independently replaceable; it cannot create a room-wide dependency chain. Show the first and last panel, internal/external corners, floor perimeter and the top wall panel under its cornice. Leave actual removal travel: a concealed hook trapped by the ceiling is not removable.

For tile-faced modules, separate joints within a prefabricated unit from release joints between units. Do not bridge the latter with grout/adhesive and then claim dry reversibility. Price the perimeter and cut pieces as real manufactured parts, not site filler. Quiet footfall, no rocking/rattling, secure edges, cleaning and tactile solidity are essential. Account for substrate flatness, out-of-square walls, manufacturing/setout tolerance, thermal/moisture movement, timber grain, wear, repeated lifting, damaged edges and a lost retainer. Solve adjustment in an accessible, limited support system, not in hundreds of moving panel parts.

The maintenance objective is **no routine service or finish renewal requiring destruction of finished surfaces**. It is not a claim that plaster is universally defective or that necessary mineral backing must be removed.

## 4. Services and protective boundaries

Keep the permanent structure and the relevant fire, acoustic, airtight and waterproof boundaries independent of routine decorative removal wherever practicable. Ceilings and overhead panels require their own positive retention; hanging-wall geometry does not authorise gravity-only ceiling support. Removing a floor finish must leave safe permanent support or an explicitly controlled opening, not an unguarded hole.

Services have their own supported, segregated, isolatable routes and accessible joints. Skirting, designated panels, reveals and grilles expose those routes without becoming pipe/cable supports by accident. Detail socket and switch positions, electrical enclosures, radiators, door thresholds, ventilation and wet transitions in the room composition. Keep live components protected while a decorative cover is off. A lifted finish over a permanent deck **does not** expose services below that deck: draw the actual access layer. Do not promise arbitrary service access behind every visible panel.

Preserve current floor/finish reservations until an owning architectural rewind changes them. Check complete carriers, pads, seals, coatings and consumables against the whole-house material policy; concealed polymers are not exempt. State cleanability, water detection/drainage, dust/pest paths, corrosion and incompatible metal/contact risks for the actual assembly. No unqualified product or safety performance is implied by a pleasing section.

## 5. Prefabrication and the one-hour ambition

Use one measured current-house room as the first complete demonstrator, with an ordinary dry-room timber scheme and a credible tile/wet-edge variant. Assemble and inspect the kit off-site against defined datums before proposing rapid delivery. Include labelled components, replaceable spares, handling protection, installation order, border/corner pieces and long-term remanufacture from durable records. Show how an imperfect building is surveyed and adjusted before the finishing kit arrives.

Define a reproducible timing protocol before quoting speed: exact room/finish scope; crew size; tools; room dimensions; prepared-shell tolerances; installed carriers and separately tested service infrastructure; staged components; included sockets/grilles/trims; start and finish criteria; and final fit/cleaning checks. Report **workshop labour, shell preparation, transport/carrying/unpacking, finish assembly, service connection/commissioning and snagging separately**, with total labour as well as elapsed time. Do not hide unfinished work outside the stopwatch. The ambition is approximately one hour for the declared prepared-room finish assembly, not construction of the shell or blanket commissioning of utilities.

Aim for no on-site cutting, wet finishing or cosmetic filler to obtain acceptable fit. Treat tolerance failure and corrective work as results, not excuses to redefine the trial. A digital sequence is not a witnessed physical trial. Do not buy, manufacture or commission a mock-up without its actual authorisation. The pre-site book can present a rigorously bounded proposal and protocol; it must not state that one-hour assembly has been achieved without evidence.

## 6. Executable room kit, not another standalone study

Use the shared current-design document, admitted assembly families and compiler contracts. Author stable room/component IDs, finish and support parameters, units, datums, selected/candidate status, service interfaces, release travel, tolerances and evidence references once. Derive the kit layouts, quantities, perimeter/cut parts, visible joints and supported access diagnostics from that source. Keep taste choices explicit rather than embedding them in a renderer.

The concept packet must demonstrate at least **a module-size edit and an opening or service-position edit** through the production path: changed geometry, affected schedules and access results agree; dependent evidence becomes stale or unsupported as appropriate; an unaffected control remains unchanged. Include a missing/invalid reference case. Fixed manufactured hardware must not be silently scaled. Declare unsupported domains and computational debt accurately. The browser editor is a consumer of this same model, not a separate room-kit geometry store. Do not reopen a completed compiler packet just to claim automatic support for a new mechanism.

## 7. Delivery, approval and completion

The [first occupied Study comparison](crafted-room-kit-study.md) records the furnished views, scaled release junctions and executable set-out.

`PCO-CRAFT-ROOM-KIT` records only this owner-direction/graph registration. Its evidence is this brief, the charter/plan links, typed dependency and regression tests. It is not a design result or an owner approval.

`IAS-CRAFT-ROOM-KIT` is one bounded IAS/HAD successor to the completed earlier concept, using the existing assembly integration. It begins independently of the unresolved structural/inlet/TRIAD work. Deliver:

1. A sourced precedent comparison and a decisive simple wall/floor recommendation, including rejected mechanisms and no-change/reuse options.
2. A coordinated furnished-room visual pack with the finest fixed-interior comparator, timber/tile options, near-invisible supports and intentional brass interfaces; representative 1:5 sections and enlarged 1:2/1:1 release/edge joints.
3. A source-bound kit schedule, complete service/access and installation/replacement sequences, a declared assembly-timing protocol and the input-change proofs above.
4. `project/ias/crafted-room-kit-result.md`, linking the actual outputs, affected current sources, exact checks, conflicts, unresolved claims and the questions for the owner.

The new result must enter `TRIAD-GATE` before `HAD-OWNER-DESIGN-REVIEW`. Share early images when available, but do not invent an extra approval or treat that preview as acceptance. Existing `IAS-WORKABLE-DETAILS` then develops the **owner-selected** family: manufacturing interfaces, perimeter details, tolerances, full material schedule, services and proof plan. BCR/ADE/DEL consume it through their existing routes. Final owner design acceptance still precedes plate freeze; physical print acceptance remains separate. No new default external-review or physical-build prerequisite is added to the book.

A failed variant can be rejected with evidence. The overall room-kit result cannot claim success if it supplies only a mechanically removable but aesthetically inferior room, a beautiful image without viable release geometry, or an assembly essay without source-bound design outputs. Surface a precise rebrief decision when no credible candidate survives. Do not silently replace this critical objective with the earlier continuous-finish default or count the registration as its completion.
