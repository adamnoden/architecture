# IAS-03 — Bedroom 4 connected wet-assembly successor

**Working candidate, not adopted house geometry or IAS-03 completion.** This develops the [earlier corrected section](ias-03-bedroom4-wet-section.md); that Mira comparison remains provenance, not a falsely matched JT assembly. The aim is one supported floor, continuous water boundary, actual tray/support/waste, reachable connections and a complete release sequence. A favourable drain datum alone cannot select the room.

## Controlled architectural changes

The accepted source is RH-LAYOUT-REVISION-02, inspected on main `7516984f`. The Bedroom 4 allocation itself remains an unadopted HAD proposal. Keep the 22 deck, 238 framing reserve, 55 service zone and 15 ceiling; do not pay for wet construction by shaving them.

The new wet partitions are explicitly constituted as 90 stud zone + 12.5 dry board + 25 wet assembly = **127.5**, instead of assuming that the undifferentiated 125 envelope already contains a spare 15 on each face. Hold the proposed dry outer faces, so each wet partition face moves inward 2.5. On the west envelope only, the existing 15 finish allocation becomes 25, costing 10 inward. These are architectural comparison dimensions, not structural, fire or acoustic certification. Studs/grounds at no more than 400 mm centres support the proposed 12.5 KERDI-BOARD; board joints, fixtures and hand-support anchors require their actual backing.

The resulting wet clear is **x427.5–2335 / y8295–10822.5**. A retained NE 1000-square tray begins x1335/y9822.5. The NW alternative begins x427.5/y9822.5 and introduces direct shower exposure at the retained west window y9700–10700, sill 750. The separate HAD successor owns the door, enclosure and carrying states, including any change from the former 900 rough aperture.

## Complete floor and wall comparison

The leading conventional floor uses **6 mm floor backerboard**, rather than inheriting the unselected 12.5 carrier from the 26.5 trial. Waterproofing remains a separate continuous system; moisture-resistant cement board is not a waterproof room.

|---|---:|---|
| Gap-filling cementitious bedding | 3 | Authored compressed allowance; Hardie specifies a 6-notched application, not a guaranteed 3 cured thickness |
| Hardie Backer floor board | 6 | Manufacturer floor-overlay product; mechanical fixing, staggered joints and joint reinforcement required |

This is a dimensioned installation target, not a tolerance-free product guarantee. A sample of the actual board, bedding, taped joint, membrane corner and tile must fit the 25 envelope with full support and approved adhesive beds. If it does not, revise the local level and threshold openly. Do not flatten a required joint or starve the tile bed to retain a nominal number. Local triple corners cannot silently use the same single-band allowance.

Use the board manufacturer's minimum 25 mm floor screws, at least 12 per full 1200×800 board in its four-by-three pattern, with 15 edge / 50 corner offsets; modified pieces need their actual fixing layout. All fixings and reinforced board joints sit **below** the continuous membrane. The 22 deck is still a structural reservation: select its grade, support, fixing, load/deflection and moisture conditions with BCR-STRUCTURE. Backerboard is not a replacement structural floor, and neither the 25 finish nor a product load claim proves the 238 framing.

Wet walls use 12.5 KERDI-BOARD + 4.5 solid adhesive allowance + 8 tile = 25. KERDI-BOARD's coating supplies the field water boundary; all joints, washers, corners and penetrations must be completed. On the west envelope, actual fixing/support behind the replaced 15 finish must be reconciled without borrowing cavity or structural depth. Heavy fixtures, enclosure fixings and a hand support connect to designed grounds, not foam, tile or a removable cover.

The DITRA alternative was researched as an uncoupling route, but is not combined with this floor. In particular, ARDEX's AF200 Plus page says it is not suitable for wet areas despite a separate waterproof-plywood FAQ; that ambiguity is not used as permission for this assembly. The current conventional board/fully bonded membrane comparison avoids silently interpreting that boundary. Its remaining product-compatibility checks are stated below.

## Water boundary and first vulnerable edge

Carry KERDI continuously under the tray and removable boxing, across the room, and into the cabinet base before installing fixtures. Continue to KERDI-BOARD walls with KERDI-KEBA/COLL-L and prefabricated corners; retain at least 50 overlap. Movement joints receive the flexible system detail, rather than being bridged rigidly by grout. Pipe penetrations use correctly sized KERDI-MV collars on the actual fixed pipe/sleeve, not a seal against an unrestrained removable cover. Fixing penetrations are deliberately detailed and tested; a dot of silicone on a decorative screw is not the water boundary.

At the two tray-to-wall edges compare KERDI-TS, its own corners, KERDI-FIX and COLL-L, with the tray fully supported in its final position. Verify its bond to the actual JT surface. KERDI-FIX is a required TS system component; do not replace it with a generic sealant. Include CP cutting protection where the exposed wearing seal will be renewed. The inward enclosure joints and external seals still follow the enclosure's own installation instructions.

The doorway comparison is a solid mineral saddle, **crest 15 above wet FFL / 20 above dry FFL**. The membrane turns up and over a supported substrate beneath the wearing saddle, returns at both jambs and connects to the wall boundary. Jamb timber and its cut end stay behind this return. HAD owns the actual bevel/foot crossing, outward leaf, underside clearance and carrying consequences; this is expressly a step, not level access. A flush alternative has easier passage but no raised water stop. Neither choice is an unlimited flood defence.

The saddle is the proposed visible spill boundary only after all lower penetrations and perimeter junctions are shown above or sealed below it. A damaged collar, floor fixing or membrane joint can become the earlier vulnerable location. No emergency floor drain is invented. A stopped shower waste can spill from the enclosure onto the room floor; a supply leak may persist until isolation. The response is visible inspection and reachable isolation, followed by drying and repair; no guaranteed alarm, retention volume or automatic shut-off is credited.


## Tray, first joint and actual renewal reservation

Compare the matched JT FW90 with a conventional **McAlpine ST90CP10-70** alternative. The latter is explicitly a compatibility investigation: a 90 hole alone does not establish local tray thickness, clamping engagement, seat seal, support or a joint product warranty. The named 9.5 and 12.5 washers are alternatives selected by the actual tray outlet thickness, not stacked spacers. The manufacturer's 35 axis dimension is below the **body top**, with 70 to its bottom. It must not be read from the wet tray face.

The [located calculation and A3 sheet](studies/bedroom4-connected-assembly.svg) compare a west-facing NE corner outlet at (1470,10687.5), a west perimeter axis x502.5, cabinet entry y9500 and an explicitly **unselected stack reference** y9375. A north-facing body cannot follow the old zero-radius turn: the 60 centreline lead is shorter than the body's 82-to-union dimension, before adding any connector. The west-facing alternative removes that immediate clash but remains conditional on the actual joint and all moulded leg positions.

The straight **T29-LN** connector is a stronger alternative to inventing the supplied angled bend's output datum: its manufacturer drawing identifies a coupling nut/flat washer for a 1½ BSP waste. Its dimension 56 excludes an exploded compression-nut projection. Do not call 82+56 the installed length or transfer a FW90 diameter to this assembly. The real thread/seal engagement, socket insertion, connector diameter and pipe/clip envelope must be drawn before calling this a fitted route.

The NE centreline topology is 2280 including 125 inside the cabinet reference, requiring 41.04 fall at the retained 18 mm/m comparison. A whole 45 tray envelope, maximum 140 support and maximum named 12.5 washer would give a conservative 92.5 body-axis comparison **only if** the local underside and support/pad inclusions hold. A 60 outside fitting comparison then leaves 21.46 at the reference stack. These are admission tests, not measured clearances or manufacturer tolerance bounds. The NW alternative topology 642.5 leaves 50.935 under the same assumptions but incurs the wet-window redesign. The NW drawing also has only 60 from its waste centre to the west axis: its short west leg is not an installed 82-long body plus connector. A south-facing body and actual connection geometry would need a new route. A positive conditional number does not select either route.

Whole housing renewal requires more space than the pipe. The north cover becomes **250 deep ×180 high**, with 18 faces and a 214 internal width. The eccentric axis 135 from the north wall leaves 97/117 to those inner faces. An authored 160-wide component-plus-handling reservation therefore has 17/37 lateral margins. This is **not** a claim that the actual trap body has a particular diameter. The initial removable top/room-side segment is 300 long adjacent to the tray, x1035–1335; a 235 westward withdrawal is a trial task, subject to actual body/connector, thread-release drop, hands/tools and every required leg. Covers release independently, while the continuous floor/wall membrane stays underneath. The wider cover is a real spatial cost that HAD has reviewed in its action model.

For replacement: isolate supply; open the independent cover; support and drain the branch; undo its actual accessible union; hold the lower body through the opened west access while using the supplied flange spanner from above; unscrew and lift out the upper flange, separate the selected gasket, then translate the released body into the opened box; inspect/dry; reassemble with the specified seal and test before closing. This reverses the manufacturer’s threaded installation rather than assuming the whole thread must drop downwards, but the actual hand/tool reach, gasket release and residual vertical clearance remain to be drawn. If the required leg or connection prevents that sequence, reject this renewal arrangement or deliberately redesign it. Top cleaning of a trap basket is not housing replacement. Whole tray replacement additionally disturbs the bonded wall connection: remove enough local tile/band to reinstate full overlaps, renew the wearing seal/cut protection and test. It is not damage-free demountability.

## Cabinet service envelope and the real spatial cost

The back-to-wall Duravit 211509 pan requires an independent cistern. Compare Thomas Dudley Vantage 2 using the current Issue P installation, rather than treating a small cistern body as a complete installation. Its body is 460 wide × 369 high × 125 deep; the manufacturer describes a 500-wide × 200-deep × 800-high furniture application and separately requires allowance for isolation, piping and the button. The two rear mounting positions are 234 apart; their actual supporting grounds and release actions belong in the cabinet.

The coordinated cabinet comparison grows from the former 750 length to **900 gross**, x427.5–727.5 / y8650–9550. This deliberately spends 150 farther south. With authored 18 side, back and front panels, the internal plan is 264 deep × 864 long. Retain the original pan y8800–9155, centre y8977.5, rather than moving it north to rescue the rejected Sigma comparison. A centred 500 application occupies y8727.5–9227.5, leaving 59.5 to the south inner side and 304.5 north. These are clear reservation intervals, not a cistern-plus-stack fit claim. They preserve the 667.5 between pan and tray faces for HAD's actual movement analysis.

The narrower alternatives were challenged against the actual hardware. Geberit Sigma 8's 410 body does not fit a 500 reservation once its 660-wide brackets are included. An 825-gross Dudley cabinet centred on the original pan also fails to contain the centred 500 application within its authored side panels; shifting the pan north restores cabinet margin but reduces the north passage by 50. The 900 comparison pays for both the real service allowance and the more useful passage. It does not add those margins to the manufacturer’s internal component clearances.

Dudley's top datum X must be 440–740 above the actual pan flush-inlet centre Y; the vertical tail requires at least 70 and the horizontal flush-bend dimension B is limited to 260. These are controlled relationships, not permission to guess Y or reuse a generic pan's soil-outlet level. The final cabinet height, actual flush pipe cut, soil connector and stack branch remain unlocated until the Duravit connection drawing and actual product tolerances are reconciled. The 500 application does not contain the adjacent stack. Front/top servicing must remain possible after all water, soil and pneumatic connections, panel fixings and the pan are present.

## Remaining coordinated decisions

The current comparison has no admitted tray/branch route yet: actual support positions, local drain seat/washer, outlet bend and connected stack must be reconciled with the NE/NW routes. A cistern, flush pipe, pan connector, stack/vent and all their release states must fit the real cabinet; a back-to-wall pan does not contain its own cistern. The wall/floor choice requires actual support and product compatibility; its water boundary needs the complete penetration/threshold set and a controlled installation check. These duties remain design work, not a generic request for a professional to make an unfitted room work. IAS-03 and the house source remain unchanged until the coordinated alternative earns adoption.
