# IAS-02W — wet-room and kitchen variants

**Scope:** interior variant design against IAS-01V/01H, IAS-02S and HAD-01S.
**House:** current layout family; no sanitary fit or waterproofing approval.

## Chosen direction for comparison

Use wet-side water control and independently supported fixtures, with ordinary
maintenance biased to accessible cabinet or dry service sides. Keep the quiet
HAD S-A room language; use S-B coherent access joinery only where it actually
improves the task. No universal demountable wet-room cassette is justified.

| Variant | Arrangement | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| W-A conventional wet assembly + accessible fittings | Continuous designed wet substrate/boundary; local replaceable wearing surfaces and reachable components | Leading control; actual complete build-up still required |
| W-B independent dry-side service face | W-A wet boundary retained during ordinary valve/trap/cistern service where geometry permits | Develop at actual service tasks; not every component fits the small V3 aperture |
| W-C tiled removable wet hatch | Wet surface and seals disturbed for routine component access | Reject as routine default; retain only if the real task has no better route and complete boundary reinstatement is designed |
| W-D universal wet cassette | Surface, waterproofing, fixture support and services exchanged together | Reject as house-wide answer; unnecessary coupled duties and product dependence |

## Dimensions: do not hide the wet build-up

The 20 mm intermediate-floor finish allocation is already occupied. The trial
12.5 carrier + 1 membrane + 3 adhesive + 10 tile is 26.5 mm: **6.5 mm deficit**,
before falls, levelling or heating. A 1:80 trial fall across a 1000 mm shower
field would add **12.5 mm geometric level difference**. This is a comparison
slope only, not a prescribed system, drainage capacity or statutory criterion.

A wet-wall trial 12.5 carrier + 1 membrane + 3 adhesive + 8 tile is **24.5 mm**.
Against a 15 mm assumed room-side finish budget it costs **9.5 mm extra**.
Where a partition's 125 mm total has not selected separate layers, do not
pretend that a 15 mm allowance already exists on both sides.

The leading design cannot be adopted while saying every finished face/FFL is
unchanged. Compare an actual thinner complete system, a supported substrate
reallocation, or a bounded level/depth rewind. Do not reduce the structural deck,
framing or a safety boundary to pay for tile. Falls, drain body, trap and waste
gradient must appear in an actual section. Ground-floor wet zones have the
separate unresolved 300 mm reserve; they do not inherit a solved floor.

## Fixture and service support

Support basins, WCs, wall cabinets, rails and other consequential loads from
their designed grounds/frame/structure, not the decorative access face or tile.
Finish removal must not drop a supported fixture. Locate anchors and any
waterproof penetrations deliberately, with an inspectable reinstatement method.
No grab-rail or wall-hung WC capacity is inferred from generic board strength.

For basin/trap access, a cabinet door and removable local shelf may outperform
a wall panel. Keep the actual trap/union and tool sweep reachable without
demolishing a worktop. For concealed cisterns, separate operating flush access
from full component/whole-cistern replacement. For shower controls, compare
dry-side service against a complete front-service product rather than demanding
a rear hatch irrespective of geometry.

W-B's ordinary access sequence is: isolate; protect dry-side working space;
release the independent face; support/disconnect the actual component; replace
and test disturbed water/waste connections; inspect/dry; refit the dry face.
The waterproof wet face remains intact only if the actual construction makes
that claim true. A hole through an acoustic/fire partition needs its own duty.

## Small en suite: explicit selected-design hold point

U-ENS3 has 1075 mm nominal clear north/south depth before local additions.
Two added 9.5 mm trial finish costs would leave 1056 mm if those costs truly
fall on opposite affected faces; do not deduct them from unrelated walls.
No three-fixture layout or practical door operation is claimed.

HAD-01S's alternatives remain: actual compact fixture fit, a bounded local
room/door/service-front rewind, or explicit programme reallocation. IAS supplies
the actual build-up and maintenance consequences. A shower rectangle, reduced
symbol or generic tiny basin is not evidence of ordinary usability.

I00-C1's shifted panel trial does not solve this room. Nor does fitting a small
isolation valve prove cistern exchange. Before IAS-03 adopts this occurrence,
the real sanitary/door/support/access arrangement must work or be explicitly
rewound; unresolved remains visible in the selected-family handoff.

## Kitchen and utility

Keep the perimeter kitchen control and a clear pantry approach. Do not introduce
an island with hidden water/waste solely to enrich the image. Support cabinets
and worktops independently of service covers. Removable plinths give local
access but are not the sole route to isolation hidden behind appliances.

At each wet cabinet/appliance, name:

- normal source and local isolation;
- first surface wetted by a failed hose/joint;
- where leakage becomes visible before entering permanent fabric;
- local containment and its cleaning/overflow consequence, if used;
- appliance withdrawal and hose/waste disconnection;
- drying and reinstatement.

Prefer fewer concealed joints and reachable isolation before adding trays,
sensors or drains everywhere. If a tray hides a slow leak, lacks a visible
discharge or obstructs withdrawal, it may be worse than the control.

Utility/plant are stronger candidates for room-scale failure management than
dry pantry storage. Compare local appliance containment with a whole-room wet
assembly using the actual release volume, floor levels, doorway and discharge.
No decorative floor drain, tanking or automatic shut-off is selected by naming
the pattern. The plant door remains 1400 mm nominal, not proven equipment passage.

## Wet/dry threshold and surfaces

Terminate water control at a physical designed boundary. Removable dry skirting,
architrave or decorative stone is not itself the wet-room containment line.
A threshold must coordinate floor support, movement, water and passage; its
height cannot be selected by appearance before OAS and actual external/internal
duties are known. No unselected upstand is represented as level access.

Keep vulnerable timber edges out of direct splash/failure paths unless their
actual detail earns the duty. Mineral material alone is not waterproof.
Dry-side joinery needs its own credible leak declaration and drying path.
