# IAS-01H — horizontal assembly candidates

**Scope:** dimensioned candidate comparison, not structural/acoustic design.
**House:** RH-LAYOUT-REVISION-02 / RH-LAYOUT-FAMILY-02.

## The actual section

FFL 3450 minus the ceiling below at 3100 gives **350 mm**. The present section
allocates 20 finish + 22 deck + 238 nominal framing + 55 service zone + 15 ceiling.
The total is 350; unallocated depth is zero. These are representation decisions,
not selected members or certified boundary build-ups. The ground-floor 300 mm
reserve is a separate unresolved package; it does not inherit the intermediate
floor's layers or engineering results.

The [shared contract](../triad-interface-contract.md), IAS-00 and the existing
floor research require an excellent conventional control. Do not pursue a
house-wide access floor simply because the book discusses replacement.

H3 cannot recover 40 mm by silently reducing the unengineered framing zone to
198 mm, deleting ceiling layers or counting the 55 mm underside zone twice.
An explicit engineered/source reallocation would be needed; current tasks do
not justify that house-wide burden. H1 equally requires an actual complete
floor family; arithmetic alone supplies no acoustic, fire or levelling result.

## H0 — what remains replaceable

Distinguish wearing finish, substrate/deck, structure and ceiling boundary. A
replaceable wearing layer does not make the deck a routine access panel. Prefer
a finish fixing/bonding method whose renewal does not routinely consume the
structural deck, where compatible with the actual product and performance duty.
The method itself is not selected here.

Keep ordinary wet joints, valves and other recurrent-maintenance items out of
an inaccessible floor zone by route design. Long-lived continuous services may
cross through designed corridors, but that does not author an unknown pipe,
duct or cable to fit the 55 mm allocation. No member drilling or notching is
selected by a service diagram.

At the perimeter, allow the actual floor's movement and terminate independently
of skirting. The skirting may conceal an edge gap but must not clamp a moving
floor or be bonded to it. Gap size is a product/design input, not a universal
10 mm default. Finish exchange begins at a designated edge/threshold/closure,
with a removal direction that survives fixed cabinetry and door frames.

An opening does not automatically require a threshold strip. If the floor
continues, it continues. If a movement, wet/wear or envelope interface earns a
threshold, its support and release must be coordinated, not merely overlaid.

## H2 — a complete access operation, if a task earns it

Compare a local hatch with a narrow access band and with dry-side wall access.
The hatch carrier needs support on all intended load-bearing edges, positive
location/capture and a reachable first lift. It is not an unsupported cut in
the deck. Structural trimming/support belongs to engineering.

1. Isolate the service and clear the actual work/handling field.
2. Remove the local closure without dismantling a fixed kitchen or whole floor.
3. Lift the supported carrier with an ordinary specified tool/handle; protect
   brittle finish edges. A suction cup alone is not guaranteed on every finish.
4. Work through an actual clear opening, with temporary guarding/support where
   needed; do not load an unprotected opening edge.
5. Inspect supports/seats, renew damaged local parts and reinstate positive
   capture, edge alignment and all interrupted boundaries.
6. Check movement, edge trip, rattle and footfall before returning to use.

Neither a 600 mm commercial module nor a rail grid is selected. Bonding stone
or tile to a removable carrier may be legitimate, but its mass, support,
deflection, edge protection and lifting route must earn the detail. PHY-02
remains held; this comparison does not activate a new physical programme.

## Ceiling and floor edge

Routine access from below must not require cutting a protected ceiling or
breaking a wet/air/acoustic boundary. The 15 mm ceiling line is not by itself
a selected fire/acoustic system. Keep ordinary lights and small terminals
maintainable from their intended side; larger components need their own route.

Existing nominal floor seats, gaps and framing directions remain unengineered
source choices. No ceiling cornice supports the structural floor, and no trim
bridges a deliberately independent ceiling edge into the wall. Existing PHY-03
engineering and PHY-04 movement-junction questions retain their scope.

## Wet-floor pressure test

A trial 12.5 mm carrier + 1 mm waterproof layer + 3 mm adhesive + 10 mm tile
needs **26.5 mm** above the retained deck: **6.5 mm beyond** the current 20 mm
finish allocation, before any falls, levelling or heating. These are declared
comparison dimensions, not a product/system specification.

Do not call a wet-room floor solved by drawing a thin tile line. IAS-02W must
resolve the actual substrate, falls and water boundaries or record a source
rewind. Do not thin the structural deck to pay for an aesthetic surface.

## Result and falsifiers

H0 leads; H1 remains a bounded finish-budget comparison; H2 needs a real task;
H3 is rejected as a current-section drop-in. No ceiling/FFL, source allocation,
member, product, fire/acoustic rating or access hatch is changed.

[Section studies](studies/ias-core-sections.svg) show the budget comparison.
Reconsider if a selected current service task cannot be reached, a complete
technical build-up cannot fit, or physical evidence rejects ordinary footfall,
movement or repeated reinstatement. Simplification or a source rewind is a
valid result; hidden depth is not. IAS-02S/02W and the HAD/OAS junctions consume
this candidate family before IAS-03 selection.
