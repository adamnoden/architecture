### Whole-boundary cost decides, not the accessory count

Routine bell/cover cleaning already takes place from above without either coupling or lower hatch. A cold pipe joint would replace the two pipe welds (about0.38m) during rare retirement. It leaves about2.33m pan field/upstand/curb seams and0.69m welded floor diaphragm: **about3.02m site seams remain**, including below-floor work. It therefore does **not** remove the access platform, occupied-house hot-work protection or reinstated fire/air/acoustic closure. The reference's3.40m total remains a real cost, not an absolute prohibition; skilled time, protection, inspection, downtime and expected renewal events have not been priced.

**Decision:** do not select PC068 as an isolated improvement to this unchanged wet module. It adds a permanent functional polymer and a new seal-renewal duty while neither its independent replacement nor the claimed access/hot-work deletion is earned. This is a rejection of that isolated placement, not of conventional cold joints or all permitted residual material. Keep the all-welded reference unselected too. The next useful comparison is a **whole renewable wet boundary**: distinguish ordinary top service, replaceable cold seals and eventual pan/body retirement, give both directions actual incoming/replacement/support/protection and lifecycle costs, and obtain explicit MAT/owner disposition for any actual residual. A credible modular furnishing or temporary furniture export may simplify rare access, but must carry its real parts, mass, route, storage and restoration against the complete scene. No additional catalogue family, false geometric pass or IAS completion follows from this checkpoint.
