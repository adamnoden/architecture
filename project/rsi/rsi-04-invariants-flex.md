## Purpose

RSI-03 establishes that the current 12 m shell is deployable on the selected 15 m frontage but materially frontage-sensitive. That earns successor exploration. It does **not** earn indiscriminate shrinking.

This packet freezes the design constitution for the rough-plan stage.

The central rule is:

> **Protect architectural principles, not inherited dimensions.**

M3/B4 dimensions are evidence of one design, not automatically invariants of Ovid's House.

## Four classes

### `PROTECTED`

A successor must preserve the principle unless a separate freeze-break argument is raised.

### `HIGH_RESISTANCE`

May change, but the candidate must state the architectural gain that justifies the cost.

### `FLEXIBLE`

Legitimate design variables. Existing values have no presumption of survival.

### `PROJECT_SPECIFIC`

Questions that belong to a real site/project and must not be converted into canonical house geometry by invention.

## Protected principles

### Courtyard as real architecture

The courtyard remains a primary internal spatial/environmental object. It may change size, proportion and modest position; it may not collapse into a decorative lightwell merely to win footprint.

### Serviceability

`No service inside permanent fabric` remains binding. Services stay in designed accessible zones/cavities and remain replaceable.

### Accessible ≠ exposed

The successor must remain calm domestic architecture. Making maintenance possible does not licence visible technical clutter everywhere.

### Wet/service clustering

Wet and technical functions remain deliberately clustered enough to keep infrastructure short, legible and maintainable.

### Room quality

Compactness cannot be bought with mean rooms. Principal rooms must retain genuinely good usable proportions and the ceiling/spatial quality expected of the project.

No current room dimension is protected merely because it already exists.

### Stair quality

The stair remains a serious architectural circulation object with deep-tread quality and credible clearances. Its exact topology and position are free.

### Domestic hierarchy

The front-to-rear sequence and relationship to the garden remain legible. The new plot should make the garden relationship stronger, not turn the house into a deep-plan object detached from it.

### Georgian composure

The outward house remains capable of calm classical/Georgian composition. RSI may change bay widths and opening positions; it may not solve the site by producing arbitrary modern massing and calling the doctrine optional.

### Replacement logic

Major replaceable elements must have a credible route in and out. This now explicitly includes courtyard-facing elements; the route may no longer be hidden behind the assumption of generous exterior land.

### Geometry first

Water, movement and maintenance interfaces continue to prefer geometry and deliberate interfaces over brittle sealant/chemistry dependence.

## High-resistance properties

### Courtyard centrality

The court should remain central enough to organise the house. A meaningful offset is allowed if the resulting architecture is better, not simply because a rectangle becomes easier to pack.

### Two-storey order

Two storeys remain the default. A basement or extra floor is not an acceptable cheap trick for recovering footprint.

### Distinct domestic territories

The house should retain meaningful distinct principal spaces. Width pressure must not automatically produce one giant generic open-plan room.

### Two useful detached flanks

One side can be more technical, but both side elevations should remain inspectable and ordinarily reachable. A nominal 200–400 mm leftover slot is not a successful detached-house solution.

### Front composition

Internal optimisation must still permit a composed front. The façade cannot become the residue of plan packing.

## Flexible geometry

The following are explicitly free to move:

- shell width;
- shell depth;
- total floor area;
- exact courtyard dimensions and offset;
- stair position/topology;
- room width/depth distribution;
- gallery/corridor dimensions above quality thresholds;
- service-suite packing;
- technical-side bias;
- secondary-room front/rear allocation;
- side-window number/position;
- exact bay widths and opening centres;
- structural span direction/grid;
- partitions and local doors;
- front/rear opening widths;
- external plant position.

This is deliberately broad. RSI-05A is supposed to explore architecture rather than preserve B4 by inertia.

## Project-specific facts that must remain outside the canonical plan

Do **not** freeze a successor around guessed values for:

- scaffold/tower/outrigger width;
- crane/lift strategy;
- a particular ASHP model;
- sewer/outfall geometry;
- utility rights/routes;
- exact neighbour windows/privacy rights;
- planning verdicts;
- title boundaries;
- ground/foundation parameters;
- flood/groundwater/contamination;
- detailed vehicle/refuse/fire access.

These can challenge a real deployment later. They cannot be invented to justify a canonical plan today.

## Collision-specific design brief

RSI-05A must attack one problem in particular:

> **Can the plan return meaningful side territory while preserving the courtyard, room generosity, serviceability and classical composure that justify the house?**

The technical flank currently risks becoming all of these at once:

- ordinary passage;
- plant/meter/isolator territory;
- wall-base inspection/drainage territory;
- façade/rainwater maintenance access;
- material replacement route;
- temporary-work territory.

A successor does not necessarily need a wider technical flank. It may instead separate or relocate some duties more intelligently. That is a legitimate architectural move.

## Rules for RSI-05A

The rough-plan stage must:

1. generate **3–5 materially different** concepts, not cosmetic permutations;
2. show ground + first floor together;
3. place every concept on `RSI-RS-01`;
4. retain current B4 as a comparison/control where useful;
5. show shell/courtyard/stair/service geography and approximate room dimensions;
6. state exactly what each scheme gains in side territory and what it gives up;
7. show the intended courtyard replacement route;
8. check the unchanged concept against the 14 × 40 m sensitivity site without designing separately for it;
9. remain rough enough that rejecting a scheme is cheap;
10. stop at **OWNER GATE A** for Adam's selection.

No programme-internal scoring system may choose a winner.

## Result

RSI-04 permits substantial architectural change but protects the reasons Ovid's House exists.

The successor brief is therefore **not**:

> make the house smaller.

It is:

> **buy back robustness on ordinary frontage without spending the project's spatial and maintenance principles to do it.**

With this contract, RSI-05A rough-plan generation is authorised.
