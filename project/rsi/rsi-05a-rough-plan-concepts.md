## Purpose

Present a deliberately small family of rough architectural organisations for owner selection before detailed plan resolution.

These are **concept diagrams**, not coordinated plans. They are allowed to be rejected cheaply. No concept is selected, frozen, or promoted into Reference House authority by this record.

## Comparison discipline

A crucial control is held approximately constant: **shell area minus open courtyard area per floor**.

```text
CONTROL B4       12.0 × 10.8  court 3.6 × 3.3  → 117.72 m²
A FAMILIAR       11.0 × 11.8  court 3.4 × 3.4  → 118.24 m²
B NARROW+DEEP    10.5 × 12.3  court 3.2 × 3.6  → 117.63 m²
C COURT-LED      11.2 × 11.7  court 3.6 × 3.6  → 118.08 m²
D SERVICE-SPINE  10.8 × 12.0  court 3.4 × 3.5  → 117.70 m²
```

These are rough envelope comparisons before wall-thickness deductions, not claimed net internal areas.

The point is to compare **how roughly the same amount of house is shaped and organised**, rather than allowing a narrower concept to win simply because it contains less house.

## CONTROL — B4

```text
shell:          12.0 × 10.8 m
court:           3.6 × 3.3 m
15 m site:       1.75 m technical / 1.25 m principal side
14 m sensitivity:1.25 m technical / 0.75 m principal side
```

Current best provisional coordination.

Ground-floor idea remains the present drawing / hall-stair / study front, family-dining-kitchen garden sequence, east-side gallery/service band and central court.

Upper floor retains four-bedroom geography, stair/landing, east gallery/bathroom, dressing/linen and court.

**OPEN QUESTION**

## A — NARROW / FAMILIAR

```text
shell:          11.0 × 11.8 m
court:           3.4 × 3.4 m
15 m site:       2.25 m technical / 1.75 m principal side
14 m sensitivity:1.75 m technical / 1.25 m principal side
```

The conservative challenger.

Keep the current domestic hierarchy recognisable but buy back one metre of frontage by adding depth instead of squeezing every room.

Ground concept:
- drawing room front-west;
- hall/stair front-centre;
- study front-east;
- family room west of the court/rearward;
- east gallery + service band;
- garden dining behind court;
- kitchen rear-east.

Upper concept follows the same recognisable four-bedroom / court / bath / landing structure.

**GAINS**
- +1.0 m total side territory versus B4;
- familiar circulation and room order;
- little programme upheaval;
- 14 m plot becomes materially less hostile.

**COSTS**
- still inherits many B4 organisational assumptions;
- may prove that we changed the rectangle without really rethinking the house.

**OPEN QUESTION**
- is this the elegant obvious correction, or merely a timid one?

## B — NARROW + DEEP / REBALANCED

```text
shell:          10.5 × 12.3 m
court:           3.2 × 3.6 m
15 m site:       2.50 m technical / 2.00 m principal side
14 m sensitivity:2.00 m technical / 1.50 m principal side
```

The strongest width-for-depth trade.

Do not preserve B4 rectangles by habit. The front becomes a clearer tripartite sequence — drawing / entry hall / study — while the stair moves beside the courtyard rather than consuming the front hall as its principal organising fact.

Ground concept:
- balanced three-part front;
- stair beside court on the principal side;
- court remains a real environmental room;
- east gallery + service band;
- family / dining / kitchen form a three-part garden edge.

Upper concept:
- two front bedrooms around a central landing;
- principal rear-west;
- rear-east bedroom;
- bath/service stacked on technical side;
- dressing/linen at rear-centre.

**GAINS**
- +1.5 m side territory versus B4;
- strongest ordinary-frontage robustness of the non-service-specialist concepts;
- stair ceases to dominate the entrance hall;
- front elevation can become a composed tripartite order.

**COSTS**
- longest/deepest shell;
- risk of over-long circulation or rooms becoming too sequential;
- 3.2 m court width must prove it still feels like a courtyard, not a slot.

**OPEN QUESTION**
- does greater depth make the house richer, or merely longer?

## C — COURT-LED

```text
shell:          11.2 × 11.7 m
court:           3.6 × 3.6 m
15 m site:       2.15 m technical / 1.65 m principal side
14 m sensitivity:1.65 m technical / 1.15 m principal side
```

Start from the courtyard and circulation figure rather than from inherited room rectangles.

Ground concept:
- drawing / axial entrance hall / study form the front;
- square 3.6 m court remains the compositional centre;
- stair shifts to the side of the court, leaving the entry axis visually clean;
- family / dining / kitchen address the garden;
- gallery/service band remains technical-side biased.

Upper concept mirrors the court-led circulation: bedrooms occupy the outer corners, with stair/landing and wet/service functions organised around the court.

**GAINS**
- restores a square, strongly legible courtyard;
- +0.8 m side territory versus B4;
- strongest axial front-to-court-to-garden idea;
- potentially the most architecturally composed plan rather than merely optimised plan.

**COSTS**
- stair beside the court consumes valuable perimeter depth;
- may privilege composition over room efficiency;
- requires careful proof that the court axis does not become theatrical.

**OPEN QUESTION**
- if we forgot M3 dimensions and remembered only the doctrine, would this be closer to the house we would draw?

## D — SERVICE-SPINE

```text
shell:          10.8 × 12.0 m
court:           3.4 × 3.5 m
15 m site:       2.35 m technical / 1.85 m principal side
14 m sensitivity:1.85 m technical / 1.35 m principal side
```

Attack the RSI-03 problem through service topology as well as footprint.

Keep an explicit internal technical/wet spine on the service side so the **external** technical flank is primarily a maintenance/replacement route rather than simultaneously passage + plant bay + equipment corridor.

Ground concept:
- drawing / hall-stair / study front;
- family on principal side;
- court central;
- gallery beside court;
- compact internal service spine on technical side;
- dining/kitchen to garden.

Upper concept stacks bathroom/wet functions over the same spine while bedrooms remain perimeter rooms.

External plant strategy should terminate toward rear/service staging rather than casually occupy the side passage.

**GAINS**
- +1.2 m side territory versus B4;
- explicitly attacks route/equipment coupling;
- strongest service-topology proposition;
- near-identical rough enclosed area to B4.

**COSTS**
- risk of turning one side of the house into an over-dense service band;
- must avoid making domestic spaces feel organised around infrastructure;
- may solve a site problem more than a spatial one.

**OPEN QUESTION**
- can the service spine disappear experientially while remaining exceptionally maintainable?

## Important boundary

None of these room rectangles are resolved wall geometry.

They do not yet prove:
- exact clear room dimensions after wall build-up;
- furniture capacity;
- stair headroom/structure;
- door conflicts;
- bathroom/service fitout;
- façade opening rhythm;
- fire/escape;
- exact maintenance equipment fit.

Those are intentionally downstream of concept selection.
