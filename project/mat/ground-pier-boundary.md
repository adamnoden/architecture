## Architectural decision

**Withdraw the upper-plate-only complete floor route.** The plate interrupts capillary transfer into the upper pier, but the RC 200 mm field at −180..−380 mm remains connected to the lower pedestal, grid and ground supports beneath it. Ground moisture can follow **ground → pile → RC grid → RC field → bare-glass joints**, without crossing the upper plate. Drainage and ventilation do not erase that solid capillary path; glass joints are not a membrane. The conditional suspended-concrete moisture hypothesis has not demonstrated ownership of this actual connected source.

The next real comparison is **lower-source cuts at every actual support connection**, using the whole-house signed head/pile envelopes including wind uplift and an actual bulk-water exclusion strategy, against **one real separate field boundary**. If the lower cuts earn the complete source break, delete the redundant upper L plates and price the full support count/fabrication. Do not retain duplicate capillary machinery by default. The local upper-splice geometry and sensitivities below remain recoverable evidence of what they do and do not solve. The separate field is now the preferred **development reference**, for the comparison below. Neither complete route is selected or admitted.

No surface temperature, condensation result, whole-floor U-value or whole-house energy result is earned. The actual surface/energy model must decide whether that thermal cost is acceptable or needs a simpler local mitigation.

The concrete route remains unearned while the connected support capillary source persists. A future conditional route also requires an effectively drained and ventilated void, no assigned hydrostatic exposure and an assessed site gas class compatible with the tested floor/joint construction. [Approved Document C, 2013 edition](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a8192a0e5274a2e8ab54b5f/BR_PDF_AD_C_2013.pdf), §§4.17–4.20, distinguishes suspended-concrete moisture performance and drainage/membrane conditions; §5.5 requires wall/floor damp continuity. It is not a blanket gas or concrete waiver. Actual jurisdiction, site and project performance remain separate. If the eventual site requires a barrier this construction cannot demonstrate, redesign the field/plot interface or admit a properly evidenced alternative; do not announce gas safety or add a universal membrane for every hypothetical site.

Inspect and test the accessible plate perimeter, folded return and concrete joint before upper concrete, glass or plaster conceals them.
