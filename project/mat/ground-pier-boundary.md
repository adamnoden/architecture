## Architectural decision

Prefer developing the **direct RC load path with a solid metal capillary splice and a conditional suspended-concrete floor boundary**, rather than forcing a load-bearing insulation course into a moment connection. Accept and quantify the thermal bridge as a design cost. This recommendation is conditional: exact opposed-anchor/plate forces, edge continuity, movement, concrete/joint air-moisture performance and admissible site conditions still have to work together. Neither a plate drawing nor a material label closes the assembly.

| Route | Structural / capillary consequence | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous RC through the mineral DPC | Simple continuity for force; concrete/reinforcement bypass the intended capillary cut | Retain as a negative control, not a demonstrated dry pier |
| Solid plate across the full RC pier, anchored steel attached to opposite faces | Compression, tension and shear cross the structural metal plate; no continuous bar or open hole crosses the capillary plane | Constructive preferred comparison; opposed anchors, plate bending/welds, edge joins and corrosion remain active |
| Load-bearing mineral/thermal course | Must carry the actual compression, moment, shear and uplift, with its own anchored restraint and water treatment | No existing unadopted AAC/PERINSUL/320 mm glass comparison is silently promoted; thermal insulation alone is not a moment splice |
| House-wide welded pan | Can provide a separate field boundary only with actual support, seams, movement, penetrations and repair | Conditional challenger if the concrete/site route fails; no universal contaminated-site requirement is invented |

## Exact structural source and finite geometry

The candidate replaces the 140 mm inner-core corner material locally with four RC L-piers. The SW polygon runs from 262.5 mm to 1262.5 mm, has **1000 mm physical legs** and a conservative 900 mm flexural section; the other corners mirror it. It is an explicit material/load-path change at the existing inner arch-abutment territory, not unchanged masonry. The actual source gives 60.807 kN shear and 265.056 kNm moment per leg. The preliminary **splice** card is 500 kN compression, 100 kN uplift, 75 kN shear and 300 kNm moment. It is distinct from the older **grid/pile-head** 850 kN / ±100 kNm domain.

The solid plate occupies −50..−30 mm over each complete L footprint. Lower RC rises from grid top −180 mm to its underside; upper RC continues from its top. Opposed anchored steel terminates on the plate faces. A thin nonstructural flange extends 20 mm normally for the water/air return, reduced to 10 mm at the leg ends: the raw window at 1300 mm retains **27.5 mm** after that end flange, against the source's 25 mm set-out comparison. The plate has no anchor hole or continuous rebar penetration. The 20 mm envelope is not an already adequate plate, stock product or weld specification; STR owns the actual force transfer, development, cover and local plate/weld design.

Each exact L footprint is 0.2604 m². At the stated 7900 kg/m³ density sensitivity, each 20 mm plate weighs **41.14 kg**, or **164.57 kg for four**, before anchors, edge returns, welds and waste. This is shop fabrication and supported mechanical placement, not a hand-lift claim. Both plate faces and all opposed anchor attachments must be inspected before concrete conceals them. Choosing the direct load path does not make this fabrication burden disappear.

## Continuous local boundary without sealing distribution access

The preferred comparison retains **15 mm finish / 65 mm distribution / 100 mm glass / RC 200 mm**, at 0/−15/−80/−180/−380 mm. No sheet is placed immediately beneath the finish. All ordinary distribution remains accessible from above without cutting a permanent pan seam. Services' current source has no selected UFH, body or route requiring this band; its main trunk and ground sanitary/incoming routes remain above the floor.

The proposed local air-line return is a 1 mm folded metal strip at the inner pier/core face. Its 100 mm horizontal foot is cast into the floor top-cover zone at −195 mm, 15 mm below the RC top; the declared 40 mm top-steel cover leaves a **25 mm separation screen**, not a completed cage collision check. It rises beside the pier and is welded to the unperforated splice edge. Above the plate, a return continues to +150 mm behind the dry-side mineral plaster. A 3 mm mechanical key zone plus 1 mm sheet leaves **23 mm** of the 27 mm plaster thickness. No fixation or field bar may pierce the air-line sheet. The mineral plaster still stops at FFL 0; a bare-glass base return occupies the below-floor gap rather than allowing mortar/plaster to wick around the DPC.

This locates the intended path **RC field → cast-in folded return → solid plate/edge return → dry-side keyed plaster**. It does not assert that concrete/metal bond or keyed plaster is already an air/gas seal. The actual edge, end and construction-joint details require matched specimens and a stated leakage allocation. Services has not yet selected a whole-house airtightness target; no 0.6 ACH or 3 m³/(h·m²) pass is invented here. The RC/masonry upper return must also respect STR's separate ±10 mm infill movement requirement; this rigid base strip is not a qualified ±10 mm movement product. Ground/slab relative movement, shrinkage and temperature must be assigned before selecting the cast-in reference.

The full-pan challenger lies **below distribution**, at −80..−79 mm, leaving 64 mm distribution and retaining RC 200 mm / glass 100 mm. It is explicitly unselected. Services found no selected geometric conflict with that 1 mm cost, but the full field still needs actual extent, support, weld/seam, expansion, penetrations and repair. No gross area or mass from the retired warm-grid topology is imported. The earlier immediately-under-finish position is rejected because it would seal ordinary access.

## Thermal price and site conditions

The generated one-dimensional sensitivity uses the actual four L footprints through a 180 mm path containing 160 mm RC and 20 mm metal, with authored/previous-family conductivities 2 and 15 W/(m·K). It gives **12.807 W/K** for those equal-temperature-face routes. The same-area 100 mm glass thought comparator gives 0.427 W/K: the difference is **12.380 W/K**, or **247.6 W at an authored 20 K difference** and **742.8 kWh at an authored 2500 K·day scenario**. These are sensitivity prices, not actual heat loss or London weather. They omit surface resistances, lateral spreading, the real beam/floor geometry and the three-dimensional L junction. No surface temperature, condensation result, whole-floor U-value or whole-house energy result is earned. The actual surface/energy model must decide whether that thermal cost is acceptable or needs a simpler local mitigation.

The conditional concrete route requires an effectively drained and ventilated void, no assigned hydrostatic exposure and an assessed site gas class compatible with the tested floor/joint construction. [Approved Document C, 2013 edition](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a8192a0e5274a2e8ab54b5f/BR_PDF_AD_C_2013.pdf), §§4.17–4.20, distinguishes suspended-concrete moisture performance and drainage/membrane conditions; §5.5 requires wall/floor damp continuity. It is not a blanket gas or concrete waiver. Actual jurisdiction, site and project performance remain separate. If the eventual site requires a barrier this construction cannot demonstrate, redesign the field/plot interface or admit a properly evidenced alternative; do not announce gas safety or add a universal membrane for every hypothetical site.

## Installation, inspection and remaining decision

Shop-fabricate the solid plate and opposed anchors, inspect both faces, then cast the lower pedestal/floor and the finite embedded return without puncturing it. Inspect and test the accessible plate perimeter, folded return and concrete joint before upper concrete, glass or plaster conceals them. Continue the mineral DPC to the plate edge without mortar bypass; cast the upper pier, protect the returns, then complete the dry-side keyed plaster and accessible distribution/finish. Record an actual crack/joint inspection, air/moisture test and repair/retest method before closure and again after relevant movement/commissioning. A buried structural splice is not an ordinarily renewable gasket; its exposure, corrosion protection and design life must match that consequence.
