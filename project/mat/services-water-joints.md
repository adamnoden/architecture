# MAT-SERVICES — water joints, isolation residues and repair access

**31 October 2026. Bounded S01 design comparison; MAT-SERVICES remains planned.** This advances the [single constituent register](constituent-audit.md) under the [material policy](../../docs/reference-house/material-policy.md), using the existing [BCR services geography](../bcr/bcr-services-result.md) and [IAS access hierarchy](../ias/ias-02s-services-access.md). It selects no water-system size, installed product schedule or owner exception.

## Decision for the house

**Develop metal-sealing compression connections at independently accessible plant/utility service points.** The evidenced Conex family offers a normal copper-joint mechanism without an added sealant or a designed elastomer sealing ring [1,2]. It is a useful alternative to assuming every flame-free copper joint needs a polymer. Match the actual fitting, tube temper, water conditions and duty before selection; this is not a declaration that every compression fitting or supplied accessory is free of synthetic constituents.

Exclude the unmodified black-EPDM **B Press** water family and **VSH Prestex PB350 item 254202** from the default no-synthetic assembly: their declared seals conflict with the policy [3,4]. This is a materials disposition, not a finding that those products are unsafe. An approval listing does not waive the owner's requirement. Do not remove required seals or substitute home-made valve internals.

Keep distribution runs as continuous as the actual route permits, with necessary service connections at declared wet-service fronts. Do not make every removable finish a water-joint location. A standard stopped coupler is not automatically a separating union: the connection design must show its first disengagement and replacement movement. Where that cannot be achieved, change the local arrangement or accept an explicitly designed cut-and-repair task; do not label it simple disassembly.

## A 22 mm connection is larger than the drawn pipe

Use 22 mm only to interrogate BCR's existing packing input. It is not a hydraulic selection. Source [2] gives the following distinct witnesses:

| Witness | Source dimension | Meaning here |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary brass straight coupler 301, **GG-1020301--** | Overall fitting length B **52 mm**; insertion E **25 mm**; nut spanner size **33 mm** | Body engagement and tool interface must be represented. The spanner opening is not the nut's full outside envelope or a wrench operating field. |
| Slip coupler for burst-tube repair 301BP, **GZB1020301BP** | Overall fitting length B **100 mm** | A distinct repair component to investigate. Its length is not an allowable damage gap, sliding-travel requirement or proof that it can be installed in the proposed bay. |

BCR's three trial water envelopes are **72 = 22 + 2 × 25 mm**, with the 25 mm radial allowance authored for that packing study. They are not three specified pipes or a required insulation thickness. As a sensitivity check, substituting even the 33 mm nut tool interface while retaining the same allowance gives **83 mm**, 11 mm beyond the old envelope. This is not a proposed 83 mm installed envelope: corners, actual insulation, supports and tool movement still need their own objects. It shows why the bare-pipe packing result cannot qualify the connection.

For a stopped coupler between two immovable opposed tube ends, undoing the nuts does not remove the stops or provide withdrawal space. The inferred failure is in that particular release argument, not in compression technology generally. Show which pipe, fitting or independently supported module moves first, and its unobstructed path. Do not shorten engagement, remove stops or assume surrounding pipework will flex. The dedicated 301BP repair lead deserves a separate worked sequence; it is not an interchangeable 301 or an already-qualified service union.

This reuses the [RWT first-movement reasoning](../rwt/copper-terminal-renewal.md) at a different service and actual comparison size. It transfers no rainwater approval, large-bore dimension or court-boundary result.

## Separate the plant service break from pipe repair

Use the **east-facing service side of BCR's plant water reservation** as the first location for this comparison. Its [700-square reservation and eastward equipment-renewal witness](../bcr/bcr-services-result.md#plant-fit-connections-and-working-space) identify the territory to develop. They contain no actual union positions, projecting tails or pipe-release sequence. The next detail must preserve the working region, the equipment's route and separation from dry equipment.

Compare a **central union on independently supported equipment-side tails** for ordinary component exchange. The YP11/08252 supplies a real example of that construction: its cone interface is a separate joint from the two capillary tube ends [6,7]. The published product table and installation text were visually inspected. Its current catalogue dimensions and the older guide's dimensions are not used as withdrawal travel; bind the supplied revision and actual mating geometry before sizing a service field.

Material admission remains held. PTFE conflicts with the default policy, while the alternative jointing compound still needs an exact identity and complete constituent review. The manufacturer's metal-sealing label does not remove that instruction. Do not transfer Conex's ordinary compression-joint statement about additional sealant to this different union, or remove a required constituent to obtain a nominal material pass.

| Intervention at the plant service front | Intended retained construction | Consequence for the detail |
|---|---|---|
| Exchange the component through a qualified union interface | Fixed distribution and its supported union half; the capillary end joints can remain made | Identify which half moves with the component and include every retained tail in the moving object. Show access to the nut and counter-hold, clearance to disengage the mating faces, and the first supported movement before the full equipment transport begins. |
| Renew a fixed union half, its pipe end or a failed adjacent tube | Only the pipework outside the declared repair length | Provide a separate cutting/joining and reinstatement task. A central union's existence does not make its soldered end replaceable by undoing the central nut. The retained building and other services need protection during the actual repair; the preparation, consumables and access belong to that larger task. |

This is an allocation of maintenance work, not an installed-fit result. Loosening a union nut does not prove that two retained faces can separate or that the released nut clears surrounding insulation and supports. A rigid component between opposed connections still needs an evidenced first movement; their relative axes, engagement and available travel must be shown together. Do not assume that the pipes flex, that two nominal union ends are interchangeable, or that the catalogue's overall length is a removal allowance.

At this plant location, draw the fixed distribution, independently supported moving component and all actual connection faces **before** reusing BCR's body-only transport sweep. If a necessary first movement leaves that represented envelope, enlarge the studied moving object and recheck the route at its owning source. If the connected component cannot be released within the available working region, change the connection arrangement or make the larger cut-and-repair operation explicit. No extra service strip, equipment orientation or room dimension is adopted here.

The 301BP remains a separate repair lead. Its longer body has not earned a routine separating-joint function or a qualified installed sliding sequence. The useful advance is a named central-union comparator and a distinction between ordinary component exchange and ultimate pipe-end repair, with the exact union's jointing-material hold retained. The complete valve, isolation/drain arrangement, insulation and first-release geometry remain open.

## Connect the choice to the house

| Owning location or task | Required development consequence |
|---|---|
| Plant/utility water connections | Develop a separately supported wet-service assembly reachable from the existing working region. Show operating access, tools on both connections, the first release movement and a route for the removed part. The 700-square water reservation is a body comparison, not space already granted to valves, insulation and service work. Preserve the plant replacement route and separation from electrical equipment. |
| Riser and upper service front | Repack the actual joints and supports, then draw a contiguous access section. BCR's 300 mm unassigned gap and the incomplete panel-to-lining relationship remain adverse findings. A visible valve through the 650 × 550 opening does not prove tool reach or body withdrawal. |
| Horizontal distribution and crossings | Retain IAS's exclusion of water from decorative dry skirting. Do not hide ordinary service joints in the 55 mm floor-service allowance or assume a joint may cross structure. Place a necessary disconnect on an accessible side of the separately designed fire/acoustic/moisture boundary; insulation, sleeves and support constituents remain in S01/S10. |
| Failure and repair | Provide a local visible leak-declaration and drying path, independent supports, safe isolation and draining. Closing the valve being replaced is not its own upstream isolation. The eventual plumbing design must show how the affected section is made safe, removed, reconnected and recommissioned without dismantling permanent finishes or disturbing other services. No test pressure or installation procedure is prescribed here. |

The **PB350 example remains excluded**, but makes the distinction between joint and system concrete: solving the copper-to-fitting seal has not solved the valve. Any next valve candidate must disclose its working seals, lubricants and visible handle as well as the body, and earn the same operating and replacement access.

## Remaining comparison and handoff

Capillary joining remains an alternative for an appropriate fixed run, rather than an assumed plastic-free fallback. The current [Delcop Endfeed family](https://conexbanninger.com/en/ranges/brazing/delcop-endfeed/) includes soldered/brazed copper-alloy fittings; its dated [June 2017 UK flyer](https://conexbanninger.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Delcop-End-Feed-Sales-Flyer.pdf) shows separate flux, heat and solder in the illustrated process. The complete consumables, retained residues and repair heat exposure require assessment. A compact fitting does not make occupied-house hot work or routine cutting disappear.

Compare the whole accessible service assembly: actual tube and fittings, suitable isolation, supports, insulation and facings, boundary treatment, jointing/commissioning work, leak response and replacement. No current quotation, installed length, service-life advantage or cost saving is established. No sleeve, acoustic pad or required insulation is omitted to obtain a material pass.

**S01 now has an evidenced compression development route, exact adverse press/valve constituents and a connection-specific access test.** It remains unresolved as an installed water system. The next owning IAS/BCR detail must bind the actual water duty and exact product revisions to the plant connection, a real boundary and a complete first-release/repair sequence. MAT-SERVICES's electrical, waste, heating, ventilation and equipment obligations remain open; this comparison changes no house geometry, packet lifecycle or exception decision.
