# MAT-ENVELOPE — mineral ground boundary and floor junction comparison

**30 October 2026. Bounded design study; no construction selection or packet closeout.** This develops the first ground/wall/floor comparison required by the [MAT programme](material-integrity-programme.md), from the [constituent register](constituent-audit.md). The [material policy](../../docs/reference-house/material-policy.md) and [ground-water policy](../../docs/reference-house/ground-water-policy.md) control the decision. Source keys below identify primary evidence inspected on this date.

## Design result

Develop a **bonded DPC1 brick wall-base assembly with an independently continuous floor moisture boundary** as the first conventional mineral comparison. Retain suitable slate as a challenger requiring its own product and joint evidence. Retain **bare FOAMGLAS T4+ slabs as a floor-insulation component candidate**, while excluding the current lined BOARD T4+ and PERINSUL HL products from the unmodified no-synthetic shortlist. These are decisions about what to develop or set aside, not acceptance of a house assembly.

The decisive unresolved interface is where a rigid mineral wall DPC meets the floor boundary, including at internal supports and the court threshold. Extra mineral thickness cannot compensate for an open junction. Compare an above-slab continuous non-sheet barrier first for that junction and its intervention route; keep the manufacturer's below-slab insulation system as the reference control. An above-slab location is conditional on ground chemistry, structure and a qualified complete build-up, not a permission to rearrange the manufacturer's layers.

No plot, groundwater level, foundation, ground slab or installed product schedule is selected. The represented [section source](../../compiler/reference-house/georgian/section-source.ts) and RWT ground-floor reserve are spatial controls, not available construction thicknesses. This study changes no model dimensions, support levels or room geometry. MAT-ENVELOPE remains planned: the rest of the envelope and a complete accepted ground assembly are still unresolved.

## Mineral wall baseline and useful extra margin

BDA Design Note 7 describes DPC1 bricks for buildings, maximum 4.5% absorption by mass using the stated five-hour boil method, in at least two bonded courses with M12/designation (i) mortar. These courses constitute one bonded DPC. The note distinguishes rising-damp resistance from resistance to downward water percolation. It is dated January 2011 and cites older standards: it is a comparison basis, not a current product approval or authority to use that mortar indiscriminately elsewhere. [B1]

Bind the eventual unit declaration, frost/exposure suitability, exact mortar constituents and ground chemistry to the actual wall and load. No exact DPC1 SKU or suitable DPC slate was established by this study. The manufacturer's DoP retrieval process illustrates why a product family alone is insufficient: Wienerberger ties declarations to the supplied product label/batch identity. [P1]

The owner asks for substantial construction with generous evidence-led margin. The following comparison directs that margin toward a failure mechanism instead of a numerical multiplier. These are design hypotheses to test with BCR/STR and MRA, not quantified reliability gains.

| Development move | Benefit to establish | Counter-effect or bypass to resolve before adoption |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional bonded DPC1 baseline | A continuous capillary interruption through the intended wall/leaf width | Incomplete beds, joints, returns and adjoining floor connections; one bonded assembly is not independent redundancy |
| Additional bonded DPC courses | Whether a particular defect path, exposure or construction tolerance is usefully reduced | More material changes levels, thermal paths and workmanship exposure; it does not close a bridge around the assembly or stop downward cavity water |
| More slate depth or an extra slate course | Whether the exact grade and bedding gain useful robustness | Extra interfaces may change sliding, restraint and bearing; broken or poorly supported pieces and joints remain consequential |
| More protected, inspectable wall-base height and separation from wet landscaping | Whether the chosen section reduces bridging and splash exposure while remaining maintainable | Accessible routes, court geometry and future raised paving/soil must still fit; no generic height proves flood safety |
| Protection of the junction during construction and access to the finished perimeter | Whether the actual continuous boundary survives later trades and can be inspected | Concealed mortar droppings, render/plaster, screed, service fixings and retained debris can reconnect wet and dry sides |

The development priority is **a complete bonded baseline, an explicit floor junction and protected bypass routes**, followed by added material where its benefit is established. Do not introduce a brick/slate/metal sandwich merely to count barriers. The [solid-wall and arch comparison](../../docs/research/solid-walls-and-masonry-arches.md) and STR retain bearing, shear, sliding and arch-thrust decisions.

## Two floor-boundary arrangements to detail

Approved Document C separates ground moisture, groundwater damage and hazardous ground gases. Its ordinary ground-supported-floor example excludes pressure-water conditions and requires continuity between floor membrane and wall/pier DPCs. It allows the damp layer above or below concrete, with ground contamination capable of requiring the lower location. Above-concrete bituminous or similar moisture/vapour-resisting protection is described separately. A gas barrier may also perform the damp duty when correctly detailed; the reverse is not established. [A1]

| Relationship | A — manufacturer's below-slab control | B — above-slab boundary challenger |
|---|---|---|
| Source identity | UK FOAMGLAS 1.1.10, 10 February 2025 | Separate study prompted by AD C; not a FOAMGLAS approved variant |
| Floor-field sequence, ground toward room | Prepared load-bearing support/levelling; loose glass slabs; PC 74A1 scratch coat; damp/waterproof layer; additional friction separator; concrete slab; independently selected room finish | Engineered support and thermal arrangement; concrete slab; qualified continuous moisture/vapour boundary with required separation/protection; finish. Insulation location and every lower layer remain to qualify as a whole |
| Material change needed | Resolve exact scratch-coat additives and both membrane/separator products without losing their distinct functions | Resolve an exact non-synthetic barrier, its substrate/separator, movement details and finish compatibility; do not infer that a generic asphalt floor is the barrier |
| Wall and pier connection | Bring the buried floor boundary into the mineral DPC with an actual supported transition, protected before slab placement | Bring the upper boundary into the mineral DPC with an actual transition compatible with floor movement and later finish renewal |
| Construction and intervention consequence | Continuity and damage inspection are principally before concealment; later access can involve the structural slab | Boundary may be reachable from the room side by removing finishes, but its connection beneath masonry may remain inaccessible; access is not proof of easy repair |
| First reason to reject or redesign | No qualified non-plastic separation system; critical transition lacking credible construction verification, protection and intervention strategy; unacceptable water/ground/load condition | Ground chemistry or required slab protection demands a lower barrier; no qualified rigid-DPC junction; incompatible movement or finish; unsuitable thermal/support arrangement |

In control A, joints are staggered and tight-butted. The application sheet specifies approximately 1 kg/m² of PC 74A1 at about 1 mm as its scratch coat, then overlapped layers installed to their own instructions; the second layer protects the insulation against friction from concrete expansion. Its PE example conflicts with the material default, but deleting that layer deletes a stated duty. High groundwater/pressure requires particular design. [F4]

PC 74A1's current sheet is **9 February 2026**, replacing 13 December 2024. It lists mineral constituents and unspecified additives; the latter remain unresolved. Its generic wall adhesive/skim instructions do not replace system 1.1.10's application-specific scratch coat. Neither an A1 fire classification nor a mineral description is a full constituent declaration. [F5]

For B, MAC's flooring guidance distinguishes grades and separating membranes, including a glass-fibre separator for grades I–III and conditional use for grade IV. It also carries movement joints through the floor and requires suitable watertight joint interfaces. Its skirting drawing shows a projecting DPC beneath asphalt; that is **not** an evidenced connection to a rigid brick/slate DPC. Glass-fibre naming does not disclose a separator's binder. [M1, M2]

Therefore B is a useful junction-development priority, not a completed alternative. Do not combine guidance fragments into a claimed tested recipe. There is no retrieved evidence of an entirely no-synthetic floor moisture/gas system connected to the proposed mineral wall baseline.

## Section topology and consequential junctions

This handoff defines connected duties without inventing a plot datum, a safe water margin or a buildable thickness. The first coordinated section must show a continuous moisture boundary across the occupied footprint **and** its independent thermal and load paths. It must also show what happens outside that boundary when the primary drain is blocked and the receiving system is flooded, under the existing ground-water/RWT policy.

| Section location | Required connection or separation to draw next | Consequence for the current shortlist |
|---|---|---|
| Straight external/court wall base | Trace wet foundation/support through both wall leaves; show each applicable DPC, the floor connection, cavity water exit and finish terminations | Bonded brick DPC cannot be substituted for a cavity tray handling downward water. Maintain an inspectable exterior edge and prevent finish/debris bridges; BDA requires continuity and addresses compression/sliding/bond. [B1, B2] |
| Floor edge and inner leaf | Connect floor moisture line to the inner-leaf DPC; separately trace insulation around the slab edge and foundation support | Excluding PERINSUL leaves a real thermal-junction question. Compare geometric insulation continuity with the structural support; no bare-glass wall-bearing substitution is earned |
| Internal wall, pier or arch support | Continue the floor boundary into or around every support that can carry ground moisture; distinguish a support founded below the boundary from one borne above it | A dry floor field does not isolate a wet internal support. Any proposed wrap, stepped DPC, pier collar or load above the barrier needs its own movement/load detail; no hidden extra slip plane |
| Court door threshold and jamb returns | Join sill weathering, wall DPC and floor line without a route beneath/around the end of the threshold; keep drainage and structural support distinct | OAS/HAD/RWT must reconcile access, backwater and support. Neither a low sill nor a thermal block is evidence of submerged watertightness. The historical PERINSUL comparison is materially held |
| Service penetration | Locate a deliberate crossing with support and sufficient access to form, inspect and renew the actual damp/gas seal | Prefer a route avoiding the critical boundary where the service design permits. Metal pipe or sleeve alone does not clear the annulus, collar or retained sealant; MAT-SERVICES owns the connected equipment |
| Corner, step and movement joint | Carry the boundary through the three-dimensional return and movement without relying on an unshown sheet lap | General sheet-overlap guidance is not a rigid-DPC connection detail. A changed level adds a junction obligation; it is not extra redundancy [B2] |

These are the drawing obligations to feed the existing MRA assurance process, not a new moisture rating system. Thermal analysis must use the actual wall/floor section and surface conditions; compressive capacity must use the actual load, bearing, sustained performance and restraints. Product data do not establish either result for this house. Floor, slab, wall, arch and construction loads cannot be collapsed into one catalogue compression number.

## Buildability, procurement and intervention comparison

Ordinary bonded masonry is the first construction comparator because it uses a recognisable wall-building operation and keeps the wall DPC as part of the bonded support. That is a design preference, not evidence that a suitable local brick/mortar supply or workmanship is already secured. Suitable slate introduces a different sourcing, handling and bedding question. Retain it only with an exact DPC-use declaration and a complete joint proposal.

The glass-floor control requires substrate preparation, protection through later trades and several distinct layers; bare-glass procurement alone cannot price the work. The above-slab challenger may reduce the depth of later intervention, but can introduce specialist asphalt work, finish limitations and difficult wall connections. No comparative local labour availability, delivered quote, service-life guarantee or cost range was obtained. Do not turn these qualitative differences into a procurement recommendation or a thousand-year prediction.

Price the alternatives on the **same future section and quantities**: linear metres and full width of wall/pier DPC; floor area by exact insulation and barrier build-up; corners/steps/thresholds/penetrations by count and detail; temporary protection, specialist visits, inspections, access and reinstatement. Distinguish initial material/labour from intervention and replacement consequences. Adding courses increases some quantities, but no cost or reliability multiplier follows automatically.

Before concealment, the relevant delivery record needs the actual product/batch and formulation, approved junction, prepared substrate, complete bedding/bonding, continuity at returns, protected penetration and absence of bypass debris. Inspection and any representative junction testing must be designed for the selected failure modes; none has been performed here. Keep the completed boundary protected while floors and finishes are installed. Later maintenance must preserve the exterior clearance and accessible junctions instead of relying on routine destructive cuts through loaded walls.

## Bounded handoff and remaining work

This study narrows the product/options set, fixes the independent functional lines and identifies where the next section must earn continuity. Update **G02, G03, G05, G10 and X02** through this source rather than creating a second constituent inventory. It approves no exception and no complete installed assembly.

The next useful design work is to bind one actual DPC1 unit/mortar and one suitable slate comparator, then resolve **one complete straight wall/floor junction for each floor-boundary location** with exact retained constituents. Follow with the internal support, threshold, penetration and movement/level-change details above. Compare their structural/thermal and intervention consequences before choosing added mineral depth. If neither floor-boundary arrangement can meet the owner's material policy and necessary performance, return to geometry and construction alternatives; the failed comparison does not approve the polymer control.

Site evidence, ground chemistry/gas, water levels, loads and exact section dimensions remain with DPL/BCR/STR and the owning assembly designers. Above/below-slab position cannot be finalised ahead of those inputs. Roofs, wall insulation beyond this junction, air/vapour transitions, openings, glazing and exterior finishes remain within the broader MAT-ENVELOPE packet and are not resolved by this study.
