## Authorisation and earned outcome

The owner explicitly rejected plastic throughout the house where practicable, including hidden components and especially visible ones; preferred simple free shedding to designed ground receivers where robust, otherwise copper collection; requested English-hedge and brass-channel investigation; and strengthened blockage tolerance. This result converts that direction into binding source authority and bounded executable work. It does not claim to have designed the resulting house.

The [charter](../owner-house-charter.md) now links the [whole-house material policy](../../docs/reference-house/material-policy.md), [MAT programme](material-integrity-programme.md) and [rainwater rebrief](../rwt/rainwater-elegance-brief.md). MAT is the only new workstream. RWT is extended rather than duplicated.

Material avoidance is a strong owner-specific default prohibition, with no hidden-parts exemption and no pre-approved polymer exceptions. Required safety/performance functions cannot be omitted for purity. A difficult residual goes back to the owner with actual redesign and material alternatives, not a generic 'necessary plastic' waiver. Natural materials and non-plastic alternatives still need their own safety, compatibility and repair evidence.

## Research changed the brief in concrete ways

- The earlier **PVC preferred / PE fallback** carrier recommendation is superseded in its owning interpretation. Its numerical model, JSON, SVG, regression tests and historical source identity are retained. A metal substitution requires re-analysis of the complete pipe, sleeve, support, joint, movement and renewal arrangement.
- Existing IAS wet-room candidates include **KERDI-200 polyethylene and KERDI-BOARD XPS**, confirmed by their manufacturer. Concealed waterproofing is therefore part of the redesign, not an exception inferred from tiling. A cement board alone is not a complete replacement water boundary.
- A manufacturer counterexample defeats the shortcut 'wood fibre means plastic-free': [Gutex Thermosafe Homogen technical data, p.1](https://www.gutex.co.uk/Downloads/PDF/Technisches%20Datenblatt%20-%20EN/Gutex_Thermosafe%20Homogen_TDB_en.pdf), visually inspected, declares 4.0% PUR resin and 0.50% paraffin. This product is an audit example, not an installed-house choice. A binder-free cellular-glass core and mineral-insulated electrical cable are alternative families, but their adhesives, facings, joints and terminations remain separate audit duties.
- Historic England supplies a precedent for deep eaves and ground collection **and** a warning that gravel land drains near foundations can concentrate water there. The new receiver must have an actual safe outlet; infiltration is neither assumed nor selected.
- Hedges receive no essential hydraulic or splash-safety credit. The no-plant, winter, clipped/debris and blocked-receiver conditions must work. The indexed 2026 hedge rainfall-attenuation study is a useful research lead, not evidence for a concentrated high-eave stream.
- The flow and trajectory method explicitly separates known arithmetic from missing physics. Pitch and roof material do not uniquely establish exit velocity, wind drift or splash width; a ballistic illustration is not a validated eaves design.
- 'Two pipes' is replaced by an actual surviving-path duty: either critical primary element blocked, full defined failure-case flow, reachable secondary inlet, independently functioning destination, and a safe final spill assessment. Shared blockage/backwater and common debris remain adverse cases.
- Brass-channel alloy, runoff compatibility and finish are explicit questions. A copper/brass headline does not authorise plastic backing, clearcoat or hidden polymer joints.

The programmes retain primary-source links, applicability limits and unresolved evidence. No manufacturer contact, product purchase, physical test or real-site fact is invented.
