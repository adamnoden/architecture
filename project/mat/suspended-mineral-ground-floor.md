# Raised suspended mineral floor: an alternative to the difficult membrane junction

**31 October 2026 · MAT-ENVELOPE bounded comparison · not for construction.** Starting main `d81aab99cd8900395deffc16a26f1490fb9658f6`. Read the [existing ground-boundary comparison](envelope-ground-boundary.md), [material policy](../../docs/reference-house/material-policy.md) and [passive-dryness policy](../../docs/reference-house/ground-water-policy.md). This adds a third construction strategy; it does not select a floor, omit a required barrier or modify the house.

## Decision

**Carry a raised, ventilated suspended concrete floor forward alongside the two ground-supported boundary arrangements. Do not assume that an asphalt-to-rigid-DPC connection is the only possible non-plastic route.** The moisture strategy is different: separate the floor from the ground and protect the support/junction paths, rather than depend solely on a continuous ground-supported membrane. Qualify that strategy before adding an equivalent membrane back by habit; retain one wherever the actual site, floor system or other performance duty requires it.

This is a credible alternative to investigate, **not a universal membrane exemption**. It is also not a way to create a serviceable undercroft inside a nominal 150 mm ventilation gap. A failed suspended alternative does not approve the old plastic option; it returns the project to the full assembly comparison.

## The connected wall-to-floor proposition

The drawing is an **ordinary straight-wall trial, not a threshold detail**. Its wall widths come from the existing provisional source: 102.5 outer leaf, 160 cavity and 155 inner leaf **including finish**, total 417.5 mm. Those are allocations, not accepted masonry, clear cavity or structural bearing widths. STR retains the structural floor/support selection.

1. Continue the proposed mineral capillary break through the actual loadbearing wall/support below the floor-bearing zone. The existing DPC1/slate evidence remains the qualification route. Show all beds, returns, internal supports and the controlling wet/dry datum; do not represent a substantial DPC as an infinitely thin sheet. A supplier's requirement for a DPC does not approve this particular rigid bearing interface.
2. Keep the suspended floor and its end construction protected from **lateral cavity water**, not only upward capillarity. An intact DPC below a beam cannot stop water arriving at its end from the side. Retain the existing clear-cavity/drained-tray alternatives and their own fill, outlet and constituent requirements; no cavity tray is deleted by adopting suspension.
3. Put the thermal layer and room finishes above the structural comparison, while resolving the wall-edge thermal bridge and air closure separately. This avoids pretending that a glass insulation core automatically seals joints or has masonry-bearing qualification. The exact floor insulation, screed, separator, perimeter strip, grout and concrete admixtures remain undisclosed/unselected at whole-assembly scope.
4. Provide a genuinely continuous air route through the underfloor topology, including around or through properly designed internal supports. The wall cavity is not that route. Qualify any duct crossing for water entry, outward drainage, support, vermin protection, free area and constituents. Do not perforate the bonded mineral DPC or notch a structural beam to obtain it.

This reduces one potential dependence on a floor-field DPM-to-wall lap only **if** the complete suspended solution earns that result. It does not erase the wall DPC, damp/gas duties, beam-end protection or the weathered court threshold. No gas membrane is waived. The current site and ground-gas conditions are unknown.

### The vent competes with the substantial DPC

For one **straight, below-DPC** ventilation route, let `V` be floor-soffit-to-solum clearance, `B` the whole DPC-band depth below that soffit, `E` the complete duct envelope and `a/b` its upper/lower separations. A necessary vertical condition is:

`V >= B + E + a + b`.

The drawing uses **B=150 mm only as an authored two-nominal-course territory**, not an actual brick/mortar schedule; all beds and the proper DPC datum require qualification. Its ventilation probe is a **100 mm clear bore**, not the complete duct. Even ignoring duct walls and all separations, this particular arrangement consumes **250 mm**. Thus a 150 or 225 mm void cannot contain this particular straight route below that band. It does not reject suspension, other vent positions or a properly designed offset route.

The drawn 300 mm void leaves **50 mm** beyond the nominal band and clear bore. Its illustrative 20 mm upper and 30 mm lower gaps spend that entire remainder: **no duct-body allowance remains**. A hypothetical 120 mm complete envelope would exceed that stack by 20 mm. The actual envelope is unknown; the test deliberately does not report a fit. A more generous void, a different support/DPC arrangement or a verified offset may be better, but each changes the connected section.

In the drawn trial, the band is shown directly below the bearing, with its lower edge 150 mm above the assumed outside-ground/solum plane. This is a geometric coincidence of the authored 630 mm section, **not resolution of the relevant mineral-DPC datum or a statutory height selection**. The bearing overlap is schematic. Cavity fill and its structural duty remain unshown/unresolved; neither the air space nor this DPC position supplies the required cavity-water clearance.

The **quantity screen is only `12 m × 1,500 mm²/m`**, using one represented 12 m exterior wall length. S1 §4.19(b) requires the greater of the wall-length and floor-area criteria, opposing-wall paths and ventilation reaching every part. No served floor area, internal support openings or whole-house vent schedule is established here. The 215 × 65 face rectangle is not its 1,300 mm² free area; the complete route must retain adequate net passage through the grille, liner, joints and any other restriction, with actual airflow/distribution checked. A 65 mm outside-height rectangular liner is not a 100 mm clear pipe. The applicability/equivalence of a particular rectangular crossing remains to be qualified against S1's pipe guidance.

The last two columns reuse **V=300, B=150, a=20, b=30**, floor soffit **−330**, DPC-band bottom **−480**, and outside ground equal to solum at **−630 mm**. They are not new adopted levels. For component outside height `H`, the optimistic depth bound is `Vmin = B + a + H + b`; bedding, interfaces and tolerances can increase the envelope. YA14 and YA15 exceed this particular 300 mm arrangement by **40 and 115 mm**. YA13 leaves **35 mm** beyond the nominal component and stated gaps; it has no complete fit or airflow pass.

With each nominal component-envelope top at **−500 mm**, its bottom clearance is `V − B − a − H − ΔG`, where `ΔG` is outside ground above solum. The table uses `ΔG=0`. Thus the two larger outside envelopes project below that trial ground plane. TE115 does not locate the lowest open-hole edge, so this does **not** establish that an aperture is below ground. Raising outside ground reduces the envelope clearance; moving the vent changes the connected section and its drainage/approach duties. The +65 mm YA13 envelope result is not a safe splash, flood or weather clearance. The actual lowest aperture, suitable mouth height and site levels remain unselected.

The **200 mm liner is a cavity-crossing component**, not a declared complete 417.5 mm wall sleeve. Against the provisional 160 mm cavity it leaves only **40 mm in total** for any end engagement before tolerances; this is not a qualified bearing length or a selected position. The outer and inner masonry throats, their joints to the liner and the room-side termination remain to be drawn. The 155 mm inner allocation includes finish and does not define the below-floor masonry. Keep cavity water from entering that passage or bypassing the mineral DPC, while preserving the separate cavity drainage route. The S4 membrane illustration does not qualify a mineral replacement for this duty.

The opening also needs its actual masonry support, bedding and construction order. An airbrick or liner does not inherit the engineering-brick DPC's load evidence; do not cut the bonded DPC or a floor member to fit it. Qualify every retained mortar/additive, treatment and accessory. For maintenance, distinguish clearing the exterior apertures and the **whole hidden passage** from replacing embedded units. ### Raising the building is a different comparison

For a deliberately synthetic case where solum remains level with fixed surrounding ground **150 mm below the old FFL**, the three total depths would require relative FFL increases of **330, 405 and 480 mm** respectively. Those numbers are not the deficits in the table and are not proposed house raises. If the ground/solum rises with the floor, that relative gain disappears. DPC clearances, flood/groundwater margins, accessible approaches, court levels and planning/height consequences may require a different section. Excavating down to preserve the old FFL must not create the pump-dependent wet space prohibited by the brief.

## Services and retained boundaries

A ventilated void is neither a walkable undercroft nor proof of reachable drainage. The drawing admits **no rainwater carrier into it**. Putting a pipe beneath the suspended structure can make its joints and replacement less accessible; putting it above the structure needs additional depth, independently supported access and a leak route. Do not cut prestressed members or delete floor insulation to recover the old copper-clearance budget.

Routine W1 carrier renewal must still preserve the court's W2 boundary and every required floor/air/gas boundary. Suspension does not close an open outlet bore, make a blocked receiver safe or demonstrate threshold-piece removal. RWT's [segmented-access](../rwt/segmented-copper-access.md) and [passive site-interface](../rwt/passive-court-site-interface.md) questions remain intact. The third floor strategy is useful only if the occupied rooms, access and water routes can be coordinated without a hidden new dependency.

## Selection consequences

**Retain C as a serious geometry-led challenger; reject using it as a label to remove the membrane from ground-supported A or B.** Develop its actual bearing/DPC, vent and thermal-edge construction against those two controls. Keep the present house dimensions unchanged until HAD/STR and the owning envelope design have compared the resulting approach, ground, court and support consequences.

Before admitting C, bind the actual site and ground-gas evidence, floor system, reinforcement exposure, load paths, mineral DPC/bearing, cavity rain protection, thermal/air closure and non-plastic ventilation accessories. Check blocked/obscured vents, water entry and post-flood inspection/cleaning without relying on power. If the site or selected floor requires a continuous membrane, provide a compliant complete boundary or reject this membrane-avoidance variant; the policy is not permission to leave that function out.

**Earned:** a third supported construction strategy, a connected section exposing its DPC/vent conflict, and distinct reserve-versus-ground-level calculations. **Not earned:** DPM omission, a complete non-plastic installation, a dry site, a structural/thermal design, service access or MAT-ENVELOPE completion. No exception, canonical lifecycle, house geometry or existing study output changes.
