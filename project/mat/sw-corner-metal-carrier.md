# SW below-structure metal barrier and carrier trial

This [finite local panel and orthogonal section](studies/sw-corner-metal-carrier.svg) tests a continuous-metal alternative that **does not put a sheet through the monolithic warm slab/receiver**. The [reproducer](studies/sw-corner-metal-carrier.py) and [data](studies/sw-corner-metal-carrier.json) pin the actual warm-grid, selected HAD, and current STR station blobs. It is a development challenger: the continuous rods in STR are not silently replaced, and neither the structural route nor a complete gas/water barrier is accepted.

Place a trial 1 mm bare-metal field at **−605..−604**, and a 300 × 300 × 3 mm local plate at **−605..−602**, x/y262.5–562.5. The plate occupies the **bottom 3 mm of the nominal −605..−390 AAC course**, above the −615..−605 mortar and −765..−615 DPC. Thus the unmodified 215 mm unit and plate overlap: a rebate, cut or explicit course/level redesign is needed, with its bearing and capillary consequences. No cut unit or unchanged full-thickness AAC is qualified. The 1 mm field also occupies the bottom millimetre wherever it crosses other AAC pads or beams; each must be set out and reconciled. A continuous field-to-plate butt weld is proposed around the plate perimeter. Four split rods would terminate against opposite plate faces: lower pieces −990..−605, upper pieces −602..+6410. Their welds must not create a through-hole or leak, and the plate and metal around it must carry the actual compression, uplift, shear and moment without assuming AAC or mortar has tensile capacity. This is a **different structural station**, requiring STR to design welded connections, bearing, local restraint, anchorage and force transfer; the old four continuous rods do not remain continuously welded through an unperforated plate. Site water/gas conditions and the metal-to-mineral bearing interfaces remain unknown.

A bounded force challenge uses the STR station’s **partial two-outer-face uplift only**, −8.740 kN at (435.585,416.551). Its resultant lies inside the four-rod rectangle; one nonnegative bilinear equilibrium witness gives 1.551, 1.698, 2.622 and 2.869 kN, ordered SW, NW, SE, NE. The model checks both first moments and total force. It is not a predicted allocation: actual plate bending, welds, tension-only stiffness/contact, other roof areas, gravity, wind, horizontal and combined actions are absent. The lower plate is **225 mm below warm RC bottom and 160 mm above cold RC top**; neither this witness nor the original continuous-bar cage check proves the split connection.

For an explicit room-side test, one 1,000 × 1,000 mm panel occupies x/y600–1600. Four 40 × 40 × 4 mm metal angle rails sit on the field at −604..−564, one on each panel edge; four nominal Ø8 × 324 mm hangers at the panel corners reach the assumed slab soffit −280. The hangers and angles are on the **upper** side of the field and connect to welded tabs, so their attachment does not itself require holes through the sheet. They attach to the slab with separately designed concrete anchors; exact anchor type, embedment, slab reinforcement clash, load, mounting tolerances and access are not selected. Their positions are a local set-out, not proof that the actual entire floor has usable slab and installation access at every grid point. The nearest panel edges are **37.5 mm from the plate** on each axis; a **100 × 100 mm connector** at x/y550–650 is proposed. Its untrimmed footprint overlaps the patch only 12.5 × 12.5 mm and the panel 50 × 50 mm; trim matching boundaries and continuously butt-weld, with no unsealed lap credited. The earlier x/y512.5–650 connector left only 4.142 mm from the NE Ø20 shank; moving this trial outward increases nominal shank clearance to **57.175 mm** but leaves a much shorter patch overlap. The short joint, weld footprints and cutting/inspection access are not a strength or fit pass. Exact cuts, edge support, welding access and inspection remain to be designed. No unsupported 12 m sheet is credited.

The 320 mm glass reaches −600, so the 40 mm high rails intercept its lowest **36 mm** locally; hangers cross it vertically. The drawing is not a claim of an uncut continuous insulation field. Support and retention of cut glass, local metal heat bridges, wetting, movement and condensate require an actual 3D model and build-up; the previous thermal screen is not transferable. The warm grid ends at −380, above this field, but its AAC, mortar and the widened cold/weather-leaf receiver create separate edge/bearing and thermal interfaces. A cold receiver extended beyond the house cannot be used to bridge the drained cavity or to connect the wet weather leaf to the warm core.

Using 7,850 kg/m³ solely as a gross sensitivity, one 1 mm panel weighs **7.85 kg**, four 40 × 40 × 4 rails of 1 m each **9.55 kg**, and four Ø8 × 324 hangers **0.51 kg**: **17.91 kg** total or **0.176 kN**. Four equal shares give **0.044 kN each** for self-weight only. There is no sharing credit for the four perimeter rails; neighbouring panels would double-count common rails. Weld metal, anchors, edge strips, insulation supports, water, installation loads, wind/gas pressure, seismic effects and safety factors are absent. Neither this arithmetic nor the chosen nominal sizes proves span stiffness, anchor capacity, fixity, sheet buckling, fatigue or pull-out.

The [water-industry stainless-steel guidance](https://standards-board.water.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/IGN-4-25-02-Issue-2-August-2023-published-version-1.pdf) identifies steel sheet for groundwater separation, and [BSSA tank guidance](https://bssa.org.uk/bssa_articles/selection-of-stainless-steels-for-water-tank-applications/) prefers filled welded joints to laps or mechanical seams. Neither proves this exact house, field-to-DPC joint, gas or weld assembly. The exact alloy, filler, shielding/cleaning materials, retained finishes, anchors and protection remain undisclosed; no polymer exception or product approval is inferred.

A plausible construction test is: survey cold receiver and lower rods; fabricate and inspect the patch and lower welds; place and protect the AAC/mortar with a designed local bearing; erect the upper cage and cast the warm pad/slab; install insulated-field retention and anchors without damaging structural reinforcement; fit and weld the upper frame, strips and sheet seams; connect all perimeter and penetration transitions; perform documented accessible leak tests and protect the surface before concealment. In reality welding below a completed slab in a confined insulated void, cleaning welds, removing temporary shims and inspecting the cold face may be impossible at some locations. Full staged access, fire protection, temporary supports, corrosion-compatible grade and filler, any shielding gas/cleaner or retained coating, inspection/repair openings and replacement methods must be evidenced rather than assumed. The house field also needs real court, walls, doors, services, cold-grid crossings and independent rain/cavity details; an exterior return drawn across the entire wall would bypass the weather separation.

**Decision: the local carrier has an explicit geometry and self-weight route, but it does not deliver a complete admitted envelope.** The connected welded perimeter, accessible fabrication, anchors, thermal insulation continuity, movement and corrosion, hydrostatic and gas duty, and jointly designed load path remain unresolved. An exact non-polymer material family is not qualified. No pressure-water, ground-gas, lifetime, professional or physical test result follows from the schematic. If those spatial or structural duties cannot be resolved together, redesign the ground/support system rather than accepting an unsealed or unbuildable field.
