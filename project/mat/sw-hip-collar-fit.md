# SW hip: finite collar fit challenge

The [reproducer](studies/sw-hip-collar-fit.py), [coordinates](studies/sw-hip-collar-fit.json) and [schematic face projection](studies/sw-hip-collar-fit.svg) take the merged STR SW station and MAT ground-field trial as their exact source. This is a geometry challenge to the **unadopted** metal boundary, not a fabrication detail or an air, gas, water, structural, thermal or fire acceptance. The two south/west sheets meet at the fold (262.5,262.5); a hole in each independently drawn flat face would not by itself make one continuous corner.

The trial hip is SHS120 with a 25-degree parent roof and diagonal pitch θ = atan(tan 25°/√2). Its axis crosses that corner at +6519.080. On either face, let u measure from the fold towards the inside end of that face. The ideal bare-member intersection is the parallelogram bounded by u = 0..84.853 and z = 6519.080 + u tan(25°)/2 ± 60/cos θ. Its four (u,z) vertices in mm are (0,6455.902), (84.853,6475.686), (84.853,6602.041), and (0,6582.257). STR independently checked the signs and ideal-section arithmetic. Real steel end conditions, fillets, fabrication and survey tolerances remain outside this projection.

One **new, deliberately optimistic collar trial** places a concentric 130 mm square *internal* envelope about the 120 mm member, leaving nominal 5 mm clearance per face. A proposed 40 mm flange band outside the rectangular bounds of that internal cut is tested against the assumed sheet continuation +6410..+6660. Its bottom is +6410.637, only **0.637 mm** above the sheet start; its top is +6648.954, **11.046 mm** below the assumed end. The flange's true shape at the common fold and at the cap is not given by a rectangle: its negative-u part wraps onto the other face, and the sleeve's actual wall thickness, actual welds and tolerances would consume additional space. The numerical drawing shows an optimistic bounding enquiry, not a developed fabrication pattern. Reject this 40 mm-band trial as a credible untoleranced fit; it does not prove all collar geometries impossible. Extending the sheet farther down, changing the band, shifting or splitting the collar, or revising the support would each change actual interfaces and need a fresh joint design.

A closed surface requires both face sheets to join the collar around the complete finite sloped hip; joining a sleeve to two sheets while leaving its 5 mm annular gap open to the hollow section is a leakage path. End closures, a continuous connection to the verified member skin, member end closures, inspection and possible repair require an actual sequence and reachable welds. The member is a structural thermal bridge crossing the envelope. Its movement relative to the sheet, cap, ring and roof air layer, restraint from concrete, fire protection, corrosion, insulation and thermal bridging are unresolved. No annular weld recipe, site pressure, leak test acceptance or qualified component is asserted. The +6410..+6430 cap and adjacent steel may obstruct fabrication; spatial overlap of elevations alone is not a proven solid collision. The new structural diagonal-tie alternatives are separate unadopted geometry, not part of this source-bound calculation.

The south court threshold remains an independent open interface. HAD places the 1,800 mm rough doorway at x5100..6900, weather face y4250; the field bounding edge y3987.5 is 262.5 mm away. The nominal opening does not give the door frame, sill, levels, drainage, tolerances or bearing detail. A finite return across that interval needs those missing interfaces, including water shedding and a protected, maintainable air/gas join; simply extending a horizontal sheet across it would neither locate nor connect the frame and could cut or bypass the required support. No complete threshold is claimed.
