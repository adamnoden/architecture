# SW hip: finite collar fit challenge

The [reproducer](studies/sw-hip-collar-fit.py), [coordinates](studies/sw-hip-collar-fit.json) and [schematic face projection](studies/sw-hip-collar-fit.svg) take the merged STR SW station and MAT ground-field trial as their exact source. This is a geometry challenge to the **unadopted** metal boundary, not a fabrication detail or an air, gas, water, structural, thermal or fire acceptance. The two south/west sheets meet at the fold (262.5,262.5); a hole in each independently drawn flat face would not by itself make one continuous corner.

The trial hip is SHS120 with a 25-degree parent roof and diagonal pitch θ = atan(tan 25°/√2). Its axis crosses that corner at +6519.080. On either face, let u measure from the fold towards the inside end of that face. The ideal bare-member intersection is the parallelogram bounded by u = 0..84.853 and z = 6519.080 + u tan(25°)/2 ± 60/cos θ. Its four (u,z) vertices in mm are (0,6455.902), (84.853,6475.686), (84.853,6602.041), and (0,6582.257). STR independently checked the signs and ideal-section arithmetic. Real steel end conditions, fillets, fabrication and survey tolerances remain outside this projection.

One **new, deliberately optimistic collar trial** places a concentric 130 mm square *internal* envelope about the 120 mm member, leaving nominal 5 mm clearance per face. A proposed 40 mm flange band outside the rectangular bounds of that internal cut is tested against the assumed sheet continuation +6410..+6660. Its bottom is +6410.637, only **0.637 mm** above an extension joint fixed at +6410; its top is +6648.954, **11.046 mm** below the assumed end. The flange's true shape at the common fold and at the cap is not given by a rectangle: its negative-u part wraps onto the other face, and the sleeve's actual wall thickness, actual welds and tolerances would consume additional space. The numerical drawing shows an optimistic bounding enquiry, not a developed fabrication pattern. Reject this joint position as a credible untoleranced fit; it does not prove all collar geometries impossible. The existing local wall sheet continues below +6410. Moving the extension joint to +6370 gives a **40.637 mm** nominal distance below the same bound, without moving the hip; this is a real geometric alternative, not a proven weld or support. Its junction near the cap and its accessible fabrication sequence remain to be designed. Changing the band, shifting or splitting the collar, or revising the support would also change actual interfaces and need a fresh joint design.

A closed surface requires both face sheets to join the collar around the complete finite sloped hip; joining a sleeve to two sheets while leaving its 5 mm annular gap open to the hollow section is a leakage path. End closures, a continuous connection to the verified member skin, member end closures, inspection and possible repair require an actual sequence and reachable welds. The member is a structural thermal bridge crossing the envelope. Its movement relative to the sheet, cap, ring and roof air layer, restraint from concrete, fire protection, corrosion, insulation and thermal bridging are unresolved. No annular weld recipe, site pressure, leak test acceptance or qualified component is asserted. The +6410..+6430 cap and adjacent steel may obstruct fabrication; spatial overlap of elevations alone is not a proven solid collision. The new structural diagonal-tie alternatives are separate unadopted geometry, not part of this source-bound calculation.

The south court threshold remains an independent open interface. HAD places the 1,800 mm rough doorway at x5100..6900, weather face y4250; the field bounding edge y3987.5 is 262.5 mm away. The nominal opening does not give the door frame, sill, levels, drainage, tolerances or bearing detail. A finite return across that interval needs those missing interfaces, including water shedding and a protected, maintainable air/gas join; simply extending a horizontal sheet across it would neither locate nor connect the frame and could cut or bypass the required support. No complete threshold is claimed.

## One finite sleeve direction

Use an axis coordinate s measured in the uphill direction from the common sheet corner. The **130 mm internal square envelope** intersects the two folded face planes from s = −21.432 to +89.875 mm. A finite conceptual sleeve from **s = −30 to +100 mm** therefore contains the whole ideal face intersection, with **8.568 mm** at its outer end and **10.125 mm** at its inner end. A full continuous connection would have to follow the actual two-plane folded cut, rather than only one elevation. The following end extents are axis-aligned bounds of the *internal* envelope; each x or y range is a separate projection, not a simultaneous corner point:

| Sleeve end | x and y projections, mm | z projection, mm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| s −30 mm | 181.999..302.708 | 6447.954..6571.416 |
| s +100 mm | 269.300..390.009 | 6488.663..6612.125 |

The envelope's lowest z is nominally **17.954 mm above** the source cap top +6430; a real sleeve wall, root, flange, weld and construction tolerances reduce that. At the outer end, the x/y lower projection remains nominally **29.499 mm inward** of the q152.5 glass/cavity interface. STR independently minimized the same ideal sleeve envelope over the cap plan: it reaches **+6450.637 mm** at the common corner. The separate trial 20 mm hip block would rise to **+6450**, leaving only **0.637 mm** ideal vertical separation. Its footprint and packing are not authored, so this is a critical clearance warning, not a demonstrated solid collision. The represented eave-ring runs begin at longitudinal 562.5, outside the sleeve projections below 391; unmodelled saddles and brackets are not screened. None is a clearance proof against the actual block, other members, roof layers, finishes, fixings or installation tooling. The tiny axial end margins do not establish access for a continuous joint. Unlike an imagined closed cut, the 5 mm gap extends along the member and must be positively closed at a finite location, while the hip's own hollow interior and the roof-side air layer also require separate continuous boundaries. No actual end-ring dimensions, welding procedure, joints to the sheets or inspection access are selected.

A joint needs to accommodate movement as well as form a closed surface. [RADCOFLEX's rectangular metal expansion joint](https://radcoflex.com/products/metal-expansion-joint/rff-rww.html) is evidence that welded rectangular metal bellows and weld ends exist for low-pressure air/gas ducts and axial compression. It is **not** evidence of a suitably sized part, lateral or rotational movement rating, ground-gas or water performance, permissible fire/corrosion duty, service life, or a way to connect this particular field and structural hip. The sheet-to-sleeve, sleeve-to-member and roof-layer interfaces remain a single unfinished design, as do repair access and non-polymer material compatibility. The only defensible outcome here is a finite candidate envelope and explicit negative control, not an installed barrier or accepted collar.
