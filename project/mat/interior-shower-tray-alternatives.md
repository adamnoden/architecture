## Question and result

Can an enamelled-steel tray replace the visible resin body in the [Bedroom 4 wet-assembly comparison](../ias/ias-03-bedroom4-assembly-successor.md), with a credible support, water boundary and replacement route?

**Carry the steel/enamel body forward, with a west-facing drain as the next access comparison.** The manufacturer's base-tray drawing locates the drain differently from the former corner outlet. The existing north-box access and waste-route arithmetic cannot transfer. A new access face at the tray's west side is a useful design direction; its opening, working space, support and matched waste still need to be resolved.

The research now binds the nominal 1000-square principal-support candidate to **Foot-System B50-3064**. Its clamped perimeter frame must remain distinct from the proposed removable west service face. The next whole-tray removal comparison starts with tray and frame together, subject to release of every remaining connection; this is a design direction, not a manufacturer's dismantling procedure. The complete installation remains undisclosed, including an additional-support recommendation that names a plastic/epoxy accessory. The examined current BetteCompact+ waste is plastic, despite an optional enamel cover. The integral Upstand removes an exposed silicone wall joint but retains concealed sealing. No complete no-plastic assembly, exception, purchase specification or service life is accepted.

## Matched frame, separate service face and first moving unit

**Retain the support loop during ordinary waste access.** The 1000-square nominal tray falls within B6's case A, whose two side lengths are below 1200 mm. Its rail formula gives **1000 − 390 = 610 mm** for each straight rail. This is a component length, not the opening between installed feet, an access-panel width or a waste-withdrawal clearance. The manual shows the corners and rails joined into a perimeter frame, placed against the inverted tray and clamped under its rim before the assembly is lowered into position. The separate foot-position options do not authorise removing a loaded support to reach the waste. [B6, pp7, 10–13; B9]

For the existing west-face station at **(1335, 10322.5)**, develop an independently removable fascia with its own reachable fixings, below the retained west rail and clear of the corner/support components. Its opening must admit the selected waste body and tools while the tray remains supported. The rail and clamps are part of the load path; they are not removable fascia fixings. Neither the 610 mm rail nor the 210 mm drain offset proves a usable opening, and the published diagrams supply no complete installed rail/foot/waste envelope to close that question.

Whole-tray renewal has a different first moving object. **Carry the tray with its clamped frame into the next removal comparison**, after proving release of the waste connection, wall ties, connected finishes and any other restraint. This authored direction follows the shown assembly relation; B6 gives no validated reverse-removal sequence. An alternative that separates the frame in situ must first demonstrate access to the underside clamps, temporary support and controlled release. Do not reduce the carrying object to the nominal 25 mm tray depth or claim that the feet can retract after enclosure merely because they adjusted during installation.
