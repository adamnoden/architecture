## A materially different crossing: split rods at a metal diaphragm

A possible redesign replaces each continuous shank at the band with separate upper and lower rods welded to opposite faces of a single **unperforated 300 × 300 × 3 mm metal diaphragm**, x/y262.5–562.5, z−574..−571. The upper pieces run z−571..+6640 (7,211 mm) and lower pieces −990..−574 (416 mm). The plate is embedded in the AAC zone; it is an interruption across the four rod paths, not a proven seal around its perimeter. The four welds on each face, plate bending, tear-out, compressive contact, embedment and lateral reversal would have to be designed together by STR; no assumption of a moment-free connection or equal rod force is justified. Unlike the original continuous rods, this would change the structural station and needs a new structural analysis.

The finite geometric test removes the four *through-holes in this plate*; it does not remove penetrations through the remainder of the mineral band, or demonstrate that the plate perimeter joins a gas-tight field barrier. A plate edge ending inside porous AAC/mortar is not a capillary break or an air/gas seal. Neither a bonded mineral joint, a metal-to-field transition nor a compatible movement detail is evidenced here. The study therefore **fails as a complete crossing**, despite being a more useful redesign candidate than loose annular fill. The UK Health Protection Agency's [radon evidence review](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7ea84aed915d74e6225a5d/RCE-11_for_website.pdf) explains that radon enters principally with airflow and calls for a continuous barrier and sealed joints and penetrations; its example uses a polymer membrane. That example establishes the continuity duty, not approval of that material here or suitability for an unknown site. The actual site gas assessment determines the relevant gas, pressure and protection strategy; an unventilated or otherwise defective detail must not be accepted on this analogy. The existing [ground-boundary comparison](envelope-ground-boundary.md) likewise identifies no evidenced all-non-synthetic floor moisture/gas system connected to the mineral wall DPC; the diaphragm cannot fix that field-level gap. Its above-slab challenger also has to preserve site-specific slab protection and accessible movement/renewal duties.

Using the same deliberately idealised steel λ50 W/mK and four gross Ø20 areas, a 3 mm full-area plate in series with 382 mm total rod length gives approximately **0.1645 W/K**. It does not erase the steel bridge; the continuous-rod 385 mm indicator is **0.1632 W/K**. Fixed end temperatures, perfect contact and no edge leakage or three-dimensional spreading are assumed. Plate mass is 2.12 kg at 7,850 kg/m³, before welds. A 3D coupled corner model and surface/condensation assessment still decide whether the real detail is acceptable.

The rigid crossing transfers relative warm/cold receiver displacement and rotation into the rods, welds, concrete and perimeter junction. Shrinkage, thermal change, settlement and cyclic shear need compatible load and movement limits; there is no demonstrated sliding or flexible sealed interface. A practical test sequence would prefabricate and inspect both sets of welds, anchor the lower rods, protect the plate and prove its perimeter join before enclosure, then install the long upper rods, cage and warm concrete. This sequence is a challenge for access, tolerance and repair, not evidence of constructability. Proceed only if an evidenced non-polymer field barrier and movement-capable transition, force path and installation sequence can be reconciled; otherwise redesign the ground/support system rather than adopt this plate or strip the barrier.

## Whole-field welded-metal challenger

The unjoined mineral floor is not fixed as a design rule. A more ambitious alternative extends the diaphragm into a **continuous welded bare-metal floor field**: trial 1 mm sheet at z−574..−573, joined to the 3 mm split-rod patch at z−574..−571, with field seams, corners and every necessary penetration continuously welded. The plate would be part of the field, not a loose lap onto an assumed gas barrier. The bare metal would need a continuous, qualified supported transition around every wall, court edge, threshold and service, and an engineered connection to the wall DPC/weather details. The old four continuous rods cannot simply pierce it; either use the structurally redesigned opposed-rod patch above or separately design and weld a complete collar at every rod. The latter restores continuous rods but welds the sheet directly to each moving, loaded member. Neither option is structurally or environmentally qualified.

There is a credible metal precedent but no transferable house certification: the UK water industry's [2023 stainless-steel application guidance](https://standards-board.water.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/IGN-4-25-02-Issue-2-August-2023-published-version-1.pdf) identifies stainless sheet for groundwater separation; the [British Stainless Steel Association's tank guidance](https://bssa.org.uk/bssa_articles/selection-of-stainless-steels-for-water-tank-applications/) prefers filled welded joints to laps or mechanical joints. Tank/groundwater service does not establish radon or other gas performance, strength or installation in this building. Exact alloy, sheet, weld filler and flux or shielding gas, surface treatment, any retained coating, protection/separation and every accessory must be disclosed and assessed under the material policy; no polymer layer or unapproved exception is assumed. Chlorides, trapped moisture, crevices, dissimilar metals and welding damage require a site-specific corrosion assessment rather than the word stainless as proof.

**The geometry tests against the suspended floor:** the candidate field lies at −574..−573, below the slab bottom −280 and inside the mineral band at the corner; much of the span is therefore an unsupported void rather than a substrate. A carrier, fixing scheme, penetration count, working space, gravity and any water pressures must be designed; simply drawing a plane across it gives no support. The 12 m × 12 m *gross bounding square*, not the actual floor area or a takeoff, would contain 1,130.4 kg of 1 mm sheet at an assumed 7,850 kg/m³, plus 1.413 kg for the local extra 2 mm patch, before overlaps, welds, supports or openings. At an illustrative expansion coefficient 16×10⁻⁶/K and uniform 20 K change, free expansion over 12 m is 3.84 mm. Actual temperature, restraint, differential concrete shrinkage, creep, settlement, out-of-plane load and fatigue are unknown. Welded folds or movement seams would themselves need a demonstrably gas- and water-tight, corrosion-compatible detail and access; a simple rigid seam is not a movement solution. The field also spreads heat around the cold penetrations, so the isolated rod indicator cannot determine the three-dimensional corner loss or surface temperature.

The conceptual construction order is: set and inspect cold anchors and patch; install an independently designed carrier; lay metal sheets and make accessible continuous seams/penetration collars; connect the whole perimeter and court/service interruptions; inspect and test the entire assembly before concealment; protect it while building the warm floor and pier; provide later inspection and repair access without cutting the boundary for routine work. Exact field layout, welded seam sequence, underside access, leak-test method and criteria, protective layers and repair procedure are absent. **Result: reject this as a completed assembly, but retain it as a whole-boundary redesign to test**, rather than concluding a continuous metal field is impossible or treating an unsupported material sketch as approved tanking. Site gas and groundwater conditions, independent structural verification, gas/water testing, fabrication and long-term durability remain separate admission gates.

A second placement tests whether that carrier can be avoided: trial the 1 mm field immediately **above** the warm slab, z−80..−79, using the slab as its continuous support and occupying 1 mm of the 65 mm floor-distribution reservation. This trades the large under-slab carrier for a different discontinuity: the full-height RC pier and other upstands pass through the sheet. A factory-fabricated welded sleeve and welded field seam could surround each upstand, but the top of that sleeve still needs an evidenced gas- and capillary-tight, movement-compatible connection to a continuous wall/roof boundary; a clamp against bare concrete is not evidence of one. Finishes and the slab protection/separation must be redesigned without claiming the 65 mm layer retains unchanged function, and site water or contamination may demand a barrier below the slab instead. Compare whole-system support, field-to-wall geometry, protection and repair before choosing either plane. This upper plane is a testable placement, not a proven seal or approval to move the ground boundary.
