## A materially different crossing: split rods at a metal diaphragm

A possible redesign replaces each continuous shank at the band with separate upper and lower rods welded to opposite faces of a single **unperforated 300 × 300 × 3 mm metal diaphragm**, x/y262.5–562.5, z−574..−571. The upper pieces run z−571..+6640 (7,211 mm) and lower pieces −990..−574 (416 mm). The plate is embedded in the AAC zone; it is an interruption across the four rod paths, not a proven seal around its perimeter. The four welds on each face, plate bending, tear-out, compressive contact, embedment and lateral reversal would have to be designed together by STR; no assumption of a moment-free connection or equal rod force is justified. Unlike the original continuous rods, this would change the structural station and needs a new structural analysis.

The finite geometric test removes the four *through-holes in this plate*; it does not remove penetrations through the remainder of the mineral band, or demonstrate that the plate perimeter joins a gas-tight field barrier. A plate edge ending inside porous AAC/mortar is not a capillary break or an air/gas seal. Neither a bonded mineral joint, a metal-to-field transition nor a compatible movement detail is evidenced here. The study therefore **fails as a complete crossing**, despite being a more useful redesign candidate than loose annular fill. The UK Health Protection Agency's [radon evidence review](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7ea84aed915d74e6225a5d/RCE-11_for_website.pdf) explains that radon enters principally with airflow and calls for a continuous barrier and sealed joints and penetrations; its example uses a polymer membrane. That example establishes the continuity duty, not approval of that material here or suitability for an unknown site. The actual site gas assessment determines the relevant gas, pressure and protection strategy; an unventilated or otherwise defective detail must not be accepted on this analogy.

Using the same deliberately idealised steel λ50 W/mK and four gross Ø20 areas, a 3 mm full-area plate in series with 382 mm total rod length gives approximately **0.1645 W/K**. It does not erase the steel bridge; the continuous-rod 385 mm indicator is **0.1632 W/K**. Fixed end temperatures, perfect contact and no edge leakage or three-dimensional spreading are assumed. Plate mass is 2.12 kg at 7,850 kg/m³, before welds. A 3D coupled corner model and surface/condensation assessment still decide whether the real detail is acceptable.

The rigid crossing transfers relative warm/cold receiver displacement and rotation into the rods, welds, concrete and perimeter junction. Shrinkage, thermal change, settlement and cyclic shear need compatible load and movement limits; there is no demonstrated sliding or flexible sealed interface. A practical test sequence would prefabricate and inspect both sets of welds, anchor the lower rods, protect the plate and prove its perimeter join before enclosure, then install the long upper rods, cage and warm concrete. This sequence is a challenge for access, tolerance and repair, not evidence of constructability. Proceed only if an evidenced non-polymer field barrier and movement-capable transition, force path and installation sequence can be reconciled; otherwise redesign the ground/support system rather than adopt this plate or strip the barrier.
