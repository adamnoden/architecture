# Continuous metal ground-field direction

The [plan and datum comparison](studies/ground-metal-field.svg), [data](studies/ground-metal-field.json) and [reproducer](studies/ground-metal-field.py) develop one candidate after the SW corner's below-slab pan failed to establish a complete boundary. This is an **unadopted field topology with decisive fit failures**, not a specification, an installed DPC, a gas/water-tight assembly or an approved structural change. It pins the selected HAD scene, MAT receiving-grid comparison and merged STR corner station.

The preferred development basis is the selected HAD **RC200 slab top −180 / soffit −380**, with the joined-ground reference’s separately unselected 100 mm bare-glass layer above it. The candidate is a continuous, fully welded **1 mm bare-metal sheet at −381..−380**, with a 300 × 300 × 3 mm plate at the actual SW pier **−383..−380**; their top faces align and their undersides step by 2 mm. The upper rod ends start at −380 and the lower rod ends terminate at −383, on opposite plate faces. A sealed butt join around the plate and field joins are design intentions: the welds, actual metal grade, compatibility, tensile force route, plate bending and compression/shear contact are unqualified. The plate lies immediately below the warm-girder soffit, so it avoids a sheet cutting the warm RC, but occupies **3 mm of the 10 mm upper mortar bed** and leaves 7 mm below. The neighbouring 1 mm field occupies the upper millimetre and leaves **9 mm below**. The earlier −383..−382 field split the bed into 7 mm below and 2 mm above; it is retained only as a negative control. The contact planes are real potential slip and leakage interfaces; do not credit the original continuous mortar bond or monolithic structural transfer. The full 215 mm AAC and 150 mm mineral DPC may be retained geometrically, not automatically accepted with this altered bearing.

## One whole-field control, not a completed field

A first bounding field spans x/y262.5..11737.5, the outer ordinary core's exterior faces. It excludes the courtyard core-face rectangle x/y3987.5..8012.5 and the outdoor-loggia core-face strip x4447.5..7552.5 from y8012.5 to11737.5. The latter x values come from the selected 519.5 loggia sidewall cores, not an imagined mirror of the ordinary SW wall. The plan is a single connected **bounding surface**, with **103.909 m²** gross after those rectangular exclusions, not the actual surface area, a net cut list, a warm-floor takeoff or proof that every strip is constructible. At 1 mm and an assumed 7,850 kg/m³ the sheet-only gross sensitivity is **815.7 kg**, before seams, support, wastage or openings. The real perimeter, internal grid and all intersecting walls must be set out against the actual house before using a quantity or asserting coverage.

Four interfaces falsify a claim of completion:

- **SW:** the plate/rod junction meets the −50..777 cold receiver, the exterior weather leaf, the 50 mm drained cavity and the 110 mm wall glass. The metal must not make a wet-to-warm bypass, block cavity discharge or rely on the old 600-square node. Rod, reinforcement, weld and AAC/mortar contact are a jointly designed STR/MAT task.
- **South court doorway:** the selected rough door spans x5100..6900 at weather face y4250. The bounding field edge at y3987.5 is **262.5 mm away**. That interval contains a real support/threshold/weather transition; an unconnected line on the plan is not a sealed pan, door joint or first-harm route.
- **Loggia and north portal:** the clear outdoor route and its 519.5 sidewalls have separate water, thermal and movement duties. The rectangle is a conservative geometric cut at structural-core faces, not a drawing of the door arch, cavity head, sill or their continuous sealed interfaces.
- **Services and internal support lines:** wall, pipe, cable, drain, structural and threshold crossings need actual centre, extent, sleeve, welded return, movement, access and repair. None may be silently omitted to retain an unbroken-looking sheet.

For the earlier −383..−382 control, the separate [joined court/service reference](joined-ground-floor-and-court-boundary.md) creates another exact failure. Its ordinary mineral DPC field starts at −380 and a plane at −383 lies **3 mm inside that bonded DPC band**, not necessarily in the upper AAC mortar described by the other comparison. The band diagram does not locate the exact upper brick/bed interface, so the affected constituent cannot be asserted without a bond schedule. Its retained W1 carrier has crown −378 and invert −538: the proposed sheet passes through its section **5 mm below its crown**. The independent W2 tray and A/G lid at −310..−316 with seal to −318 have their own functions. A flat sheet or a generic sleeve would obstruct W1 renewal and potentially bypass W2 or A/G. A deliberately stepped field, reinforced bridge and sealed, inspectable W1/W2/A/G transition must be set out against the actual court and spine paths. These are incompatible conditional floor references, not simultaneous selected constructions; neither can be erased by assuming the other.

A nominal unanchored sheet does not intersect those internal envelopes; actual backside tabs or embedded fixings must be set out against the cage, cover, concrete and cap. A continuous line in this local detail is not an accepted gas, moisture or air boundary.

## One service-box crossing candidate

This geometry does **not** yet solve the crossing. Concrete would be cast or placed against the sheet; support below it, protective layer and friction-separation duties must be established without inserting an undisclosed polymer. The 458.5 mm added depth changes thermal bridges, soil movement and groundwater pressure, and the underside is not automatically accessible for welding, inspection or repair. W2 telltale/discharge and its fixed collars must remain independently functional, and opening W1 must not disturb the retained field. Welded corners, longitudinal/turn connections, seal tests, material compatibility and movement still require explicit design. If those cannot be made to fit the actual piers and site, this candidate fails rather than licensing a hole or deleting a separation duty.

## HAD-aligned formwork and support trial

The preferred 1 mm field is **−381..−380**, while the 3 mm plate remains **−383..−380**. Their upper faces align; their undersides have a real **2 mm step** requiring a continuous welded transition. In the 10 mm upper bed this leaves **9 mm below the 1 mm field**, but only **7 mm below the 3 mm patch**. Unlike the earlier field at −383..−382, there is no 2 mm mortar layer above the sheet. No associated bearing, lateral restraint, bond, seal or masonry joint is qualified.

The two source slab alternatives lead to different load paths. Under the unchanged selected **HAD slab soffit −380**, the sheet is directly against RC. It might serve as permanent formwork only if the temporary support, pour, concrete-to-metal contact, structural action, movement, seal, inspection and repair are designed together. At an assumed concrete unit weight 25 kN/m³, 200 mm of wet RC gives **5 kPa dead load**, before placement workers, wet-concrete concentration or other loads. An intentionally simple 1 m wide, 1 mm thick, simply supported elastic steel beam-strip screen at E200 GPa gives the following; it ignores plate action and is a warning about the stated idealization, not a sheet design:

| Unbraced span | Gross elastic bending stress | Gross elastic deflection |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 mm | 37.5 MPa | 0.391 mm |
| 250 mm | 234.4 MPa | 15.3 mm |
| 1,000 mm | 3,750 MPa | 3,906 mm |

The 1,000 mm estimate is far outside small-deflection elastic validity; it is not a physical prediction. It exposes why a bare 1 m unsupported sheet cannot be accepted from its self-weight. Temporary backing with a demonstrable actual load path, substrate, achievable support spacing and removal sequence is required. No steel grade, allowable stress, design factors or capacity is inferred.

A finite [temporary backing cell](studies/ground-metal-formwork.svg) at x/y1000..2000 has no intersection with the selected source’s represented straight ground-wall plan rectangles; this does not check slab steel, underground services, props, access or construction. It uses eleven 40 × 40 × 4 mm upper angles at 100 mm spacing, six crossing 40 × 40 × 4 bearers at 200 mm spacing, and **36 prop heads** on a 200 × 200 mm grid. Two angle layers put the nominal prop top at about **−461**, below the 1 mm field. For the 5 kPa wet RC alone, an interior 200 × 200 mm tributary gives **0.20 kN** at a prop. Sheet, seventeen 1 m angles and 36 trial Ø6 × 40 mm upper-face studs weigh **48.74 kg** per cell before props, welds, fixings, bracing and site bearing. If that isolated-cell metal mass is uniformly added to the 5 kPa wet-RC sensitivity, the interior 200 × 200 mm tributary becomes **0.219 kN**; shared edges may reduce the real mass and concentrated placement loads may increase demand. This is not a prop design. A gross ideal 200 mm simple-span screen for the 40 × 40 × 4 angle gives 1.55 MPa / 0.0011 mm for an upper 0.5 N/mm line and 3.10 MPa / 0.0023 mm for a lower 1 N/mm line; these omit lateral torsion, connections, bending about other axes, wet-pour concentration, construction loads and design factors. They are not a falsework or prop capacity pass. The metal needs a real foundation/sole bearing, working and removal access; **36 prop heads in this isolated cell** is a significant construction burden; neighbouring cells may share edge points, but no house-wide prop count or realistic site plan is established. The upper-face studs are a trial to retain the sheet after backing removal, with weld, embedment, rebar fit and integrity unresolved; no composite slab action is credited.

The prop grid is **not** automatically a stud grid. STR challenged a hypothetical 200 mm regular stud grid against H12/150 reinforcement: residues repeat at 0/50/100 mm, so for any phase at least one centre is within 25 mm of a bar; after 6 mm and 3 mm radii its best clear is **16 mm**, below the illustrative 22 mm comparator. A 40 mm stud from −380 to −340 also overlaps a hypothetical bottom H12 at −355..−343. The H12/150 and 25 mm cover values are source-bound **first-floor** trial values; there is no established ground-slab cage to which that result can be transferred. As a geometric alternative only, 49 independently set-out points at the centres of an assumed 150 mm mesh in this cell have **66 mm** plan clearance to those hypothetical bar axes. They are not a selected stud pattern: actual ground reinforcement, the two layers, coverage, edge transitions, welds, load transfer and construction still need an owning design. Do not tie the stud positions to the temporary 200 mm prop grid by assumption.

At the actual SW corner, the source-bound 300-square pad remains **−380..−80**, so it rises **100 mm above the −180 slab top** through the joined reference's full 100 mm glass layer. If the first-floor SW-L inner-axis rectangle were projected onto the ground, its intersection would be **230 × 230 mm = 0.0529 m²**, with **0.0371 m²** outside; the actual ground slab footprint has not been established, so those areas are conditional rather than an actual takeoff. In this HAD-aligned build-up the pad replaces local glass and adds a thermal bridge; its actual interface must be cast/reinforced into the slab and wall without assuming a duplicate 0.09 m² slab or a hidden cut. At λ0.041 W/mK, 100 mm glass has a core-only R of **2.439 m²K/W**, versus **7.805 m²K/W** for the separate 320 mm comparator. These are not whole-floor U values. The gap in geometry and layer position, along with the old source's 850 mm loggia cold-receiver hypothesis, must be reconciled before a complete thermal or takeoff calculation.

Under the **MAT warm-grid comparison**, the ordinary slab soffit is −280, **100 mm above** this same field. Only the receiving girder reaches −380. The sheet cannot be treated as a slab form or credited with the 5 kPa slab load there unless a real bridging substrate changes that construction. Conversely, hanger/rail support must avoid the −380 girder and preserve the 320 mm glass. These are competing source arrangements to reconcile, not two views of the same installed section. Casting onto metal may also change shrinkage, friction, condensation, concrete restraint, fixing and inspection. ## Movement across actual support lines

A metal field cannot be locked to a whole 11,475 mm span and treated as movement-free. At illustrative uniform Δ20 K, free strain gives **3.672 mm** for α16×10⁻⁶/K steel and **2.295 mm** for α10×10⁻⁶/K concrete: **1.377 mm** difference. These are assumed coefficients and temperatures, not actual relative movement or a required joint rating. Creep, shrinkage, temperature gradients, restraint and differential settlement need their own load and movement analysis.

For a finite trial, a transverse metal movement feature at y6000 would run in the two field strips on either side of the excluded courtyard. The selected HAD ground-wall segments show that this line crosses the **protected-stair-east axis x2894.5** and the **service-spine axis x9300**. A proposed 40 mm wide, 15 mm deep folded-metal loop cannot simply continue through those receivers and their mineral DPC. It needs actual supported, sealed transitions around both load-bearing interfaces and at the courtyard edge, with no unsealed overlap, water bypass or severed masonry bearing. This is a reason to redesign the exact path and detail, not proof that every flexible metal joint is impossible. The fold's alloy, fatigue, support, water/gas tightness, ability to take imposed movement, inspection and eventual repair are unproved; a folded shape alone is not a qualified movement joint.

## Sequence and evidence

A trial sequence is to establish the cold support and surveyed lower anchors; lay the mineral DPC/AAC and altered upper bed; fabricate and inspect the plate and its lower welds; erect the temporary below-field backing; position and weld the field and upper-face retention studs, including actual box/perimeter/service returns; inspect and test every accessible seam; place the upper cage and reinforcement; cast the warm receiver and HAD slab against the field; cure and remove the temporary backing only after the concrete and retention have adequate designed strength; then install the separately supported glass, screed and finish above. A full field integrity test and protection before and after casting must be designed. This sequence is not a demonstrated method: welding under reinforcement, locating and testing seams against the girders, protecting glass, thermal movement, access to both sides, temporary support and later repair need a physical plan. The full field must tolerate its actual support and differential settlement without relying on brittle bonded mortar or an unproved sliding seal.

A specific **material-family comparator**, not a selected sheet, is [Aperam 316L stainless](https://www.aperam.com/product/316l-1-4404/). Its published coil/sheet range includes **0.40–15 mm thickness** and width up to **2,000 mm**, so the proposed 1,000 × 1,000 × 1 mm gross panel lies within the stated family dimensions; this does not prove an available stock size or suitable supplied finish. Its [316L data sheet](https://www.aperam.com/sites/default/files/documents/FT_316L_en_web.pdf) gives typical cold-rolled/annealed E200 GPa, density 7,900 kg/m³, conductivity 15 W/mK at 20°C and mean expansion 16×10⁻⁶/K over 20–100°C. At that typical density the **gross 103.909 m² bounding sheet weighs 820.9 kg**, compared with 815.7 kg at the earlier 7,850 assumption; the 840 mm box-wrap segment is 12.74 kg rather than 12.66 kg. The published page title names 1.4404 while its norms list also shows 1.4401, so bind the actual supplied grade and certificate instead of silently resolving the identity. Surface film/coatings, edge finish, weld filler/shielding, anchors, separator, corrosion in the actual ground/concrete chemistry and every retained accessory are still unknown. A steel family, room-temperature typical properties and stated dimensional range do not establish a gas/pressure-water or structural system, service life, material pass or an exception.

The UK water-industry [stainless sheet guidance](https://standards-board.water.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/IGN-4-25-02-Issue-2-August-2023-published-version-1.pdf) identifies groundwater-separation applications; the Nickel Institute's [sheet-lining guide](https://nickelinstitute.org/media/1816/stainlesssteelsheetliningofsteeltanksandpressurevesselsl_10039_.pdf) describes vacuum-box leak examination of accessible welds. These are useful evidence for an engineered metal concept, not a gas-barrier certification or an acceptance criterion for this building. Exact alloy, filler, shielding gas, cleaning, anchors, coatings, separators, sealants and temporary/retained constituents remain to be identified under the material policy. The inspection medium and its removal must be assessed too. Groundwater chemistry, pressure, ground gas, corrosion and movement remain site-dependent.

**Result:** retain the HAD-aligned flush field for further joined design, but do not call it a continuous installed barrier. The earlier above-sheet rail is rejected on the unchanged HAD slab; the replacement falsework is a demanding unqualified hypothesis, the mortar/metal load route is unresolved, and threshold, weather, service, movement and repair closures are not yet drawn or proven. A complete finite assembly and internal structural/thermal challenge precede any product, site, physical or professional acceptance. No owner exception or 1,000-year performance is asserted.
