# ROF — Roof Systems Architecture

**Issue:** #991  
**State:** ROF-00/01/02/03/04 complete; junction comparison is next

**Current results:** [ROF-03 covering families](rof-03-covering-system-families.md), [ROF-04 assembly and support](rof-04-assembly-and-support.md) and [ROF-02 topology and geometry](rof-02-topology-and-geometry.md), using the [ROF-01 evaluation frame](rof-01-evidence-and-evaluation.md) and retaining the [ROF-00 baseline](rof-00-baseline-and-constraints.md).

## Purpose

Establish the roof of Ovid’s House as a first-class architectural and technical system rather than a residual silhouette, inherited constraint or drawing assumption.

ROF asks:

> **What roof architectures remain weatherproof, dryable, inspectable and locally repairable for the longest time, with the least dependence on perfect workmanship, hidden chemistry and heroic maintenance?**

The programme compares **failure topologies**, not categories by name. `flat`, `pitched`, `slate`, `copper` and similar labels are candidate families, not conclusions. A roof is judged by geometry, interfaces, material behaviour, water concentration, movement, drying, access, local repair and renewal.

## Naming and authority

The project/publication is **Ovid’s House**. The **Reference House** is the controlled exemplar within it. The former HSA name is historical only and must not be used for current project identity.

The current Reference House 25° inward/outward twin-pitch ring roof and symbolic collection routes are a controlled baseline/candidate. They are not a final roof-system conclusion. PLT represents controlled source; it does not own roof design.

The current project-level **no-flat-roofs** constraint remains operative until ROF explicitly earns an upstream rewind. ROF-00 records it as an inherited challengeable constraint. Opening ROF does not silently repeal it.

## Ownership boundary

ROF owns roof-system architecture from topology through weathering assembly and maintainability proposition:

- roof faces, ridges, hips, valleys, abutments, edges, eaves and concentration points;
- pitch/form families and their architectural consequences;
- covering families including slate/tile, sheet metals and flexible/low-slope waterproofing;
- substrates, separation layers and fixing concepts at system-selection scope;
- thermal movement accommodation and material compatibility at conceptual/detail-selection scope;
- air/vapour/insulation/ventilation/drying topology and assumptions;
- roof junction families;
- penetrations, hatches, flues/vents/anchors and future-penetration discipline;
- inspection, access, local repair, renewal and replacement strategy;
- explicit roof collection interfaces handed to RWT;
- Reference House roof selection jointly reconciled with HAD and RWT.

ROF does **not** own structural sizing, wind engineering, final condensation/thermal proof, building-side rainwater conveyance after collection interfaces, site drainage, overall architectural expression independent of technical consequences, drawings, or delivery acceptance. Those remain with BCR/EPR/PHY, MRA/EPR, RWT, DPL, HAD, PLT and DEL respectively.

## Governing comparison model

Do not begin with a scalar `roof complexity index`, joints-per-square-metre score or weighted beauty/reliability ranking. Preserve the dimensions that create different failure behaviour.

A useful analytical unit is:

> **performance-critical interface × interface sensitivity × consequence of failure × detectability/recoverability**

Examples:

- a continuous sheet roof can reduce open laps while increasing movement, substrate and specialist-workmanship sensitivity;
- a slate roof can contain many laps while allowing highly local, visible and replaceable damage;
- a simple geometry can still be bad if it traps water behind inaccessible upstands;
- a nominally robust material can still be bad if its underside or junctions cannot dry or be inspected.

The programme should identify **hostile topologies** and **good recovery behaviour**, not merely preferred materials.

## Programme

### ROF-00 — constitution, baseline and inherited constraints

Freeze the present roof state and authority boundary before research changes anything.

Required output:

- current Reference House/PLT roof baseline and exact provisional assumptions;
- inventory of roof-related doctrine, patterns, MRA questions and unresolved PLT/BCR obligations;
- explicit status of the no-flat-roof constraint;
- ROF↔RWT, ROF↔HAD, ROF↔MRA and ROF↔BCR/EPR/PHY/DEL ownership seams;
- comparison vocabulary and rejection of premature scalar ranking;
- shared interface requirements for `ROF-RWT-IFACE`.

### ROF-01 — evidence, failure model and evaluation frame

Research and freeze the comparison dimensions needed for later candidate work.

At minimum cover:

- bulk-water shedding and concentration;
- primary and secondary defence behaviour;
- junction count/type/criticality;
- workmanship sensitivity;
- thermal/moisture movement tolerance;
- trapped-water and underside-wetting potential;
- drying potential;
- failure visibility and detection latency;
- local repairability and replacement extent;
- access and inspection burden;
- realistic service-life relationships without false precision;
- wind, fire and material-compatibility dependencies.

Standards and guidance are baselines/evidence, not automatic project design answers.

### ROF-02 — topology and geometry families

Compare bounded geometry families such as simple gable/hip forms, courtyard/ring roofs, valleys, parapets, mansard/compound forms and flat/low-slope conditions.

Develop a topology grammar for:

`face / ridge / hip / valley / abutment / edge / eave / penetration / collection point / concentration point`.

Identify which topologies inherently create trapped or difficult-to-maintain water paths and which remain robust under ordinary degradation.

### ROF-03 — covering-system families

Completed at comparative research scope: [covering-system result](rof-03-covering-system-families.md). No covering or pitch selected.

Run three separable studies that can proceed in parallel after ROF-01:

- **ROF-03A — slate/clay/tile families**;
- **ROF-03B — lead/copper/zinc/stainless and related sheet-metal families**;
- **ROF-03C — membrane/flexible-waterproofing and low-slope families**.

Compare laps/seams, movement, substrate dependence, fixing, corrosion/compatibility, local damage, workmanship, repair and renewal. Do not select a covering independently of topology and assembly.

### ROF-04 — assembly below the covering

Split into two bounded packets:

- **ROF-04A — insulation/air/vapour/ventilation/drying topology**;
- **ROF-04B — substrate/fixing/support-interface/movement/wind assumptions**.

These packets select plausible architectural/system topologies and expose technical obligations. They do not manufacture competent structural or building-physics proof.

### ROF-05 — junctions and penetrations

Resolve candidate families for eaves, ridges, hips, valleys, verges, wall abutments, roof/wall transitions, hatches, flues/vents, anchors and future penetrations.

For each interface classify whether it should be:

- eliminated by topology;
- simplified;
- exposed/inspectable;
- independently replaceable;
- accepted as a high-care detail with explicit evidence/maintenance burden.

Prefer geometry before sealant dependence where practical.

### ROF-06 — access, maintenance, repair and renewal

Trace realistic sequences for:

- inspection;
- clearing collection interfaces;
- replacing one local covering element;
- renewing flashings/seams;
- accessing roof-space components;
- replacing penetrations;
- eventually recovering/replacing major roof areas.

The roof must not be declared durable merely because its nominal materials last a long time. Reachability and local recovery are part of the proposition.

### ROF-07 — system synthesis and candidate families

Combine:

`topology × covering × underlay/build-up × junctions × access/renewal`.

Produce a deliberately small candidate set. Red-team combinations and reject locally attractive choices whose whole-system interfaces, failure paths or maintenance burden are poor.

No final Reference House selection occurs here.

### ROF-RWT-GATE — Reference House roof/water reconciliation

Shared ROF/RWT/HAD gate after ROF-07, RWT-07 and current HAD-03 convergence.

The gate selects and reconciles the actual Reference House roof and building rainwater architecture as one system. It must establish:

- architectural form/cornice/eaves expression corresponds to the selected roof assembly;
- roof collection interfaces correspond to the selected RWT network;
- courtyard/opening/threshold consequences are reconciled without taking OAS authority;
- access/maintenance routes are plausible;
- material compatibility is explicit;
- current PLT geometry is retained only where the design earns it;
- conflicts trigger explicit rewinds rather than silent cross-stream edits;
- unresolved structural/building-physics/hydraulic/workmanship claims have named BCR/EPR/PHY/DEL/MRA routes.

The existing IAS/OAS/HAD TRIAD-GATE remains intact as the house-wide coordination gate, but it becomes dependent on this roof/water gate. This avoids turning the triad into a five-workstream committee while ensuring roof/water design cannot bypass it.

### ROF-09 — formalisation / regression admission

After the selected roof is stable, decide which topology/interface facts genuinely deserve deterministic representation or regression checks.

No compiler expansion merely because the system is representable. Formalisation is downstream of architectural authority and is not itself a construction-validation claim.

## Interfaces

- **RWT** owns collection/conveyance after explicit roof collection interfaces and feeds back drainage consequences capable of forcing topology reconsideration.
- **HAD** owns roof expression, silhouette, eaves/cornice and visible material language; it cannot overrule a demonstrated technical failure topology for appearance alone.
- **OAS** retains opening/threshold authority. ROF/RWT can request a rewind but cannot silently alter it.
- **MRA** attacks the selected system after coordination. It does not design the preferred roof.
- **BCR** turns selected system obligations into construction-resolution dispositions.
- **EPR** owns competent structural/building-physics judgement where required.
- **PHY** is used only for genuinely material physical propositions that require testing.
- **DEL** owns workmanship, inspection, commissioning, procurement and acceptance consequences.
- **OCG** may later use roof maintenance/replacement as intervention scenarios.
- **PLT** represents resolved authority and preserves current roof drawings as historical/provisional evidence until rewound.

## Non-goals

ROF is not a campaign for metal roofs, a campaign against flat roofs, a roof-style survey, a structural-engineering package, a condensation-simulation programme, a second MRA, site drainage design, a proprietary-product search, or a scalar optimisation problem.

## Exit gate

ROF closes only when:

- the comparison model is evidence-informed and bounded;
- a small coherent system candidate set exists;
- failure, maintenance and renewal consequences are explicit;
- the selected Reference House roof has passed ROF-RWT-GATE;
- any change to inherited roof constraints is explicit and justified;
- unresolved technical claims have named evidence owners;
- no PLT drawing or current convention has been mistaken for design authority.

Closure establishes a controlled architectural roof-system proposition, not structural, building-physics, regulatory, product or workmanship approval.
