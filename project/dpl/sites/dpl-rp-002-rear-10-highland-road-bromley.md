## 1. Why this site is in the pilot

This site is selected as the pilot's **backland / narrow-access / mature-tree planning-history** condition.

Unlike `DPL-RS-01`, the site has a documented history in which access, tree protection, neighbour relationships and backland character are already demonstrably material.

A previous single three-bedroom house on the garage land to the rear of No.10 received planning permission in 2019. That fact makes one-dwelling development a meaningful real-site question while proving absolutely nothing about whether the Reference House is acceptable.

The site attacks:

- `SI-P02` datum / approach / accessible arrival;
- `SI-P05` construction / maintenance / landscape;
- `SI-P06` context / environmental mitigation;
- DPL-10B D3 design-led/context judgement;
- DPL-11 access/private-right/boundary separation;
- DPL-09B tree/ground/foundation evidence dependencies.

## 6. DPL-10B consequences

### D3

This site is an excellent D3 case because **site capacity cannot be inferred from site area**.

The historical sequence itself demonstrates context sensitivity:

- a larger five-house backland proposal was refused;
- a later one-house scheme was granted.

That does not make the 2019 house or Reference House the optimum current design.

```text
D3: JUDGEMENT REQUIRED / UNRESOLVED
```

### D5 / D7

The narrow/private backland route means principal arrival and inclusive access require real evidence, not a generic “front path”.

No individual M4 category is assigned by this screening record.

### D6

No Reference House planning proposal is being evaluated here; D6 proposal metrics remain out of scope for DPL-14B.

## 7. Admission rationale

**Admit as `COVERAGE_ADDITION`.**

The site contributes:

- Bromley borough coverage;
- a genuine backland/garage-land condition;
- a narrow private/partially unmade access route;
- materially active tree/TPO history;
- neighbour/character planning sensitivity;
- one-house historical permission without allowing permission to become a compatibility claim;
- explicit drainage obligations in historical permission;
- a strong empirical demonstration of DPL's rule that **permission for another design is external evidence, not a Reference House pass**.
