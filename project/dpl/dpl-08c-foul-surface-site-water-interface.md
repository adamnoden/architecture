## 1. Question

> **What must be true where the controlled Reference House’s foul, roof, courtyard and perimeter-water geography meets real land and external drainage infrastructure, without inventing a sewer, outfall, permeability, invert, pipe size or engineering solution?**

DPL-08C answers that question by freezing the **handoffs and duties**, not the drainage design.

Its governing rule is:

> **The house owns where and how water reaches the site boundary of architectural authority. The site interface owns how evidenced land/network conditions are reconciled with those handoffs. DPL-09C owns the England statutory obligations. A competent drainage/civil designer owns hydraulic and construction adequacy.**

The packet finds no present reason to change the controlled roof, courtyard or internal service topology.

The controlled service geography provides:

It intentionally preserves:

- visible outward overflow behaviour;
- accessible ground-level gully / inspection geography.

RH-08 explicitly does **not** select a buried perimeter ring drain or final site discharge destination.

### Courtyard water — `RW-COURT-01`

The court handles principally its own open-to-sky catchment rather than the primary roof catchment.

Controlled topology is:

- accessible primary collector toward the east/service side;
- short, straight, joint-minimised gravity route beneath service/circulation geography;
- external service-side inspection point;
- **independent higher-level emergency overflow**;
- visible above-ground emergency discharge before occupied thresholds become the failure weir.

Final hydraulic sizing, inlet level and receiving surface remain technical design.

### DRW-10C analytical evidence

The later analytical drawing programme preserves the same reading at publication scale:

- inspectable perimeter collection;

- courtyard primary collector relationship;
- independent emergency overflow.

DRW-10C deliberately refused gutter profiles, downpipe dimensions, buried drain centrelines/depths/gradients, overflow level, site discharge and hydraulic sizing. DPL-08C keeps that refusal intact.

# 3. Five controlled building-side handoffs

The architectural/site interface is divided into five handoffs. They may connect to common infrastructure later, but remain semantically distinct.

## `W-H01 — Foul external handoff`

**House supplies:**

- concentrated foul topology in the service geography;
- accessible stack-base / rodding provision;
- service-side below-ground exit geography;
- accessible external inspection point before the site route.

**House does not supply:**

- external sewer destination;
- invert level;
- connection right;
- sewer ownership/capacity;
- pumped/gravity verdict;
- external pipe size/fall/material;
- treatment-plant requirement.

The site response begins **after the controlled external inspection handoff**.

## `W-H02 — Outer roof-water ground handoffs`

**House supplies:**

- four outward-draining roof fields;
- inspectable outer collection;
- four side/rear downpipe families;
- visible outward overflow behaviour;
- accessible ground-level gully / inspection positions.

**House does not supply:**

- a buried perimeter drain;
- common manifold/ring geometry;
- infiltration destination;
- attenuation volume;
- watercourse/sewer destination;
- final below-ground route.

The four ground points may later combine only where the site strategy earns that combination.

## `W-H03 — Courtyard primary-water handoff`

**House supplies:**

- accessible courtyard collector;
- short straight route under service/circulation geography;
- accessible external service-side inspection point.

**House does not supply:**

- downstream outfall;
- downstream pipe size/fall;
- attenuation/infiltration decision;
- permission to merge the route invisibly with a blocked or inaccessible system.

The protected architectural proposition is the **short inspectable court escape route**, not its eventual receiving network.

## `W-H04 — Courtyard emergency-overflow handoff`

This is intentionally not the same handoff as `W-H03`.

**House supplies:**

- independent higher-level overflow path;
- visible external service-side outlet;
- architectural duty that abnormal court water escapes before occupied thresholds become the failure weir.

**House does not supply:**

- exact overflow crest level;
- hydraulic capacity;
- final external receiving geometry;
- permission to connect the overflow invisibly back into the same failure-prone route it is meant to bypass.

The later site solution must preserve **functional independence at the credible blockage/failure condition**.

## `W-H05 — Building-edge / site-surface-water handoff`

This handoff covers water that is neither the controlled foul route nor a primary roof/court pipe route, including:

- surface grading immediately around the house;
- splashback / wall-base moisture geography;
- principal-approach and threshold surface water;
- local maintenance-hardstanding runoff;
- abnormal overland water reaching the building edge.

The building supplies moisture/weathering duties and protected threshold/plinth relationships. The site response must keep surface water from turning those architectural interfaces into the drainage system of last resort.

No universal trench, gravel strip, channel or perimeter drain is selected here.

# 4. Site evidence required before a response can be selected

The package consumes evidence; it does not invent it.

## 4.1 Geometry / levels

At the relevant assurance profile, potentially required facts include:

- topographical survey tied to an authoritative datum;
- house/platform proposed absolute datum once authored;
- street, garden and boundary levels;
- existing/proposed sewer or outfall invert levels where relevant;
- watercourse/ditch/other receiving levels where relevant;
- local threshold, approach and external-surface falls;
- retaining/slope conditions that materially affect route feasibility.

A marketing contour, planning sketch or synthetic Reference Site fall is not automatically technical drainage evidence.

## 4.2 Sewer / drainage infrastructure

Potential evidence includes:

- public/private sewer records;
- sewer owner / statutory undertaker identity;
- surveyed position/depth/diameter/material where decision-critical;
- connection point and consent position;
- capacity/connection evidence where required;
- build-over / proximity / diversion constraints;
- private-drain rights/easements where relevant;
- existing drainage condition where reuse is proposed.

Silence in a listing or planning record does not mean mains drainage is available.

## 4.3 Ground / infiltration

Where infiltration is considered, the site may need evidence for:

- soil/permeability/infiltration performance;
- groundwater;
- contamination / ground-gas interaction where relevant;
- foundation / retaining / basement interaction;
- trees/root conditions;
- neighbouring structures/boundaries;
- water-quality/environmental constraints.

A garden or unpaved area is **not evidence of infiltration feasibility**.

## 4.4 Flood / exceedance / environmental context

Potential inputs include:

- authoritative fluvial/tidal/surface-water/groundwater evidence where applicable;
- observed overland flow paths;
- planning flood/drainage constraints;
- watercourse/riparian/environmental authority evidence;
- site-specific exceedance routing assumptions and downstream consequences.

These remain evidence objects with their own dates/spatial scope.

## 4.5 Legal / access / maintenance

A technically plausible route may still be unavailable without:

- right to connect or cross land;
- legal easement/wayleave where applicable;
- undertaker/environmental consent;
- accessible chamber/inspection geography;
- permanent maintenance access;
- practical replacement/jetting/rodding route.

Physical possibility is not legal entitlement.

# 5. Response hierarchy — classes, not selected solutions

DPL-08C may identify response **classes** once the relevant evidence exists. It does not choose or size them.

## 5.1 Foul response classes

Possible later classes include:

- gravity connection to an evidenced lawful sewer/outfall;
- pumped foul strategy where a competent design and the applicable target/consents permit it;
- private treatment / non-mains route only where actual site evidence and regulatory/environmental authority require/permit it;
- rerouted external route that preserves the controlled service-side building handoff.

No class is preferred merely because it is conventional.

A gravity route is not “proved” until elevations, connection position and technical design support it.

## 5.2 Surface-water response classes

Possible later classes include, where lawfully and technically justified:

- infiltration;
- controlled attenuation / discharge;
- watercourse or other lawful receiving body;
- sewer connection;
- combinations of the above.

DPL-08C does not encode the statutory priority/hierarchy as its own authority. DPL-09C owns the England requirement/guidance model; planning/environmental targets may add separate constraints.

## 5.3 External conveyance / inspection classes

A later design may coordinate:

- separate or combined external collection routes;
- inspection chambers;
- rodding/jetting access;
- gullies/silt traps;
- local open-channel or surface routing;
- attenuation/infiltration elements;
- pump/treatment infrastructure where genuinely required.

The admissibility test is not visual tidiness. The response must preserve inspection, replacement, failure visibility and the protected building-side topology.

# 6. Interface duties that must survive any selected solution

## `W-D01 — Inspectability`

Every high-consequence transition from building system to site system must retain credible inspection/clearing access.

Concealing a junction is not made acceptable merely by calling the whole route “below ground”.

## `W-D02 — Failure visibility`

Credible blockage/overflow should fail toward observable external geography before hidden building fabric or occupied thresholds become the first detector.

The exact hydraulic proof remains external design.

## `W-D03 — Courtyard primary / emergency independence`

The emergency route must remain meaningfully independent of the credible primary-route blockage it protects against.

Two pipes joining immediately upstream of the same inaccessible blocker do not satisfy the architectural duty simply because they are drawn as two lines.

## `W-D04 — Threshold/plinth coordination`

Drainage design must coordinate with DPL-08B duties:

- step-free approach;
- weathered threshold;
- wall-base moisture management;
- maintenance territory;
- protected internal level topology.

No one duty is allowed to defeat the others by local optimisation.

## `W-D05 — Maintainable external geography`

Chambers, gullies, cleanouts, pumps/filters if ever needed, and other maintainable components must remain accessible without routine demolition of permanent architecture.

## `W-D06 — No speculative crossing of protected architecture`

External/site drainage must not regain convenience by sending high-consequence routes across protected principal rooms or through the courtyard/house centre contrary to RH-06/RH-08 topology.

## `W-D07 — Evidence-bound discharge`

No discharge destination exists in the deployment case until supported by the required site/authority evidence.

`UNKNOWN` remains a valid state.

## `W-D08 — Exceedance has somewhere harmless to go`

Where abnormal surface water or system overflow is credible, the later design must identify a route/state that does not rely on occupied interiors, low thresholds or concealed wall build-up as the sacrificial overflow path.

DPL-08C defines the duty, not the storm event/hydraulic calculation.

# 9. Rewind and eventual incompatibility triggers

The following are **triggers for explicit decision**, not automatic site failure.

## `W-R01 — Internal-roof drainage required`

A site response can only work by converting the controlled outward roof topology to internal outlets/concealed roof drainage.

**Action:** Reference House RH-08 rewind or reject the site/response.

## `W-R02 — Primary courtyard topology must change`

A lawful/technical response requires a new primary inner-courtyard roof gutter/outlet or another material rewrite of `RW-COURT-01`.

**Action:** RH-08 rewind or reject the site/response.

## `W-R03 — Protected-room crossing required`

Foul or high-consequence surface drainage can only be routed by crossing protected principal rooms/court contrary to RH-06/RH-08 service geography.

**Action:** Reference House service/architectural rewind or reject the response/site.

## `W-R04 — Threshold / level architecture becomes the drainage solution`

The only apparent response requires materially changing the one-level internal topology, moving principal entrance order, or accepting occupied thresholds as normal/emergency overflow control.

**Action:** DPL-08B / owning Reference House gate rewind or reject the response/site.

## `W-R05 — No maintainable route`

Every technically plausible route requires inaccessible chambers/junctions or routine demolition of permanent architecture for foreseeable maintenance.

**Action:** unresolved pending alternate response; eventually rewind/incompatibility only after admissible alternatives are exhausted.

## `W-R06 — No lawful / technically credible discharge route`

After applicable evidence and admissible response classes are genuinely tested, no lawful/technically credible foul and/or surface-water route remains.

**Action:** potential `INCOMPATIBLE` blocker, but only the later deployment-decision layer may issue that conclusion after route-exhaustion semantics are satisfied.

DPL-08C itself does not emit `INCOMPATIBLE`.

# 13. Red-team findings

## “Use one perimeter drain for everything”

**REJECTED.** It collapses independent foul, roof, court, wall-base and surface duties before site evidence exists.

## “The garden means use a soakaway”

**REJECTED.** Unpaved land is not permeability, groundwater, contamination, spacing or legal evidence.

## “Planning shows a sewer/drain, so connection is proved”

**REJECTED.** Proposal/condition/history is not current surveyed asset, capacity, right or connection evidence.

## “The RH-08 H3 hierarchy already solves DPL-09C”

**REJECTED.** RH-08 used the regulatory baseline to avoid overcommitting site discharge. DPL-09C must now recover and version the actual operative source itself.

## “Emergency overflow can simply rejoin the main route”

**REJECTED as a default.** Independence must be meaningful relative to the credible failure being mitigated.

## “A difficult sewer level means the house is incompatible”

**REJECTED.** Pumped or alternative lawful response classes may exist. Incompatibility requires evidence and route exhaustion.

## “A pump solves anything”

**REJECTED.** Pumping is a specialist/system response with failure, maintenance, power and regulatory consequences; it is not a generic architectural escape hatch.
