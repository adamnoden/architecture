# DPL-10D — Bromley adversarial borough planning replay

**Issue:** #415  
**Parent programme:** #299  
**Authorised by:** DPL-C / #412  
**Baseline `main`:** `e9ae04b6c535b1a675510ab1024b3da2a14b3b84`  
**Research date:** 2026-10-22  
**Proof site:** `DPL-RP-002` — rear of 10 Highland Road, Bromley

## Decision

> **PASS — the Croydon borough-plug-in architecture survives only as a narrow semantic envelope; Bromley materially narrows what may be treated as generic.**

The reusable seam is not “a Croydon-shaped local plan with different policy numbers”. It is:

- exact borough/source identity and status;
- explicit parent strategic-target identity;
- typed local obligations with applicability and method class;
- numeric rules only where genuinely explicit;
- qualitative design/amenity matters preserved as judgement;
- guidance/SPDs kept separate from development-plan policy;
- site-specific designation applicability kept separate from target semantics;
- proposal-specific decisions kept as external evidence;
- emerging sources kept non-operative until authority changes;
- no list-order, recency or “more local wins” precedence.

Everything more specific must remain borough-owned until further evidence earns generalisation.

## 1. Current source/status position

### Operative development plan

Bromley Council's current Local Plan page states that the **Bromley Local Plan was adopted on 16 January 2019** and, together with the London Plan, is used to determine planning applications.

The 2019 Local Plan therefore remains the operative local development-plan source at the 2026-10-22 target snapshot.

### Local Plan review

The council's current review pages record:

- Issues and Options Regulation 18 consultation in 2023;
- a second Regulation 18 **Direction of Travel** consultation from July to September 2025;
- the Direction of Travel document as a preferred-direction document which **does not contain draft policies**;
- an intention, stated on that page, to consult on a Submission Draft in Summer 2026.

At the research date the current official review surface does not expose a Submission Draft as operative development-plan policy. DPL therefore retains the review material as **emerging / non-operative**.

The programme does not infer from the missed stated timetable that a hidden draft exists or that emerging policy has acquired weight.

### Guidance

The **Urban Design Guide SPD**, adopted 5 July 2023, is live supplementary guidance/material consideration. The document itself states that it guides interpretation of adopted policies and does not create new policy.

This is the opposite of Croydon's proof-history wrinkle: Croydon's former suburban SPD was revoked; Bromley has a live borough-wide design SPD. A generic borough model must therefore represent guidance status, not assume either “SPD present” or “SPD absent/revoked”.

### Neighbourhood planning

The current council material records no active designated neighbourhood planning layer relevant to this proof. No neighbourhood policy is injected by convenience.

## 2. Policy replay against DPL-RP-002

### Policy 3 — backland and garden-land development

Bromley has a first-class local policy specifically for the proof site's morphology.

Policy 3 accepts new residential development on backland/garden land only where all of its criteria are satisfied, covering:

- character/appearance/context relative to scale, design and density;
- landscape, habitat, play and amenity-space loss;
- privacy, sunlight/daylight and traffic disturbance;
- a high standard of separation and landscaping.

This cannot be reduced to a scalar or a few distances. The controlled historic five-house refusal on the larger rear-of-8-and-10 site demonstrates that these questions are materially live, but the refusal remains proposal-specific evidence rather than a reusable site fail.

**Anti-overfit result:** Croydon's broad DM10 design/amenity family is not a generic borough policy shape. Bromley exposes a distinct backland-specific policy family.

### Policy 8 — side space

Policy 8 provides a genuinely bounded numeric term:

- for proposals of two or more storeys, at least **1 m side space** should be retained to the site boundary for the full height and length of the building.

But the same policy expects **more generous separation where higher standards already exist**.

Therefore DPL may eventually run the 1 m floor natively once exact survey/title/proposal geometry exists, while retaining a judgement remainder. A numeric pass cannot become an overall Policy 8 pass.

DPL-RP-002 currently lacks survey/title-grade parcel geometry, so even this small native check remains unresolved for the proof site.

### Policy 37 — design of new development

Policy 37 owns a broad design-quality/context/inclusive-access/amenity relationship. The 2023 Urban Design Guide SPD informs that judgement as guidance.

This remains an external planning/architectural judgement obligation rather than a numeric engine.

### Policy 73 — development and trees

Policy 73 requires development to take account of desirable trees on-site and adjoining land, supports retention/protection and uses TPOs for important trees; supporting material calls for BS 5837-style evidence where important trees are threatened.

The controlled corpus contains historical evidence that area TPO **BB9 (1960)** affected the site/context. Bromley's current council service, however, directs current TPO status to its maintained protected-tree records/map/enquiry process.

**DPL does not promote the historic TPO reference into a current 2026 site fact.** Current designation coverage, tree condition, positions, ownership and RPAs remain evidence inputs.

This is a particularly useful anti-overfit result: a borough target owns the consequence of a designation; the site manifest/evidence owns whether the designation currently applies.

### Policy 30 — parking

This is the strongest temporal/conflict stress case.

The adopted 2019 Local Plan contains explicit local residential parking minima/maxima by PTAL and bedroom count. But:

- the London Plan 2021 post-dates that local plan;
- Bromley's current January 2025 validation requirements direct new residential parking to London Plan Policy T6/T6.1 standards;
- Bromley's 2025 Local Plan review material explicitly notes that London Plan 2021 residential parking standards limit the scope for bespoke Bromley standards.

DPL-10D therefore **refuses to execute the 2019 local parking table natively** until the DPL-10A conflict/temporal-composition model has established the exact current relationship.

Validation guidance does not itself repeal development-plan policy. Equally, an adopted 2019 table is not blindly executed as if the 2021 strategic plan did not exist.

This exposes a real method class missing from Croydon: **explicitly unsupported pending policy-conflict composition**.

### Policy 32 — road safety

Policy 32 requires development not significantly to adversely affect road safety.

That obligation is highly material to DPL-RP-002 because the historic record describes private, partially unmade backland access approximately 3.2–3.8 m wide in the larger 2017 application context.

That historic width is not current survey evidence and cannot answer swept path, visibility, emergency/refuse access, pedestrian safety, gradient or legal-right questions. Policy 32 remains evidence/judgement-led.

### Policies 115/116 — flood risk and SuDS

The Local Plan and current validation requirements preserve a distinct local planning water obligation.

For new dwellings/garden land where runoff or sewage demand increases, current validation guidance expects drainage evidence and, where due, topography, discharge/runoff, SuDS, geology/infiltration, exceedance and maintenance information.

This composes with DPL-08C and DPL-09C but does not duplicate them:

- Bromley planning owns local planning evidence/judgement;
- DPL-08C owns the architectural/site water interface;
- DPL-09C owns England Part-H-family statutory obligations;
- specialist drainage/civil evidence remains external.

The 2019 permission's drainage condition proves only that drainage was a due matter for that proposal.

## 4. What the borough plug-in now means

After two adversarially different boroughs, `dpl.boroughPlanningSlice.v1` survives as a **semantic envelope**, not a promise of uniform policy structure.

The common contract now earned is:

```text
borough target identity
+ exact research date
+ parent strategic target identity
+ source/status snapshots
+ typed obligations
    + applicability
    + method class
    + required evidence
+ separate guidance references
+ separate proposal-specific decision evidence
+ explicit excluded/held sources
+ explicit composition and proof boundary
```

It does **not** mean:

- every borough has the same policy families;
- every borough has a local numeric amenity rule;
- every borough has one SPD or the same SPD status;
- all local numeric rules are executable without strategic conflict analysis;
- site designations belong inside the target rather than site evidence;
- “local” outranks “strategic”.

The JSON envelope therefore survives; the Croydon content model does not become London's generic local-plan ontology.

## 5. New bounded runtime implication

Bromley exposes one new thing DPL-12C must handle honestly:

> a local obligation can be structurally representable while its native method is **unsupported because current cross-plan conflict/temporal composition is unresolved**.

The Bromley target represents Policy 30 with method kind `UNSUPPORTED` under `LOCAL_POLICY_CONFLICT_UNRESOLVED` rather than inventing a current numeric parking answer.

DPL-12C should preserve this fail-closed state. It should not add a generic policy-conflict solver merely to make the target compile green.

## 8. Rejected abstractions

### “Every borough target is Croydon with renamed policies”

Rejected. Bromley separates backland, side-space, general design and tree policy differently.

### “All adopted local numeric policy can be run natively”

Rejected. Policy 30 demonstrates a later-strategic-plan conflict/composition problem.

### “Historic TPO evidence establishes current designation”

Rejected. The target knows what tree/TPO status would mean; current site evidence must establish whether it applies.

### “SPD rules are borough policy”

Rejected. Bromley's live Urban Design Guide SPD remains guidance/material consideration.

### “The five-house refusal proves the site is hostile to housing”

Rejected. One different one-house scheme received permission. Both decisions remain proposal-specific evidence.

## 9. DPL-12C handoff

DPL-12C may now safely assume:

1. `dpl.boroughPlanningSlice.v1` is a demonstrated outer semantic envelope across **two materially different boroughs**;
2. borough obligation families themselves remain target-local;
3. local numeric checks require explicit source terms plus admitted facts and may retain judgement remainders;
4. a structured obligation may legitimately use `UNSUPPORTED` where cross-plan policy conflict is unresolved;
5. guidance/source status and proposal-specific decision evidence remain separate;
6. current designation applicability must come from site evidence, not historic target/proof-case material;
7. no generic all-borough planning engine has been earned.

DPL-10D passes if repository validation confirms the target/review are internally coherent.

> **Two boroughs justify a reusable authority/evidence envelope. They do not justify a reusable borough policy ontology.**

That is the correct abstraction boundary for DPL-12C.
