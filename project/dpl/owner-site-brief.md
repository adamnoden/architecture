## Spatial screen before a shortlist claim

At the inspected source, the wall-line bounding square is 12 m each way: 144 m² including the open 3.5 m square court, which is 12.25 m². The difference, 131.75 m², is nominal wall-line ring area, **not** usable floor area, GIA or an actual planning measurement. A 12 m-wide parcel containing a 12 m shell does not establish room for projecting roofs, works or boundary duties.

For an initial measured overlay, place the actual house outline within the evidenced usable parcel at an explicit rotation. Distinguish legal boundary, surveyed physical features and any buildable-area assumption. For a square aligned with rectangular plot axes, a preliminary dimensional ledger is `12 m + west field + east field`, and `12 m + front field + rear field`; the fields remain undimensioned until their actual uses and constraints are tested. They must contain relevant projections as well as any required clear territory. Do not add a roof allowance twice where it already occupies a tested field, or count incompatible simultaneous uses as shared space. A rotated square has different bounding extents; area alone cannot establish fit on a narrow, tapered or irregular plot.

The represented eaves are +6800 mm and ridge approximately +7791 mm relative to ground-floor FFL. These are architectural source coordinates, not approved building height or a complete roof-assembly envelope. Absolute house datum, surveyed ground, covering/terminal projections and the applicable measurement basis are unknown. The drawn −150 mm outside grade is a study value, not the land's level. An assessment must show the full section against real neighbours, terrain and constraints.

Plan labels such as north/east name the current drawing axes. They do not establish geographic orientation on a future site. Rotate a measured model with its entrance, garden, court, windows and service side together; evaluate sun, daylight, overheating, privacy, views and noise against actual context before choosing orientation. No preferred compass bearing is selected here.

## Current house findings that a plot must not conceal

The completed investigations below are bounded evidence, not a technically approved house. An attractive site does not resolve an internal essential design gap.

**Water and level territory.** [BCR-WATER](../bcr/bcr-water-result.md) measures 60.0625 m² of nominal inner-roof plus court catchment if the inner roof spills toward the court; treating it as only 12.25 m² of direct rain is false. This is a geometric stress case, not a selected design storm or drain size. [RWT-06's retained partial](../rwt/rwt-06-access-court-transition.md) compares an eastward primary crossing ending at x12000/invert−265 with a distinct higher relief ending at−130, relative to the study FFL. Against the schematic −150 exterior grade, the first requires a lower receiving transition and the second has only 20 mm separation. Neither is adopted. Real survey, receiving water levels and a harmless, lawful exceedance route must replace that schematic relationship. No assumed lower garden, drain connection or valve grants discharge. The grouped floor crossings, actual lowest vulnerable court boundary and high-level access remain unresolved internal admission conditions; future HAD/IAS/RWT geometry must be reappraised rather than forced onto a plot.

**Roof and court maintenance.** The accepted [roof-access investigation](../../docs/research/roof-access-and-renewal.md) rejects the inspected ordinary mobile-tower configuration at useful eave-working height: its 4072 × 4768 mm stabilised footprint exceeds the 3500 mm court. A lower arrangement fitting the court does not reach the required task. The purpose-designed scaffold comparison remains conditional on real bases, ties, access, handling and temporary weathering. More land outside cannot enlarge the enclosed court. It can, however, provide actual external support, unloading, component/set-down and alternative logistics territory where a competent method permits it. No neighbouring-land dependence is assumed. The [external maintenance brief](../../docs/delivery/external-maintenance-access-requirements.md) requires task-specific access, including upper-window/receiver replacement and replay with mature planting.

**Renewal through the occupied house.** [BCR-SERVICES](../bcr/bcr-services-result.md) and [BCR-OCCUPATION](../bcr/bcr-occupation-result.md) distinguish plant reservation, body packing and actual removal. The source nominal 1400 mm external plant aperture and internal door trials are not established clear handling passages. A plot assessment must connect an actual release/withdrawal route to a usable outside set-down and onward delivery/removal route, with operators, thresholds, turning and temporary support included. Court scaffold-component transport likewise cannot be inferred from nominal 2400 mm garden/court doors. Clear apertures, furniture, finishes and occupation restrictions must be tested with the actual object and method.

**Candidate foul-route change.** The later [Bedroom 4 allocation study](../had/had-03-bedroom4-allocation.md) proposes a rear-west stack and a separate below-ground foul route. It remains unadopted and does not replace the accepted service-side handoff. If adopted, a real-site assessment must add its exact outlet/inspection, level, ground-crossing and renewal territory; it cannot assume that a usable east-side connection also serves the west-side proposal. Its possible effects on garden and maintenance space belong in the comparison.

**Proportionality.** [BCR-PROPORTIONALITY](../bcr/bcr-proportionality-result.md) compares conventional controls with technical alternatives without invented prices or life savings. Constrained ground, permanent access apparatus, pumping or dependence on specialist renewal can make a superficially fitting plot a poor match for the owner's resources and maintenance appetite. Those consequences warrant explicit comparison and a cost/owner decision, not a numerical pass obtained by adding speculative future technology.

## Shortlist disposition and possible adaptation

For each future nominated plot, record one plain-language disposition with the source dates and unresolved questions:

- **Retain for bounded assessment:** a plausible spatial/contextual relationship exists at the stated evidence level; this is not suitability or acquisition advice.
- **Seek specific evidence:** name the missing fact, why it changes the decision, who can obtain/interpret it and what conclusion is withheld. Unknowns do not become favourable scores.
- **Bring an adaptation choice to the owner:** show the retained-house option and the concrete departure, its room/garden, maintenance, cost and technical consequences, and the owning design stream.
- **Decline the present proposal/plot:** only for an evidenced material mismatch with the owner's priorities or constraints at the declared scope. Do not claim all technical alternatives impossible merely because one trial fails.

First test placement/rotation and site-level/external-route choices while retaining the accepted shell and internal relative levels. These still need context and professional evidence. Changes to roof/collector topology remain with ROF/RWT; room/opening/service geography with HAD/IAS/OAS. If viable options require reducing or reshaping the 12 m shell, losing the court or changing the principal spatial order, present an explicit commissioned-house departure or reject the plot. There is no automatic resize, universal conformance pass or obligation to spend enough to make every plot work. A simpler maintainable solution may be preferable; no adaptation is adopted here.

## Evidence request for real-plot activation

The next manual gate is DPL-COMMISSION. The owner may nominate a plot for a defined assessment without purchasing it or completing all due diligence. Supply or reference:

1. The real parcel identity and dated plan/evidence sufficient to distinguish it; identify uncertainties in extent or identity.
2. The decision to support and explicitly authorised assessment scope, including what may be inspected or obtained and who may receive the output. No search, adviser contact, appointment or spend follows automatically.
3. Available plans, surveys, photographs, levels, planning/context material, drainage/utilities and ground records, with author, date, spatial extent and limitations. Mark absent material absent.
4. Relevant owner priorities/known limits from OB-01–15 and any choices still open. Do not postpone a bounded assessment solely because every eventual design answer is unavailable; state which stronger decisions must wait.
5. A missing-evidence/responsibility list and agreed private storage/sharing references for sensitive site, financial, household or contractual information. Keep public repository references minimal; do not publish private records by default.

Record activation in `project/dpl/commission-activation.md` under the successor contract. The later assessment must maintain a fact/assumption/unknown ledger tied to actual evidence, show the measured house/site overlay and critical sections, evaluate the named interfaces above, and return retain/adapt/reject choices with responsibilities. DPL-SITE-BRIEF completion only makes this manual gate available; an agent cannot satisfy it with a synthetic parcel or simulated owner decision.
