# 3. What the Reference House positively demonstrates

The current RH gives physical form to a bounded-change strategy in five ways.

## 3.1 High-consequence systems are concentrated

Wet, foul, plant and major distribution work is biased toward a service flank, service suite, bounded riser, high-service rooms and deliberate crossings rather than dispersed indiscriminately through slow fabric.

## 3.2 Fast components can be distinct from slow hosts

The clearest example is the window hierarchy: permanent masonry opening, tolerance/fixing zone, finite-life frame and removable trim are conceptually separate. Plant/equipment likewise has dedicated replacement geography instead of dependence on principal rooms.

## 3.3 Serviceability is not granted universal territory

The RH repeatedly refuses speculative infrastructure:

- no house-wide access floor;
- no house-wide removable lining;
- no universal manifold;
- no generic mixed-services plenum;
- no second soil stack merely to preserve a deleted remote en-suite;
- no permanent roof hatch merely because roof work exists;
- no universal structural flexibility for future wall removal.

This refusal is part of the successful result, not an omission OCG must repair.

## 3.4 Complete replacement geography matters more than nominal access

05B–D confirm the existing distinction between a hatch/opening and a real intervention: approach, working position, isolation/disconnection, withdrawal, replacement route, reclosure and recovery remain separate obligations.

## 3.5 Some occupied loss is intrinsic and proportionate

A complete bathroom refit removes the only full bathing room; a kitchen refit removes the kitchen; major structural replanning may make occupation inappropriate. OCG does not call these facts failures merely because the consequence envelope is large.

# 6. Negative controls — protected conclusions

## CI-10 remote en-suite

The house is allowed to say **no** to a later wet-room request that sits outside the selected wet-service geography. Reintroducing it is a new brief/route problem.

This protects OCG from becoming generic future-proofing.

## CI-13 major load-bearing replanning

The house is allowed to have enduring structure. A major alteration can be engineered later without the original architecture carrying permanent transfer capacity, demountability or universal low-disruption provision.

This protects OCG from erasing permanence.

Together the controls establish:

> **Bounded change is a proportionality claim, not a promise of universal reversibility.**
