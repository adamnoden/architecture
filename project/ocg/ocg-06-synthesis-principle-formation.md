# OCG-06 — synthesis and principle formation

**Programme:** OCG — Occupied Change  
**Packet:** #617  
**Baseline:** `521ed3d5d3e8fcb35cb0cec9fe229d1dc10b4cf8`

## 1. Question

What, after the full research and Reference House audit, is genuinely new enough to retain as OCG authority?

The answer must be smaller than the machinery that produced it.

---

# 2. Principal synthesis

OCG did **not** discover a missing house system.

It discovered a useful cross-cutting **consequence test**:

> **Change should be bounded.**

Technical form:

> **For a foreseeable intervention, the consequence envelope should remain proportionate to the thing being changed and to the permanent architectural burden required to contain it.**

A reader-facing explanatory form is:

> **A small foreseeable change should not propagate through unrelated fabric, services and occupied life without good reason.**

This is the smallest defensible surviving OCG principle set.

It is deliberately not a promise that every intervention is small, clean, silent, quick or compatible with continued occupation.

---

# 3. What is genuinely new

The distinctive OCG contribution is not access, isolation, replaceability or maintenance geography individually. Those already exist upstream.

The new contribution is to ask, for the **complete intervention**, what happens beyond the technical object:

- what permanent fabric is disturbed;
- what services are interrupted;
- what occupied functions/routes are lost;
- where dirty/noisy/wet/hazardous work propagates;
- how people, tools, replacements and waste move;
- what reinstatement and cleaning remain before normal use resumes.

That is the **intervention consequence envelope**.

The envelope remains primarily an **internal analytical object**. Its value is in preventing a project from calling something “maintainable” merely because a panel opens or a component can theoretically be reached.

The complete-sequence audit likewise survives internally:

`arrive → protect/contain → access → isolate → disconnect → remove → extract → bring replacement → install → reconnect → test → close → reinstate → clean → return to use`

It is an audit frame, not a construction specification.

---

# 4. What is not new OCG doctrine

The architectural means that repeatedly helped are already owned elsewhere.

| Surviving consequence | Existing owner |
|---|---|
| design for maintenance/repair/alteration over time | GP1 — Build for time |
| avoid consuming slow fabric at fast-system renewal rate | GP2 — Preserve the permanent fabric |
| separate support/fixing/sealing/finish/disassembly functions where useful | GP3 — Design the interface |
| keep failure detectable, contained and repairable | GP4 — Make foreseeable failure detectable, containable and repairable |
| approach/working/handling/withdrawal/replacement geography | GP5 — Give maintenance a geography |
| concentration of wet/high-consequence service topology | existing service-topology strategy/patterns |
| dry-side access / technical-boundary independence where justified | existing wet-service/interface detail owners |
| controlled manufacture / reversible assembly where proportionate | manufacturing strategy / GP6 discipline |
| enduring rooms/axes/stairs/structure where change does not earn itself | GP7 — Let permanence be architectural |
| contractor containment, sequencing, cleanliness and temporary service | delivery / contractor method |
| real boundary, sewer, utility, staging and access facts | DPL/site |

Creating `GP12 — Bounded Change` would therefore duplicate the constitution and imply more novelty than the evidence supports.

**Decision: no new numbered governing principle.**

`Change should be bounded` survives as an OCG synthesis/review principle and possible reader-facing compression of consequences already distributed across GP1–GP5 plus the governing proportionality constraint.

---

# 5. Architectural consequences that survive synthesis

These are consequences of existing authority under the OCG lens, not newly OCG-owned patterns.

## 5.1 Avoid routine demolition as access

Short-life inspection/replacement should not repeatedly require destruction of unrelated slow fabric where a proportionate interface or topology can avoid it.

## 5.2 Prove work and withdrawal, not nominal reachability

A hatch, door or labelled route is insufficient. Relevant interventions need credible approach, working, disconnection, withdrawal and replacement geography at the maturity available.

## 5.3 Bound isolation where the service architecture earns it

A local intervention should not automatically disable unrelated systems/rooms merely because distribution topology was never considered. But universal valve/manifold granularity is not inferred.

## 5.4 Keep routine access away from consequential technical boundaries where practical

Dry-side access is preferable to repeatedly opening waterproofing, weather, airtightness, fire/acoustic or other critical layers where the architecture can achieve that proportionately.

## 5.5 Localise reinstatement

Where a faster-life component or service changes, adjacent slow finishes should survive where a modest deliberate interface can make that happen.

## 5.6 Concentrate permanent intervention infrastructure

Few high-value service zones can earn area and technical duties; ubiquitous cavities, access floors and removable linings carry too much permanent burden without stronger evidence.

## 5.7 Use temporary works for temporary problems

Dust partitions, protection, extraction, sequencing, housekeeping, temporary service and waste handling remain contractor/delivery tools unless permanent architecture materially prevents competent use of them.

## 5.8 Prefer off-site preparation only where it removes real occupied-site work

Prefabrication is not an OCG virtue by itself. It earns value only where it reduces fabrication/wet-work/recovery burden without moving the problem into tolerance, proprietary dependence or logistics.

---

# 6. Proportionality boundary

OCG-01D and the two negative controls establish the limit of the principle.

## CI-10 — remote en-suite

A possible future programme change does not earn a second stack, universal wet routes or speculative service volume in advance.

## CI-13 — major load-bearing replanning

A possible future structural alteration does not earn universal transfer capacity, demountable structure or deliberate structural indeterminacy.

Therefore:

> **Bounded change is not universal reversibility.**

The permanent burden of making an intervention easier must itself be compared against the frequency, consequence, retrofit difficulty, area, cost, carbon, fire/acoustic/moisture/thermal/structural duties, workmanship and architectural effect it creates.

Accepting disruption can be the correct whole-life architectural answer.

---

# 7. Rejected attractive hypotheses

The programme now rejects as general propositions:

- every service should be reachable from a removable panel;
- every room should have a universal service cavity;
- all floors should be accessible or demountable;
- every branch should have maximum local isolation;
- every future service should have reserved spare capacity;
- every finite-life component should be replaceable without meaningful occupancy consequence;
- every room refit should preserve the room's function during work;
- a house should carry duplicate bathrooms/kitchens to maintain renovation continuity;
- all fast layers should use dry construction;
- prefabrication is inherently less disruptive;
- a reversible assembly is inherently lower-carbon or more durable;
- maintenance convenience outranks acoustic solidity, airtightness, moisture safety, fire robustness or architectural calm;
- a computational disruption score can represent the consequence envelope honestly.

---

# 8. Reader-line disposition

Candidate:

> **The house should remain a house while it is being changed.**

The research supports its **human motive**, but not its unqualified literal form.

CI-08 and CI-09 show that ordinary complete room refits can legitimately remove bathing or cooking function. CI-13 shows that major slow-fabric alteration may legitimately make continued occupation inappropriate.

Therefore the line is **not doctrine and not a general performance promise**.

It may survive later editorial review only as a clearly bounded aspiration applying to ordinary foreseeable maintenance/replacement/refit where continued occupation is otherwise reasonable.

The more robust reader-facing statement is:

> **A small foreseeable change should not become a whole-house event without good reason.**

OCG-09 owns the final publication decision.

---

# 9. Maturity / proof boundary

OCG synthesis does not raise the maturity of any detail, product or Reference House assembly.

The research supports the **method and proportionality proposition**; OCG-05 gives virtual-design evidence that the current RH can embody it without generic flexibility infrastructure. Product packing, window mock-up, wet-side/dry-side closure, fire/acoustic service closures, real-site logistics and contractor method remain with their existing proof owners.

No physical prototype has been manufactured by OCG and no claim depends on pretending otherwise.

---

# 10. OCG-06 result

**PASS.**

Smallest defensible surviving set:

1. **Principle:** `Change should be bounded.`
2. **Technical meaning:** intervention consequence should remain proportionate to the thing being changed and to the permanent burden of containing it.
3. **Internal method:** consequence envelope + complete intervention sequence.
4. **Limit:** bounded change is not universal reversibility; accepted disruption is sometimes the correct result.
5. **Ownership:** the architectural means remain with existing GP/pattern/RH/delivery/site authorities.

No new constitutional provision, pattern family, RH geometry, compiler proof, drawing or publication prose is authorised by this synthesis.
