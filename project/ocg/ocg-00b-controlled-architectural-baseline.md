## Purpose

Freeze the architectural propositions that already exist before OCG begins recording renovation frictions, defining an intervention model, conducting occupied-work research or auditing the Reference House.

The baseline is a **test fixture, not an endorsement**. Later OCG work must be able to say whether an intervention consequence was already addressed, deliberately accepted, unresolved, site-dependent or genuinely exposed by the programme without rewriting what the project believed beforehand.

The packet answers only:

> **What does Ovid’s House and the current Reference House already provide, reject, hold or leave unresolved that is relevant to occupied change?**

It does not improve the answer.

# 2. Governing architectural posture

Current constitutional propositions relevant to occupied change are already established in [`governing-principles.md`](../../docs/manuscript/governing-principles.md):

- the house is designed for repeated maintenance, repair, alteration and renewal rather than as an object frozen at completion;
- slower permanent fabric should not be consumed routinely by shorter-lived systems;
- interfaces between lifespan layers are designed elements;
- credible failures should be detectable, containable and repairable where practical;
- maintenance requires geography: approach, working space, handling and replacement paths;
- ordinary parts and understandable assembly are preferred where they perform well;
- changeability is concentrated where it earns itself rather than making the whole house generic;
- physical legibility, isolation and stewardship information should survive generations;
- technical access and replacement must be resolved architecturally where they materially affect occupied space.

The same constitution also freezes a restraint important to OCG:

> **Serviceability is pursued proportionately.**

The project does not promise zero disturbance, universal removability or permanent provision for every speculative future intervention.

# 3. Permanent fabric and changeable layers

## 3.1 Permanent structural and envelope baseline

The current Reference House is intentionally conventional in its slow layers.

It uses, at architectural coordination level:

- substantial loadbearing masonry inner leaves to external and courtyard envelopes;
- engineered-timber first-floor framing between permanent support lines;
- a structural deck retaining diaphragm responsibility;
- local framed openings only where the stair, service zone and other owned crossings require them;
- a conventional masonry outer weathering leaf, drained/insulated cavity and substantial structural inner leaf;
- direct quiet internal finish by default.

The slow envelope remains technically complete when faster trim, joinery or optional lining is removed. Weather, air, thermal and moisture duties do not depend on decorative architraves, skirtings or a removable room lining.

## 3.2 Conventional surfaces are the incumbent

Principal rooms currently use **continuous conventional floors**.

Quiet permanent walls currently use **direct mineral/plaster finish by default**.

The house does not currently justify:

- a house-wide raised access floor;
- a fully panelised principal-room floor;
- a universal removable wall lining;
- a whole-room fixing rack or technical grid.

These are not omissions accidentally waiting for OCG to correct. They are deliberate applications of the project’s proportionality rule unless later evidence earns a change.

# 4. Service geography and distribution

## 4.1 High-service geography is concentrated

The current house concentrates its highest service intensity on the service/eastern flank rather than distributing primary technical infrastructure through principal rooms.

Current selected geography includes:

- a ground service suite of approximately **2.9 × 4.0 m gross**;
- within it, a dedicated plant/distribution room of approximately **2.9 × 2.2 m gross**;
- utility and ground WC retained within the same high-service family;
- the kitchen immediately adjacent to the service geography;
- the upper family bathroom stacked over/adjacent to the service suite;
- an approximately **900 × 700 mm clear** vertical service reservation;
- one primary soil/vent stack;
- distinct ground and upper dry-service routes;
- low-temperature heating distribution launched from the same service geography.

Principal drawing/family/bedroom wall fields remain deliberately free of primary wet, soil, large-duct and plant routes. The courtyard is not used as an opportunistic principal utility route.

## 4.2 Controlled utility entry

The selected Reference House occurrence groups water, electricity and communications geographically at the service side while keeping them separately penetrated and isolatable.

Because the Reference House has no real site, the east/service-side approach is a **surrogate project geometry**, not a universal compass fact. A real utility approach may mirror or materially alter it.

Current intent is that utility entries terminate into the plant/service geography rather than traversing principal rooms.

## 4.3 Vertical service zone

The selected vertical zone is controlled service geography, not a generic mixed-services shaft.

Current coordination provides approximately **900 × 700 mm clear** and a larger provisional structural opening envelope for lining, closure, support and tolerance. Its represented service families include, subject to detailed design:

- soil/vent;
- domestic water;
- segregated dry services;
- ventilation trunks where current whole-house ventilation coordination requires them;
- possible small-bore heating distribution;
- visible/inspectable failure geography at the base.

The exact fire/acoustic closure, support, congestion, separation and final service sizes remain technical-design obligations.

## 4.4 Horizontal routes

The house selects coherent horizontal distribution rather than a whole-storey plenum.

Current arrangement distinguishes:

- ground dry distribution from the plant/service suite through service/gallery and selected perimeter geography;
- upper dry distribution from the vertical service zone through gallery/landing geography;
- low-temperature heating routes;
- short wet/gravity branches concentrated around high-service rooms.

Primary dry trunks are not intended to consume the structural joist depth as an unplanned service void.

Large wet, foul and ventilation routes use explicit owned geography. Dry services receive more flexibility only where they remain accessible and do not take over quiet rooms.

## 4.5 Structural crossings are owned interfaces

Utility, riser, foul, extract, ventilation, heat-pump, courtyard-drainage, soil-vent and PV crossings are treated as registered consequential crossings rather than permission for arbitrary later drilling.

Each consequential crossing must eventually own the duties that actually apply: structure, fire, acoustic, weather, air, thermal, moisture and reinstatement.

# 5. Isolation and failure geography

The baseline contains **accessible principal isolation**, but it does not currently claim a universal individually isolatable manifold system.

Current selected relationships include:

- main water stop/isolation immediately accessible in plant/service geography;
- principal distribution joints and valves biased toward visible/reachable service territory;
- individual appliance isolation at kitchen/utility service edges;
- maintainable bathroom valves, cistern/service components and selected controls biased toward dry-side access;
- consequential joints kept accessible where practical;
- concealed final water runs kept short and joint-minimised where practical;
- visible/inspectable leakage at high-service/riser geography rather than silent migration into slow fabric;
- sensing may supplement physical failure strategy but is not the sole defence.

`Individually Isolatable Manifold Distribution` remains a **held candidate**. The existence of a plant room does not select it automatically.

# 6. Floors, ceilings, skirting and access zones

## 6.1 Structural floor remains structure

The structural deck and joists remain primary structure. Service access is organised around them rather than by declaring the whole floor a disposable service layer.

The house selects the strategy **Separate Structural Floor from Changeable Layers Where Proportionate** without selecting a house-wide access floor.

Selective floor access remains held and must be earned by a real recurring task.

## 6.2 Room-edge dry-service access

The Reference House does select bounded dry-service routes at room edges.

Current implementation direction permits, where proportionate:

- substantial removable skirting;
- selected jamb/architrave or door-surround datums;
- local backplanes or service-wall zones;
- controlled fixing/service datums.

These are local architectural interfaces, not a continuous technical rack around every room.

## 6.3 Ceiling and wall junction

The principal wall/ceiling junction selects a movement/slip relationship in principle: wall and ceiling finishes may terminate independently where movement demands it, with any cover belonging coherently to one assembly.

The exact physical junction remains an active proof question. OCG-00B does not convert that unresolved interface into a removable-ceiling system.

# 7. Wet rooms, kitchen and laundry interfaces

## 7.1 Kitchen

The kitchen sits adjacent to the high-service geography.

Current consequences include:

- water/waste branches remain short;
- appliance valves/joints are biased toward an accessible service edge;
- sink/dishwasher waste can reach the primary stack without traversing principal floor structure;
- cooker source capture uses a short dedicated service-side discharge and remains independent from whole-house ventilation;
- hood/fan cleaning and replacement occur from the kitchen side rather than through a concealed distant route.

The architecture does not assume that every future kitchen alteration is consequence-free. It freezes a service edge and short route family that later OCG work can test.

## 7.2 Utility / ground WC

Laundry appliances and the ground WC remain in the ground service suite, sharing the concentrated drainage/ventilation family.

The utility is intentionally **ground-floor**, while bedrooms and main bathing remain upstairs. HFI has already identified the resulting laundry task route as something to test proportionately rather than assuming that duplicated or relocated wet appliances are automatically superior.

## 7.3 Upper family bathroom

The family bathroom is the principal upper wet room and is closely coupled to the vertical service zone.

Current implementation direction includes:

- highest-consequence soil/waste branches kept short;
- maintainable valves, cistern/service components and selected shower controls biased to the dry/gallery side;
- waterproofing remains independent from access-panel removal;
- concealed final runs are short/joint-minimised where practical;
- inspection/service access is not allowed to become the waterproofing layer.

## 7.4 Remote en-suite is deliberately absent

A previously considered principal en-suite was **deleted** when its service burden was tested.

It would have required some combination of destructive cross-structure gravity routing, repeated penetrations, a service bulkhead, a second soil stack or moving the primary service topology for one convenience room.

The former reservation is used as dressing/linen/storage. Reintroducing an en-suite is a brief/topology change requiring new route proof, not a casual future partition change.

This is a critical frozen negative fact for OCG: the present house has already rejected one form of service sprawl rather than designing permanent infrastructure for it.

# 8. Replaceable components, panels and finishes

## 8.1 Replaceable does not mean every surface is a panel

The project manufacturing strategy establishes a gradient:

1. site-built permanent structure and enduring envelope;
2. a deliberately small amount of semi-permanent interface infrastructure;
3. manufactured replaceable architectural assemblies where they earn it;
4. commodity technical components kept standard where practical.

Faster-changing layers have a stronger presumption toward reversible assembly, but this remains a presumption rather than a universal rule.

## 8.2 Selected replaceable assembly families

Current selected/accepted directions include:

- window frames as replaceable assemblies in permanent masonry openings;
- doorsets as replaceable assemblies in genuine permanent openings;
- removable internal trim/architrave around those assemblies;
- local service/access enclosures where required;
- removable skirting or other local service datums where proportionate;
- commodity fans, pumps, valves, sockets, switches, controls, appliances, sensors and similar technical components remaining ordinary products where practical.

## 8.3 Replaceable architectural lining remains held

A fully replaceable dense architectural wall lining is **not part of the current house baseline**.

It remains an optional physical challenger that must approach the solidity, calm, fixing behaviour and workmanship robustness of excellent conventional finish while proving enough reversible-access value to earn itself.

The house remains complete if that challenger fails.

## 8.4 Reversibility claims require real removal

The existing manufacturing strategy requires repeated custom reversible work to be proved by first-article **removal and reinstatement**, not by looking removable in a drawing.

Dry assembly is preferred for faster-changing layers where appropriate: screws, bolts, clips, gravity seating, removable beads, captive fasteners and accessible standard fixings.

Adhesives and wet trades are not banned. Their use across lifespan layers must account for what is destroyed at renewal.

# 9. Windows, doors and thresholds

## 9.1 Windows

The current window relationship is:

**permanent masonry opening → mounting/tolerance zone → mechanically fixed replaceable frame → removable internal trim**.

Routine glass/hardware/service work may be biased inward where sensible, while complete frame replacement still retains an external-maintenance consequence.

Product-specific jamb/head/sill geometry, air/water/thermal continuity, fixing and withdrawal remain outstanding proof.

## 9.2 Doors

Current doorsets use the analogous hierarchy:

**permanent opening → adjustment/fixing zone → replaceable doorset → removable architrave**.

Fire, acoustic, security, tolerance and replacement-sequence variants remain technical-detail work.

## 9.3 Thresholds

Principal entrance and garden/courtyard openings aim for substantially level, quiet interfaces in which weathering, wear, movement and replacement are explicit duties.

The baseline does **not** assert that level access is already technically solved. Real approach levels, drainage/weathering, doorset integration and finished-product geometry remain unresolved/site-dependent.

# 10. Drainage inspection and access

## 10.1 Foul / soil

The current service topology uses one primary 100 mm-class soil/vent stack in the controlled vertical zone.

It serves the upper family bathroom directly and the ground WC, utility and kitchen through short branches.

Current access provisions include:

- accessible stack-base / rodding geography in or immediately adjacent to plant/service territory;
- direct drop to below-ground drainage within the service suite;
- designed service-side building crossing;
- external inspection chamber before the assumed onward site route;
- deliberate avoidance of routing foul drainage beneath the courtyard merely to shorten a line.

Actual sewer position, site drainage and statutory/supplier facts remain real-site obligations.

## 10.2 Roof and courtyard water

Roof fields fall outward from the courtyard toward inspectable external collection edges.

The current geometry deliberately avoids:

- a principal inner courtyard gutter;
- internal roof outlets;
- valleys collecting water toward the middle of the house;
- rainwater pipes through principal occupied rooms.

The courtyard has a short primary surface-water route biased toward the service side, inspectable at both ends, plus an independent higher-level visible overflow before water reaches occupied-room thresholds.

Hydraulic sizing and real-site discharge remain unproved.

# 11. Plant and equipment replacement routes

## 11.1 Plant room

The plant room is not a leftover cupboard. It owns working, isolation and replacement geography.

Current architectural provision includes:

- internal approach from the service/gallery route;
- approximately **2.9 × 2.2 m gross** room reservation;
- a **nominal 1000 mm clear external replacement-opening target**, subject to actual plant;
- direct replacement route to service-side external access;
- water-resistant/failure-aware floor geography;
- no dependence on ordinary kitchen cabinetry for major plant access.

The current working heat-source family is air-to-water heat pump with low-temperature wet distribution and DHW plant in this geography. Whole-house ventilation equipment is also currently expected within plant/service territory.

Product-level packing, actual weight, handling, door/opening size, acoustics and final withdrawal geometry remain outstanding.

## 11.2 External plant and roof equipment

The roof is deliberately sparse and does not host routine mechanical plant merely because space exists there.

A PV-capable roof reserve exists, but array layout must preserve drainage, inspection and access. A battery is not selected merely because PV is reserved.

# 12. Prefabrication and assembly philosophy

The current manufacturing proposition is:

> **Build the permanent house. Assemble the changeable house inside it.**

Its frozen qualifications matter:

- prefabrication is not inherently good;
- controlled manufacture is strongest where repeatability, interface precision, future replacement or remanufacture make it valuable;
- the strongest standardisation target may be the hidden interface rather than the visible architecture;
- workshop finishing may reduce wet work or contamination, but cost, carbon, transport and logistics still count;
- skilled site craft remains correct where it produces a better durable result;
- semi-permanent interface infrastructure must stay deliberately small and must not become a proprietary second structure inside the house;
- commodity technical mechanisms should remain ordinary products where practical.

OCG therefore begins with **selective reversible assembly**, not an assumption that the building should become a kit of universally replaceable panels.

# 13. Maintenance geography

Existing maintenance doctrine already treats accessibility as a complete task route rather than a hatch or nominal clearance.

For external work the current model includes:

**site/service entrance → approach/material route → equipment/support position → setup/clearance → safe working position → target component → withdrawal/waste/replacement route**.

The Reference House currently protects, subject to real-site proof:

- service-side plant and utility access territory;
- representative outer-façade/roof-edge temporary-access positions;
- rear working geography;
- a garden-to-courtyard logistics route for dismantled tower components and replacement material;
- at least one useful courtyard temporary-access position;
- courtyard drainage and overflow inspection points.

Roof maintenance currently relies on planned temporary scaffold/tower access rather than a permanent roof hatch. This is deliberate: a hatch would add another envelope interface while not providing edge protection or a working platform.

External maintenance access remains site-sensitive. Ground bearing, boundaries, neighbour/highway dependence and mature landscape cannot be truthfully frozen for the site-agnostic Reference House.

# 14. HFI-derived adaptability baseline

HFI is complete as a programme, but several of its architectural consequences remain relevant inputs to OCG.

Current Reference House propositions include:

- principal entrance and internal ground-floor route intended to be substantially level;
- entrance-storey habitable-room capacity preserved so a later temporary sleeping arrangement may be possible;
- principal circulation is used rather than creating a separate second-class accessible route;
- principal stair uses 18 risers, three flights, full landings, no winders and a selected **300 mm going** as a project preference rather than statutory minimum;
- upper sleeping and main bathing remain upstairs;
- wet utility/laundry remains downstairs;
- repeated-use controls and interfaces should minimise unnecessary bodily/cognitive compensation;
- specialist equipment remains demand-led unless the actual brief requires it.

Important non-claims are also frozen:

- no through-floor lift reservation is currently selected merely for speculation;
- no generic claim of stairlift compatibility exists without product/parking/transfer/residual-route geometry;
- entrance-storey fallback sleeping does not itself prove complete accessible washing/care provision;
- an upstairs laundry is not an HFI rule; the current downstairs laundry burden is to be tested against smaller workflow/storage/set-down interventions before topology change;
- missing product/site/user facts are not architectural failures merely because they are not yet known.

The HFI proportionality test remains applicable: durable enabling capacity is worth protecting where it is cheap now, destructive later and broadly useful enough to earn itself; specialist provision remains demand-led.

# 16. Outstanding proof and site-dependent facts

The baseline must not convert coordination into technical adequacy.

Still external or unresolved are, among other things:

- structural member/connection/foundation capacity;
- fire strategy and final compartment/interface detailing;
- detailed MEP sizes, duties, pressure losses, separation and controls;
- product-level plant packing and replacement handling;
- final pipe/duct/cable routes where specialist design changes coordination assumptions;
- product-specific window, door and threshold details;
- final wet-room waterproofing/access assemblies;
- actual acoustic performance;
- final drainage sizing and real discharge route;
- real utility approaches and supplier constraints;
- real site levels, boundaries, ground bearing, neighbour/highway rights and staging space;
- exact external maintenance temporary works;
- mature landscape effects;
- cost and whole-life-carbon burden where serviceability provisions are material;
- physical proof of non-standard reversible assemblies/interfaces;
- user-specific specialist equipment or care requirements.

OCG must later distinguish a genuine intervention defect from a fact that is simply outside the current design maturity or site authority.

# 18. What OCG inherits for later testing

Without judging them, OCG now inherits a coherent set of existing architectural bets:

- **concentrate** high-consequence services rather than distribute them everywhere;
- **isolate and expose** consequential service relationships where proportionate;
- **route around** permanent structure rather than make it an accidental service void;
- **keep wet-room waterproofing independent** from service access;
- **make major technical components replaceable** through real working/withdrawal geography;
- **use local removable interfaces** where repeated change earns them;
- **retain conventional continuous construction** where its whole-life case remains stronger;
- **avoid speculative permanent infrastructure** merely because it could make an imagined future easier;
- **preserve complete maintenance routes**, including object/waste/replacement logistics;
- **preserve modest life-course capacity selectively**, while keeping specialist equipment demand-led.

Those are the incumbents. OCG-00B does not claim they succeed under occupied intervention. It ensures that later work tests the project that actually exists rather than a retrofit-friendly version invented after the question was asked.
