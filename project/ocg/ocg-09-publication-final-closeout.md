# 3. OCG-09A — publication and doctrine re-entry

## 3.1 New numbered doctrine principle

**Decision: NO.**

`Change should be bounded` is valuable, but its architectural means are already distributed across existing authority:

- GP1 — Build for time;
- GP2 — Preserve the permanent fabric;
- GP3 — Design the interface;
- GP4 — Make foreseeable failure detectable, containable and repairable;
- GP5 — Give maintenance a geography;
- existing service-topology / wet-service / manufacturing / permanence principles;
- delivery and DPL where the consequence is method- or site-owned.

Creating a new numbered principle would imply that OCG discovered a new architectural system when the programme's strongest result is instead a **cross-cutting consequence test of existing systems**.

No constitutional or numbered-doctrine edit is earned.

## 3.2 New pattern family

**Decision: NO.**

The recurring architectural responses already have legitimate owners. Repackaging them beneath an OCG pattern namespace would duplicate authority and make future maintenance harder.

The consequence-envelope method stays in `project/ocg/` as internal review machinery.

## 3.3 Current manuscript / private-edition freeze break

**Decision: NO CURRENT FREEZE BREAK.**

The OCG result is intellectually useful but does not presently justify reopening the controlled private-edition manuscript.

Reasons:

1. **The architectural content is already present.** OCG principally changes how existing maintainability/serviceability claims are interrogated, not what architecture the book currently proposes.
2. **The new contribution is methodological.** The intervention consequence envelope and complete intervention sequence are valuable internal audit tools but would read as project bureaucracy if exported wholesale.
3. **The strongest reader line is compression, not new evidence.** Adding it now would alter emphasis more than substance.
4. **The physical-edition strategy rewards restraint.** The book should not expose every internal assurance mechanism merely because it exists.
5. **No OCG finding makes the current manuscript misleading.** OCG-05/07 found no architectural defect or stale publication claim requiring correction.

Therefore OCG does not block the current PUB-01 / PR-02 controlled edition and does not reopen it.

## 3.4 Candidate reader lines

Two formulations were tested.

### `The house should remain a house while it is being changed.`

**Rejected as present doctrine/publication claim.**

It is memorable but too absolute. A full bathroom or kitchen refit can legitimately remove a domestic function, and major structural work can legitimately make continued occupation inappropriate.

### `A small foreseeable change should not become a whole-house event without good reason.`

**Retain as FUTURE_EDITORIAL_OPTION only.**

This formulation is substantially better because it preserves proportionality and the possibility of accepted disruption. It may be reconsidered after external-reader feedback or a later edition if it materially clarifies existing GP1–GP5 content.

It is not inserted into the current private edition by OCG-09.

# 5. OCG-09C — final adversarial red team

## 5.1 Maintenance fetishism

Attack: the project could use renovation pain to justify a house dominated by access hatches, service voids and demountable interfaces.

Result: **survives attack.**

The programme explicitly rejected ubiquitous access floors, universal removable linings, generic mixed-service plenums, universal manifolds, duplicate wet routes and speculative structural flexibility. OCG-07 made no architectural change.

## 5.2 Area / volume / cost / carbon burden

Attack: low-disruption change can become permanent overprovision paid for every day of the building's life.

Result: **survives attack.**

OCG-01D requires the permanent response burden to be compared with the intervention consequence. CI-10 and CI-13 demonstrate that accepting future disruption can be the correct whole-life answer.

## 5.3 Fire / acoustic / moisture / air / weather / thermal / structural risk

Attack: removable and accessible construction can weaken the technical boundaries that make a house durable and safe.

Result: **survives attack with explicit proof debt.**

OCG gives no automatic credit for an access interface. Technical performance remains with the owning detail / engineering authority. Modelled occurrence is not performance proof.

## 5.4 Workmanship and tolerance burden

Attack: theoretically reversible assemblies may depend on unrealistic installation precision and degrade after repeated access.

Result: **survives attack.**

OCG-02 counter-evidence explicitly records connector, tolerance, visual-damage and repeated-removal risks. Bespoke interfaces require owner/prototype proof only where actually selected.

## 5.5 Proprietary dependence and obsolescence

Attack: replaceability infrastructure can make the house more dependent on proprietary components or future supply chains.

Result: **survives attack.**

No general reversible-kit architecture is selected. Off-site manufacture and modularity earn credit only where they delete real occupied-site work without importing disproportionate dependency.

## 5.6 Security

Attack: components made easy to remove for maintenance may also become easier to attack or compromise.

Result: **survives attack within owner boundary.**

OCG never equates removability with technical adequacy. Window/opening and other envelope assemblies retain security, fixing and closure duties under their existing owners.

## 5.7 Zero-disruption promise

Attack: the programme's motivating language could drift into a claim that renovation should be clean, silent, quick or consequence-free.

Result: **rejected.**

No such promise survives. Occupied change permits room loss, local service loss, decant and major disruption where proportionate.

## 5.8 Contractor-method conflation

Attack: permanent architecture could claim credit for containment, housekeeping and sequencing that are actually competent delivery practice.

Result: **survives attack.**

Temporary partitions, extraction, protection, work sequence, temporary services, cleaning and return-to-use remain principally delivery/contractor owned.

## 5.9 Speculative future-proofing

Attack: every imaginable future service or layout change could be used to justify spare routes, capacity or structure.

Result: **survives attack.**

CI-10 and CI-13 explicitly protect the right to say no. A later brief change must earn its own route or structural response.

## 5.10 Reference House overfit

Attack: OCG could infer a universal doctrine merely because the current RH performs reasonably against a hand-picked scenario set.

Result: **survives attack with maturity limit.**

The 13 canonical scenarios cover different consequence mechanisms and include hostile negative controls. OCG-05 nevertheless remains virtual-design evidence only. It does not convert the RH into empirical proof of renovation outcomes in all houses.

## 5.11 Compiler inflation

Attack: once the consequence ledger exists, the project may be tempted to turn it into a score or optimisation target.

Result: **rejected.**

OCG-08 makes explicit negative admission decisions. Narrow source-owned route/access/isolation/clearance facts may later be encoded by their owning domains; OCG itself receives no compiler layer.

## 5.12 Bureaucracy leaking into the book

Attack: the project's internal assurance machinery could make the publication read like a compliance system rather than architecture.

Result: **survives attack.**

The consequence ledger, proof families, status vocabularies and complete work sequence remain internal. No publication taxonomy is created.

## 5.13 Novelty where ordinary practice suffices

Attack: OCG could treat standard competent construction practice as evidence that permanent architecture needs a novel solution.

Result: **survives attack.**

Temporary works remain temporary; ordinary methods remain valid; novelty is admitted only where permanent architecture materially changes an otherwise disproportionate consequence.

# 8. Re-entry triggers

OCG should reopen only if new evidence creates a real question not owned adequately elsewhere, for example:

1. a physical prototype shows that a selected maintainable interface causes disproportionate collateral work;
2. specialist design demonstrates that a nominal replacement route cannot work without architectural change;
3. a real deployment exposes systemic occupied-change propagation not represented by the current scenario set;
4. repeated external-reader criticism shows the book materially understates the relationship between maintainability and occupied life;
5. a proposed future architectural system claims low-disruption change and needs the OCG consequence test.

Do not reopen merely because another maintenance detail remains unresolved.
