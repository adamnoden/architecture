# G-01 Topology Comparison — D4 Preliminary v0.1

**Status:** preliminary comparative analysis; D4 is **not complete** until all three trial plans have been phase-checked and annotated from usable source drawings  
**Purpose:** compare the spatial organisation of the three G-01 trial cases before dimensional/proportional analysis, and determine which topological concepts the future grammar must be able to represent.  
**Rule status:** no G-01 rule is promoted by this document.

> **Do not mistake one beautiful historical plan for the grammar that produced an architectural culture.**

## 1. Why compare topology before proportion

The project began with proportion as an obvious candidate for formalisation.

The source work is already showing that **spatial organisation carries architectural information before dimensions are known**.

Three rooms may share the same width:length ratio and still participate in completely different houses because one is:

- reached ceremonially through another principal room;
- distributed directly from a central hall;
- hidden behind a service route;
- privileged by a double-height volume;
- part of a principal circuit;
- or embedded in an urban double-pile plan.

The first D4 question is therefore not:

> what ratio did Georgian architects prefer?

It is:

> **what kinds of spatial relationships recur, how are they ranked, and which survive across very different Georgian domestic morphologies?**

## 2. Evidence status

This comparison uses only relationships already supported strongly enough to discuss qualitatively.

### Marble Hill House

Evidence is currently strongest.

The English Heritage November 2024 plan supplies:

- all principal floors;
- named rooms;
- a scale bar;
- phase-coded fabric;
- explicit later-demolished service-wing information.

Historic England also holds extensive measured-plan/elevation/section material.

Current confidence for qualitative topology: **HIGH**.

Metric extraction remains deferred until the target historical phase is reconciled against the measured sheets.

### Danson House

The Historic England fabric/conservation research gives unusually rich textual evidence for:

- principal-floor room relationships;
- primary/service stair relationship;
- original versus later door states;
- blind-window decisions;
- floor framing.

Current confidence for principal-floor topology: **HIGH**, despite direct scaled-plan extraction remaining outstanding.

### 76 Dean Street

Authoritative evidence confirms:

- double-pile morphology;
- four-bay urban façade;
- off-centre entrance;
- principal and service stairs;
- first-floor hierarchy.

Historic plan/elevation/section material is located at Historic England and the V&A/GLC collection, but direct usable source inspection remains incomplete.

A historic GLC/V&A survey-plan reproduction has now been directly inspected and supports the broad ground-/first-floor double-pile geometry, principal stair position and secondary/service stair condition. Historical room uses and exact P1 doorway states remain unresolved.

Current confidence for broad survey-state topology: **MEDIUM/HIGH**.  
Current confidence for exact 1732–33 topology: **MEDIUM/LOW**.

Accordingly, 76 Dean Street remains a **morphological falsification case** for D4 until the survey state is phase-reconciled, but it is no longer planless.

## 3. A common comparison vocabulary

Before comparing buildings, D4 needs a vocabulary that does not assume one plan type.

### Space role

- PRINCIPAL
- SECONDARY
- CIRCULATION
- SERVICE
- PRIVATE
- THRESHOLD
- EXTERIOR / COURT

### Route role

- PRINCIPAL ROUTE
- SECONDARY ROUTE
- SERVICE ROUTE
- OPTIONAL / ALTERNATIVE ROUTE

### Relationship type

- DIRECTLY CONNECTED
- ADJACENT BUT NOT CONNECTED
- VISUALLY ALIGNED
- AXIAL
- SEQUENCED THROUGH
- SERVED BY
- VERTICALLY RELATED

### Hierarchy signals

- centrality;
- room rank;
- route privilege;
- sectional height;
- opening/elevation rank;
- degree of connection;
- sequence position.

These are analytical labels only.

They are not yet ontology entities or G-01 syntax.

## 4. Trial topology A — Marble Hill House

### Morphology

Compact freestanding Palladian villa with later service-wing development.

### Ground-floor organisation

The phase-coded English Heritage plan shows a central **Hall** within the principal block.

The Hall is flanked by:

- Breakfast Parlour;
- Dining Parlour.

Deeper/side conditions include:

- Great Stairs;
- Stone Staircase;
- Housekeeper's Room;
- circulation toward the later service wing.

A deliberately simplified topology is:

~~~text
      BREAKFAST       HALL        DINING
       PARLOUR       [P/C]        PARLOUR
           \          |          /
            \         |         /
          DEEPER CIRCULATION / STAIRS
             |                 |
        STONE STAIR        GREAT STAIRS
             |
       SERVICE DIRECTION
~~~

This diagram is not a reconstruction of every doorway.

It captures the architectural fact that the principal Hall and flanking parlours form one ordering system while stair/service organisation forms another overlapping system.

### First-floor organisation

The first floor places the **Great Room** centrally, flanked by chambers/dressing spaces.

The Great Room is double height; the second-floor plan records its upper volume.

This means room hierarchy is not merely planimetric.

The Great Room's architectural privilege is expressed through a combination of:

- centrality;
- size;
- sectional height;
- relationship to the surrounding chambers.

### Preliminary genotype reading

Marble Hill suggests a family that might be described as:

> **central distributor / principal flanks + deeper circulation/service layer**

But that description must not become “the Georgian plan”.

It is one morphology.

## 5. Trial topology B — Danson House

### Morphology

Compact freestanding neo-Palladian villa organised around a dramatic central stair core.

### Principal-floor organisation

Historic England's fabric research describes:

- Entrance Hall — north;
- Dining Room — east;
- Saloon — south;
- Library — west;

clustered around an elliptical, top-lit stair.

A service stair occupies an adjacent western bay.

Most importantly, the original east-of-stair space was a closet rather than a Hall→Saloon passage.

The Saloon was therefore reached from the Hall through either Dining Room or Library.

Simplified topology:

~~~text
                    ENTRANCE HALL
                    /           \
             DINING ROOM       LIBRARY
                    \           /
                       SALOON

                  [PRIMARY STAIR]
                  [SERVICE STAIR]
~~~

This is not a distributor-hall tree.

It is closer to a **principal circuit**.

### Sequence is part of the architecture

If the Saloon can only be reached through another principal room, then the plan cannot be represented adequately by:

- room coordinates;
- adjacency;
- area.

The **route through the house is architectural information**.

The sequence:

~~~text
HALL → DINING → SALOON
~~~

is not equivalent to:

~~~text
HALL → SALOON
~~~

even when the same rooms exist.

### Primary/service circulation

Danson also demonstrates two circulation systems in close physical proximity:

- dramatic primary stair;
- conventional service stair.

This is useful for the Long-Life House because it suggests the model should distinguish **circulation role** from merely “a stair object”.

### Preliminary genotype reading

Danson suggests:

> **central circulation core + principal room circuit + paired subordinate service circulation**

This differs materially from Marble Hill while remaining recognisably within the Georgian/Palladian domestic world.

## 6. Trial topology C — 76 Dean Street

### Morphology

Urban terraced townhouse.

Known authoritative facts include:

- double-pile plan;
- four storeys plus basement;
- four-window-wide front;
- entrance in the second bay from the right;
- principal stair;
- service stair;
- stronger first-floor/piano-nobile treatment.

### What can already be concluded

Even before the historic floor plans are fully inspected, 76 Dean Street defeats several dangerous simplifications.

A coherent Georgian house can have:

- urban party-wall constraint;
- an even-numbered façade;
- no central entrance bay;
- principal hierarchy expressed vertically rather than through a villa-like central room;
- separate principal/service circulation within a narrow plan.

Therefore neither Marble Hill's distributor pattern nor Danson's circuit can be promoted as universal.

### What must remain unresolved

Until the directly inspected GLC/V&A survey plan and the located Historic England material are reconciled to the 1732–33 target phase, D4 should **not** invent:

- exact room graph;
- door sequence;
- room IDs/functions;
- topological depth values;
- metric bay relationships.

The survey-state plan supports a front/rear double-pile organisation with entrance/primary stair on one side and a distinct secondary/service stair condition continuing vertically. It also shows that the ground- and first-floor subdivisions are not identical.

The provisional morphology label remains:

> **urban double-pile + vertically expressed principal hierarchy + dual circulation**

That is enough for morphological comparison, not yet for original-phase rule derivation.

| Property | Marble Hill | Danson | 76 Dean Street |

| Broad morphology | compact freestanding villa | compact freestanding villa | urban terrace |
| Primary organiser | central Hall / principal block | central stair core | constrained double-pile stack |
| Principal-room topology | hall + flanking rooms | room circuit | not yet fully resolved |

The table is deliberately qualitative.

## 8. First important conclusion — G-01 needs morphology profiles

A single topology rule set would either:

- overfit the freestanding villa;
- reject excellent urban Georgian houses;
- or become so vague that it constrains nothing.

The grammar therefore likely needs an upstream concept such as a **morphology profile**.

Possible future profiles might include:

- COMPACT_VILLA;
- TERRACED_TOWNHOUSE;
- COURTYARD_HOUSE;
- LINEAR / WINGED HOUSE.

These names are placeholders.

A profile would determine which families of spatial production are available.

It would **not** determine all subsequent architecture.

For the Long-Life Reference House, the likely future profile is explicitly **courtyard-based**, which is not something we should pretend is directly inherited from Marble Hill or Danson.

## 9. Second important conclusion — topology is more than adjacency

D4 already requires at least four distinct graph-like views.

### Connectivity graph

Can a person move directly between two spaces?

### Route graph

Which path is principal, secondary or service?

### Sequence graph

What spaces must be traversed, and in what architectural order?

### Visual / axial graph

What can be seen or aligned even when there is no direct route?

These can disagree.

A door can create connectivity without creating a principal sequence.

Two rooms can share an axis without a door.

A service route can occupy the same circulation core while remaining architecturally subordinate.

A future semantic model that stores only adjacency will therefore be inadequate.

## 10. Third important conclusion — vertical topology is first-class

All three trial cases already warn against solving “the plan” first and section later.

Marble Hill uses double height to privilege the Great Room.

Danson layers:

- service / terrace conditions;
- principal floor;
- bedroom floor;

around a major vertical stair volume.

76 Dean Street expresses changing social hierarchy through the first-floor piano nobile and its façade treatment.

The grammar therefore needs a **vertical spatial graph**, not merely storey numbers.

Candidate relationships include:

- ABOVE / BELOW;
- DOUBLE_HEIGHT_OVER;
- OVERLOOKS;
- STAIR_CONNECTS;
- PRINCIPAL_STOREY_OF;
- SERVICE_BELOW;
- SECTIONALLY_SUBORDINATE_TO.

These remain candidate semantics until tested further.

## 11. Fourth important conclusion — service topology should be parallel, not residual

In all three cases, service/circulation arrangements matter to the architectural reading.

They should not be modelled as leftover space after principal rooms are generated.

A future grammar should be able to ask:

- what is the principal movement network?
- what is the service movement network?
- where may they coincide?
- where should they remain separate?
- where do service spaces touch principal rooms?
- how does service topology change between floors?

This is directly relevant to the Long-Life House, whose technical-service topology is already a first-class doctrine concern.

## 12. Fifth important conclusion — symmetry is a relation over a domain

D4 reinforces the D3 schema correction.

“Is the house symmetrical?” is a bad computational question.

Useful questions are:

- is this façade symmetric about axis A?
- are these two room fields paired?
- is this storey symmetric?
- is the principal block symmetric while service morphology is not?
- does the external opening rhythm remain ordered despite an off-centre door?
- is symmetry exact, approximate or deliberately broken?

This avoids the common trap:

> classical architecture = whole-building bilateral symmetry.

The evidence is already richer than that.

## 13. Sixth important conclusion — topology needs historical phase

A building's graph changes through time.

Examples already encountered:

- Marble Hill's later service wing alters service topology;
- Danson's inserted Saloon/closet door changes the principal-floor route graph;
- later restoration can reinstate or reinterpret an earlier state.

Therefore a spatial edge should eventually be capable of meaning:

> CONNECTS(A,B) during phase P.

Topology without time can be historically false even when every wall is accurately surveyed.

## 15. What appears transferable across the cases

The trial cases do **not** yet support a shared plan graph.

They do suggest transferable higher-order questions.

For every proposed house ask:

1. Which spaces are principal?
2. How is that hierarchy expressed?
3. What organises arrival?
4. What are the principal routes?
5. What are the service/secondary routes?
6. Which sequences are intentionally ceremonial or private?
7. What is the principal vertical relationship?
8. What symmetry domains exist?
9. What plan/elevation relationships are intentional?
10. Where does service morphology yield to principal architecture?
11. Which relationships survive dimensional change?
12. Which graph changes would fundamentally alter the architectural language?

That may be closer to a reusable grammar foundation than any fixed plan template.

## 16. Mutation tests suggested by topology

Before ratios, the first topology mutations should include:

### T-M01 — bypass a ceremonial sequence

At Danson, add a direct Hall→Saloon route to the original topology.

Question:

> how much architectural identity is lost by one apparently convenient door?

### T-M02 — collapse primary and service circulation

Merge a distinct service stair/route into the principal circulation.

Question:

> is the separation merely historical social convention, or does it carry a generally useful spatial hierarchy?

### T-M03 — remove sectional privilege

Give Marble Hill's Great Room an ordinary single-storey volume.

Question:

> does plan topology alone retain the same hierarchy?

### T-M04 — centre the urban entrance

Force 76 Dean Street's entrance onto an abstract façade centre.

Question:

> does simplistic symmetry improve the house, or destroy a legitimate urban ordering relationship?

### T-M05 — append service morphology indiscriminately

Attach service space to a villa without preserving principal order.

Question:

> what separates legitimate subordinate service growth from architectural corruption?

These are **research mutations**, not claims that the historical buildings should be changed.

## 17. Implication for the “impossible to lay out badly” ambition

The original ambition survives, but at a more sophisticated level.

The future system should not say:

> every principal room must be 3:2.

It may be able to say something closer to:

> this morphology requires a legible principal hierarchy; your proposed move eliminates the only coherent arrival sequence, makes service circulation dominant, destroys the declared symmetry domain and leaves the principal room sectionally indistinguishable from its subordinates.

That is an architectural compiler error worth having.

## 18. D4 completion criteria

Do not mark D4 complete until:

1. Marble Hill target-phase room/opening IDs are reconciled to measured sources;
2. Danson scaled principal-floor/section evidence is directly inspected;
3. 76 Dean Street historic ground/first-floor plans are directly inspected and phase-checked;
4. all three use the same role/relationship vocabulary;
5. connectivity graphs are recorded;
6. principal/service route classes are recorded;
7. vertical hierarchy is recorded;
8. visual/axial relationships are distinguished from movement;
9. uncertainty is explicit;
10. comparison is repeated after source corrections.

Only then should candidate **spatial genotype families** be named with confidence.

## 19. Current position

D4 has already falsified one tempting route:

> **there will not be one canonical Georgian plan graph at the centre of G-01.**

The more promising structure is:

~~~text
MORPHOLOGY PROFILE
        ↓
ROLE HIERARCHY
        ↓
SPATIAL TOPOLOGY
        ↓
ROUTE / SEQUENCE / VISUAL ORDER
        ↓
SECTIONAL HIERARCHY
        ↓
METRIC + PROPORTIONAL RESOLUTION
~~~

This is provisional.

It is also materially more useful than starting with room ratios.
