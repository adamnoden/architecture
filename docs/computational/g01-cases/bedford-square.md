# G-01 Case BSQ — Bedford Square urban family

**Case ID:** G01-BSQ  
**Analysis role:** SUPPLEMENTARY — D5/D6 urban measurement and coupling case  
**Corpus class:** later-Georgian London terrace / square composition  
**Status:** web-source pass v0.1  
**Evidence position:** mixed A1/B — public measured plans and direct historical dimensions are inspectable online; sectional/elevation evidence is partly source-qualified but not yet fully extracted  
**Purpose:** supplement 76 Dean Street with an urban case whose public evidence can carry measurement, while testing architectural order that operates above the individual-house scale.

> **76 Dean Street tests whether villa-derived rules survive an urban house. Bedford Square tests whether the individual house is even the only relevant unit of composition.**

## 1. Why this case was added

76 Dean Street remains useful because it is an early-Georgian, constrained double-pile townhouse with an off-centre entrance, vertical hierarchy and principal/service circulation. Its publicly inspectable metric evidence is weaker than the villa cases, however.

Bedford Square is not a replacement for Dean Street. It is a separate source-led supplement.

The public-domain *Survey of London* volume for St Giles-in-the-Fields exposes measured ground/first-floor plans for several Bedford Square houses, including Nos. 1, 6, 11, 25 and 48. The same source records direct site dimensions and alteration histories. A separate Georgian Cities source identifies a measured house plan/section at 1:400 and frontage at 1:200 by Niels Rohweder.

This is enough to make Bedford Square useful for D5/D6 without lowering the evidence standard.

## 2. Web-only evidence rule

The research environment for this pass is the public web.

An archival catalogue entry saying that a drawing exists is **not** enough for quantitative extraction if the drawing itself cannot be inspected. That distinction matters because Historic England catalogue records exist for several promising Georgian houses but currently expose no usable image.

For this case:

- inspectable public-domain measured drawings may support measurement, subject to image quality and phase;
- direct dimensions stated by the *Survey of London* may be used as direct evidence;
- source pages that identify a measured drawing and scale but whose enlargement is unavailable may establish provenance/scope, not extracted dimensions;
- later secondary reproductions may help locate an image but do not silently upgrade its authority.

## 4. Case identity

- **Place:** Bedford Square, Bloomsbury, London
- **Main construction:** 1775–80, with individual houses entering occupation over the following years
- **Morphology:** planned garden square of terraced townhouses
- **Architectural condition:** individual dwellings composed as continuous sides with stronger central features
- **Material language:** predominantly stock brick with controlled classical/coade-stone detail
- **Research importance:** the dwelling, plot, terrace side and square all participate in architectural order

The square is therefore a useful corrective to a grammar that assumes every architectural relation terminates at the wall or ownership boundary of one house.

## 5. Phase discipline

Bedford Square is a family rather than one pristine frozen specimen. Each measured house needs its own alteration note.

### No. 6

The *Survey of London* states that the house first appears in the ratebooks in 1781. At the survey date it was in two occupations, No. 6 and 6A. The hall had been divided and some earlier openings closed, but the survey judged the premises otherwise broadly as originally erected.

Consequence:

- overall geometry, major stair position and much room structure are useful;
- the 1914 doorway graph cannot automatically be treated as the 1781 doorway graph;
- room ratios should not be promoted from the divided plan without reconstruction evidence.

### No. 10

The survey explicitly records considerable ground-floor alteration.

Consequence: retain as an alteration/counterexample case, not a clean D5 derivation plan.

### No. 1

The survey records a distinctive Leverton-type composition and a full-width entrance hall divided into three bays, with the stair in the left bay. No material plan alteration is identified in the passage currently used for this analysis.

Consequence: strong D6 topology/coupling case; metric extraction remains tied to the measured plan.

### What the four-house mean does and does not mean

The mean frontage is **not** an assertion that every one of Nos. 24–27 measured exactly 24.7 ft. The source gives the combined parcel and identifies it with four present houses. The derived mean is therefore evidence of the order of the urban module, not an individual-house dimension.

This distinction is precisely the kind of provenance D5 needs.

## 8. D5 finding — urban proportion has more than one scale

The previous D5 seed concentrated on room width:length:height relationships in Marble Hill and Danson.

Bedford Square adds another necessary scale:

```text
SQUARE / URBAN FIELD
        ↓
TERRACE SIDE / BLOCK COMPOSITION
        ↓
PLOT / HOUSE MODULE
        ↓
ROOM / STAIR / OPENING
```

A grammar of urban Georgian domestic architecture can therefore contain dimensional relationships that are neither room ratios nor façade-window ratios.

The parcel evidence also warns against importing freestanding-villa dimensions into the townhouse profile. The narrow urban module is a morphological condition, not a degraded villa.

## 9. D6 — row-level order crosses dwelling boundaries

This is the strongest result of the case.

The Bedford Square building contracts required a uniform row. Each side reads as a larger architectural composition with a central pedimented feature.

Georgian Cities records that on two sides the central feature covers **two houses**. A central pilaster falls on the division between two symmetrical dwellings, creating tension between classical centrepiece logic and the actual terrace-house subdivision.

This means:

> **the architectural centre of the elevation need not belong to the architectural centre of one dwelling.**

The current D6 vocabulary already records coupling direction per attribute. Bedford Square shows that it also needs **scope**.

## 10. D6 modelling correction — coupling has an owner/scope

A coupling record should identify not only:

- attribute;
- direction/authority;
- subject entities;

but also the architectural scope at which the relation exists.

Candidate scope levels:

```text
COMPONENT
ROOM
DWELLING
ROW / BLOCK
SITE / URBAN FIELD
```

Examples:

### BSQ-CPL-01 — centrepiece versus house division

- **scope:** ROW / BLOCK
- **attribute:** centre / vertical emphasis
- **elevation condition:** centrepiece spans two dwellings on selected sides
- **dwelling condition:** property division occurs at the centrepiece centre
- **relationship:** aggregate elevation order does not coincide with one dwelling centre
- **confidence:** high qualitative

### BSQ-CPL-02 — uniform row versus individual plan

- **scope:** ROW / BLOCK → DWELLING
- **attribute:** frontage order
- **external authority:** strong estate/row composition
- **internal freedom:** individual plans and entrances must negotiate within the imposed frontage family
- **confidence:** high at aggregate level; house-specific mapping remains to be measured

### BSQ-CPL-03 — No. 1 entrance/hall/stair

The *Survey of London* describes the entrance as giving direct access to a hall extending the full width of the house and divided into three bays; the stair occupies the left bay.

- **scope:** DWELLING / ROOM
- **attribute:** entrance → hall/stair organisation
- **relationship:** the centrally expressed entrance does not imply a centred stair; internal order is asymmetrically resolved within a composed frontage
- **confidence:** high qualitative; exact centreline geometry belongs to measured-plan extraction

## 11. Why this matters beyond Bedford Square

Without `scope`, a compiler could make a false inference such as:

```text
principal facade is symmetric
therefore
principal entrance / room / stair must occupy the same global centre
```

Bedford Square and 76 Dean Street attack that assumption from different directions:

- Dean Street: a coherent individual townhouse can have an off-centre entrance within an ordered front;
- Bedford Square: the strongest façade centre can belong to the **row**, not to one dwelling at all.

This strengthens the existing G-01 position that symmetry and alignment are scoped relations.

## 12. D6 mutation seed — individualise the centrepiece

### D6-M06 — make each central dwelling compositionally autonomous

Take a two-house centrepiece condition and redraw it so each dwelling receives its own independent classical centre/pediment logic.

Tests:

- row-level order versus dwelling-level order;
- whether the block loses coherence while each individual house becomes more self-contained;
- whether `scope` correctly explains the difference without calling one geometry universally wrong.

This is a mutation definition only. It has not yet been architecturally evaluated.

## 13. Relation to 76 Dean Street

Do not replace Dean Street.

The two cases now have different jobs:

| Case | Primary job |
|---|---|
| 76 Dean Street | early-Georgian urban counterexample; off-centre entrance; vertical hierarchy; route differentiation; strong UNKNOWN discipline |
| Bedford Square | later-Georgian measured urban family; dimensional module; aggregate/row composition; scope-aware D6 coupling |

This is stronger than forcing one case to satisfy every evidence need.

## 14. Current verdict

**ADMIT as supplementary D5/D6 case.**

Promote one modelling correction, not an architectural rule:

> **Every plan/section/elevation coupling relation must state the scope at which it operates.**

No Bedford Square room-ratio invariant is promoted.

No claim is made that a roughly 25 ft terrace module is a G-01 rule.

The next quantitative task is to extract phase-qualified room/opening geometry from the best publicly inspectable measured plans and compare it with the direct villa dataset rather than infer precision from low-resolution reproductions.
