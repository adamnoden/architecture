# H1 Heating Strategy Decision — v0.1

**Status:** H1 paper-domain family decision; not an HSA-wide or Reference House mandate  
**Purpose:** choose one low-carbon H1 research family that preserves service accessibility and avoids making the floor assembly carry unnecessary heating-service dependency.

## Decision

For the bounded H1 research domain, select:

**HEAT-ASHP-RAD-01 — air-to-water heat pump with low-temperature hydronic radiators**

Do not use embedded wet underfloor heating as the H1 baseline.

This is a domain simplification and lifecycle choice, not a doctrine that radiators or heat pumps are universally preferred.

## Why

### Regulatory/future fit

The 2026 Future Homes Standard requires new homes to use low-carbon heating routes; government guidance states gas boilers will not meet the performance standard. Air-to-water heat pumps are explicitly addressed in Approved Document L 2026.

The earlier Part-L route used by the S0/S1 fixture also supports heat pumps and encourages low-temperature wet systems.

### HSA fit

The relevant HSA principle is **separation of lifetimes and deliberate interfaces**, not a blanket prohibition on services entering long-lived construction.

Low-temperature radiators offer a useful H1 baseline because they are:

- visible and legible;
- individually replaceable;
- isolatable;
- straightforward to inspect;
- compatible with accessible distribution routes.

Embedded wet underfloor heating places a large service network inside a floor build-up whose other layers may have different lifetimes. That may still be a rational project choice where comfort, energy performance and construction evidence justify the coupling; H1 simply does not depend on that harder case for its first bounded heating family.

A removable or otherwise well-separated floor-heating system could become a later domain extension if technical and physical evidence justify it.

### Complexity

The selected family requires:

- one external heat-pump unit;
- one accessible internal hydraulic/plant interface;
- one accessible distribution network;
- replaceable room emitters.

It does not require every occupied floor to become part of the heat-distribution assembly.

## Design-temperature position

Do not hard-code one heat-pump flow temperature as architectural truth.

Competent heating design must determine:

- room-by-room heat loss;
- design external temperature;
- emitter output;
- flow/return temperatures;
- heat-pump capacity;
- controls.

H1 assumes a low-temperature design route and records the selected design flow temperature as evidence.

A design above 55°C leaves the ordinary H1 low-temperature route unless explicitly justified.

The research preference is:

> **size fabric and emitters so the heat pump can operate as cool as practical, especially outside peak conditions.**

## Hot water

The heat pump may also charge a dedicated hot-water cylinder in the accessible plant/service core.

The potable-water distribution system remains a separate wet-service family.

Do not collapse:

- space heating water;
- domestic hot water;
- cold water

into one generic hydronic network.

## Current authority

This family remains part of the frozen H1 paper domain. It supplies a bounded technical condition against which the computational model was exercised.

Reference House heating selection remains downstream of real fabric performance, site conditions, passive environmental design, comfort, plant geography and competent services design. An H1 family does not become an HSA architectural requirement merely because it is computationally convenient.
