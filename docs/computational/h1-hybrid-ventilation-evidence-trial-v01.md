# H1 Hybrid Ventilation Evidence Trial 01

**Status:** completed research evidence trial  
**Date:** 2026-10-04  
**Family under test:** [VENT-HYBRID-STACK-01](ventilation-family-hybrid-stack-v01.md)  
**Question:** is the proposed hybrid passive-stack family a physically and professionally credible H1 research family, and what may the compiler legitimately claim without project-specific specialist design?

## 1. Verdict

**FEASIBILITY / EVIDENCE-BOUNDARY PASS.**  
**PROJECT PERFORMANCE / RELEASE PASS: NO.**

The research supports all of the following propositions:

1. passive-stack ventilation is an established domestic ventilation principle rather than an invented compiler construct;
2. low-pressure mechanical assistance that leaves a passive flow path is an existing hybrid-ventilation technology class;
3. low-rise housing has been built around deliberately passive-first ventilation and subjected to post-occupancy monitoring;
4. published residential simulation research supports the plausibility of controlled passive/hybrid systems maintaining indoor-air quality while reducing ventilation-related energy compared with continuously mechanical reference systems in the cases studied;
5. the England Part-F framework permits alternative natural/mechanical combinations where equivalent performance is demonstrated.

The evidence does **not** establish that the exact H1 house, stack geometry, inlets, controls, terminals or assist device meet Part F or the project's IAQ/moisture criteria.

Therefore VENT-HYBRID-STACK-01 is credible as a **bounded H1 research topology whose airflow adequacy remains an external specialist proof obligation**.

It is not credible as a compiler-native performance proof.

## 2. Why this distinction matters

The programme has already accepted similar proof boundaries elsewhere.

For example, H1 may natively understand:

- structural support topology while member adequacy remains externally engineered;
- heat-source/emitter/service geography while heat-loss and hydraulic sizing remain externally designed;
- drainage topology while detailed sizing/falls remain externally evidenced.

Ventilation should be treated consistently.

The compiler needs enough knowledge to:

- form the intended airflow topology;
- reject obviously incompatible geometry;
- generate the right performance obligations;
- scope the external evidence;
- invalidate that evidence when relevant geometry/products/context change.

It does **not** need to become a validated multizone airflow solver before the family can exist at paper-research scale.

## 3. England regulatory finding

The current Approved Document F Volume 1 (2021 edition) explicitly states that:

- ventilation may be natural, mechanical or a combination;
- the systems described by the Approved Document are examples;
- other systems may be acceptable if they demonstrate an equal level of performance;
- its simple natural-ventilation route is scoped to less-airtight dwellings;
- outside the system-specific table, expert advice should be sought.

For H1, which deliberately aims for controlled low infiltration, the consequence is not “natural ventilation is prohibited”.

It is:

> **the easy prescriptive natural-ventilation route is unavailable; a specialist performance/evidence route is required.**

The 2026 successor Approved Document retains the same broad technology-neutral structure. Its commencement belongs to the compiler target and is not silently back-applied to an earlier target.

## 4. Evidence source A — established passive-stack design practice

BRE Information Paper IP 13/94 remains a recognised design reference for passive-stack ventilation.

Scottish domestic guidance has historically made the physical arrangement unusually explicit and is useful here as technical precedent, **not as the England compliance target**. It describes:

- ceiling extract from kitchens / wet rooms;
- near-vertical duct to roof terminal;
- stack effect plus wind as motive forces;
- insulated ducts through cold spaces;
- separate ducts reducing room-to-room cross-flow;
- short, low-resistance routes;
- roof-terminal geometry/weather requirements.

Older Scottish supporting guidance also gives representative separate-stack diameters of 125 mm kitchen, 100 mm utility/bathroom and 80 mm WC and warns about nearby buildings/topography and stack geometry.

These dimensions are **not imported into H1 as rules**. They establish that the topology and its sensitivities are conventional enough to define a bounded family.

## 5. Evidence source B — real low-rise housing precedent: Gannochy Trust

The strongest architectural precedent found is the Gannochy Trust Lifetime Neighbourhood in Perth.

The development contains 48 new one-, two- and three-bedroom houses completed in 2020.

Published project material describes:

- an energy-efficient / substantially airtight fabric ambition;
- secure over-window ventilators;
- a central ventilation stack terminating in a distinctive roof “chimney”;
- Passivent Airstract roof-terminal technology;
- a ventilation strategy developed with Glasgow School of Art's Mackintosh Environmental Architecture Research Unit (MEARU);
- limited mechanical extraction alongside the passive system rather than a complex all-air mechanical network.

A services-engineer project account describes the houses as using a combination of dMEV and Passivent Airstract roof-terminal ventilation, with CO2-based control and manual override associated with the passive-stack strategy.

### Post-occupancy evidence

The Gannochy Trust subsequently published a summary of a year-long MEARU study covering eight occupied homes from May 2023 to May 2024.

Monitoring included:

- temperature;
- relative humidity;
- CO2;
- additional kitchen air-quality measurements;
- airtightness tests;
- ventilation fan tests;
- thermal tests;
- occupant surveys.

Reported findings were valuable precisely because they were not uniformly flattering:

- temperatures were generally comfortable;
- no monitored home overheated during warm weather;
- CO2 / air quality was generally good most of the time;
- humidity was sometimes higher than ideal;
- the natural Passivent ventilation system was reported to work well overall;
- some mechanical extractor fans were weaker than expected, apparently associated with long duct routes;
- occupants could benefit from clearer guidance on night ventilation.

### What Gannochy proves

It proves that a passive-first, deliberately designed ventilation architecture in modern low-rise housing is credible enough to take seriously and can perform well in occupation.

### What Gannochy does not prove

It does not prove VENT-HYBRID-STACK-01 for H1 because:

- it is in Scotland under a different regulatory regime;
- its exact house geometry and fabric differ;
- its mechanical component is described as dMEV rather than our exact low-pressure stack-assist arrangement;
- its complete calculation/model/commissioning package is not presently in this repository;
- the published post-occupancy summary itself records humidity and extract-performance issues.

This is a **precedent**, not transferable certification.

## 6. Evidence source C — low-pressure hybrid stack technology

Aereco's current UK hybrid-ventilation material describes the exact physical principle H1 is investigating:

- purpose-provided air admission to dry rooms;
- extraction from wet rooms;
- passive-stack-sized ducts;
- non-permanent low-pressure mechanical assistance;
- assistance introduced when natural forces are insufficient;
- controls based on conditions such as temperature, wind or pressure;
- operating pressure broadly comparable to natural ventilation;
- fan geometry intended not to obstruct passive flow when stopped.

Its VBP/VBP+ products provide real examples of roof-level assistance designed to preserve a natural path on fan shutdown.

However, the current UK material mainly positions the complete hybrid system toward apartments / multi-family buildings and refurbishment of existing passive-stack systems.

Therefore:

**technology-class evidence = strong**  
**exact detached H1 product-family evidence = insufficient**

H1 must not silently re-label a collective apartment product as a detached-house certified system.

## 7. Evidence source D — Passivent hybrid hardware and modelling

Passivent currently supplies natural and hybrid roof/facade ventilation hardware in the UK.

Its Hybrid Plus Airstract roof terminal explicitly offers passive, mixing and boosted modes and can be represented in IES Virtual Environment for whole-building simulation.

Passivent also offers project design support including bulk airflow calculations, thermal modelling and commissioning.

This strengthens the conclusion that the architecture is engineerable using current industry tools and products.

But the current Hybrid Plus range is primarily presented for larger spaces and is not itself an off-the-shelf proof of the proposed H1 wet-room stack network.

Again:

**available engineering ecosystem = yes**  
**H1 release evidence = no**

## 8. Evidence source E — residential research

Two published studies are particularly relevant.

### Turner & Walker, 2013

A simulation study compared passive, controlled-passive, hybrid and mechanical residential ventilation across multiple climate zones.

It found that controlled passive and hybrid systems could achieve equivalent IAQ to the study's continuous-mechanical reference while using less ventilation-related energy in the modelled cases. It also found that passive systems benefit strongly from maximum-flow control to avoid over-ventilation.

This directly supports two H1 decisions:

- passive airflow must be controlled rather than romanticised;
- hybrid assistance/control is a rational response to variable natural driving force.

It does not constitute UK regulatory evidence.

### Jreijiry, Husaunndee & Inard, 2007

A numerical study modelled low-pressure hybrid ventilation for a single-family house in several European climates and compared it with mechanical extract ventilation.

The study supports the basic proposition that single-family low-pressure hybrid ventilation can be simulated, controlled and compared on IAQ, comfort and energy grounds.

Again, it is research support rather than an H1 compliance certificate.

## 9. First-principles pressure sanity check

This check is deliberately simple and is **not** an airflow design calculation.

For buoyancy alone, a useful first approximation is:

~~~text
ΔP ≈ ρ g h (ΔT / T)
~~~

Taking:

- air density ≈ 1.2 kg/m³;
- stack height = 6 m — representative research assumption for a two-storey house, not frozen H1 geometry;
- indoor absolute temperature ≈ 293 K;

produces approximately:

| Indoor/outdoor temperature difference | Idealised buoyancy pressure |
|---:|---:|
| 2 K | 0.48 Pa |
| 5 K | 1.20 Pa |
| 10 K | 2.41 Pa |
| 20 K | 4.82 Pa |

Real flow is then altered by:

- duct friction;
- bends;
- grilles/dampers;
- terminal resistance/pressure;
- wind pressure;
- facade pressure;
- inlet/transfer resistance;
- stack cooling;
- interaction between openings.

Aereco describes its hybrid system as operating at low pressures in roughly the 5–15 Pa range at extract grilles.

The comparison is revealing:

> In mild weather the buoyancy drive of a house-height stack can be tiny. Mechanical assistance is not an embarrassing exception to passive design; it is the mechanism that makes a passive-first topology robust when the weather stops helping.

It also warns us not to assume that “mostly passive” means “fan almost never operates”. The annual duty must be modelled for the actual house and climate.

## 10. Failure modes exposed by the evidence

The evidence sharpens, rather than relaxes, the family.

### E1 — weak natural drive

Warm/mild weather can collapse buoyancy pressure.

**Required response:** assisted mode sized against the residual deficit.

### E2 — excessive / adverse wind effect

Wind can help, over-ventilate or create adverse pressure depending on terminal/facade conditions.

**Required response:** terminal pressure data + control + whole-house model.

### E3 — duct resistance

Gannochy post-occupancy reporting specifically reinforces the danger of long extract runs.

**Required response:** preserve short/vertical service-core geometry and make route resistance part of evidence invalidation.

### E4 — humidity control

Good average CO2 does not prove wet-room moisture control.

**Required response:** RH/moisture criteria remain separate evidence obligations.

### E5 — occupant/control interaction

Background inlets and night/purge ventilation can be compromised by occupant behaviour.

**Required response:** automatic background performance should not depend on occupants remembering to open windows; purge/night strategies need legible controls and handover.

### E6 — heat loss / draught

No heat recovery is a real penalty.

**Required response:** compare annual ventilation heat loss and comfort against MVHR before final Reference-House selection.

### E7 — product mismatch

Collective-housing hybrid hardware cannot be silently treated as a detached-house product family.

**Required response:** product selection remains external until a real H1 configuration exists.

## 13. Mutation/invalidation test

This external-evidence structure passes the paper invalidation test.

### Move wet core four metres

Stales:

- stack route;
- pressure-loss calculation;
- possibly terminal relationship;
- airflow evidence.

Does not inherently stale:

- room hierarchy;
- structural evidence outside affected penetrations;
- unrelated window weather evidence.

### Substitute assist fan

Stales:

- fan curve;
- stopped-state resistance;
- acoustic data;
- control compatibility;
- assisted/passive performance calculation.

Does not inherently stale:

- stack geometry;
- room route roles.

### Improve airtightness

Stales:

- ventilation airflow model where infiltration was an input;
- make-up-air/inlet sufficiency evidence.

It does not count as an automatic ventilation improvement or failure.

### Change external-noise context

May stale:

- inlet selection;
- purge assumptions;
- overheating route.

It need not stale the wet-room stack geometry itself.

This is the selective invalidation behaviour the compiler concept requires.

## 14. Promotion decision

The evidence is sufficient to **promote the semantic/topological family for H1 paper research**, but not its technical performance.

New status:

**VENT-HYBRID-STACK-01 — H1 RESEARCH-SUPPORTED TOPOLOGY / EXTERNAL PERFORMANCE PROOF REQUIRED**

Meaning:

- the compiler may offer/select the family inside bounded H1 research;
- it may derive its inlet/transfer/stack/assist/evidence obligations;
- it may test geometry and dependency relationships that lie inside the declared family;
- it may not return ventilation-performance PASS without scoped specialist evidence;
- H1-PAPER-01 may use the family and should correctly fail release if that evidence is absent.

This is a narrower and more honest promotion than “trusted ventilation system”.

## 15. Reference-House selection remains provisional

This trial does **not** close the architectural comparison with MVHR.

The project currently prefers hybrid passive stack because it offers:

- fewer active dependencies;
- simple and legible airflow geography;
- strong fit with the wet/service core;
- graceful passive residual behaviour;
- low-pressure rather than high-pressure assistance;
- accessible replacement of active parts.

MVHR may still win where the real house/site shows that:

- ventilation heat recovery has large value;
- outdoor pollution/noise makes distributed passive inlets undesirable;
- filtering matters materially;
- winter draught comfort is poor;
- the hybrid fan would in practice run almost continuously;
- the hybrid proof/control burden becomes more complex than the balanced system it was intended to avoid.

The machine must still earn its place — in either direction.

## 16. Programme consequence

The internal **C-014I3 feasibility gate is closed at paper-research level**.

The external airflow-performance obligation remains open and must be visible in H1-PAPER-01.

Therefore hybrid ventilation no longer needs to block preparation of the full-house paper fixture merely because a real ventilation consultant has not yet been appointed.

The remaining internal pre-H1-PAPER blockers are:

1. one ordinary wet-zone waterproofing family;
2. one controlled envelope-penetration family;
3. one structured external-product-evidence trial whose applicability/substitution/invalidation is exercised explicitly.

The actual competent external review remains separately outstanding.
