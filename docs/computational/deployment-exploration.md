# Deployment under explicit assumptions

A house can be studied on a provisional site before every boundary, level and connection has been measured. The result answers a conditional question: what would follow if the stated conditions held?

For Ovid's House, the exploratory model keeps the Reference House unchanged. It places the house once, then varies the surrounding assumptions. A separate evidence record preserves what is actually known about the real parcel. A successful hypothetical placement does not fill gaps in that record.

## What a trial can establish

Consider a rectangular site with the house's front fixed 5.2 m behind the assumed street edge. With the 12 m front parallel to the street, the 12 m by 10.8 m footprint requires a rectangle at least 12 m wide and 16 m deep for bare containment. If the experiment reserves 1 m at each side and 3 m behind, those dimensions become 14 m by 19 m.

The reserved strips are project test choices. They are not established requirements for adequate maintenance, construction access, gardens, fire separation or planning acceptance. A site with those dimensions still needs its own layout and technical assessment.

Levels can expose a different problem. A floor 300 mm above the approach needs 6 m of sloping route at an illustrative gradient of 1:20. A 5.2 m front setback does not provide that length directly. The designer can investigate another route through the site interface while preserving the internal floor arrangement. Landings, turns and route width still need to fit.

Area can sometimes establish a simpler conditional obstruction. The unchanged footprint occupies 129.6 m². If a reported 97 m² were the complete available parcel, no orientation could contain it. The useful next question is whether that reported extent is correct and complete.

## How uncertainty is retained

The eight-site exploratory study uses explicit rectangular, slope and access hypotheses. Some approximate areas and access descriptions come from retained site records. Most parcel dimensions are unavailable, so numerical shapes remain openly uncalibrated alternatives. They are not reconstructions of the actual sites.

A finding may hold at every tested sample, change as assumptions change, or fail at every tested sample. Sample counts are not probabilities. An adverse slope calculation identifies a support, grading or water-design task; it does not decide that the task is soluble or impossible.

Each hypothesis records its input basis, replacement evidence, fixed placement and limitations. The study supports provisional architectural work and helps identify which measurements would change a decision. Evidence-backed site compatibility, regulatory approval and construction adequacy remain separate questions.

## A completed conditional case

The final worked case assumes a flat, usable 17.4 m by 30 m street-front rectangle. These are hypothetical dimensions, not measurements of an identified property. The unchanged house is placed 9 m behind the street edge, leaving 2.7 m at either side and 10.2 m behind.

The front territory provides a 6 m sloping approach for the 300 mm rise, with a 1.5 m landing at each end. The total length is 9 m. This resolves the earlier direct-route length problem in the geometric model. Complete access adequacy remains an explicit premise, including the doorway, manoeuvring space and detailed route conditions.

The study assumes that ground/support, water, drainage, utilities, rights, context, access/logistics and outstanding technical duties admit workable detailed solutions without changing the protected house. Those propositions are premises, not findings established by the geometric check. Under that declared scope the design study is complete. Verification on an actual commission remains a separate task.
