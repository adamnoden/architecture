# Lighting and accessible service layers

**Research study - 21 November 2026.** This study answers the owner's request to reconsider lighting from first principles, alongside the Crafted Room Kit, service skirting and a possible stand-off space behind wall panels. It records evidence, candidate arrangements and a reasoned development recommendation. It does not select a house-wide lighting scheme, alter accepted room geometry, approve products or material exceptions, or establish measured lighting/acoustic performance.

**Project baseline inspected:** `131471446d43a31ecea53c62577b17effd86a62c`. The [owner-house charter](../../project/owner-house-charter.md), [Crafted Room Kit brief](../../project/ias/crafted-room-kit-brief.md) and [whole-house material policy](../reference-house/material-policy.md) remain governing sources. The owner's attraction to lamps, wall-light panels and ceiling panels is an exploration preference, not a preselected answer.

## Research judgement

Develop **a lamp-dominant lighting scheme with selective fixed fittings**, supplied through accessible, bounded service routes. Compare it fairly with a fully portable arrangement, a wall-led arrangement and a simple ceiling-fitting arrangement. Judge them by the same activities, room geometry, visual quality, controls, renewal tasks and material obligations. A ceiling location should neither be compulsory nor disqualified before that comparison.

Develop the proposed panel stand-off as **usable service space**. Give acoustic improvement no automatic credit, and explicitly investigate possible acoustic harm. An air gap, decorative panel and hanging rail are not a sound-insulation specification.

The most useful immediate outputs are a lighting performance brief, a small set of candidate patterns, wall/ceiling service-interface requirements and a controlled comparison plan. Doctrine should retain the transferable principles: protected long-lived fabric, independent support, accessible renewal, straightforward operation and evidence proportionate to the claim. A chosen mounting height, socket count or cavity depth belongs to a narrower design decision.

A plausible eventual result is a house with many movable lamps, a few excellent wall fittings, very few ceiling fittings, and no need to make every ceiling demountable. Another legitimate result is a composed panel ceiling in a room where its appearance and access benefits justify it. The research must allow both.

## 3. Design the light before choosing its mounting surface

### Activities, views and surfaces

CIBSE identifies glare, reflections, shadows, surrounding brightness and flicker as relevant alongside task illuminance. Its cited guidance has a workplace scope; the visual principles are useful here, but its numerical workplace recommendations are not domestic legal minima. [S1]

For this house, describe a room through actual activities and observer positions: reading in a chair, seeing a face across a table, using a mirror, preparing food, finding a shelf label, cleaning the floor and moving between rooms. Record standing, seated and lying viewpoints where relevant. A floor-average lux figure cannot answer all those questions.

For each candidate, examine light on the task and the surroundings, direct views of bright sources, reflections in screens and mirrors, shadows cast by bodies, and the ability to change the arrangement. Visual hierarchy is legitimate: the objective is not identical brightness on every surface.

An uplighter makes the ceiling part of the optical arrangement without attaching a fitting to it. Surface finish therefore becomes an assumption in the lighting design. A later change to dark paint or strongly coloured panelling can invalidate the earlier result; CIBSE explicitly discusses the effect of surface reflectance. [S1]

### Daylight and time

The courtyard, external windows, reveals, shutters, curtains and furniture orientation belong in the lighting brief. The SLL's August 2026 statement recommends daylight as the primary daytime contribution where possible and treats electric light as complementary. It also stresses timing and exposure at the eye, and warns that colour temperature alone is insufficient. This is a position statement, explicitly not a design standard. [S2]

The design implication is to compare day and night conditions separately. A pleasant evening reading arrangement is not evidence that a deep room works on a dull winter day. Conversely, daytime capability does not justify making the whole house bright at bedtime. No personal medical regime or universal exposure prescription is adopted here.

### Colour and dimming

Judge candidate sources on actual timber, skin, food, paint, fabric and brass samples. A warm appearance does not by itself establish colour rendition. DOE describes TM-30 as a set of complementary colour-rendition measures rather than a single colour-temperature label. [S3]

Dimming needs a specified combination of source, driver, control and load. DOE's research identifies interactions among those elements and the dimming level, with more than one metric needed to describe temporal modulation. [S4] A practical trial should include full output, ordinary use, minimum stable output, startup, power restoration, audible buzz and visible instability. A phone recording can reveal some faults but is not a calibrated performance certificate.

### Proposed operating conditions

| Condition | What the candidate must demonstrate |
|---|---|
| Daylight with shading | Useful occupied positions, controllable glare, appropriate supplementary light |
| Ordinary evening | Comfortable faces, room orientation and chosen atmosphere |
| Concentrated task | Adequate light at the actual work, with useful surrounding light |
| Cleaning and inspection | Visibility of floors, shelves, corners and service tasks without impractical lamp rearrangement |
| Night movement | A legible route and controls with an appropriately low disturbance setting |
| Unfurnished or rearranged room | A defined way to obtain useful light without depending on one exact furniture composition |
| Failure or replacement | Predictable behaviour when a lamp, driver, control or optional automation is unavailable |

These are proposed project comparisons. They do not require a seven-button keypad or seven automated scenes. Several conditions may be served by a simple switch and one dimmer.

## 4. Competing lighting arrangements

| Candidate | Principal advantage | Main challenge | Research disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portable lamps throughout | Fittings follow activities and furniture; simple individual replacement | Room controls, lead routes, central tasks, unfurnished use | Keep admissible if it meets the complete brief |
| Lamps with selective fixed wall light | Adaptable personal light plus stable background/circulation provision | Wall space, fixing positions, panel depth and driver access | Leading hypothesis for general living rooms |
| Wall-led general light with local task lamps | Stable room illumination with clear ceilings | Wall occupation, sight-line glare, surface finishes and central-task shadows | Separate competent comparator to the lamp-dominant hypothesis |
| Simple ceiling fitting plus local lamps | Direct coverage of central tasks with few components | High-level renewal, ceiling route and architectural fit | Essential competent comparator; may win in particular rooms |
| Local ceiling carrier or service strip | A deliberate accessible interface for useful overhead services | Retention, seams, working access and local depth | Develop only where a real task justifies overhead access |
| Whole demountable ceiling | Potentially coherent craft and broad access | Greater mass, joints, retention, handling and boundary obligations | Separate candidate; not a consequence of permitting one pendant |

Compare equally resolved examples, with similar care spent on appearance. A poor bare ceiling lamp is not a fair control for a carefully staged lamp scheme. Compare complete installed and replacement burdens; fewer holes need not mean fewer components or lower cost.

### Room tendencies to test

Living rooms, bedrooms and the study are strong candidates for movable task lamps and a separately considered background contribution. Kitchens need explicit perimeter-worktop and central-island cases. Mirror lighting and shower visibility should be examined separately in bathrooms, with fittings and wiring suitable for the actual wet location. Stairs, circulation, plant and storage need dependable lighting for their particular routes and maintenance tasks. Dining-room pendants or hall lanterns may earn their place architecturally as well as functionally. These are starting hypotheses, not a room-by-room selected schedule.

## 5. Make service skirting useful without fixing every outlet at skirting height

The useful proposition is accessible horizontal distribution with short, declared branches to accessories. England's Approved Document M, Volume 1, paragraph 1.18 gives the ordinary Category 1 guidance of 450-1200 mm centre-line height for switches and sockets serving habitable rooms. This is guidance for its stated new-dwelling scope, not a claim that every existing low socket is unlawful. [S5]

A low trunk can feed a removable vertical service piece, a panel-mounted accessory or an appropriate joinery interface. The socket height and the route height need not coincide. The existing IAS arrangement already allows declared rises; use that mechanism.

Provide outlet coverage against at least two plausible furniture arrangements. Count usable connection positions and safe lead paths, not merely sockets. A socket behind a wardrobe may be physically present yet useless for daily lamp control. Avoid a layout that requires a lead across a doorway or normal walking route.

Compare ordinary general-purpose outlets with clearly identified room-controlled lamp outlets. Established round-pin lighting outlets help distinguish the lighting circuit but reduce compatibility with ordinary supplied plugs. Do not assume that every outlet can safely or usefully be centrally dimmed: general appliances and lamp electronics have different requirements. A reasonable first control comparison is physical room on/off control with lamp-local dimming, against a deliberately specified compatible dimming arrangement.

Keep normal operation understandable to a guest. Optional software can add convenience, but the design should demonstrate useful manual operation during its absence. This is a proposed resilience requirement, not an argument for forbidding automation.

Do not turn generous socket provision into an unbounded custom live busbar behind decorative timber. Use an evidenced electrical system with supported routes, suitable enclosures and explicit isolation. Decorative removal should leave live parts protected.

## 6. A wall-light panel should be a durable carrier

Separate three things: the architectural face, its mechanical support, and the electrical equipment. A bespoke timber or mineral face can host an ordinary replaceable luminaire. The light source, driver, connector and heat-management parts need not become bespoke simply because the visible composition is special.

For each proposed panel, record the complete moving mass, centre of gravity, support and restraint, release direction, connector access, permitted service slack, required isolation and first safe resting position. A light attached to a face can change its handling substantially. If the service remains independently supported behind a removable cover, show that relationship instead of assuming the face carries it.

Use existing local release pockets and independently removable mouldings. A cable, driver box or sconce projection must not occupy the space needed to lift or withdraw the panel. A failed driver should not require dismantling several decorative panels, a radiator or a fixed cabinet.

Keep ordinary picture fixing and electrical routing legible in the concealed design. Provide safe fixing zones, depth limits and replaceable attachment provisions. An invisible cable cavity that makes every future picture hook hazardous would fail the house's ordinary-use objective.

## 7. Ceilings deserve the same questions, not automatically the same hardware

The Ovidian principle applies above the room: a short-lived service should have an appropriate renewal route that preserves slower-lived structure and finishes. Several implementations can satisfy that principle:

1. A simple surface-mounted fitting with accessible connections and a deliberate route.
2. A small independently removable ceiling service carrier or rose assembly.
3. A composed service strip where several nearby components justify it.
4. A removable perimeter element where access, movement and appearance all work.
5. A complete decorative panel ceiling where its whole-room value outweighs the added assembly burden.

The first option remains a legitimate control. Do not build a whole ceiling system solely to avoid a small well-designed fitting interface.

Overhead panels require their own support, positive retention and controlled-removal arrangement. Wall hanging geometry cannot simply be rotated overhead. Commercial PanelSafe products illustrate a separate restraint function during ceiling-panel servicing; their existence does not approve their materials or capacities for this house. [S6]

Distinguish panel support, luminaire support and any service restraint. Include the worker, ladder or platform, first released edge, temporary support and safe first rest. A tether can help control removal but is not a substitute for correctly designed primary support.

Preserve required fire/acoustic/air boundaries during ordinary decorative renewal wherever practicable. A detachable visible panel cannot casually replace a tested protective ceiling. Reuse the wall/ceiling junction prototype's comparison and movement logic, while designing the actual new assembly and checking current room-height and structural/service reservations. No spare ceiling depth is assumed.

## 8. The proposed inch behind the panels

### Three dimensions that must stay separate

| Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Overall lining depth | Permanent structural datum to finished room face |
| Gross stand-off | Fixed backing/protective plane to rear of decorative panel |
| Usable routing space | Actual connected space left after rails, supports, fixings, enclosures, bends and required clearances |

A nominal rail dimension is not proof of the third. A 25 mm-deep route may cease to be usable where it meets a rail, turns a corner or passes an enclosure.

### The current Study has no spare 25 mm void

The [gauged wall-transition study](../../project/ias/crafted-study-wall-transition.md) allocates a maximum 26.35 mm stack within a 27 mm finished-face datum: 0.5 mm fixed air plane, 0.5 mm correction, 6.5 mm fixed board, 6.35 mm cleat, 2 mm fitting space and 10.5 mm complete moving cassette, before measured packing.

That is a tightly fitted whole lining. A hypothetical 25 mm gross stand-off plus the 10.5 mm complete moving cassette already totals 35.5 mm before separate backing. Keeping the existing 7.5 mm fixed-plane/correction/board allowance makes the simple comparison 43 mm before additional local packing or other allowances. Rails occupy part of the gross stand-off; demanding 25 mm clear at every crossing may require more. This arithmetic is illustrative, not a selected revised stack.

Any increase must propagate to reveals, door returns, skirting alignment, floor area, furniture and removal clearances, and the leverage on supports. Do not obtain it by quietly cutting permanent fabric or taking depth from a protected layer.

### Comparison recommendation

Compare initial gross stand-offs of 25, 38 and 50 mm, measured from the fixed backing/protective plane to the rear of the decorative assembly. Determine usable connected routes separately after rails, equipment and clearances. Revise these exploratory dimensions when actual components require it. Compare a shallow general space with local deeper accessory pockets against a uniformly deeper lining. The target is the shallowest robust arrangement, not the shallowest drawing.

Manufacturer data illustrates why one universal inch is premature: Varilight lists 25 mm minimum boxes for some accessories, 35 mm for other ranges and 47 mm for shaver sockets. Those are product-specific wall-box minima, not complete panel-stack dimensions or approved product selections. [S7]

Keep the gap on the appropriate room side of the building's thermal and air-control arrangements. Government internal-wall-insulation guidance recommends a separate service cavity ahead of protected layers where possible to reduce service penetrations. That retrofit guidance supports the separation principle; it does not prescribe this new house's wall section. [S8]

## 9. Acoustics: a separate performance question

| Desired outcome | What must be examined |
|---|---|
| Less echo within the room | Absorption and reverberation, including furnishings and exposed surface behaviour |
| Better speech or music privacy | The complete separating wall, doors, gaps and frequency-dependent transmission |
| Quieter footsteps or machinery | Vibration input, structural connections and resilient/isolated support where justified |
| Less sound bypassing the wall | Flanking through junctions, floors, ceilings, ducts and service routes |

An imperforate decorative face with wool behind it is not equivalent to an exposed porous room absorber. It might be designed as a resonant absorber, but that is a distinct frequency-dependent proposition. Do not use the word soundproofing to merge these different jobs.

NRC measurements on masonry with added drywall show that a shallow additional leaf can reduce transmission loss near its mass-air-mass resonance. A larger gap or different mass changes that resonance; a higher overall rating need not eliminate a poor frequency band. [S9]

For explanation only, NRC's massive-backing approximation for an unfilled cavity is `f ≈ 60 / sqrt(m × d)`, with panel mass per area `m` in kg/m² and depth `d` in metres. An assumed 10 kg/m² panel gives approximately 120 Hz at 25 mm, 97 Hz at 38 mm and 85 Hz at 50 mm. These are not predictions for the room kit: its support stiffness, panel bending, joints, size and damping are unmodelled. No dB improvement follows from this calculation. [S9]

Rigid rails provide a vibration path. NRC's cavity-wall research shows why mechanical separation, mass, cavity depth and absorption must be considered together; adding soft infill cannot be assumed to cancel a rigid connection. [S10]

A useful comparator does exist. British Gypsum B226008 uses a specified plastered brick wall, 35 mm cavity, 25 mm infill and a 12.5 mm board. Its Rw 57 dB and Rw + Ctr 50 dB describe that complete laboratory assembly, not the benefit of its gap. It requires its specified supports, joints and seals. A removable timber/tile panel is a different assembly. [S11]

The recommended standard service-panel concept therefore receives **no automatic acoustic improvement credit**, and must also be challenged for rattle, hollowness, resonance and new flanking. Keep the underlying separating boundary complete. Open decorative joints within one room need not connect to its neighbour, but they prevent an unsupported claim that the decorative skin is a sealed additional acoustic leaf.

Use special acoustic panels or linings selectively where the room requirement earns them. Their support and depth may legitimately differ from the ordinary decorative family. Absorbent fill can alter cable cooling and, against external fabric, thermal/moisture conditions; assess its actual placement. It is not a universal harmless filler.

## 10. Electrical, fire and material constraints

Shallow concealed wiring needs a deliberate protection design. Demountable decoration is not a general exemption, and a 50 mm cable-protection threshold should not be mistaken for a rule that every service cavity must be 50 mm. An RCD does not physically stop a fixing from penetrating a cable. Electrical Safety First's technical guidance explains protected routes and additional-protection considerations; final details must use the applicable current standard rather than an isolated older FAQ. [S12]

The IET edition checker identifies BS 7671:2018+A4:2026 as the current edition after the transition ending 15 October 2026. This study checked public guidance, not the complete purchased standard, and does not declare a wiring design compliant. [S13]

Specify supported routes, segregation, enclosure access, connector suitability, cable-bend space, mechanical protection, conductor thermal conditions and bonding where applicable. Prevent the rail/fixing system from entering reserved wiring zones. A decorative metal cover is not automatically compliant containment, and an accessible driver still needs adequate thermal conditions inside its actual enclosure.

Do not make a connected hidden void around the house by default. Required fire and acoustic boundaries, floor junctions, openings and service crossings need explicit closure details. The official Approved Document B publication contains current and forthcoming amendments; applicability must be selected for the eventual project, rather than inferred from the download filename. [S14]

The whole-house material policy applies to installed cable insulation, connector bodies, accessory interiors, driver encapsulation, seals, coatings and pads. A brass face or metal casing is not a complete constituent declaration. The tested British Gypsum comparator includes an acrylic sealant; its published acoustic result does not grant that material an Ovidian exception. [S15]

Acoustic infill also needs constituent evidence, including binders and facings. A mineral-wool description or a bio-based binder claim does not establish compliance with the material policy.

Compare complete safe systems and record unknown constituents. No part of this proposal authorises removing electrical insulation, required seals or fire protection to obtain a nominal material-purity claim. Loose possessions are a separate category under the existing policy; movable lamps do not make the installed distribution system exempt.

## 12. Interface and computational consequences

Before any candidate becomes an active design dependency, extend the current shared model rather than create a second lighting-room geometry store. The initial research record itself needs no new solver.

The useful future inputs are stable room/surface IDs; actual reflectance assumptions; fitting location, orientation, photometric evidence and replacement identity; task and observer positions; furniture obstruction; scene/control relationships; route and isolation identifiers; complete carrier dimensions and mass; support and release envelopes; material status; and evidence/application boundaries.

Useful checks include a light/driver obstructing a panel release, a cable crossing a fixing zone, an accessory exceeding local depth, an enlarged lining reducing a door or furniture clearance, and a surface edit invalidating an earlier lighting result. A saved room edit should regenerate affected geometry and schedules and mark affected analysis stale. Missing photometry or acoustic evidence must remain unknown, not become a favourable default.

Detailed optical calculation may use an appropriate established analysis tool and remain outside the browser editor. The shared model should supply the inputs and retain source revisions. It should not infer a room's lighting quality from watts, a lamp count or a decorative render. Likewise, a geometric access pass cannot certify an electrical assembly or acoustic performance.

An interface schedule should declare: function; exact supported location/envelope; load path; retention/removal sequence; power and control connection; isolation; electrical/thermal envelope; protected-boundary crossings; complete materials; and replacement/evidence identity. Keep product-specific constraints explicit and avoid scaling hardware dimensions as if they were decorative geometry.

## 13. A comparison that can change our mind

### Desk research completed here

This study has compared primary lighting guidance, an actual shallow acoustic assembly, measured masonry-lining research, public electrical/accessibility guidance, component dimensions and the current repository's relevant design records. It has found support for accessible distribution and replaceable interfaces, but no basis for a universal ceiling ban, a universal one-inch cavity or an acoustic rating for the proposed panel system.

### Next design comparison

Use the existing occupied Study as one source-bound case and a difficult central-task kitchen case as another. Compare competent portable, wall-led and simple ceiling-led solutions under identical geometry, finishes and activities. Add a fully panelled ceiling only where it answers an actual question. Record total components, access operations, material residuals and eventual installed/renewal costs; do not invent prices before quantities and products exist.

A lighting result should identify required illuminance at named tasks, relevant views and glare/reflection observations, source/finish quality, controls and failure behaviour. Select any numerical targets from appropriate activity guidance and the agreed user brief; mark proposed targets as such. The current study contains no simulated lux results because no exact luminaire photometry has been selected.

### Service-bay comparison

Develop one representative corner with skirting, a reachable accessory, a light-bearing panel, its top release, and a door/reveal interface. Compare shallow/local-pocket and deeper-continuous layouts with actual components. Count overall wall depth and usable connected routing space separately. Examine cable support, corners, electrical enclosures, driver heat, fixing exclusions, access, isolation and restoration.

### Renewal and ordinary-use comparison

Rearrange furniture; remove one lamp; substitute an ordinary compatible replacement source; change a wall finish; disconnect optional control software; and replace a driver or panel. Record what must move, what must be isolated, how parts are supported and whether the room returns to acceptable appearance and function without destructive repair. Test a lost retainer and modest set-out error against criteria fixed beforehand. No one-hour installation result is claimed.

### Acoustic comparison where relevant

First identify a nuisance and a room pair. Compare the protected wall with the actual panel/rail/service assembly, including joints and representative flanking paths. Examine frequency-dependent transmission and room reverberation separately. A small service mock-up can reveal rattle and poor access; it cannot establish a whole-wall laboratory insulation rating. No physical specimen, procurement or measured acoustic result is claimed by this research.

### Selection rules

- Prefer the arrangement that meets the complete brief with the least disruptive installation and renewal burden.
- Accept a fully portable room if it actually works; do not add a wall light to satisfy a slogan.
- Accept a useful ceiling fitting if its complete arrangement is better; do not panel the entire ceiling by implication.
- Select a shallow cavity only if the real components and operations fit robustly.
- Keep an ordinary service panel acoustically uncredited if that is the honest result, while still rejecting demonstrable acoustic harm.
- Preserve owner aesthetic judgement at the existing interior-review gate. Research does not supply that verdict.

## 14. Disposition and handoff

**Recommended for development:** a lamp-dominant arrangement, accessible skirting distribution with selected rises, standard replaceable electrical parts hosted by crafted faces, and comparison of local overhead interfaces before a full ceiling system.

**Supported general direction:** deliberate separation of permanent/protected fabric from changing fit-out, independent support and safe access. Existing doctrine and patterns already carry most of this lesson.

**Not selected:** socket quantities, a house-wide circuit/control architecture, a universal mounting surface, a universal cavity depth, acoustic infill, an overhead fixing system, or any specific product/material exception.

**Rejected as assumptions:** an inch of empty space automatically soundproofs; an existing 27 mm lining contains 25 mm free services space; a wall clip is an overhead support design; a custom decorative face requires custom electronics; a competent ceiling comparator can be omitted; a room render proves lighting performance.

The existing IAS/HAD interior route owns candidate room development and owner review. MAT owns constituent/material decisions, BCR the relevant coordinated construction questions, and the current computational contracts own any later adopted editable inputs. Use those routes for consequential implementation. This evidence study creates no parallel work queue, new approval gate or claim of increased house maturity.
