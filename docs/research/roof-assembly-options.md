# Roof assemblies: drying, support and renewal

**Scope:** architectural comparison for Ovid’s House, reviewed 29 October 2026. No roof build-up, insulation thickness, fixing schedule or engineering performance is selected. Read with the [roof evaluation frame](roof-failure-evaluation.md), [topology study](roof-topology-families.md) and [current house basis](../reference-house/current-house-basis.md).

## Finding

The layer beneath a covering can determine the roof's failure and renewal long before the visible material wears out. A plausible assembly must connect five things: the room's air and thermal boundary, moisture escape, secondary water discharge, the covering's support and movement, and an achievable repair sequence. A material called breathable, a space labelled ventilated, or a drawn roof thickness does not establish those connections.

For the current pitched ring, retain two ordinary starting comparisons: an insulated ceiling beneath an unheated roof space, and a fully specified over-structure insulated roof. A slope-insulated, ventilated assembly remains conditional on connected airways at the ring's corners and junctions. An improvised mixture of these arrangements is not an acceptable shortcut. This is a research shortlist; neither starting comparison has earned selection or demonstrated fit.

## Name the functions before naming the roof

**Air control** limits unintended bulk air movement; **vapour control** moderates diffusion; **thermal continuity** limits heat loss and cold surfaces. One material may perform several duties only where its joints, edges and penetrations are detailed for them. Room ventilation does not ventilate a roof void, and an underlay's vapour permeability does not establish a usable air route through a complete roof.

**Secondary drainage** receives specified entry through the covering and needs a continuous fall and a safe exit. It is not automatically a temporary roof, a permanent primary membrane or protection against a backed-up collector. **Ventilation** needs actual inlet, connected passage and outlet, including at supports and barriers. **Drying** needs a destination for moisture, a route and a condition under which the route works. Removing a vent in favour of a non-ventilated system therefore requires an entire alternative moisture strategy, not a renamed cavity.

A ceiling-insulated loft is not the same construction as a ventilated sloping assembly simply because both may be called cold roofs. Equally, insulation above rafters does not by itself demonstrate that every moisture-sensitive element is warm enough. Use the layer sequence and boundary location in decisions rather than these ambiguous names alone.

## What the represented roof actually reserves

The current source depicts a 25° ring, +6800 mm outer/court eaves and a 2125 mm ridge inset. Its shaded roof band is a **300 mm vertical subtraction**, not a specified 300 mm build-up measured perpendicular to the face. On a planar 25° face that distance corresponds to approximately **271.9 mm normal separation**: `300 × cos(25°)`. The band contains no scheduled rafters, insulation, air passage, battens, deck or covering. Neither number is available insulation thickness.

The represented ceiling has an underside at +6550 mm, a member zone to +6690 mm and a separate provisional storage deck to +6712 mm. In the principal section at `x = 5900 mm`, the south storage run extends from `y = 1000` to `3250 mm`. Subtracting the roof band and deck top leaves about **254.3 mm** at either end and **778.9 mm** at the ridge (`y = 2125 mm`). These are source-derived geometric differences before adding a selected assembly, obstructions or working space. They neither select a storage function nor prove that a person, tool or replacement component can reach the roof underside.

At the eave reference line the lower roof trace is +6500 mm, already below the nominal ceiling underside. This is a demand for a located wall-head/eave section, **not a demonstrated clash in an occupied room**: the wall, cornice and ceiling termination occupy different positions. Do not subtract two remote datums and declare a complete construction impossible. Conversely, do not preserve a uniform layer stack by silently thinning insulation or blocking the airway at the edge.

## Ring-specific water and air routes

The topology study identifies four inner valleys ending at court corners. The shallow roof space is not evidence of an uninterrupted annular air circuit. Structural members, valley backing, fire barriers, insulation and future penetrations can divide it. For each proposed roof-space or above-insulation passage, draw its entry, exit, clear continuity, local restriction and inspection route. Do not assume two nominally opposite vents communicate around a corner.

At both inner and outer eaves, locate separately the primary covering edge, secondary-drainage exit, ventilation opening, insulation return and wall air-boundary connection. A collector or cornice must not block the airway or turn secondary drainage behind the wall. At a court corner, reconcile valley support, underlay continuity and its drainage receiver with the concentration of primary runoff. An underlay discharging into the same blocked collector is not an independent overflow route.

A fire barrier and a ventilation route can each be legitimate requirements; drawing one through the other does not reconcile them. The selected assembly needs the relevant fire strategy and compatible details, including penetrations and interfaces with walls. No barrier spacing or fire classification is established here.

## Support, movement and attachment

Trace loads through the complete chain: **covering → clips/battens/deck → counter-battens or brackets where present → primary members → wall/support → building restraint**. Thermal insulation is not an assumed structural fixing substrate. A long fastener through insulation needs a designed load path, bearing/compression behaviour and suitable attachment; a sound tile fixing cannot compensate for an unproved batten or support connection. Preserve separate requirements for gravity, wind uplift, in-plane restraint and temporary works.

| Interface | Comparison question | Evidence needed before acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| Discrete covering on battens | Can underlay water pass beneath the horizontal supports, and can one covering piece be replaced without destroying that path? | Selected covering gauge/pitch, batten grade/span/fixing, underlay support and wind classification, actual drainage exit |
| Sheet covering on continuous support | Is the support suitable for underside chemistry, smoothness, moisture, joints and local loading? Which layers can move relative to it? | Compatible substrate/separation system, support joints and moisture conditions, fixed/sliding zones, seam/end/penetration movement details |
| Over-insulation support | What crosses the insulation, what carries load, and what prevents crushing, sliding or a thermal bypass? | System-specific attachment and structural/thermal assessment using actual member positions and exposures |
| Valley/ridge/edge support | Can backing and restraint coexist with water and air routes? Are local loads and wind zones addressed? | Located detail and load path; no uniform field spacing extended blindly to edges or corners |
| Added equipment or future opening | What new load, penetration, restraint or access duty enters the assembly? | Explicit design change, weather/air/thermal reinstatement and review of the affected support. No assumed PV or walkable roof |

The [shared roof/rainwater compatibility record](roof-rainwater-material-compatibility.md) owns directional contact, runoff and contamination cases. Add actual covering, support, adhesive and fixing products there when known; this study creates no competing generic compatibility chart.

Support stiffness and dimensional change matter even without collapse: movement can distort a water path or strain a seam. Allowing covering movement is different from permitting substrate instability. The study selects no timber species, sheet grade, member size, fastener length or wind pressure. Roof form and real exposure remain inputs to competent design; generic edge-fixing numbers are not substitutes.

## Challenge cases and recovery

| Initiator | Route and first vulnerable receiver | Recovery or rejection test |
|---|---|---|
| Ceiling/hatch air-control defect | Moist indoor air reaches a colder concealed layer; the first visible stain may occur after hidden wetting | Show inspection and repair of the air boundary and a verified drying route; do not diagnose every stain as construction drying |
| Wet deck or insulation enclosed during construction | Moisture remains between layers whose outward/inward drying may be restricted | Specify an evidenced moisture/closure check and weather-protection sequence with the selected system. No invented universal moisture threshold |
| Insulation or storage blocks an airway | One bay loses its proposed drying route while neighbouring vents remain visible | Locate the blockage and intervention path. Reject the drawing-only ventilation claim if it cannot be checked or restored |
| Local covering damage plus blocked eave collector | Entry reaches secondary drainage whose exit may be submerged or redirected | Trace the common failure to the actual receiver with rainwater design; two layers sharing this route do not count as independent protection |
| Covering movement restrained at a clip, flashing or penetration | Strain transfers into seam, fixing or substrate | Establish the intended freedom and repair extent; investigate the system, rather than merely sealing the visible crack |
| Buried underlay or vapour-control layer requires renewal | Apparently sound covering, battens, deck or internal finish may have to be removed | Write the actual removal and reinstatement order, access, temporary weathering and retained-layer condition; compare local repair with whole-field stripping |

No numerical service-life advantage follows from these cases. A mechanically demountable assembly may still require broad dismantling to reach a buried layer; a bonded system may have an effective local repair if evidence supports it. The intervention, rather than the label, decides the comparison.

## Admission and next evidence

Carry the ceiling-insulated and over-structure families into coordinated comparison, with the slope-ventilated alternative retained where its junctions can support real air routes. Eliminate any candidate that relies on inaccessible routine intervention, an unlocated secondary discharge, unproved essential support, or an assembly that only fits by silently moving house geometry. Uncertainty may remain in research; it cannot be converted into a selected construction proposition.

The next assembly record should include a located layer sequence and thickness budget; primary/secondary water routes; air/vapour/thermal continuity; ventilation or a complete alternative drying strategy; structure and movement interfaces; fire/compatibility evidence; closure checks; and local/major renewal sequences. Roof and rainwater designers must agree the eave and valley receivers. Competent structural/building-physics review and any warranted physical trial remain real evidence tasks. This comparison advances those decisions without claiming that the roof is buildable, condensation-safe or approved.
