# Solid walls and masonry arches

**Working study — not yet complete.** This evidence-led option comparison is being developed for [STR-MASONRY](../../project/str/structural-architecture-programme.md). It follows the [structural brief](../reference-house/structural-brief.md), using the accepted [current house](../reference-house/current-house-basis.md). It selects no wall, arch, member, capacity or revised house geometry.

## Emerging decision

Develop a small family of real, role-specific masonry walls. A solid internal enclosure can satisfy the owner's wish for substance without becoming a bearing wall. Give loadbearing walls continuous support to the ground; give every other heavy wall an explicit supporting floor or wall and lateral restraint. Do not make all walls 215 mm or 440 mm thick by default.

The useful initial comparison is between solid dense aggregate blockwork where space is tight and bonded clay brickwork where wall depth, exposed craft or structural arches earn it. A thick wall does not prove its load capacity, stability, sound separation or fire resistance. Those are assembly and load-path questions.

For openings, prefer development of genuine segmental masonry arches where useful rise can be provided. Retain true flat/gauged arches for the Georgian rectangular opening vocabulary, with particular attention to their horizontal thrust and abutments. A brick arch supported entirely by a concealed lintel is a conventional comparison, not evidence that the compression preference has been realised.

## The current house sets the question

The accepted shell is 12000 mm square around a 3500 mm court. The envelope is drawn at 417.5 mm overall, dry partitions at 125 mm, and the intermediate floor at 350 mm total. All of the floor package is allocated. These are geometric reservations, not selected masonry or floor constructions.

The most consequential occurrence is the ground-floor court-south doorway, nominally 2400 mm wide at x 4800–7200. The entrance-void edge-frame lines at x 4862.5 and 7137.5 fall inside it. [BCR's structural study](../../project/bcr/bcr-structure-result.md) therefore rejects an assumed direct bearing onto solid wall there. A real arch might provide a different candidate receiving structure, but only if its depth, supporting piers, thrust path, concentrated reactions and construction sequence work together. The nominal 550 mm side strips are geometry, not proved abutments.

The source floor framing begins at z 3170, the represented seat at z 3130, the ceiling is z 3100 and the nominal external door head is z 2850. A segmental opening cannot retain both a full-width 2850 mm rectangular doorset and substantial arch rise/ring depth beneath that floor without demonstrating the required volume. A lower rectangular doorset with a shaped fanlight, or an arched opening, is a candidate for study rather than an adopted alteration.

## Wall roles before wall thickness

| Role | Required structural account | What must not be inferred |
|---|---|---|
| Gravity-bearing wall or pier | Loads from above, eccentricity, bearing, openings, vertical continuity and foundation demand | Wall thickness alone is load capacity |
| Stability/restraint wall | Direction of action, connections and a continuous lateral route to the ground | Every room division braces the house automatically |
| Solid nonbearing enclosure | Own weight, supporting floor/wall, head/edge restraint and movement allowance | Nonbearing means weightless, removable without consequences, or free of restraint |
| Wall with stated future capacity | A defined future load case and complete supporting route | Masonry automatically reserves unspecified extra-storey capacity |

A partition must not become accidentally loadbearing when the floor above deflects onto it. Conversely, a designed bearing or restraint connection cannot be replaced with a casual slip joint. The role controls the detail.

## Arch families to test

| Family | Role in the next study | Conditions that matter |
|---|---|---|
| Segmental structural arch | Preferred curved family for suitable internal and external openings | Real rise; adequate ring and abutments; defined thrust route; nonuniform loading and support movement |
| Flat/gauged structural arch | Retained for rectangular Georgian heads | Effective depth and arch action despite a visually flat soffit; substantial side resistance; carefully formed units/joints |
| Semicircular arch | Local comparator where the height and architectural use warrant it | Greater crown height and variable clear headroom; not forced across every domestic doorway |
| Decorative brick head carried by a beam/lintel | Conventional control and explicit exception | Carrying member and its replacement/fire/movement duties remain real; the visible brick is not credited with structural arch action |

An arch carries load between its springings; it does not solve an upper wall shifted sideways into another plane. A complete exterior occurrence must identify the inner structural leaf, the outer weathering leaf, the cavity and environmental transitions separately. A weathering-leaf arch cannot receive an inner-floor reaction simply because both are drawn at the same head level.

## Interfaces that remain live

- Keep services outside permanent masonry. The finished wall, service front, opening mount and first release each consume real space.
- Door passage, closure, seals and any fire/acoustic duty remain OAS questions coordinated with the arch's actual geometry.
- Masonry and its connections need deliberate movement accommodation. The absence of structural timber does not eliminate thermal, moisture, creep or settlement effects.
- Temporary centering and the sequence of building the arch, abutments and surrounding wall must be stated before a construction proposition can be accepted.
- The [ground-water policy](../reference-house/ground-water-policy.md) owns the capillary-barrier requirement. Any capillary-break/DPC planes crossing a pier or supporting wall require bearing, shear, sliding and restraint assessment; an arch-thrust path cannot assume uninterrupted friction through them. This study selects no damp-protection assembly.
- Materials, mortars, finish binders, ties, sealants and movement-joint constituents remain subject to MAT. A mineral wall does not establish a plastic-free installed assembly.

## Evidence being used

Sources below were inspected on 30 October 2026. Their scope and edition remain part of the claim. Detailed numeric and geometry studies follow before this packet is complete.

1. [Brick Industry Association, Technical Note 31 — Brick Masonry Arches (1995)](https://www.gobrick.com/media/file/31-brick-masonry-arches.pdf). Arch forms, structural versus supported arches, thrust, abutments, movement and construction. US technical guidance; no UK compliance claim.
2. [Concrete Centre / BDA — Eurocode 6 masonry vertical resistance guide (2013)](https://www.brick.org.uk/uploads/downloads/d-eurocode-6-masonry-vertical-resistance.pdf). Material resistance, geometry, eccentricity, slenderness and restraint are distinct inputs to masonry design; not a member selection for this house.
3. [Imperial Bricks — standard brick dimensions](https://www.imperialbricks.co.uk/guidance/standard-brick-size-in-the-uk/). Explains 102.5 mm half-brick and 215 mm full-brick construction. Trade thickness terminology does not prescribe a wall here.
4. [Thomas Armstrong — solid dense concrete blocks](https://www.thomasarmstrongconcreteblocks.co.uk/lightweight-dense-concrete-blocks/solid-dense). A real solid dense block family, with published configurations, widths and density. Individual declared values do not prove this house's wall assembly or product availability.
5. [LABC — vertical movement joints in loadbearing brickwork](https://www.labc.co.uk/news/vertical-movement-joints-in-loadbearing-brickwork). Material movement and structural stability must be considered together. Its customary polymer joint fillers are not adopted under the owner's material policy.
