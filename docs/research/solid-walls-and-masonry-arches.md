# Solid walls and masonry arches

**Develop a house of substantial, role-specific masonry walls, with genuine arches at suitable openings.** Use solid 140 mm concrete masonry as the first space-conscious comparison, 215 mm bonded clay brickwork for selected permanent walls and arch-bearing regions, and 190 mm solid concrete masonry as an intermediate case where its product and construction are justified. Develop segmental arches first; retain true flat/gauged arches for rectangular Georgian heads. None is yet a selected assembly.

This study applies the owner's [structural brief](../reference-house/structural-brief.md) to the accepted [current house](../reference-house/current-house-basis.md). It compares substance, support, space and construction rather than assigning capacities. The calculations establish geometric consequences and deliberately simple weight sensitivities. They do not establish that a wall, arch, floor reaction, abutment or foundation is adequate.

## Solidity is a wall quality; bearing is a job

A solid wall can enclose a room without supporting the floor above. It still has considerable weight and needs support and restraint. Conversely, making every enclosure loadbearing can place new loads on unsuitable floor lines or force awkward rooms. The useful ambition is a permanent masonry plan whose important support lines are intelligible, with solid nonbearing walls where the room arrangement calls for them.

| Wall role | What must be accounted for | Proposed use in this house |
|---|---|---|
| Gravity-bearing wall or pier | Loads, eccentricity, openings, local bearing and a continuous route to foundations | Deliberately chosen aligned spines, envelope inner leaves and arch abutments |
| Stability/restraint wall | Direction of action, connections, effective wall length and lateral route to ground | Selected crosswalls and returns; not every division credited automatically |
| Solid nonbearing enclosure | Own weight, supporting floor or wall, lateral/head restraint, movement and accidental contact with the floor above | Other permanent room divisions, including upper-storey divisions only where support is provided |
| Combined gravity and stability wall | Both sets of duties, with compatible joints and connections | Often sensible, but an explicit designation rather than an assumption |
| Future-capacity wall | A stated future load case and the supporting structure beneath it | Only where a real future use justifies the provision |

The wall's unit strength is not its installed resistance. Mortar, unit configuration and orientation, eccentric loading, effective height, restraint and concentrated loads all matter in masonry design [1]. Adding walls also adds weight; it does not automatically add useful redundancy. A wall designated nonbearing must not acquire an unintended floor load when the structure deflects onto it. Its movement allowance must still provide the required lateral restraint.

The preferred support hierarchy remains: align the upper bearing line over walls or piers below and continue to foundations; use a genuine arch for a gap **within that line**; redesign an awkward arrangement; then assess a specific transfer exception. An arch does not relocate a wall into a parallel plane. A solid upper wall placed across a floor needs a designed supporting route even when it is called a partition.

## A small, real wall shortlist

With common UK bricks, 102.5 mm is half-brick thickness and 215 mm is one-brick thickness. Two 102.5 mm widths with a 10 mm joint make 215 mm; two 215 mm lengths with a 10 mm joint make 440 mm [2]. These are dimensional descriptions, not a minimum requirement. The owner's clarified preference for solidity does not require a blanket two-brick-thick wall.

| Candidate | Representative construction and role | Decision for further design |
|---|---|---|
| **140 mm solid dense concrete masonry** | Single solid-unit leaf with bonded courses; plaster or other finish stated separately. Bearing, stability or enclosure role assigned occurrence by occurrence | **First comparison for ordinary internal walls and constrained envelope inner leaves.** Thomas Armstrong publishes a real 140 mm solid dense family [3]. Verify constituents, mortar, actual wall resistance and the owner's material/finish response before selection |
| **190 mm solid concrete masonry** | Single solid-unit leaf, appropriate coursing and bonded intersections; finish separate | **Retain as an intermediate option, not a default.** Forticrete's 2016 guide documents a solid 190 mm family [4]; the particular current product, UK supply, declarations and constituent inventory remain to be established |
| **215 mm bonded clay brickwork** | Full-brick solid wall with a through-thickness bond and filled joints; finishes separate. Develop arch rings, skewbacks and adjoining clay abutments together | **Preferred craft-led comparison for selected permanent spines, expressive internal walls and arch-bearing regions.** Its depth must earn its place in the inhabited plan; no capacity follows from the number 215 |
| **100/102.5 mm solid masonry** | Conventional thin solid enclosure comparison, with proper support/restraint | Useful control where space is exceptionally tight; **not the starting expression of the owner's substantial-wall preference** |
| **440 mm brickwork everywhere** | Two-brick-thick solid wall, before finishes | **Reject as a general planning rule.** Local thickening may be justified by a real abutment or support duty, but the whole-house cost in mass and space has not earned this default |
| **Current 125 mm dry-partition reservation** | Existing planning geometry; no installed stud-wall assembly accepted by this study | Retain only as the dimensional control against which the new plan is measured |

The product distinction matters. The Thomas Armstrong solid family lists 75, 100 and 140 mm widths; a separate 215 mm hollow product is not evidence of a 215 mm solid wall [3]. “Dense”, “solid”, a unit's declared strength and a finished wall's acoustic rating are different claims. Hollow or cellular masonry may be structurally useful, but it is not quietly substituted for the owner's requested solid construction.

For the first clay-arch option, develop the arch and the masonry receiving its thrust as a coherent clay region. A brick arch dropped into concrete blockwork needs a deliberate junction and movement strategy, not an assumed perfect bond. Clay and concrete masonry have different moisture movements [5]. A local 215 mm inner arch/pier region may be preferable to thickening the whole envelope; its projection, junction and room consequences must be drawn.

It assumes **20 kN/m³** for every masonry core to isolate thickness, and **15 mm finish on each face** for the internal-wall area comparison. Neither is a product specification. Weight excludes openings, finishes, loads from above and load factors; floor area is for one straight 10 m wall, without junction overlap.

| Solid core | Illustrative finished wall | Bare masonry line weight | Extra floor area occupied versus the current 125 mm reservation, per 10 m |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 140 mm | 170 mm | 8.68 kN/m | 0.45 m² |

For example, the 215 mm case is `0.215 × 3.1 × 20 = 13.33 kN/m`. That is a load input to a later floor/foundation study, not a floor's permissible partition load. Product-specific masonry density and actual openings replace this common screening assumption at the next stage.

## Which arches deserve development?

A structural masonry arch must carry its assigned loads through the ring and into its supports. It introduces horizontal thrust and needs out-of-plane stability; shallower forms generally demand more from their abutments [6]. No promise of a house without tension follows. Floor diaphragms, wall restraint, uplift connections, ties or a deliberately chosen tension member can remain necessary and should be disclosed.

| Family | Supported leaf/load and appropriate occurrence | Main trade-off and study decision |
|---|---|---|
| **Segmental clay masonry arch** | Internal solid wall over a doorway; or a structural inner-leaf head carrying explicitly assigned loads. Exterior weathering leaf receives its own arch/support | **Preferred curved family.** Give it useful rise and a continuous thrust route; assess the actual floor point reactions, adjacent openings and support movement |
| **True flat/gauged arch** | Masonry over a rectangular internal or exterior opening, within its defined leaf | **Retain selectively.** A nearly flat soffit does not remove arch action, ring depth or side thrust. Wedge geometry, joints, effective depth and abutments must be designed; a flat row of rectangular bricks is not automatically an arch |
| **Semicircular arch** | Smaller internal passages or a larger opening whose height is deliberately available | **Local architectural comparator.** Rise is half the span. At 2400 mm span it consumes 1200 mm of rise before ring depth, a poor fit beneath this floor without a major section change |
| **Brick head carried by a lintel or beam** | Assigned masonry/floor loads carried by the actual beam; visible arch may carry only its own limited duty | **Conventional control or explicit exception.** It can preserve a rectangular opening and reduce masonry thrust demands, but retains beam flexure, bearings, fire/movement duties and material obligations |

A manufacturer's “structural arch” is not necessarily the compression structure intended here. AAB describes products with brick slips bonded to concrete or steel backing [7]. Such assemblies must be assessed as their actual carrying systems. They are not credited as a self-supporting voussoir arch, and their adhesive/constituent requirements do not disappear because the visible material is brick.

For a simple illustration of the pressure toward larger abutments, an ideal three-hinged **parabolic** arch carrying a uniform vertical load per horizontal metre has `H = wL²/(8f)` and `V = wL/2`: taking moments about its crown gives `H/V = L/(4f)`. Here V is one end reaction; total vertical load W = 2V, so the alternative normalisation is `H/W = L/(8f)`. For a 2400 mm span, rises of 600, 300 and 150 mm give ratios of 1, 2 and 4. This equilibrium illustration is **not** an analysis of the circular arches drawn here, actual concentrated floor loads or a flat arch. In particular, substituting zero rise is invalid. Actual masonry analysis must account for the force path within a finite ring, crushing, sliding and the supporting wall [8].

### Inside: a credible small-opening envelope

The current upper `bed2-door` is nominally 900 mm wide, at x 3450–4350 on the wall at y 2650. A circular segment with springing **2250 mm above first-floor level**, rise **225 mm** and an illustrative **215 mm radial ring** has its intrados crown at 2475 and extrados crown at **2690 mm**. It leaves **410 mm** to the 3100 mm relative ceiling datum.

This is a useful interior family to develop: a rectangular renewable door assembly can sit beneath the springing, with a shaped closure or fanlight above, or an appropriate open passage can follow the curve. The masonry aperture dimensions are not clear passage or ordered doorset dimensions; frame, threshold, seals and tolerances must be deducted. A fire or acoustic boundary needs a complete suitable closure, not an open decorative fanlight.

The example provisionally assigns only the arch and wall above to the head. If this wall later supports a floor or roof, that is a new load case. The wall's nominal right-hand strip is 450 mm; it is not a proved abutment. Its lower support and the whole wall line must be checked. Nearby, the ground-floor study doorway ends only 100 mm before its wall meets the cloak crosswall, illustrating why an arch plan must include junctions rather than simply replace every rectangular head symbol.

### Outside: the courtyard opening and the entrance void

The consequential wide opening is the ground-floor court-south doorway, **x 4800–7200**. The entrance-void edge-frame lines **x 4862.5 and 7137.5** lie inside it, only 62.5 mm from its ends. The [earlier structural study](https://github.com/adamnoden/architecture/blob/main/project/bcr/bcr-structure-result.md) therefore cannot assume direct support onto solid masonry there. Its alternative offset frame introduces a 262.5 mm setback from the void edge; that comparison is not an adopted solution.

A real **inner-leaf** arch is worth developing as a possible receiving structure for those edge-following reactions. If its bearing, load distribution and complete floor arrangement work, it could avoid that particular offset. A weathering-leaf arch cannot receive the floor reaction by visual association, and an arch cannot by itself prove that the complete floor has no cantilever.

The current floor framing starts at **3170**, its represented seat begins at **3130**, the ceiling is **3100**, and the nominal door head is **2850 mm**. The 250 mm door-head-to-ceiling interval already contains head/environmental duties; the source represents a 200 mm head depth, not an empty 250 mm cavity-tray allowance.

| Authored circular segment | Intrados crown | Extrados crown with 215 mm radial ring | Consequence |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Current head 2850 used as springing, rise 600 | 3450 | 3665 | Crown passes 495 mm above framing start; this combination does not fit |
| **Lower springing 2250, rise 600** | **2850** | **3065** | Leaves 35 mm to ceiling, 65 mm to represented seat bottom and 105 mm to framing start, before detailing |

With the ring held at 215 mm, retaining a 2850 mm springing leaves only 35 mm of rise below the ceiling. That is not the useful 600 mm-rise segment proposed here. **The promising option therefore changes the door composition:** a lower rectangular doorset with a curved upper closure/fanlight, or a shaped opening. This is a proposed design direction, not a change to the accepted house.

The circular intrados radius is `2400²/(8 × 600) + 600/2 = 1500 mm`. A concentric 215 mm radial ring reaches 3065 at its crown and extends 172 mm laterally beyond each opening edge at its outer skewback. The sheet shows that actual annular geometry rather than a vertically offset curve. **Radial ring depth and wall thickness are independent dimensions.** The 215 mm number in this illustration sizes neither a structural wall nor an adequate arch.

The geometric result earns further development, not acceptance. Resolve the two leaves and their separate loads; actual reactions and eccentricity; ring and spandrel behaviour; skewbacks; pier compression and sliding; out-of-plane restraint; adjacent openings and return-wall participation; foundations; and movement. The nominal **550 mm** side strips have no demonstrated capacity. Tray, cavity closure, insulation, door mounting and renewal routes still have to fit without cutting the compression path or using the 35 mm crown clearance twice.

## The details decide whether the promise is real

**Abutments and movement.** A movement joint can sever the wall region intended to resist thrust; placing one by habit above the crown or near a springing can invalidate the structural proposition. Joint position belongs in the arch analysis, alongside lateral bracing [6]. Do not rely on neighbouring arches to cancel thrust without testing the actual load combinations and interrupted wall arrangement. Roof reactions or uplift/restraint from the selected roof system must be carried by the same named walls; this study does not add a second roof owner.

**Damp protection.** The [ground-water policy](../reference-house/ground-water-policy.md) owns the non-plastic damp barrier and its generous evidence-based margin. It expressly does not demand three independent systems or a literal thickness multiplier. Every proposed barrier crossing an abutment or supporting wall must be compatible with compression, shear, sliding and restraint. Arch thrust cannot assume continuous friction through an unassessed DPC plane. No slate, engineering-brick or hybrid assembly is selected here.

**Construction.** Design the centering, temporary bracing, support beneath it, build order and release criteria for the actual ring, mortar, loading and weather. Complete and stabilise the receiving masonry before relying on it; do not load a newly completed arch with an unassessed floor installation. The CMHA guidance identifies changes in span, springing level or skewback rotation as threats to arch behaviour [8]. Lambs' gauged-brick guidance also shows the specialist setting-out, individual units and close-joint workmanship involved [9]. Temporary timber centering is distinct from permanent primary timber structure; the brief does not silently prohibit a removable tool.

**Fire and sound.** Noncombustible units are not a rated loadbearing wall, and greater mass is not a complete sound-isolation result. Specify the installed wall, joints, floor junctions, doors and penetrations. England's acoustic guidance includes a 40 dB laboratory wall criterion within its stated scope; E2 also excludes internal walls containing doors and walls separating an en-suite WC from its associated bedroom [10]. Those limits must not be mistaken for good privacy or a whole-house result. Fire strategy, wall resistance and closure duties use the applicable edition and transition provisions of Approved Document B [11]; this study grants no rating.

**Services and replacement.** Reserve accessible service fronts and planned crossings around the permanent masonry. Their space is additional to the bare-wall comparison. Do not recover room area by concealing routine services in chases through arch rings, piers or damp barriers. Keep the door mount and first release accessible independently of the masonry. Opening geometry, seals and replacements remain coordinated through the [permanent opening / replaceable door pattern](../patterns/permanent-opening-replaceable-door.md), rather than making a crafted arch dependent on a short-lived embedded frame.

**Materials and delivery.** Bonding mortar, admixtures, renders, ties, cavity components, coatings, fillers and seals remain within the [whole-house material policy](../reference-house/material-policy.md). Mineral headlines do not prove a plastic-free assembly. Compare labour, cutting, handling, centering, drying time and local skill availability; this study invents no price, embodied-carbon saving or service life.

## Decision to carry into the house design

Develop **140 mm solid walls in the constrained general case, selectively deeper clay masonry where arches and permanent support justify it, and a 190 mm intermediate comparison**. Compare local pier/wall thickening with a coherent replan. Use real segmental arches for the first internal and court-door propositions; retain carefully evidenced flat/gauged arches where the rectangular head matters. Reject universal 440 mm walls, unproved heavy partitions on a nominal floor, decorative beam-backed arches counted as compression structure, and side-shifted upper walls supposedly solved by an arch below.

The next paired ground/upper plan must locate every wall role, follow its loads to foundations, and show actual clear rooms, stair/gallery space, service fronts and opening assemblies. It must either make the court arch/edge-frame candidate work as one arrangement or replace it with a reasoned alternative. The shortlist is complete enough to commission that comparison; it is not permission to adopt the illustrative dimensions as construction.

For the book, the useful lesson is already specific: **an opening is supported by the wall around it; a substantial wall makes a claim on both space and structure; permanent masonry and renewable doors need a deliberate boundary.** These findings can inform [Repose](../manuscript/principle-08-repose.md), the opening pattern and [plan/section/elevation coupling](../grammar/g01-plan-section-elevation-coupling.md). The owner's experience of calm remains personal testimony. No universal nervous-system response or prohibition of tension is established.
