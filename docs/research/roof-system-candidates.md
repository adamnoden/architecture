# Roof System Candidates

A roof candidate must connect geometry, weathering, support, moisture control and future intervention. A promising covering on an unmaintainable collector is a poor whole system. Equally, removing a valley can create a higher edge, longer working route and larger exterior water load. This comparison retains three deliberately different propositions for the courtyard house; **none is yet admitted as its selected roof**.

The evidence comes from the [evaluation frame](roof-failure-evaluation.md), [topology](roof-topology-families.md), [covering](roof-covering-families.md), [assembly](roof-assembly-options.md), [junction](roof-junction-families.md), [access/renewal](roof-access-and-renewal.md), [material compatibility](roof-rainwater-material-compatibility.md) and [rainwater failure](rainwater-failure-and-exceedance.md) investigations. Their dated primary-source and product applicability limits remain in force. This is a synthesis of that evidence and current source arithmetic, not a new product approval, lifespan ranking or construction design. The open courtyard and the exclusion of flat/very-low-slope roofs remain governing constraints.

## Concrete representatives and evidence limits

The representative tile arrangement is **Marley Edgemere Smooth**, a concrete interlocking tile, with the manufacturer's listed tile nails and Edgemere-compatible tile/eaves/verge clips (including SoloFix as a listed option), on designed tiling battens. Its product table permits 22.5° and above with 75 mm minimum headlap; the current 25° face therefore clears that published ordinary-face threshold. This is not a site/exposure or valley acceptance. The same page connects Edgemere to Marley's dry-fix and ventilation family. The exact nail/clip distribution, cut-unit retention, batten/support spacing and junction fittings need a project fixing specification; no assumed all-nail or all-clip pattern is adopted. [REP-T1, REP-T3]

Pair it with **Marley Universal Vapour Permeable Underlay (170 gsm product)**, whose page explicitly includes cold ventilated roofs. This supplies a named secondary weathering layer, not permission to omit ventilation or ceiling air/vapour control. The representative sequence is ceiling air/vapour control and insulation → deliberately ventilated unheated space and roof structure → underlay → designed battens/any required counterbatten ventilation arrangement → fixed Edgemere tiles. Actual drainage laps, eave discharge, void continuity and the ceiling/storage conflict remain unproved. [REP-T2] Natural slate remains a separately evidenced variant; plain tiles do not inherit this product's eligibility.

For both sheet candidates, use **VMZINC PLUS Standing Seam, Structural Roof system**, taking its documented **plywood-deck** branch as the representative: receiving structure → plywood deck → continuous supported **Aludex Max** vapour barrier → suitable rigid mineral-board insulation → **VMZINC Membrane** → VMZINC PLUS sheet. The manufacturer's alternative PIR insulation is not silently substituted into this comparison. The product page permits the system from 3° to 70° and humidity classes 1–4; 25° is within that scope, but the building's class and local junctions remain to be established. Insulation must satisfy the stated compressive, dimensional-temperature and fire requirements. Special screws/clips fix through to the deck; fixed and sliding clips accommodate movement without simply crushing insulation. This is the non-vented warm build-up, not bare zinc on arbitrary plywood or a hybrid. [REP-Z1, REP-Z2]

No plywood/insulation/pan thickness, clip spacing, fixed-zone location, structural capacity or thermal/fire result is invented. The actual deck-to-building load path, warm-side continuity, construction drying, valley and high-edge detail, full normal-depth budget and removable repair extent remain admission tests. Moving the same sheet assembly to the outward-only topology requires those tests again; its product pitch range does not validate the new geometry. The shared compatibility record still governs actual runoff/contact and contaminants. Copper, stainless, lead and other zinc build-ups are different candidate revisions, not substitutions within this named representative.

## What the topology change actually costs

The following calculations use nominal wall-line geometry and vertical-rain plan area. They exclude selected edge build-ups, finished drainage inverts and overhang/corner detailing. They are neither hydraulic effective-area calculations nor structural spans.

| Roof plan area normally sent outside | 83.9375 m² | 131.7500 m² |

The challenger transfers 47.8125 m² of roof catchment outward, an approximately **56.96% increase** over the current exterior roof allocation. This changes collection and failure duties; it is not a gutter sizing result. Removing ordinary inward roof runoff does not remove wind-driven wetting, high-edge defects, facade runoff or the court's own rain. Actual overhang accounting must use consistent boundaries, as the water-path investigation establishes.

Its higher court edge also changes enclosure, daylight/sky view and the architectural relationship to upper windows. Those effects have not been accepted or quantitatively assessed. The face route doubles in slope length; this does not imply a doubled structural span, because member direction and receiving supports remain unselected. It does mean the working/handling and long-sheet movement proposition must be checked on the new extent. A high court termination still needs weathering, restraint and renewal, even though it no longer carries an ordinary gutter.

Reducing pitch, lowering exterior eaves or changing the wing geometry could change the price. Each would be a separate authored architectural proposition, subject to the retained constraints and covering evidence. None is silently credited here.

## Full layer and interface admission

The current 300 mm vertical roof band corresponds to only **271.892 mm normal to a 25° face**. It is not available insulation thickness. A complete candidate must allocate structure, support, drainage/air spaces where required, air/vapour/thermal layers, attachment and covering at the actual outer eave, court edge, face, ridge/hip and valley. The first candidate must also reconcile ceiling insulation with the provisional storage deck and shallow void; the sheet candidates cannot hide over-structure insulation outside a roof envelope described as unchanged.

At the current eave the lower roof trace is +6500 while the represented ceiling underside is +6550. This is a located junction question, not proof that an occupied room is impossible. Every candidate must supply its actual wall-head section. If the complete package fails, change the candidate or explicitly revise height/ceiling/edge geometry. Do not spend the same reserve twice or treat specialist referral as a physical fit.

All three must trace gravity, uplift, restraint and maintenance loads through the covering supports to receiving structure and the building. The layered 417.5 mm envelope is not a solid fixing substrate. Continuous support for a sheet, or correct tile fixings, cannot compensate for an unproved underlying load path. The shared material record remains the single owner of directional runoff, contact, guard, substrate and contaminant compatibility.

None of these rows awards a pass. They identify where a superficially attractive family can fail as a system and what comparison must survive before architectural admission.

## Rejected combinations and retained alternatives

Reject the following propositions as presently stated:

- current ring plus an unspecified slate/plain-tile covering, relying on the 25° label;
- any covering backed by an underlay claimed to rescue unsuitable primary weathering or provide independent escape through the same blocked receiver;
- standing-seam sheet plus arbitrary substrate/insulation or a restrained long strip, without that system's underside and movement permissions;
- either current-ring candidate plus the tested high mobile tower in the 3500 court, or an indoor-only confined-space tower treated as outdoor equipment;
- outward-only topology credited with both fewer inward water paths and the unchanged current ridge height/exterior drainage load;
- any proposal using the shallow roof space as a standing maintenance route or treating a roof hatch/ridge fitting as safe working access;
- a concealed collection/compact bonded hybrid justified only by fewer visible parts, without a demonstrable failure declaration and repair boundary;
- a flat roof or unbroken roof across the court presented as a drop-in solution under the current brief.

These are rejections of specific unsupported combinations, not universal prohibitions on every compact roof, concealed collector or professionally designed access system. A new family can re-enter with a coherent rationale and evidence. A conventional unbroken gable/hip remains a control for understanding added courtyard interfaces; adopting it would change the accepted courtyard brief.

## Consequence for the house

Retain the three propositions for coordinated comparison. The first is the conventional control; the second tests the value and cost of a different covering/thermal arrangement at unchanged topology; the third tests whether removing inward roof discharge earns its height, exterior-load and access consequences. There is no scalar winner and no final material/topology preference.

Before selection, the house needs a complete located layer/support section, actual pitch/product applicability, normal and named-failure water paths through the court/exterior receiving interfaces, and credible working/renewal arrangements. Those are **architectural admission conditions where the proposition depends on them**, not a promise that later technical work will automatically approve it. Competent structural, building-physics, hydraulic, fire, temporary-works and workmanship evidence remains necessary for the resulting concrete system. Real-site unknowns must remain distinct from unselected internal geometry.

The useful outcome is a small, falsifiable comparison with costs and failure dependencies exposed. It changes no source geometry, roof constraint, material selection or construction authority.
