# Roof junctions: water, movement and replacement

**Scope:** candidate junction families for the pre-site house, reviewed 29 October 2026. No roof assembly, penetration, fastener, flashing dimension or access system is selected. Read with the [topology](roof-topology-families.md), [covering](roof-covering-families.md), [assembly](roof-assembly-options.md) and [rainwater collection](rainwater-collection-options.md) studies.

The current ring has four places where two ridge segments, an outer hip and an inner valley meet. The valley then ends at a courtyard corner. Those two junctions need distinct treatment: a weathering cap at the high point cannot solve concentrated discharge at the low point. Ordinary eaves also remain important, especially where the court edge is above a door.

The disposition is to retain exposed, supportable and repairable junction families, eliminate optional penetrations until a real need is established, and treat the ridge corners and valley receivers as details requiring particular care. This resolves the comparison for the next access and system studies; it does not declare any current junction construction-ready.

## Eave section alternatives

These functional sections state layer and water directions. Their thicknesses, products, support dimensions and levels remain candidate evidence; they must be drawn and checked with the selected system before acceptance.

**Discrete covering over a ceiling-insulated roof space.** From weather toward room: slate/tile; its battens and water-draining space; compatible underlay/support arrangement; rafters and unheated roof space; ceiling insulation; the specified air/vapour-control boundary; ceiling finish. The underlay laps **downslope onto** its supported eave discharge, beyond the wall's weather face. The covering discharges separately above it into collection. The roof-space inlet, if that system needs one, stays open behind a weather-protected entrance; insulation returns to the wall's thermal boundary without occupying that entrance. The ceiling air boundary connects to the wall air boundary below the unheated void. Neither a fascia nor underlay is assumed to carry the gutter's full wet/debris load.

**Supported covering over insulation above the structure.** From weather toward room: covering; its prescribed support/separation and any secondary drainage; insulation above the structure; supported air/vapour-control system; primary structure and interior. The actual product system determines finer layer order. Covering/deck attachments transfer loads through or around insulation by a designed connection; they cannot bear on an invented solid insulation block. At the eave, continue insulation and air control to their wall counterparts while the weathering layers turn outward to a drip/receiver. Restrain wind uplift while allowing the sheet's specified movement. A cold-roof vent opening is not added to this assembly by analogy.

At the **court eave**, both alternatives require the receiver's operating backup/overflow level, failed-water destination and removal envelope to be reconciled with roof weathering. If primary and secondary water enter the same blocked collector, they share that failure. Raising the collector's back edge, hiding it behind a cornice or adding sealant is not an independent escape strategy. The final safe route from the court to outside remains a rainwater/house-layout obligation.

## Junction-family dispositions

“Independently replaceable” below is an objective to demonstrate, not a claim that a product already meets it. Each retained family must survive the local adverse case as well as normal runoff.

| Interface / candidate family | Disposition | Water, movement and support proposition | Adverse case and replacement boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exposed outer/court eave with removable collection | **Simplify and expose** | Keep covering edge, secondary exit, ventilation and receiver intelligible; support each for its duty. Allow the roof edge and gutter to move as their systems require. | Block receiver and inspect the first roof/wall wetting point. Gutter removal must not peel away the roof underlay or leave an unrepairable wall connection. |
| Concealed or box eave behind cornice | **Accept only with particular care** | Requires a supported lining, actual fall, replaceable termination and independently effective exceedance path; concealment does not change water conservation. | A failed seam can wet construction before discovery. Reject if inspection or lining replacement requires destroying essential permanent fabric or if overflow remains unresolved. |
| Straight ridge: compatible mechanically fixed capping, or formed sheet termination | **Simplify; demonstrate replaceability** | Discrete coverings need their supported ridge/fixing/underlay system. Sheet roofs need compatible folded/upstand/cap details allowing panel movement. Ventilated and non-ventilated terminations differ. | Remove a cap/short element without unrestraining neighbouring fields. A blocked vent or cracked bed cannot be repaired by sealing the intended ventilation opening. |
| Outer hip: supported hip units or formed sheet seam/cap | **Expose and simplify** | Water sheds to both faces. Cut-covering edges, support, lap orientation and restraint remain designed; underlay continuity differs from the primary finish. | Wind loosens a small cut unit or cap. Demonstrate safe local replacement and adjacent fixing access; mortar appearance or a neat mitre is insufficient restraint evidence. |
| Four-way ridge/hip/valley meeting | **Accept with particular care** | Provide a purpose-resolved cap/saddle and secondary routes that shed onto the intended faces/valley. Accommodate movement of intersecting sheet runs rather than locking every direction together. | Inspect wind-driven entry, water traps, cap replacement and interrupted air routes. Reject a pile-up of standard two-way fittings whose overlaps, fixing and access cannot be shown in three dimensions. |
| Open valley lining; alternatively compatible purpose-made tile/soaker family | **Expose where feasible; particular care at ends** | Compare supported lining and cut-edge restraint with a covering-specific coursed valley. Neither is universally superior. Underlay must meet its intended discharge/separation detail, not adhere accidentally beneath a moving liner. | Debris restriction or displaced cut unit concentrates water. Show clearing and replacement of one lining length without permanently bonding all adjacent covering to it. Product applicability to valley pitch remains essential. |
| Valley-to-court receiver | **Expose; independently replaceable interface sought** | Valley water arrives along the diagonal into a receiver with demonstrated capture. Keep the drainage throat and emergency path distinguishable from weathering upstands and movement joints. | Receiver blockage, overshoot or withdrawal must not send water behind lining or wall. Show a temporary weathering arrangement when removing the receiver; a sealant bead across a butt joint is not the default water-shedding design. |
| Verge at a gable or alternative roof form | **Eliminated in current ring; simplify if introduced** | The present ring has eaves and hips, not a free gable verge. A gable challenger needs edge restraint, compatible underlay termination and weathering/thermal closure. | Do not fit a proprietary verge merely to satisfy an inventory. If introduced, test wind-driven entry and removal of the end unit without losing adjacent restraint. |
| Side/head wall abutment or roof-to-higher-wall transition | **Eliminated where absent; otherwise expose replaceable weathering** | Separate wall/cavity water interception, roof flashing/soakers, counterflashing and internal air/thermal junction. Upslope water must pass around the obstruction and discharge over downslope weathering. | A sealed roof-to-wall line alone cannot intercept water descending inside a wall. Require the applicable wall construction and drainage/weep detail; do not invent a cavity tray for a wall type not selected. |
| Hatch/rooflight and its kerb | **Eliminate optional openings; particular care if justified** | Opening cuts primary weathering, secondary water, support and air/thermal boundaries. A compatible upslope diversion, side weathering and downslope apron must be coordinated with kerb, frame and structural opening. | Closed weather resistance does not prove safe opening, landing, handling or replacement. Show each task and removal size before making a hatch the route to a fragile sloping roof. |
| Flue/vent/service terminal | **Minimise; retain only a demonstrated service need** | Distinguish hot flue clearances/fire/support/termination from cold ventilation ducts. Weather flashing and service support are separate duties; each air/water layer needs its own compatible connection. | A watertight boot is not fire protection, a trim collar is not a support, and a terminal is not proved clear of openings merely by being on the roof. Replace the service without tearing concealed air control. |
| Anchor/PV/equipment support and later penetrations | **Eliminate unnecessary hardware; particular care when earned** | Transfer load to verified structure, preserve covering movement and seal each interrupted layer. A non-penetrating seam clamp still loads and restrains the seam/clip/roof assembly. | Check local damage, corrosion and removal as well as initial load. No roof cap, valley lining, timber label or generic fixing is accepted as an anchor substrate. |

## Valley and ridge corner: two different sections

At a **lined valley across the slope**, the adjacent covering ends above the intended channel; its cut edges and support terminate without nails or mortar forming dams in the waterway. The chosen underlay detail either drains onto the compatible valley lining or follows another evidenced system route; the RTA's direct-rafter tile example is not universal permission to put any membrane beneath any metal. Below lie continuous or otherwise system-proven support, then the chosen thermal/air/ventilation topology. Backing and the primary valley member must not silently terminate a required airway. Along the valley, laps/seams and the receiver connection must work in the downslope direction and through the specified movement range.

At the **ridge corner**, the same materials encounter a different problem: water must leave the high meeting rather than enter a trapped fold, and the two ridge runs may need separate protected air exits. Carrying a straight ridge slot through a valley head or covering it with an unvented saddle changes that topology. A candidate must show the sequence of upper weathering over lower receiving elements, underlying water routes, independent support and permissible movement before its cap is accepted. This packet does not pretend that four coincident lines supply that detail.

For discrete coverings, conventional compatible ridge/hip components plus a deliberately resolved valley head are the first comparison. For sheet systems, compare a formed cap/saddle with compatible seams and movement breaks. Flexible membrane flashing may be a supported product-specific detail, but “flexible” does not authorise bridging an arbitrary moving joint. Apply the [shared compatibility record](roof-rainwater-material-compatibility.md) to the entire covering/flashings/lining/receiver chain, including guards, fixings and runoff.

## Penetration discipline

The current roof source and external-access study contain **no selected roof openings or standing attic**. The eastern service geography does not fix a terminal position. No new hatch, flue, vent, anchor or PV layout is created here.

For a necessary future penetration, first test whether its service can terminate elsewhere without causing another unacceptable route. Then locate it against the actual roof faces, valley concentration, ridge ventilation, member/support positions, interior route, openings and maintenance approach. Avoid concentration and complicated junction zones where practical; no arbitrary exclusion distance is invented. A cluster can reduce openings but can also combine heat, condensation, movement, access and replacement conflicts. It earns no automatic advantage.

The candidate record must contain its purpose/owner; position and opening envelope; support/load and movement path; fire/temperature/terminal constraints where applicable; primary flashing; secondary-water diversion; air/vapour/thermal reinstatement; accessible consumables; safe inspection and replacement task; and how an obsolete penetration is closed in every layer. Approve the complete roof/service interface before cutting it. A later installer may not turn an unselected location into a permanent architectural fact.
