# House Design Doctrine — Professional Red-Team v0.1

**Purpose:** attack the emerging publication before its language or preferred solutions harden.  
**Reader assumed:** sceptical senior architect / technical director, MEP engineer, building physicist, structural engineer, QS and contractor.

## Executive judgement

The doctrine is now intellectually defensible, but **v7 still contains several formulations that a strong professional reviewer could dismiss as absolutist, insufficiently cost/carbon-aware, or based on building-type transfer**. None requires abandoning the core thesis. They require narrowing claims and adding explicit checks.

The most serious missing piece is **whole-life value**: every extra void, duplicate layer, thickened structure, metal trough or future capacity has capital cost and embodied-carbon consequences. “Long-lived” is not automatically low-carbon or economically rational.

---

# Critical objections

## R1 — “Longevity first” can become an excuse for material excess

### Attack
V7 ranks longevity/serviceability and beauty above thermal performance, construction efficiency and similar objectives. A contemporary architect could read this as permission to spend material and carbon lavishly in the name of hypothetical future repairability.

A walk-in riser, deep floor zone, undercroft, oversized plant room, spare structural capacity and repeated metalwork all have resource consequences now. Their future benefit is probabilistic.

### Evidence
RICS whole-life-carbon methodology counts maintenance, repair and replacement, but also the initial product/construction impacts. LETI similarly stresses material efficiency alongside longevity/adaptability. ISO 15686-5 provides a formal life-cycle-costing framework.

### Required change
Do **not** rank carbon/resource efficiency as a subordinate nicety. Replace the hierarchy with something like:

> The doctrine's particular concern is long-life serviceability and architectural quality. Measures proposed in its name must still demonstrate proportionate whole-life cost, carbon, safety and environmental performance.

Add a **whole-life value test** for every non-standard provision.

---

## R2 — The domestic scale problem

### Attack
Many strongest precedents—Salk, hospitals, laboratories, Lloyd's, Pompidou—have service densities, operating costs and FM regimes utterly unlike a five-bedroom house.

### Required change
Every transferred precedent must include a “does not prove” paragraph. The reference house must establish domestic-scale evidence through cost plans, prototypes and maintenance scenarios rather than borrowing institutional economics.

---

## R3 — The doctrine risks solving rare maintenance events at enormous permanent cost

### Attack
A tradesperson may replace a heat pump once in twenty years. Is sacrificing valuable floor area every day for a walk-in route rational?

### Required change
Introduce **frequency × consequence × retrofit difficulty** as the triage test for maintenance provision.

A design intervention is strongest when:
- the event is frequent; or
- failure consequence is high; or
- retrofit would be unusually destructive; and
- the provision is cheap/passive to include initially.

This becomes the antidote to serviceability maximalism.

---

## R4 — Accessibility creates other failure paths

### Attack
Openable/removable construction can worsen airtightness, acoustics, fire separation, pest resistance, stiffness, durability and visual quality. A continuous service route can become a continuous smoke/noise/rodent route.

### Required change
Every access pattern must state the boundaries it crosses and how those boundaries are reinstated after access. The pattern template should include a mandatory **“boundary penalties”** field: fire, acoustic, air, vapour, water, pest and security.

---

## R5 — “Permanent fabric” is not a universal physical category

### Attack
Some buildings rationally integrate services and structure: thermally active slabs, prefabricated service/structure cassettes, embedded conduits, composite facade systems. Loblolly House is a useful house-scale example of deliberately integrated replaceable cartridges.

### Required change
Define “permanent” relationally: a layer is permanent **relative to the systems whose expected renewal it should outlast**. Prohibit avoidable entanglement, not integration itself.

---

## R6 — Designing for disassembly is not the same as designing for maintenance

### Attack
Circular-economy literature often optimises eventual deconstruction/reuse; the doctrine primarily cares about maintenance and replacement during occupation. A detail ideal for end-of-life disassembly may be awkward for routine repair, and vice versa.

### Required change
Keep the literature, but distinguish three tests:
1. **maintenance access** during service;
2. **component replacement** during service;
3. **disassembly/recovery** at major refurbishment/end of life.

Do not let DfD terminology blur them.

---

## R7 — Commodity repairability can become commodity mediocrity

### Attack
Standard components are not necessarily durable, open or long-supported. “Commodity” may mean disposable. Conversely a bespoke but mechanically simple component may be indefinitely remanufacturable.

### Required change
Replace any simple standard-vs-bespoke dichotomy with four tests:
- understandability;
- supply breadth or remanufacturability;
- standard tools/interfaces;
- independence from proprietary software/consumables.

The phrase **ordinary parts in extraordinary arrangements** remains good, but it is a preference, not a theorem.

---

## R8 — “Passive first” can become anti-mechanical ideology

### Attack
London air quality, airtightness, overheating, humidity and future climate can make mechanical ventilation/cooling desirable or necessary. Natural ventilation is not automatically healthy or low-carbon in all operating conditions.

### Required change
Keep **passive architecture does the first work**, but explicitly require measured performance and allow mechanical systems to be the correct answer. CIBSE TM59 2026 supports passive optimisation but is itself a modelling methodology, not an anti-mechanical manifesto.

---

## R9 — Physical self-documentation can become obsolete clutter

### Attack
Labels and engraved diagrams can become wrong after alteration. An incorrect permanent plate may be worse than no plate.

### Required change
Physical information must be deliberately small in scope: main isolation, major routes, boundaries and stable IDs. Detailed information lives in the building passport. Change control must update both.

---

## R10 — “Visible failure” is not always safer failure

### Attack
Routing a leak externally or making failure visually apparent can introduce freezing, hygiene, staining, insect/pest or misdiagnosis problems. Some failures should trigger automatic isolation rather than wait for observation.

### Required change
Define the hierarchy as **detectable + safely managed**, not literally visible in every case. Passive pathways are the base layer; sensors/automatic shutoff are legitimate additional layers where consequence justifies them.

# Major architectural objections

## R11 — Georgian/classical language currently contaminates the general thesis

The reference house is entitled to be Georgian/period-informed. The doctrine is not. Any sentence that makes classical language appear necessary to serviceability weakens transferability.

**Action:** reserve Georgian material for Part IV and occasional illustrations showing that the platform can support traditional domestic architecture.

## R12 — Brass risks becoming branding rather than architecture

Repeated brass/bronze edge language is potentially beautiful, but if every technical interface receives metal trim it becomes mannerism and creates cost/electrochemical/detailing burdens.

**Action:** visible metal only where it protects an edge, provides a slip/wear surface, grille, threshold, label or other real function.

## R13 — The “functional cornice” is seductive but unproven

A one-sided slip trim behind/within a cornice is plausible. It must handle actual ceiling/wall tolerances, fire/acoustic interfaces, fixing and decoration. It could also click, shadow badly or look like an expansion joint in a hotel.

**Action:** retain as **Experimental Pattern** and build a full-scale room-corner mock-up.

## R14 — Full removable floor cassettes are probably overkill

Commercial raised access floors prove access, not domestic solidity. Heavy timber/stone cassettes introduce edge wear, squeaks, rattles, lipping, lifting hazards, acoustic bridges and cost.

**Action:** favour local panels or continuous service borders first. Full field remains an experiment.

## R15 — Undercroft is an architectural decision pretending to be a service detail

It changes foundation geometry, thermal envelope, fire strategy, floor level, entrances, accessibility, cost and site relationship.

**Action:** treat it as a reference-house option selected only after a six-way sectional options appraisal.

# Missing professional apparatus

## R16 — No explicit whole-life cost method

### Add
A project-specific **Whole-Life Serviceability Appraisal** using ISO 15686-5 / RICS NRM3 logic. For unusual provisions compare:
- capital cost;
- floor/volume cost;
- maintenance cost;
- renewal cost;
- predicted avoided destructive work;
- residual/reuse value;
- uncertainty/sensitivity.

Do not promise financial payback where it cannot be demonstrated.

## R17 — No explicit whole-life carbon check on serviceability measures

### Add
For each major non-standard strategy compare initial embodied carbon against avoided future replacement/refurbishment carbon. A design that doubles material merely to make a low-risk item removable may be worse over any plausible building life.

## R18 — No maintainability brief with target tasks

### Add
Before design, define a set of representative maintenance tasks and acceptable disruption. Example:
- isolate one basin without stopping house water;
- replace principal ventilation fan;
- add data cable to bedroom;
- replace full window frame;
- clear main drainage blockage;
- renew shower valve/waste;
- reach roof outlet safely;
- replace primary heat source.

These become measurable design requirements rather than “generous access”.

## R19 — No complexity budget

### Add
Every custom family consumes design, fabrication, documentation, spares and future understanding. Set a **complexity budget**: reuse an existing pattern unless a new one produces a material lifetime benefit.

V7 already gestures at this; the publication should formalise it.

## R20 — No evidence ladder for patterns

### Add
Each pattern carries:
- **Established**;
- **Supported**;
- **Proposed**;
- **Experimental**.

And, separately, whether it has been:
- drawn;
- calculated/modelled;
- costed;
- mocked up;
- maintenance-tested;
- built;
- observed in use.

This is the closest architectural equivalent to maturity/status in an engineering RFC.

# Proposed governing constraints

The book needs a compact statement above the ten principles:

> **The principles operate within non-negotiable requirements for life safety, legal compliance, structural integrity, health and environmental performance. No longevity measure receives a free pass from whole-life cost, carbon, building physics or construction risk. Serviceability is pursued proportionately: the permanent provision should be commensurate with the frequency, consequence and difficulty of the future event it is intended to support.**

This single paragraph would neutralise a large fraction of the obvious professional criticism.

---

# Overall verdict after attack

The central thesis survives.

What does **not** survive is any reading of the doctrine as “maximise access, redundancy and permanence at any cost”. The publishable position is more disciplined:

> **Give predictable change enough space, enough information and enough interface design that it does not unnecessarily consume the architecture around it—and prove that the provision is proportionate.**

That is harder to attack, more economical, more environmentally credible and closer to what an experienced architect would actually defend in public.
