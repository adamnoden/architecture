# Primary floors and roofs: a non-timber shortlist

**Scope:** Reference House comparison, reviewed 30 October 2026. These are development priorities and explicit geometric/load sensitivities, not selected members, certified assemblies or a construction design. Read with the [structural brief](../reference-house/structural-brief.md), [current house basis](../reference-house/current-house-basis.md), [roof assembly study](roof-assembly-options.md) and [whole-house material policy](../reference-house/material-policy.md).

## Finding

Develop **short-way prestressed hollowcore floors on masonry supports**, starting with actual 150 and 200 mm product families, and **a tied steel primary roof**. Retain an **exposed masonry-vault floor as the compression-led architectural challenger**, with services deliberately relocated. Reinforced concrete and steel/mineral-panel floors remain useful secondary comparisons. The first task is to reconcile the heavy upper walls and major openings with actual supports. A nominally short ordinary span does not establish that the whole house can use one repeating floor arrangement.

This priority follows the owner's non-timber preference and the house's geometry. It is not a claim that timber is unsafe or that concrete and steel deliver greater calm, durability or acoustic quality in every assembly. The earlier [I-joist working baseline](https://github.com/adamnoden/architecture/blob/main/docs/research/primary-floor-structure-baseline.md) and [floor-to-masonry comparison](seated-floor-structure-options.md) remain useful controls; their former selection priority is superseded for this enquiry by the structural brief. Timber primary structure remains a disclosed fallback requiring the owner's decision before adoption.

The conventional spanning families retain tension resistance. Prestressed floors depend on stressed steel; reinforced concrete depends on reinforcement; steel beams bend; a tied roof uses tension members and wind restraint. A masonry vault needs a demonstrated thrust and stability system, which may also require ties. Direct bearing makes gravity support legible. It does not remove the house's diaphragm, connection and horizontal-action duties.

## What the house gives the comparison

The accepted model has a 12 m square shell around a 3.5 m court. Its ordinary wing is 4250 mm gross, **3415 mm between finished wall faces**. The represented inner structural faces are 3445 mm apart and their centres 3585 mm apart. These are three different source measurements; none selects a bearing centre, effective design span or capacity. The 417.5 mm envelope includes two leaves, cavity and finish, and is not a solid bearing wall.

The [existing structural feasibility result](https://github.com/adamnoden/architecture/blob/main/project/bcr/bcr-structure-result.md) already locates the exceptional cases:

- Heavy upper rear bedroom, wardrobe and wet-room partitions do not all align over ground walls. Their support must be reconciled with the family/kitchen plan, or separately designed; an averaged floor allowance is not a substitute for line loads.
- The entrance void removes a 2275 × 2295 mm rectangle. Its nominal side lines meet the court doorway, not solid piers. A real arch and spandrel carrying those finite reactions is one enquiry; the existing offset-frame comparison is another.
- The bridge band is 1120 mm before guards/finishes. Support, guards and service crossings must share its real section.
- The stair aperture and trimmers remain unresolved. A deeper floor or downstand can consume the headroom already tested by BCR.
- Corner plates, roof hips and inner valleys interrupt any simple four-strip interpretation of the ring.

These are candidate-design duties, not reasons to dismiss the shortlist. A new wall, pier, arch or transfer must be shown on both storeys and continued to the ground support. An arch only transfers loads within its actual structural plane; it does not align an offset upper wall by its name.

The ordinary short-way diagnostic does not accept a full-length unsupported rear segment or a corner plate. The actual supported edges, plank direction and heavy-wall reactions must be drawn together; neither the 4250 mm gross wing nor a long section-cut interval supplies that missing support plan.

## Intermediate floors: complete depth accounting

The current 350 mm floor is fully allocated: **20 finish + 22 deck + 238 framing + 55 services + 15 ceiling**. Holding the finish, service and ceiling functions leaves **260 mm for structure and every remaining upper-floor function**. Reallocating the deck/framing portion is an option-study operation, not an adopted section change.

All dimensions are millimetres. The complete accounting order is **finish → upper-interface functions → any structural topping → primary slab/deck-and-joists → service zone → ceiling**. The balance is a budget for all necessary levelling/camber, impact isolation, load-distribution and tolerances; it is not an evidenced layer specification or spare space. A function may be combined with another only through assembly evidence. The 15 mm ceiling is the current reservation, not a demonstrated fire-protection system.

The 50 mm topping is an authored sensitivity, not an adequate thickness. MPA's composite example uses 75 mm and requires the specified topping thickness at maximum camber; it does not impose 75 mm on every floor [F3]. A required topping cannot be made to disappear into a levelling allowance. If the selected assembly exceeds its column, revise the candidate or explicitly rewind the floor/ceiling/stair geometry.

### Preferred floor: short-way hollowcore, with exceptional framing resolved

Actual 150 and 200 mm families justify the starting depths [F1]. Prefer the 150 mm enquiry first because it preserves the greatest interface budget; move to 200 mm where an actual manufacturer's load/opening/deflection design earns it. Neither depth is selected by a generic span table. A deeper section with almost no remaining acoustic or camber budget may solve one problem while making the complete floor untenable.

Hollowcore includes permanent prestressing steel. Grouted longitudinal joints, designed ties and perimeter connections are part of the candidate. MPA describes diaphragm solutions both with and without a composite topping, and requires allowance for camber [F2]. This supports investigating a no-structural-topping arrangement, not assuming that every untopped layout is a diaphragm.

The supplier's conventional masonry bearing example is 100 mm; this is a coordination comparator, not an adopted minimum. Actual bearing, end tolerances, local crushing, support eccentricity and wall stability must be calculated. Published shared-wall details differ between suppliers and industry guidance, so do not turn one nominal wall thickness into a house rule. The represented 140 mm core must be checked as its own leaf. It may accommodate one designed slab end, but two opposed nominal 100 mm bearings and a joint cannot simply be placed within 140 mm: investigate a wider support, a properly designed local seat or an evidenced tied/shared-bearing detail. A DPC or bearing pad cannot be assumed to transfer shear, thrust or tension without its specified interface behaviour.

At the void, stair and riser, draw plank direction, cut widths, factory openings, headers and their support reactions. Forterra's details include prepared notches, steel trimmers and concrete infill at integrated steel interfaces [F4]. Repeating units are therefore compatible with local steel, but no beam is assumed to fit within the slab depth or bear on a jamb without checking. The accessible 55 mm service zone remains outside the primary slabs; hollow cores are not a service distribution network and later drilling is not a routine maintenance strategy.

### Two retained floor challengers

**A reinforced-concrete slab** is the heavier benchmark for a layout needing integrated local reinforcement, irregular openings or transfer duties. The 200 mm value is a section variable, not a design. Its 60 mm upper-interface balance is comparable with hollowcore 200; the mass comparison below is less favourable. It needs explicit support and reinforcement paths, deflection/crack control, curing and falsework. A monolithic label does not resolve thermal/shrinkage restraint or make later openings permissible. Retain it only if the coordinated plan reveals a benefit worth those burdens.

**Steel joists with a mineral structural panel** are the lighter, more distributable challenger. Knauf supplies an actual 25 mm panel family for use over joists, but the product page does not establish this house's joist spacing, diaphragm or complete floor performance [F5]. The 200/225 mm joist depths in the table are hypotheses for supplier/engineer testing. The 225 + 25 arrangement leaves only 10 mm beyond the held finish; that is a poor starting margin if further acoustic or load-distribution layers are needed. Connections, bridging, floor vibration, heavy walls and any required fire encasement must fit the same envelope. Do not claim that bolted joists can be removed through an occupied floor without a real dismantling sequence.

Conventional timber supplies a useful control for weight, established connections, handling and adaptation. No whole-house mass or cost advantage is assigned here without actual schedules. Steel/mineral panels are not declared plastic-free: adhesives, coatings, acoustic products, connectors and sealants remain subject to MAT.

## Mass is a support question

For a declared **one-metre-wide, simply supported strip under uniform vertical load**, each end reaction is `q × L / 2`. The two lengths below compare finished-face geometry with represented leaf-centre geometry. Neither is the selected structural span. They exclude finishes, occupants, partitions, equipment and roof load.

| Bare structure | Load basis | Area load, kN/m² | End reaction at L = 3.415 m, kN/m | At L = 3.585 m, kN/m |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Hollowcore 150 | Published ordinary product selfweight [F1] | 2.40 | 4.098 | 4.302 |
| Hollowcore 200 | Published product selfweight [F1] | 3.10 | 5.293 | 5.557 |
| Hypothetical RC 200 | Authored 25 kN/m³ density assumption | 5.00 | 8.538 | 8.963 |

A hypothetical 50 mm concrete topping at the same density adds **1.25 kN/m²**; 75 mm adds **1.875 kN/m²**. These additions are separate from the quoted hollowcore selfweights. They are not borrowed from the manufacturer's loading allowance. The product families' own load tables use specific finishes and loading assumptions and are expressly guidance [F1].

Trace actual upper-wall line loads, concentrated trimmer reactions and floor diaphragm actions to the lower walls/piers, through their DPC/bearing interfaces and into a foundation concept. Increased floor mass matters, but no soil pressure, foundation size or total house tonnage follows from these isolated strips. Ground-floor choice remains separate: a suspended precast floor may be an enquiry, but this study selects neither a ground slab nor an undercroft, pump or drainage-dependent dry section.

The [ground-water policy](../reference-house/ground-water-policy.md) requires passive dryness and a substantial evidenced non-plastic damp barrier, developing suitable slate/engineering-brick assemblies first. It does not prescribe three independent DPC systems or a thickness multiplier. Raised service clearance must be coordinated with approach, thresholds, court drainage and floor/stair datums. Every new load or arch-thrust path must respect the actual barrier's compression, shear, sliding and restraint behaviour; a material-name substitution cannot settle that interface.

## Roof: tied steel first, with a real envelope

The primary roof enquiry is a **tied steel arrangement spanning between the outer and court structural supports**, with separately designed corner, hip and valley framing. Light steel roof trusses and purlins are established residential families [R1]. Select the actual family only after framing, loading, connection and assembly comparison; this study does not name a fabricated universal steel truss.

The roof outline is not a supported ridge beam. A tied arrangement offers a credible route without assuming a continuous bearing wall below the ridge. Its tie remains necessary tension resistance, and uplift can change force signs. Chord restraint, in-plane bracing and actual joint behaviour are design duties; connection slip can affect deflection [R2]. Those actions must reach the house's stability system, not terminate at a wall-plate label.

### Account for both the slope band and the ceiling

The represented roof is 25°, with outer/court eaves at +6800 mm and ridge inset 2125 mm. Its roof band is a **300 mm vertical subtraction**, equivalent to **271.892 mm normal to the slope**. The half-face slope length is 2344.678 mm and ridge +7790.904 mm. These source values do not schedule a covering, rafters or insulation.

| Envelope comparison | Primary allocation being tested | Remaining normal slope-band budget | Separate duties still located |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Steel top-member / support depth sensitivity | 150 mm normal, hypothetical | 121.892 mm | Entire covering, secondary support, drainage/air routes and other slope layers; web/tie and ceiling insulation need their own actual positions |
| Pitched concrete panel sensitivity | 150 mm normal, hypothetical | 121.892 mm | Entire remaining roof assembly plus panel joints, ridge/eave supports and restraint |
| Pitched concrete panel sensitivity | 200 mm normal, hypothetical | 71.892 mm | Same duties with a substantially tighter budget |

The table accounts for the full band as primary allocation plus remaining functions; it is deliberately **not** a fitted roof assembly. An over-structure insulation system must fit its insulation, substrate, attachments and covering within the balance or request an explicit section change. A ceiling-insulated alternative locates insulation elsewhere, with its actual eave continuity and drying strategy; it does not omit it.

The represented roof ceiling is +6550 to +6690 mm: **15 mm lining and 125 mm member zone**. Its provisional storage deck reaches +6712 mm. The [roof assembly study](roof-assembly-options.md) records only 254.3 mm between that deck and the lower roof trace at the ends of the shown storage run, and 778.9 mm at the ridge. A roof tie, web, insulation, access and storage load cannot all occupy the same undivided reserve. A tied roof may use the roof space, but no spare truss depth or walkable access is proved. The eave/heel connection needs a located section, particularly where the drawn lower roof trace drops below the ceiling datum.

This makes **ceiling insulation beneath a tied steel roof the first complete-section enquiry**, coordinated with the already retained ROF family. Keep the over-structure roof comparator where a documented system can fit. Some published light-steel roof details include timber edge or substrate components [R1]; steel primary members do not by themselves satisfy an all-installed-material preference.

### Why the concrete roof is secondary

A hypothetical 150 mm concrete panel at 25 kN/m³ weighs 3.75 kN/m² of sloping area, or **4.138 kN/m² of horizontal plan** at 25°, before other layers. A 200 mm panel gives 5.00 and **5.517 kN/m²** respectively. These are mass sensitivities, not sized panel products or support reactions.

The extra mass must find real ridge/eave supports or a designed folded/frame arrangement, then reach the walls and foundations. The current unaligned partitions do not supply that structure. Joint restraint, cracking, lifting/falsework, thermal movement and water-sensitive interfaces add burdens. Retain a concrete roof only if a coordinated alternative demonstrates a benefit beyond eliminating timber; it is a poor first development priority for this house.

STR owns primary members, support positions, reactions, stability and permanent penetrations. ROF owns the selected covering, secondary substrates/fixings, weathering, moisture/thermal assembly and renewal, with the primary/secondary boundary explicitly agreed. The next shared schedule must state field/edge/corner support spacing, vertical and uplift reactions, in-plane forces, permitted movement, fixing substrates, access loads and opening restrictions. None is numerically supplied by the current drawing. Slate or hook-fixing interest is not selection or proof of a compatible roof system.

## Masonry vault: make the tradeoff visible

For an **ideal three-hinged parabolic arch under uniform vertical load per horizontal length**, total vertical load `W`, span `L` and rise `f`, equilibrium gives `H/W = L/(8f)`. This original statics comparison is not a real masonry line-of-thrust, buckling or stability analysis; it does not represent asymmetric loading, settlement or a vault's three-dimensional edges.

| Rise across a 3.415 m ideal span | H / W |
|---:|---:|
| 100 mm | 4.269 |
| 200 mm | 2.134 |
| 350 mm | 1.220 |
| 500 mm | 0.854 |

With an expressly hypothetical **102.5 mm crown thickness**, a 200 mm rise consumes 302.5 mm from springing intrados to crown extrados. Keeping the current 20/55/15 mm finish/service/ceiling functions gives **392.5 mm before other levelling or fill**. A 100 mm rise reduces that geometric lower bound to 292.5 mm, while doubling the ideal thrust ratio. This illustrates why shallowness cannot be counted as a free gain. Actual masonry arches need a justified thrust path and lateral stability [V1].

This rejects keeping the present flat ceiling and service arrangement beneath that vault; it does **not** reject a compression-led section replan. A concrete exposed-intrados comparator is geometrically possible:

| Exposed-vault section variable | 200 mm rise | 500 mm rise |
|---|---:|---:|
| Floor FFL / finish | +3450 / 20 | +3450 / 20 |
| Hypothetical crown fill / crown masonry thickness | 27.5 / 102.5 | 27.5 / 102.5 |
| Crown intrados | +3300 | +3300 |
| Intrados springing | **+3100** | **+2800** |
| FFL to springing | 350 | 650 |

Dimensions are millimetres. At 200 mm rise, `3450 − 20 − 27.5 − 102.5 − 200 = 3100`: the minimum clear height can match the present ceiling, while the centre rises to 3300. The 55 mm service and 15 mm ceiling functions are **removed from this location and must be relocated**, not claimed within the same space. At 500 mm rise, keeping the crown and floor fixed lowers the springing by 300 mm. Neither crown thickness nor fill is structurally sized.

Flat upper flooring creates variable spandrel fill. A declared geometric model with a parabolic intrados and **constant vertical** 102.5 mm masonry offset gives average fill `27.5 + rise/3`: 94.2 mm at 200 rise, 194.2 mm at 500. At an illustrative fill weight of 20 kN/m³ those add **1.883 / 3.883 kN/m²**, before masonry, finish and imposed loads. This offset is not a constant radial ring or a masonry design. Fill loading is nonuniform: the UDL thrust ratio above must not be applied to that mass as if it solved the actual vault.

Retain the 200-rise exposed vault for the whole-house comparison because it directly tests the owner's structural and spatial preference. It must earn its abutments or disclosed ties, upper-wall support, lateral restraint, construction centering, acoustic/thermal interfaces and maintainable service routes without chasing permanent masonry. Vault ends, the stair and bridge, and an accessible route for wet-room branches are decisive. Hollowcore remains the first conventional enquiry, but its tension-dependent prestressing cannot count as meeting the compression preference merely because the units bear on masonry. Shorter jack-arch/tile-vault bays between beams remain secondary unless a located advantage justifies their returning beam bending, ties and junctions.

## Durability, erection and renewal decide the complete system

| Family | Duties to demonstrate before selection |
|---|---|
| Hollowcore | Camber/deflection compatibility with brittle finishes and masonry; actual fire resistance of units and joints; cover/exposure and corrosion strategy; drying and water removal; openings, grout and edge closures; lifting access, installation order, temporary wall/beam stability and a complete support path before loading |
| In-situ concrete | Reinforcement and restraint/crack-control strategy; deflection/creep and differential movement; curing/closure conditions; falsework reactions; fire cover and penetrations; inspection/repair of joints and reinforcement-sensitive damage |
| Steel floors and tied roof | Member/connection stability, vibration and deflection; required fire protection and its thickness; corrosion exposure at wet interfaces, roof leaks and dissimilar contacts; protection at cut edges; temporary bracing; bolt/screw/weld access and actual replacement sequence |
| Every family | Complete acoustic assembly and flanking paths; compatible finishes; identified concealed constituents; service access outside permanent structure; foreseeable local repair and major renewal without unsupported claims of removability |

These are candidate acceptance questions, not numerical performance claims. MPA's installation code makes lifting/sequence planning and temporary stability part of precast construction [E1]. A heavy component arriving at a wall is not yet a stable completed building. Neither noncombustibility nor mass certifies fire or acoustic performance. MAT must review coatings, adhesives, pads, membranes and sealants as installed; a mineral or metallic headline is insufficient.

The sensible renewal ambition is to renew finishes, services and roof weathering without routine damage to primary structure. Primary slabs, reinforcement and grouted joints are permanent work. A removable connector does not prove an entire floor can be withdrawn through walls, partitions, guards and ceilings. Any stronger claim requires the actual dismantling, temporary support and reinstatement sequence.

## Next decisions and falsification

The next whole-house comparison should take hollowcore 150/200, the exposed 200-rise vault challenger and a tied steel roof into paired support plans. Concrete and steel/mineral-panel floors remain secondary where they solve a located problem. Internal work can advance wall alignment, plank/vault/framing direction, openings, stair geometry, complete layer budgets, access and material constituent enquiries.

Before a candidate is accepted, competent review must establish actual design actions/combinations, effective spans, slab/member/connection capacities, diaphragms, masonry bearing/eccentricity, deflection/vibration, arch or transfer reactions, fire design, durability, temporary works and the foundation/DPC interface. Existing BCR and ER-STR-01 routes retain these duties. Physical validation should test a concrete unsupported proposition—such as an acoustic junction, service-access/renewal sequence or unusual connection—not repeat a generic material comparison.

Reject or redesign a candidate if its required upper layers exceed the held floor; its reactions have no credible lower support; necessary restraint undermines an arch/partition or cannot reach stability walls; the stair/bridge or service crossing fails; its roof requires an unrepresented ridge support; or the complete moisture/fire/material assembly cannot be reconciled. These are decision-changing failures. Generic referral to an engineer cannot close an already located geometric conflict.

The [A3 comparison plate](https://github.com/adamnoden/architecture/blob/main/project/str/studies/span-envelopes.svg), [exact data](https://github.com/adamnoden/architecture/blob/main/project/str/studies/span-envelopes.json) and [reproducible calculation](https://github.com/adamnoden/architecture/blob/main/project/str/studies/span-envelopes.ts) retain source-file fingerprints and the stated arithmetic.
