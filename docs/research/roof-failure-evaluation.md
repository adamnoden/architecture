# Roof failure and recovery evaluation

**Scope:** pre-selection comparison for Ovid’s House; evidence reviewed 29 October 2026. This is a research method, not a roof specification, failure-rate prediction or compliance assessment. The [Reference House study](../reference-house/georgian-replan.md) owns its project constraints; this method does not select a roof.

## Finding

A roof should be compared as a water, moisture, movement and maintenance system. Neither fewer joints nor a longer-lived covering is enough to establish a longer-lived roof. The relevant question is what an ordinary defect can reach, how it becomes apparent, and what restoring the system requires.

This comparison keeps **interface, sensitivity, consequence and recovery** separate. The multiplication sign in the programme's conceptual model is not an instruction to multiply invented scores. There is no aggregate winner until actual candidates and their incompatible trade-offs are examined.

## Comparison contract

Each candidate uses the same record. Mark each answer **evidenced**, **conditional** (state the condition), **unknown**, **not applicable** (explain), or **rejected** (state the failed obligation). Never convert missing evidence to neutral/average performance. Record evidence authority separately from the answer: calculation, observed case, product test, guidance, design inference or preference.

| Dimension | Information the candidate must supply | Failure question |
|---|---|---|
| Bulk-water shedding and concentration | Faces, slopes, normal routes and collection edges; distinguish nominal geometry from built falls | Where does wind-driven water, local back-up or a reversed fall go? |
| Primary and secondary defence | Function and exit of each layer, continuity at edges and penetrations | Does failure of the outer layer still discharge safely, or wet a concealed reservoir? |
| Junction type and criticality | Located register of seams/laps, ridges, valleys, abutments, edges and penetrations; count repeated types without equating them | Which one defect has the largest consequence, and can several “independent” details fail by the same cause? |
| Workmanship sensitivity | Critical tolerances, sequence, inspectability before closure and repair of an installation error | Does ordinary competent variation defeat the mechanism? Specialist work is a dependency to price/verify, not an automatic rejection. |
| Movement tolerance | Restrained/free directions, length/temperature/moisture assumptions and interfaces that must slide | Which restraint or incompatible movement opens a joint, tears a layer or loads a fixing? |
| Trapped water / underside wetting | Pockets, counterfalls, absorbent/support layers and routes from wet construction or air leakage | Can the weather face remain sound while the underside deteriorates? |
| Drying | Named drying route, continuity, obstructions, vapour/air assumptions and closure condition | What happens after wetting; is “ventilated” a connected route or merely a label? |
| Visibility / detection latency | Observable symptom, observation location and who notices it; access to hidden layers | Is the first observable sign already damaged structure or interior finish? Use causal ordering where elapsed time is unknown. |
| Local repair / replacement extent | Defect isolation, elements removed, adjacent disturbance and temporary weather protection | Can one defect be repaired without sacrificing sound long-lived layers? Can the repair itself be inspected? |
| Access / inspection burden | Task, frequency basis, approach, safe working zone, support/handling and future access threats | Is the proposed maintenance actually feasible after occupation and landscaping? |
| Service life / renewal relationship | Separate covering, joints, fixings, substrate, underlay, coatings and access hardware; identify what forces what else to be removed | Does a shorter-lived inaccessible layer determine major renewal before the visible covering wears out? |
| Wind / structure | Exposure inputs still needed, uplift/load path, perimeter zones, attachment and support dependencies | Can a locally sound sheet/tile detach because another part of the attachment chain is unproved? |
| Fire / compatibility | Whole build-up and relevant fire evidence; direct contact, runoff order, adhesives/coatings and substrate chemistry | Is a material-level claim being incorrectly extended to the assembly or neighbouring system? |

The questions are the project's analytical inference from the evidence and existing project moisture threat model. They are not quoted regulatory requirements. Avoid assigning “low”, “medium” or “high” without a defined consequence and comparison basis.

## Common challenge cases

For each case record **initiator → path → receiver → first observable sign → intervention → remaining uncertainty**. Locate it on the candidate; a generic “drains outside” answer is insufficient. Retain the project moisture threat vocabulary rather than creating another taxonomy.

| Case | Minimum challenge | Boundary / disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Local weathering defect | One displaced element, open seam or failed flashing under a stated rain exposure | Trace secondary drainage and the first vulnerable layer; distinguish tolerated entry from destructive concealed wetting. |
| Blocked collection | One unavailable collection/outlet path with rainfall continuing | Identify water rise/escape and receptor at the roof-to-rainwater-system seam. Hydraulic design establishes capacity/overflow; a roof study must not conceal a trap as someone else's detail. |
| Movement restraint | A selected joint/clip cannot move as intended | Identify the new stress path and whether the resulting failure is local and discoverable. |
| Moisture from inside or construction | Air-boundary discontinuity or closure of wet material | Trace underside wetting and drying; covering continuity alone cannot pass this case. |
| Wind-related local damage | A local covering/attachment failure | Trace both falling-object and water consequences; no design wind speed or fixing capacity is invented. |
| Maintenance degradation | Fouling or an ageing seal coincides with ordinary weather | Test the plausible compound event; two nominal defences sharing the same blocked exit are not independent redundancy. |
| Repair and major renewal | Replace the most concealed maintenance-sensitive component, then renew the covering | State access, removal extent, temporary protection and effects on retained layers; include construction-stage exposure. |

These are design-screening exercises. Independent moisture review later challenges the selected coordinated system; competent review and physical evidence remain necessary where reasoning alone cannot establish performance.

## Calibration: counterexamples the method must survive

1. **Many laps, local recovery.** A tiled/slated candidate may have more joints than a sheet candidate yet permit limited replacement and a defined secondary path. It cannot be rejected by joint count. Its pitch/exposure, wind fixing and underlay details still need evidence.
2. **Few seams, concealed underside failure.** A sheet candidate may look geometrically simple while restrained movement or persistent underside wetting determines its life. E3/E4 prevent “metal lasts centuries” from answering those questions. Conversely, suitable assembly and specialist execution may address them; the material is not rejected categorically.
3. **Simple silhouette, hostile collection.** An inward-draining roof with a concealed collector may concentrate failure above occupied fabric. Compare actual escape levels and access against an exposed edge, without presuming either is adequate. The current ring is subject to this test.
4. **More backup, worse recovery.** An added waterproof layer may improve containment while trapping moisture, sharing an exit failure or making renewal destructive. Count function and independence, not layers. Removing that layer can win if the remaining assembly earns the required performance.
5. **Long-lived covering, earlier forced renewal.** Compare hypothetical systems with equal covering life but different access to a failed fixing/underlay. Do not supply invented years: record whether renewal is local, partial or requires stripping sound covering, and the evidence needed for its timing.

These are logical discrimination checks, not simulated test results or a ranking of products.

## Candidate admission and handoffs

A candidate may remain in **research** with named unknowns. It cannot be **selected** while an unresolved essential water path, spatial fit, support/access assumption or incompatible layer is necessary to its claimed success. Record rejection, redesign or the specific evidence needed; do not average a safety or concealed-wetting failure against appearance.

The topology study supplies located normal/failure routes. Covering studies supply joints, compatibility, skill and local-repair evidence. Assembly studies supply connected drying/air/vapour/support propositions. Junction and access studies then make the difficult interfaces and task sequences concrete. Synthesis compares a small coherent set under this common record.

The roof study hands explicit collection interfaces to rainwater design and reconciles form with architectural expression. Technical resolution must cover support and wind; moisture review must challenge the selected system; workmanship and delivery acceptance must be established with those who construct and maintain it. Actual site applicability, competent building-physics judgement and any necessary physical experiments remain distinct evidence obligations. None is completed by this research frame.

The inherited exclusion of flat/very-low-slope roofs remains operative. Such systems may be researched as challengers; admission requires an explicit upstream decision. The represented 25° ring has no assumed covering suitability, service life or construction approval.
