# Repose — Evidence and Limits

Ovid’s House uses **repose** for a domestic architectural objective: reduce unnecessary demands for monitoring, interpretation or corrective action. It is not a validated clinical construct and it is not represented as one experimentally measured state.

The evidence beneath that objective is uneven. Some constituent claims are established in building science and public health; some are supported but context-sensitive; others remain architectural hypotheses. The distinction matters because the project should not use psychological or neuroscientific language to make an architectural preference appear more certain than the evidence allows.

## Physical environmental conditions

The strongest evidence beneath repose is also the least exotic.

The World Health Organization’s housing and environmental-noise guidance treats temperature, noise, air quality, crowding, safety and related environmental conditions as substantive health matters. Current England guidance likewise addresses overheating, ventilation and indoor air quality, daylight, views, internal and external noise, logical planning, temperature control and retreat.

The architectural consequence is straightforward: **physical performance comes before psychological storytelling**. A room that overheats, admits chronic unwanted noise or cannot be ventilated does not become restful because its proportions are calm.

## Control

Perceived control is associated with occupant satisfaction in indoor-environment research, though the relationship varies with context and with whether the available control is actually effective.

This supports local, intelligible means of adjusting relevant domestic conditions—shade, light, heat, ventilation and privacy—without implying that every system must be manually operated or that more controls are always better.

Hellwig’s conceptual synthesis is useful here, as is work showing that perceived thermal control can moderate broader environmental satisfaction. The result is a design principle rather than a universal control formula.

## Privacy

Macedo, Ornstein and Elali’s systematic review treats privacy in housing as physical, social and psychological and shows why culture-free prescriptions are weak.

For Ovid’s House the relevant architectural objective is **regulatable exposure**: occupants should have credible ways to withdraw, close off disturbance and control ordinary degrees of social and visual contact where the programme requires them.

That supports hierarchy, doors, acoustic separation and retreat. It does not prescribe one universal plan type.

## Coherence, fascination and hominess

Coburn and colleagues found that responses to architectural-interior images could be strongly organised by dimensions including coherence, fascination and hominess, with the dimensional structure replicated in a second large sample.

The useful correction is that repose need not mean minimalism or low visual richness. An interior can be ornamented, textured or fascinating while remaining comprehensible.

The limitation is equally important: image-based evaluation is not long-duration occupation, and these dimensions should not be presented as clinical outcomes.

## Daylight

A systematic review by Jamrozik and colleagues found mixed evidence for restorative effects of indoor daylight: many tested relationships were null, alongside a substantial set of positive findings.

The appropriate conclusion is modest. Daylight is architecturally and environmentally valuable, but it should be designed together with glare, overheating, privacy, view and external-noise conditions rather than treated as a universal dose-response therapy.

## Intuitive physics and apparent support

Research reviewed by Bi and Yildirim supports the claim that visual perception rapidly extracts physical properties including stability, support and interaction.

This justifies a bounded architectural review question where structure is conspicuous: **what account of support, mass and equilibrium does the room appear to present?**

The further step—claiming that one visual account of equilibrium improves long-duration domestic comfort—has not been demonstrated. Ovid’s House may prefer apparent settlement in principal domestic spaces, but that preference remains architectural judgement rather than a neurological law.

## Claims deliberately kept outside doctrine

Several attractive theories are too weak, too broad or too easily overstated to function as doctrine.

### Prospect-refuge

The literature is mixed, particularly around refuge. Protected outlook and controllable exposure remain useful project questions; prospect-refuge should not be treated as a universal evolutionary recipe for interiors.

### Curvature

Vartanian and colleagues found effects of contour on beauty judgements but not a corresponding general approach/avoidance result. The evidence does not support claims that curved rooms are inherently calming or that rectilinear corners constitute a threat condition.

### Direct neuroarchitecture prescriptions

Recent systematic and methodological reviews show an active but heterogeneous field using varied stimuli, exposures, instruments and outcome measures. Neuroscience may contribute to architectural evidence, but an observed brain or physiological signal does not establish a design rule without a defensible chain from method to real occupied condition.

Terms such as “the brain needs”, “humans evolved to prefer”, “activates the amygdala therefore”, “natural geometry” or “neurologically correct” should therefore not carry architectural claims in this project unless direct evidence actually supports them.

## Evidence hierarchy for repose

The defensible position is cumulative:

1. several physical environmental burdens are well established;
2. control, privacy and spatial comprehensibility have meaningful supporting literatures;
3. architectural interiors can be described along dimensions including coherence, fascination and hominess;
4. human vision rapidly extracts physical stability and support;
5. Ovid’s House combines these into a domestic architectural objective—**minimise unnecessary vigilance**—while marking the step from evidence to architectural judgement whenever it occurs.

No single paper proves repose as a unified construct. That separation between evidence and architectural synthesis is a condition of using the concept seriously.
