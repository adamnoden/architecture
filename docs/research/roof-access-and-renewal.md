# Roof Access and Renewal

An opening that admits scaffold components does not establish a safe place to work. The current house's enclosed 3500 mm court makes that distinction decisive: a real ordinary mobile-tower comparison fits at a low level, but its required deployed footprint at useful roof-working heights does not fit. This rejects that access option, not every possible means of maintaining the roof.

This is an architectural comparison of foreseeable tasks, geometry and recovery burdens. It is not an equipment selection, scaffold design, contractor method statement or permission to work at height. ## The court tower test

For the specified 850/1.8 m tower, SP10's footprint exceeds the court by **572 and 1268 mm**. Both dimensions exceed 3500; turning it through a right angle does not help. Do not omit, retract or push stabilisers through a doorway, or borrow an unverified wall tie. Those would be different configurations requiring explicit manufacturer/design authority.

The smaller SP7 footprint leaves 570 mm total across the court, before walls, projections, drains, access and working space. Its 3.7 m platform would be at +3550 on represented −150 grade. Even the advertised platform-plus-2 m working level is only +5550: **1250 below the +6800 eave**. A 6.2 m platform would be at +6050, leaving 750 vertically to the eave, but the high arrangement already fails its footprint. Neither arithmetic nor the nominal catalogue working height proves the hand position, horizontal reach, force, guarding or receiver withdrawal needed.

The double-width ordinary variant does not rescue the comparison: its SP7 1.8 m footprint is 3242 × 3314, but the manual changes to SP10 above the 4.2 m platform. Even 4.2 m plus 2 m on −150 grade remains below +6800. This is a bounded check of these documented configurations, not proof that all towers are impossible.

The manufacturer's restricted-space LiftShaft700 is a separate product with props/ties and indoor-only limits. It cannot be used to reclassify the failed mobile tower or to declare the open court sheltered enough. A purpose-designed tied scaffold is a different architectural and engineering proposition.

## Components can enter only under a conditional logistics proposal

The current garden and north-court doors align at x6000, each nominally 2400 wide with a source head at +2850. A straight route can run from y12200 through the family/dining room to the court. There is no represented partition across that line; the source does not represent the room's complete furniture and carrying activity.

The manual names an 850-wide, nominal 2 m four-rung frame. The drawing tests an **authored 950 wide × 2100 high × 300 deep carrying envelope**, adding allowance around that nominal frame. This is not a measured packed product, a proof of protruding clamps/spigots, or a two-person handling envelope. The nominal apertures leave 1450 width and 750 head margin against that comparison. Actual frame, leaf, stop, handle, fanlight, sill and threshold geometry must replace those nominal values. The −150-to-0 transition is a 150 level difference; an unselected ramp, trolley or lifting action cannot be hidden in the straight line.

The transport proposal is dismantled components, not a wheeled erected tower. Braces, platforms, tools and waste have separate lengths, masses and handholds. The study does not infer that a long brace is carried horizontally across a door merely from its short dimension. A trial with actual pieces, operators, turns and protected finishes is required before occupation/use. The north room and both door approaches must be cleared and controlled during handling; residents do not share an unprotected overhead work zone. Site approach, ground bearing, unloading and exterior set-down remain unprovided inputs.

## Located alternative: purpose-designed court scaffold

Keep a conventional designed scaffold as a candidate for high-level eave/receiver work and extensive roof renewal. The drawing reserves a **700-wide perimeter working band** inside x/y4250–7750. That leaves a 2100 square central remainder before standards, guardrails, braces, access and handling. These are authored spatial comparison values, not mandated platform widths or proof of capacity. A 1000 × 1800 west-side access bay at x4250–5250/y5350–7150 deliberately consumes part of that remainder; it is a reservation to test an actual ladder/landing arrangement, not an approved stair tower or climbing route. A stair option must demonstrate its own larger complete footprint.

The candidate working level +5800 leaves 1000 vertically to the nominal eave. Its suitability depends on actual guardrail/worker/tool positions and the cornice/collector projection. The 200 overhang and 180 cornice depth in source are reservations, not a demonstrated clear hand gap. Receiver exchange may require a local loading/working bay and independent support beyond a single-person inspection platform.

Every standard/base needs a located receiving patch on the court construction, avoiding drains, covers, thresholds and unsupported finishes. Ground bearing and the court's final layer/load/fall design remain unresolved. North and south door openings x4800–7200 preclude treating those wall strips as solid support. Upper windows have intervals 4650–5650 and 6350–7350: the intermediate nominal pier is only 700 wide. A diagnostic tie patch at south-wall x6000/z3500 lies between the lower door head +2850 and upper window sill +4200, but **is not a selected anchor**. The 417.5 envelope includes multiple layers and earns no receiving-substrate strength, pull-out capacity or load path. Actual tie locations/count, bearing, reveals, cavity bridges, weather reinstatement and internal effects require competent design against the chosen scaffold.

This option remains unresolved rather than accepted. If a conventional designed scaffold cannot fit its actual bases, access, working platforms and receiving ties without disproportionate interventions, return to roof/collector topology and component design. Do not declare the obstacle a generic future contractor detail.

## Work sequences at the located roof stations

The following are design-review sequences. They identify what an eventual safe method must contain; they do not instruct an unqualified person to execute the work. All start with the actual component/defect, permissible weather, occupancy restrictions and a verified access/support plan. All end with boundary reinstatement, drainage/condition inspection and a record of disturbed work.

| Task / station | Sequence to be demonstrated and candidate consequence |
|---|---|
| Inspection, outer eave x5900/y0 and court eave x5900/y4250 | Observe from a safe ground position with appropriate remote optics first; locate wet marks/obstructions without assuming an underside view exposes concealed defects. Escalate to a working platform only for questions that require it. A clear photograph is not proof of dry hidden layers. |
| Clear a guard, receiver or collection interface; four court corners | Separate ground-reachable cleaning from +6800 component work. Establish a platform, control retained water/debris, expose the actual guard and tool path, clear without dislodging roof closures, inspect the outlet/secondary route, then reinstate. A long pole is not proof of safe force, visibility or successful clearing. The south door below the ordinary eave cannot remain an uncontrolled occupied route. |
| Withdraw a receiver / joint / bracket at the low valley corner | Identify all roof/underlay/collector dependencies; provide temporary rain capture and independent support before disconnection; remove the required guard/trim, release accessible fixings, support and withdraw the actual full body, lower it within an approved handling arrangement, then replace/reinstate/test. The actual component length, mass, residual water, movement allowance and tool sweep remain inputs. A cut-up disposal route does not prove the intact replacement can arrive. The collection design must retain the interface and temporary discharge identity. |
| One local slate/tile or small covering patch | Map the actual fixing and lap sequence; access from protected positions to release neighbouring elements without damaging retained weather layers; support replacement and restore fixings, laps and drainage. “One tile” can require a larger strip of disturbed covering. The position on the slope and actual fixing system govern; a nominally durable slate does not earn convenient repair. |
| Renew a metal seam, flashing or flexible membrane patch | Establish the particular material/finish and substrate condition, reopen only the necessary adjacent work, support any released strip, protect the dry side and inspect the exposed backing. Product-specific forming, bonding, hot-work/cure and compatible repair constraints remain mandatory. The shared [material record](roof-rainwater-material-compatibility.md) owns compatibility; access does not make a generic patch compatible. |
| Ridge corner x2125/y2125 and inner-valley strip to 4250/4250 | The straight face is 2125 horizontally and about 2345 on slope from eave to ridge; ridge rise is about 991. The diagonal valley run is about 3164 on slope. These are travel/work extents, not reaches from an eave platform. Provide a designed protected roof-working route and verified load support, keeping both inner/outer fall edges and fragile/unselected surfaces in scope. A mobile tower cannot supply forbidden roof transfer; ridge caps are not anchors. |
| Roof-space component inspection/renewal | The represented storage deck clearances are only about254–779 mm before additional actual obstructions, as the assembly study established. Reject it as a standing service corridor or implied hatch route. Compare accessible underside/local ceiling removal or planned external roof opening, including support, services, finish protection and air/vapour/thermal/fire boundary reinstatement. No such opening is adopted here. |
| Replace a future penetration or roof-mounted object | First ask whether the object can stay off the roof. If retained, identify isolation, independent load support, weather collar and inner boundary attachments; remove the whole necessary object using its actual lifting/withdrawal envelope, and reinstate every crossed layer. No terminal, hatch, anchor or rooftop plant is added by this comparison. |

A designed temporary roof may itself require larger bases/anchors and alter wind/water loads. Reuse cannot be assumed for underlays, clips or damaged backing. Compare that total burden with local repair and a simpler candidate topology. |

## Temporary weathering and renewal are part of the system

Removing a receiver below an inner valley interrupts a concentrated water path. A tarp attached to the rejected mobile tower is not a solution. The actual temporary cover/catchment needs independent supported edges, wind restraint, a named discharge and a maintained escape that does not overload the court, flood the door or obstruct the workers. Primary and secondary roof drainage must not quietly share the disabled receiver. If no safe temporary state exists for a foreseeable repair, the component division or roof/water topology must change.

Separate three scales of removal: a local covering element; an installed seam/receiver assembly; and a major roof field with its layers. Each has different adjacent-work disturbance and handling space. At the four-way ridge and concentrated court corners, independent replacement must be demonstrated by actual fixing/lap access and temporary water control, not granted by the word “modular”. A large sheet or whole receiver may need a different route from the small pieces that initially assembled its support.

Roof-system comparison receives a concrete condition: retain only candidates with a credible high-level court method, roof-slope work route and temporary recovery state at the claimed architectural scope. The current ordinary-tower option is rejected; the designed-scaffold alternative remains conditional. Rainwater, opening, occupation and construction design, supported by competent temporary-works and structural review, must resolve these interfaces before a safe/buildable house claim. No source geometry, permanent access apparatus, product selection, technical acceptance or manual gate changes here.
