# House Design Doctrine — Precedent Shortlist v0.1

**Purpose:** identify precedents that can do real argumentative work in the publication. A precedent is included only if we can say both **what it demonstrates** and **what it does not demonstrate**.

## Tier A — Core precedents

### 1. Salk Institute — Louis I. Kahn, La Jolla, 1965
**Use for:** maintenance geography; interstitial service space; permanence plus adaptation; service order as architecture.

The Salk Institute separates laboratory floors from utility/interstitial floors and locates major support systems in distinct towers. The Institute itself describes the laboratories as designed for evolving scientific needs and the utility floors as a source of flexibility.

**What it demonstrates:** substantial independent service space can preserve adaptable occupied space; technical support space can be architecturally ordered rather than residual.

**What it does not demonstrate:** that interstitial floors are economical for a house. Salk representatives themselves have stated that reproducing its full-height/partial-height interstitial-floor approach today can be prohibitively expensive.

**Book role:** major section precedent in Maintenance Geography, plus warning against literal transplantation.

Sources:
- https://www.salk.edu/about/about-salk-architecture/
- https://www.salk.edu/about/visiting-salk/virtual-architecture-tour/

---

### 2. Molenvliet — Frans van der Werf / KOKON, Papendrecht, 1977
**Use for:** support/infill; selective permanence; adaptable domestic architecture without loss of coherent character.

123 dwellings were realised with a durable support structure and resident-specific infill/facade choices. The Council on Open Building reports that the support structure has remained substantially intact while interiors and facades have adapted over decades.

**What it demonstrates:** residential adaptability can be real and long-lived rather than theoretical; stable architectural/urban order and variable infill can coexist.

**What it does not demonstrate:** our stronger serviceability/failure-management doctrines or a single-house implementation.

**Book role:** principal residential precedent in Selective Permanence.

Source:
- https://www.councilonopenbuilding.org/projects/molenvliet

---

### 3. NEXT21 — Osaka Gas, Osaka, 1993–present
**Use for:** Open Building; central utility shafts; double floors; dry construction; repeated reconfiguration.

NEXT21 separates base framework from dwelling infill and uses utility shafts and double-floor systems to facilitate changes. It continues to operate as an experimental housing platform.

**What it demonstrates:** planned separation of structure, facade, services and dwelling fit-out can support repeated residential change.

**What it does not demonstrate:** that maximum reconfigurability is desirable in a private long-life house; its experimental/institutional context is materially different.

**Book role:** service-layer precedent and foil for our argument that adaptability should be selective rather than total.

Source:
- https://www.councilonopenbuilding.org/projects/next21

---

### 4. Top-Up — FRANTZEN et al., Amsterdam, 2020
**Use for:** ordinary raised-floor technology applied to housing; utility core; layer separation.

Top-Up uses a central core and an off-the-shelf thin raised floor to distribute horizontal utilities and preserve adaptable dwelling layouts. The Council on Open Building reports a later one-bedroom to two-bedroom refit completed without disturbing neighbours.

**What it demonstrates:** domestic/residential raised-floor service distribution need not depend on exotic technology; standard systems can support user change.

**What it does not demonstrate:** the tactile/acoustic quality required for a traditional detached house or the case for full removable finished-floor cassettes.

**Book role:** important Pattern Catalogue precedent for service floors and “ordinary parts in extraordinary arrangements.”

Source:
- https://www.councilonopenbuilding.org/projects/top-up

---

### 5. Lloyd's Building — Richard Rogers Partnership, London, 1986
**Use for:** services outside primary occupied volume; maintenance access; long-life/flexible brief.

Lloyd's states that the building was commissioned with long life in mind and that stairs, lifts, toilets and water pipes were placed outside to make repair access easier and preserve the interior.

**What it demonstrates:** service accessibility and separation can become a primary architectural ordering principle.

**What it does not demonstrate:** that services should be exposed, external or visually dominant. It is almost the perfect aesthetic anti-reference for the proposed house.

**Book role:** short comparative precedent: same service logic, opposite architectural expression.

Source:
- https://www.lloyds.com/about-lloyds/the-lloyds-building

---

### 6. Centre Pompidou — Piano + Rogers, Paris, 1977
**Use for:** service externalisation; flexible plateaux; technical legibility.

Pompidou deliberately located ventilation, electricity, water, lifts and escalators outside its main internal volumes so the interior floors could remain highly flexible.

**What it demonstrates:** removing fixed services from occupied volume can generate radical flexibility.

**What it does not demonstrate:** domestic appropriateness, low-maintenance operation or the desirability of visible technical expression.

**Book role:** explicit foil in “Technology as Architecture”: the doctrine shares the separation logic but rejects the conclusion that technical infrastructure must visually dominate.

Source:
- https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/centre-pompidou-is-transforming-itself/an-iconic-architecture

## Tier B — Technical/research precedents

### 7. KieranTimberlake — Loblolly House, 2006
**Use for:** componentised domestic systems; designed connections; full-scale detail prototyping.

Loblolly House used off-site integrated assemblies and connections operable with simple hand tools. Floor/ceiling “cartridges” integrated services. KieranTimberlake later reconstructed a full-scale critical detail specifically to examine assembly/disassembly.

**What it demonstrates:** a house can be conceived as coordinated component/interface families and prototyped at critical junctions.

**Important warning:** Loblolly integrated multiple services *into* floor/ceiling cartridges. That is a useful counterexample to our strict fabric/service separation: integrated replaceable cartridges can also be a coherent strategy.

**Book role:** red-team precedent for P2/P3 and prototyping chapter.

Sources:
- https://kierantimberlake.com/work/loblolly-house/
- https://kierantimberlake.com/updates/report-from-the-shop-making-a-full-scale-critical-detail-of-loblolly-house/

---

### 8. KieranTimberlake — Cellophane House, 2008
**Use for:** design for disassembly; reversible assembly; empirical disassembly test.

The MoMA prototype was deliberately dismantled with ordinary handheld tools after exhibition, with components organised for reuse.

**What it demonstrates:** disassembly can be tested as an actual building performance rather than asserted in drawings.

**What it does not demonstrate:** century-scale durability, conventional domestic materiality or ordinary maintenance.

**Book role:** precedent for **commissioning maintainability/disassembly by doing it**.

Source:
- https://kierantimberlake.com/work/cellophane-house/

---

### 9. R128 — Werner Sobek, Stuttgart, 2000
**Use for:** reversible mechanical assembly; house-scale design for dismantling.

R128 uses modular, bolted/plugged assembly and components intended to be dismantled and returned to material cycles.

**What it demonstrates:** reversible assembly and building-level disassembly can be pursued at detached-house scale.

**What it does not demonstrate:** commodity repairability or conservative architectural permanence; it is an experimental total-system house.

**Book role:** house-scale counterpoint to the doctrine: highly reversible architecture versus selective permanence.

Source:
- https://www.wernersobek.com/projects/r128/

---

### 10. Arup Circular Building — London Design Festival, 2016
**Use for:** reversible connections; component passports/manuals; prototype mentality.

The project was conceived for recovery/reuse and is used by Arup's Circular Buildings Toolkit as a case study for reversible connections and disassembly documentation.

**What it demonstrates:** the combination of reversible physical connections and explicit disassembly information.

**What it does not demonstrate:** ordinary residential operation over time; it was a demonstrator.

**Book role:** documentation + design-for-disassembly precedent.

Source:
- https://ce-toolkit.dhub.arup.com/case_studies/25

---

## Tier C — Professional/system precedents rather than single buildings

### 11. Historic England — building-services conservation practice
**Use for:** protect permanent fabric; reversible service installation; shared routes; spare capacity; architectural concealment.

Historic England recommends minimising permanent scarring, reusing/commoning service routes, providing accessibility for maintenance/replacement, spare route capacity, and integrating routes with mouldings or other architectural elements where appropriate.

**Why it matters:** it offers the strongest conceptual bridge to the book's proposition: conservation principles normally applied to old fabric can be designed into a new building from day one.

**Book role:** central evidence in P2 and P10.

Source:
- https://historicengland.org.uk/advice/technical-advice/building-services-engineering/installing-new-services/

---

### 12. Norwegian water-damage-safe installation practice
**Use for:** visible leaks; replaceable pipe-in-pipe; drained cabinets; local isolation.

Norwegian regulation/guidance explicitly requires leaks to be easily detectable and not damage other building elements, and recognises water-damage-safe methods such as pipe-in-pipe systems and drained cabinets.

**Why it matters:** this is unusually direct regulatory support for one of v7's strongest ideas: **design the failure path, not just normal operation**.

**Book role:** principal evidence for Visible and Harmless/Containable Failure chapter.

Source:
- https://www.dibk.no/regelverk/byggteknisk-forskrift-tek17/15/ii/15-5

---

### 13. BCA Singapore — Design for Maintainability
**Use for:** maintainability as a design-stage discipline; measurable maintenance implications.

Singapore's Building and Construction Authority formalises Design for Maintainability and has published economic/productivity studies of maintainability measures.

**What it demonstrates:** maintainability can be treated systematically and evaluated economically rather than as a vague virtue.

**Limitation:** commercial/high-density Singaporean data should not be numerically transplanted into a UK detached house.

**Book role:** professional validation for Maintenance Geography and project cost appraisal.

Source:
- https://www1.bca.gov.sg/buildsg/productivity/design-for-maintainability

## Precedent strategy for the book

The book should **not** become a precedent scrapbook. Each major precedent should appear because it tests one proposition:

- **Molenvliet** — stability + change in housing;
- **Salk** — maintenance/service space as spatial order;
- **Lloyd's/Pompidou** — service separation can be architecture, but expression is optional;
- **Top-Up/NEXT21** — residential service-layer separation;
- **Cellophane/R128** — actual reversible/disassembly performance;
- **Historic England** — protect fabric through reversible service intervention;
- **Norway** — design visible, damage-limiting water failure;
- **BCA** — maintainability is a design/economic problem, not only an FM problem.

The most persuasive graphic format is likely a one-page **precedent matrix** plotting each case against the ten principles, rather than giving every project equal-length case-study treatment.
