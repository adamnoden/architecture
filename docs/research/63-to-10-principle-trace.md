# Traceability — v7 doctrine register to proposed ten principles

This table tests editorial compression. It does **not** replace the source register; multiple mappings are intentional. Life safety/legal compliance remain constraints on the full framework rather than optional peer principles.

## Proposed principles

- **P1 — Build for time.**
- **P2 — Preserve the permanent fabric.**
- **P3 — Design the interface.**
- **P4 — Make failure harmless and visible.**
- **P5 — Give maintenance a geography.**
- **P6 — Prefer ordinary parts in extraordinary arrangements.**
- **P7 — Let permanence be architectural.**
- **P8 — Let passive architecture do the first work.**
- **P9 — Make the building legible across generations.**
- **P10 — Resolve technology as architecture.**

| 28 | Maintenance design considers credible failure states, not only normal operation. | P4, P5 |
| 29 | Materials are selected with regard to how they age, behave when realistically wet, can be repaired and can eventually be replaced. | P1 |
| 30 | Essential household functions retain understandable local or manual operation where technically practical. | P9 |
| 31 | Construction moisture is managed deliberately and moisture-sensitive assemblies are not closed before they are sufficiently dry. | P4, P8 |
| 32 | Electrical systems are zoned, clearly identifiable and capable of granular isolation without destructive access. | P5, P9 |

| 34 | External electrical systems use planned, segregated routes and can be isolated without unnecessary dependence on the internal house circuits. | P5 |
| 35 | Maintenance is given an explicit geography: plant, risers, wet rooms, undercroft, roof access and external service routes are organised into understandable paths rather than scattered arbitrarily through the plan. | P5 |
| 36 | Roof spaces and roof-mounted systems are designed for safe inspection, maintenance and eventual replacement rather than treated as inaccessible residual volume. | P5 |
| 37 | Permanent walls provide designed fixing strategies for ordinary occupation so that routine hanging and mounting do not require repeated ad-hoc drilling of masonry. | P2, P7 |
| 38 | Door and window openings are permanent interfaces; frames, ironmongery, thresholds and other shorter-lived components remain mechanically replaceable without destructive rebuilding. | P2, P3 |
| 39 | The base of the building maintains a deliberate, inspectable and drained separation from soil, planting and paving. | P4, P8 |
| 40 | Kitchen extraction removes cooking moisture and contaminants at source through a maintainable external route and is coordinated with replacement air and any combustion appliances. | P8 |
| 41 | Chimneys and fireplaces, where provided, separate long-lived masonry architecture from replaceable liners and combustion appliances and remain accessible for inspection and sweeping. | P1, P2, P5 |
| 42 | Acoustic privacy and mechanical quiet are planned through room adjacency, structure and service routing rather than treated as decorative afterthoughts. | P8 |
| 43 | Acoustic separation uses mass, decoupling, absorption and controlled penetrations; service routes and later fixings do not create unintended flanking paths or rigid acoustic bridges. | P3, P8 |
| 44 | Impact sound and structure-borne vibration are controlled at their source through resilient interfaces, isolated plant and deliberately layered floor and ceiling assemblies. | P3, P8 |
| 45 | Ventilation and transfer-air routes preserve acoustic privacy and do not create direct room-to-room sound paths where privacy matters. | P3, P8 |
| 46 | Acoustic measures remain inspectable, maintainable and compatible with the building's wider principles of access, replacement and commodity repairability. | P2, P6 |
| 47 | Standard and widely supported components are preferred wherever they can satisfy the doctrine; bespoke design is concentrated where it materially improves the long-term building. | P6 |
| 48 | Custom work is developed as a small number of repeatable component and interface families rather than as unrelated one-off details. | P6 |
| 49 | A removable custom component is not sufficient by itself: important bespoke assemblies should also be reproducible from durable records using common materials, standard fasteners and ordinary fabrication methods where practical. | P6, P9 |
| 50 | Repeated or high-consequence custom assemblies are prototyped or treated as first articles before widespread fabrication, with maintenance and disassembly tested as well as appearance. | P6 |
| 51 | Important interfaces have explicit design ownership, and the procurement process preserves a lead designer responsible for the coherence of the architectural platform. | P3, P6 |
| 52 | The house is designed for functional as well as physical longevity: likely changes in occupants, climate and technology are accommodated without destructive alteration of the permanent architecture. | P1, P7 |
| 53 | Modularity is concentrated in layers that benefit from change; principal rooms, structural order and defining architectural relationships may remain deliberately permanent. | P7 |
| 54 | Fire and life safety are established as a whole-house strategy before service routes, risers, attic accommodation and security systems are fixed. | Safety constraint + P5/P8 |
| 55 | Fire-resisting and smoke-control boundaries remain identifiable and are not silently degraded by later service alterations. | Safety constraint + P3/P9 |
| 56 | Security relies first on robust physical construction and replaceable standard hardware, while emergency escape remains simple and independent of software or network availability. | Safety constraint + P8/P9 |
| 57 | Thermal comfort is addressed first through orientation, envelope, shading, mass and controllable openings before dependence on active cooling or other mechanical correction. | P8 |
| 58 | The building is designed for proportionate passive survivability during credible heat, cold and utility-outage conditions. | P8 |
| 59 | Indoor air quality follows a hierarchy of source reduction, source capture, deliberate ventilation and maintainable filtration where required. | P8 |
| 60 | The primary structure favours clear load paths, durable connections and enough legibility that future engineers can inspect, understand and repair it. | P1, P2, P9 |
| 61 | Routine cleaning is treated as maintenance: components that predictably collect contaminants have deliberate, safe and repeatable cleaning access. | P5 |
| 62 | The completed house is commissioned as an integrated building and begins life with recorded baseline performance rather than unverified design assumptions. | P9 |
| 63 | A durable building passport records the as-built platform, commissioning results, critical interfaces, maintenance information and later alterations in widely readable formats. | P9 |
