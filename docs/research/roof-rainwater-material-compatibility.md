# Roof and rainwater materials: compatibility and renewal

**Status:** comparison and shared compatibility record for the pre-site house. No covering, rainwater product, joint specification or network is selected. The [current house basis](../reference-house/current-house-basis.md) identifies the architecture; the [rainfall basis](rainfall-and-hydraulic-basis.md) governs hydraulic questions.

A durable metal does not make a durable drainage route. The decision concerns a complete chain: roof covering and flashings, valley or eaves collection, outlet, joints, supports, downpipe and receiving transition. Water can carry an upstream material's corrosion products to a different metal without the two touching. A continuous length can reduce seams while making movement accommodation and replacement harder.

This is the single shared roof/rainwater compatibility record. Later candidates should add their actual product, coating, exposure and source revision here, rather than maintain separate roof and pipe answers. The comparisons below establish constraints and useful alternatives; they do not certify a mixed-material assembly.

## Evidence and limits

Primary sources inspected on **29 October 2026** (access limits stated where only indexed primary excerpts were available):

- **Z1 — [VMZINC compatibility guidance](https://www.vmzinc.com/en-gb/technical-support/general-technical-recommendations/compatibility).** Also checked [General Technical Recommendations, March 2024, page 9](https://assets.fedrusinternational.be/gen/VMZINC/Documentation/UK/UK%20-%20general%20docs/VMZINC%20Gen%20Tech%20Recs%20March2024.pdf). Distinguishes contact and runoff restrictions, including copper, unprotected steel, timber and construction contamination. Product guidance, not a universal galvanic ranking.
- **Z2 — [RHEINZINK Standing Seam Technology checklist](https://www.rheinzink.com/fileadmin/redaktion/RHEINZINK_GLOBAL/Downloads/Technical-Documents/Checklist_StandingSeamTechnology.pdf), 17th edition, 2025, pages 44–45 and 50–53.** Jointing, expansion elements and supports for its systems. The distances depend on gutter type, size, corners and fixed points; none is a house specification.
- **I1 — [Alumasc cast-iron technical guide](https://www.alumascwms.co.uk/media/7267/alumasc-rainwater-technical-min.pdf), cast-iron installation and maintenance sections; [Apex Heritage product description](https://www.alumascwms.co.uk/products/rainwater/cast-iron-rainwater/apex-heritage-cast-iron-gutters-downpipes/).** Wet-sealed/bolted joints, optional system seals, support and coating maintenance. The large PDF was available through indexed primary excerpts; the product page was opened directly. Longevity advertising is not a project service-life prediction.
- **A1 — [Metal Gutter Manufacturers Association FAQs](https://mgma.co.uk/faqs/), questions 2–3; [Marley Alutec Flush-Fit installation guide](https://www.marleyalutec.co.uk/media/3204/marley-alutec-flush-fit-downpipe-installation-guides.pdf).** Aluminium corrosion/runoff cautions and a concrete distinction between vertical, offset and removable lower joints. These are family guidance and one product example, not interchangeable instructions.
- **C1 — [Copper Development Association, hung gutters](https://live.copper.org/applications/architecture/arch_dhb/arch-details/gutters_downspouts/hung_gutters.html).** Lapped, riveted/soldered construction, compatible supports and configuration-dependent expansion. The indexed primary section was readable; direct retrieval returned an error. US details inform the mechanism; their dimensions and climate assumptions are not imported into the house.
- **S1 — [Aperam Uginox technical bulletin](https://www.uginox.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Guide-technique-uginox-couverture_EN_Web_0.pdf), sections 1–3 and 4.5–4.7.** Distinguishes grades/finishes, contamination, joint processes, expansion and brackets. Its permissions concern named Uginox products, not every stainless alloy or adjoining component.
- **P1 — [FloPlast rainwater FAQs](https://www.floplast.co.uk/company/frequently-asked-questions/rainwater-faqs), expansion, union leakage, lubricant and fixing questions.** Demonstrates gasketed PVC-U installation requirements and movement-related pull-out. Guarantees and colour claims do not establish whole-route durability.

Manufacturer instructions provide concrete constraints but do not independently establish comparative service life, whole-life cost or installed workmanship. The following dispositions are project analysis. Quotes, purchases, installer capability and actual environmental exposure remain unestablished.

## Family comparison

| Family | Joints, support and movement | Repair and exposure burden | Useful candidate role and appearance question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cast iron | I1 uses wet-sealed bolted gutter joints, with a separate seal option for particular profiles. Fully loaded gutter support and sound fixing substrate matter; casting mass must be included in handling and support design. | Inspect coating, cuts, fixings and seals; local unbolting still requires access and temporary support. A rusting bolt or embedded fixing can enlarge the repair. | Conventional painted external goods comparator. HAD can test profile/projection against the cornice; traditional appearance does not remove repainting or lifting tasks. |
| Copper | C1 uses lapped, riveted/soldered seams and compatible support metals. Thermal movement needs dedicated provision; soldering every joint does not accommodate it. | Local cutting/re-forming or solder repair is a skilled operation. Demonstrate how an outlet or damaged length is removed without disturbing roof weathering. | Candidate where roof/collector/runoff chain is compatible. Natural patination and potential staining must be considered with adjacent finishes; no universal patina timetable is promised. |
| Zinc | Z2 permits system soldering or adhesive jointing and requires movement elements suited to the actual layout. Corners/fixed points alter the permissible arrangement. | Keep joints and expansion elements reachable; approved adhesive is still a renewal dependency. Z1 runoff/contact restrictions govern more than the visible gutter. | Candidate with a compatible covering/flashings chain. Grey/patinating expression is subject to HAD; zinc is not an automatic receiver beneath copper. |
| Aluminium | A1 shows a proprietary aluminium downpipe with sealed outlet/nonvertical joints and different vertical-joint duties. Support bends and branches; do not generalise that detail to another system. | Coating, compatible fixings, prescribed sealant and accessible dismantling matter. Unprotected aluminium must not receive copper runoff under MGMA guidance. | Painted cast/extruded/pressed-system comparator; those manufacturing families have different profiles and joints. Compare actual replaceable segments, not just colour. |
| Stainless steel | S1 distinguishes grade, finish, soldering and welding suitability; tin-coated and uncoated products are not interchangeable. Movement and brackets remain designed interfaces. | Prevent carbon-steel contamination; use the product's joint process and flux. Grade/exposure selection and local repair skill remain explicit. | Conditional collector/outlet or coordinated system candidate where its details earn the role. Do not equate “stainless” with maintenance-free or universally compatible; matt/tinned appearance needs HAD agreement. |
| Suitable PVC-U system | P1 requires insertion to movement marks, appropriate seal lubrication and product support spacing. A nominal push fit can pull out if movement is mishandled. | Demonstrate seal renewal, unclipping and segment withdrawal. Confirm declared weathering, temperature and impact suitability for the actual exposed position; colour guarantee is not structural evidence. | Ordinary lightweight external-system comparator. Profile, colour and visible ageing remain owner/HAD questions; the study neither bans polymer on taste nor accepts it everywhere. |

No family wins by an invented lifespan, price or weighted score. Compare the access and replacement task against a well-executed conventional installation. A small number of standard, accessible components is preferable only where the actual water and failure paths remain satisfactory.

## Shared compatibility record

These are **candidate interface cases**, not adopted house assemblies. Direction is essential. “Conditional” means the named missing evidence must be supplied before selection.

| Case / directional chain | Disposition and reason | Required next evidence / owner |
|---|---|---|
| Copper roof, flashing or discharge → zinc collector | **Exclude this runoff pairing** under Z1. Physical separation alone does not remove transported runoff. | ROF/RWT change upstream material or receiving system; verify the entire alternate chain, including fixings. |
| Copper runoff → exposed/unprotected aluminium | **Exclude this pairing** under A1. A generic paint or separator promise is not proof for cut edges, joints or damage. | Any coated-system alternative requires its manufacturer's actual exposure/edge/joint evidence; otherwise choose another chain. |
| Bituminous membrane, acidic timber/treatment or construction contaminant → zinc | **Exclude the incompatible contact/runoff listed in Z1:** bituminous membranes, acidic woods such as oak/red cedar, metal-salt treatments, acetic silicone and acidic cleaners. Keep gypsum/limestone cutting dust off unprotected zinc; this is distinct from acceptable limestone runoff. | ROF identifies actual underlay, substrate, treatment and exposure path; RWT reconciles the receiver. Do not infer that all polymers, membranes or timber are incompatible. |
| Zinc roof/flashings → zinc goods with its system supports | **Conditional comparator.** Same metal does not settle jointing, movement, coatings, substrate or contaminated runoff. | ROF/RWT instantiate Z1/Z2 constraints, including timber, membrane and construction-product exposure; DEL records installation controls. |
| Copper covering → compatible copper goods/supports | **Conditional comparator.** Coherent material family leaves solder quality, expansion and replacement unresolved. | ROF/RWT locate fixed/sliding points and replacement breaks; HAD checks appearance; competent detail/installer evidence follows. |
| Mineral tile/slate covering + metal flashings → painted iron or aluminium goods | **Conditional comparator.** “Tile roof” omits the flashings, valley lining, treatments and fixing metals that water actually encounters. | ROF lists every upstream exposed material; RWT checks receiver/coating/joints with its supplier evidence before selection. |
| Mixed roof runoff → specified stainless collector | **Conditional, product-specific.** S1 supports certain Uginox combinations but excludes unprotected carbon-steel contamination; it cannot approve another alloy or protect a less-resistant adjacent material by implication. | Specify grade/finish, all adjoining metals, flux/cleaning process and runoff exposure; reconcile both manufacturers' restrictions. |
| Roof runoff → PVC-U goods or elastomeric transition | **Conditional.** Avoids treating the pipe wall as a galvanic metal pair but does not prove chemical, thermal, UV, seal or movement compatibility. | Named system, exposure limits, compatible seal/lubricant and accessible replacement; check metal fasteners separately. |
| Any collector → outlet/adaptor → different pipe material | **Conditional interface**, even where each component works alone. Rigid restraints, wrong sealants or inaccessible sockets can defeat otherwise compatible materials. | RWT provides actual connection detail, relative movement, support/withdrawal sequence and tested sealing duty; no improvised mixed-brand fit. |

A candidate entry must identify: upstream and downstream product/finish; normal and failed runoff; direct contact or intervening separation; fixings/support substrate, guard/strainer materials and removable supports; wetting/drying and contaminants; fixed/moving points; joint consumables; accessible repair extent; primary evidence and unresolved owner. A guard also introduces contacts and a possible independent blockage; its compatibility does not establish its hydraulic effect. A separator must itself remain suitable and continuous through installation and renewal. It cannot cure incompatible water arriving from above.

## Applying the comparison to the house

The represented inward/outward pitched ring concentrates water at inner valleys as well as along inner eaves. A courtyard collector therefore cannot be chosen from a facade colour chart. Roof and rainwater design must trace each covering/valley/collector/outlet chain, including secondary or failed-flow paths, before selecting metals. The current drawings do not prove either safe access or a viable court-to-outside route.

For an outer exposed drop, compare an accessible conventional painted metal system with a suitable standard polymer system; retain copper/zinc/stainless alternatives where the coupled roof and architectural case earns them. For a court collector, first establish overflow consequence, safe working position and segment withdrawal. A chemically compatible material cannot rescue a trapped, inaccessible route.

A meaningful renewal comparison identifies one failed joint or damaged segment, its first visible symptom, temporary support, tools, clearance to lift/unclip/cut, protection of adjacent roof weathering, reinstatement and inspection. The Alutec example's removable lower connection shows why this needs an actual detail; it does not prove that every downpipe can be lifted out. Soldered or adhesive work must state whether repair consumes the joint or adjoining length. Check supply continuity and achievable replacement profiles rather than assume perpetual interchangeability.

Keep thermal movement distinct from building movement. State the installation condition, exposed length, relevant temperature range, fixed points and permissible sliding at each joint. Manufacturer examples demonstrate why movement matters; they do not supply one universal gap or bracket spacing for this ring. Joint watertightness, capacity, loaded supports, fire interfaces and safe access remain separate design/evidence duties. Materials comparison earns neither hydraulic certification nor construction approval.
