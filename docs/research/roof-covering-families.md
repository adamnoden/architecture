# Roof covering families: seams, movement and recovery

**Status:** comparative research for the pre-site house; no covering, pitch, assembly or supplier selected. The [roof evaluation frame](roof-failure-evaluation.md) and [topology comparison](roof-topology-families.md) govern this study. The current 25° ring is provisional, and the no-flat-roof constraint remains operative. Studying low-slope waterproofing does not repeal it.

The useful comparison is the complete weathering task: how water crosses a face and its junctions, how the covering moves and is fixed, what supports it, and what must be disturbed when it fails. A durable sheet or stone can outlast inaccessible fixings, wet substrates or critical flashings. The three studies below retain separate mechanisms rather than award one material a reliability score.

## Study A — slate, clay and concrete tile

These are water-shedding coverings with laps and discrete fixings, not a waterproof basin. The face, cut pieces, hips, ridges and valleys all need compatible details. Underlay is a secondary managed layer whose discharge must be designed; it cannot excuse an unsuitable covering or missing lap.

| Family | Critical interfaces and sensitivity | Damage, local repair and renewal | House implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Natural slate | Headlap/side coverage, slate dimensions, holes/hooks, battens and weather exposure interact. A strong slate does not preserve a corroding nail or decayed support (S1/S3). | A broken unit can be replaced using an appropriate fixing method, but reaching concealed battens or renewing underlay disturbs a larger area. Salvage needs sound material and careful removal; it is not guaranteed. | A useful conventional comparator only after a real slate/fixing/lap system fits the proposed pitch and exposure. Narrow cut pieces beside four inner valleys require their own fixing and water-path detail. |
| Clay or concrete plain tiles | Repeated double laps, gauge, nibs/pegs/mechanical fixings and roof pitch matter (S2). Curvature/profile and small dimensional differences affect actual coverage. | Individual units may be replaceable; access to nails/clips and adjacent courses must be demonstrated. A decorative ridge does not remove its independent fixing/weathering duty. | The ordinary plain-tile family cannot simply be labelled suitable at the drawn 25°. Raise/reconsider geometry or establish a specifically supported alternative. |
| Interlocking clay/concrete tiles | Profiled side/head interlocks can support different pitch ranges, but cut edges and junctions interrupt that protection. S2 requires product/site fixing design rather than weight alone. | Larger modules reduce unit count but make individual damage/removal different. Replacement depends on matching profile, fixing release and viable surrounding units. | A plausible pitched comparator, not permission to transfer one manufacturer's low-pitch claim to all interlocking tiles or to valleys. |

**Concrete eligibility counterexample:** S1 lists 500 × 250 mm slate at 25° with 95 mm headlap in its sheltered/moderate table, but no entry at that pitch in its severe/very-severe table. Its limits include eaves height and rafter length. These are source examples, not the house exposure or specification. S2 lists concrete plain tile at 35°, clay examples at 30–40°, and some interlocking products below 25°. Thus the same drawing can admit one particular system and reject another; neither “slate” nor “tile” supplies the missing evidence.

Local replacement remains attractive, but it must include a safe working position, a way to release the fixing, a replacement supply strategy and reinstatement of the secondary layer when disturbed. S3's warning against remedial underside foam illustrates how a superficially simple patch can consume inspectability and reuse. It does not prohibit purpose-designed insulation assemblies.

## Study B — sheet metal

Separate the metal from the seam type, bay size, fixed/sliding restraint, substrate and underside environment. A standing seam sheds water differently from a soldered or welded waterproof joint; a minimum face pitch does not certify a valley, submerged seam or blocked receiver. The current inner valley line is about 18.25° for the ideal 25° faces, as derived in ROF-02, and needs its own covering/lining design.

| Family | Movement, support and workmanship dependency | Recovery proposition and rejection trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Lead sheet | Formable bays and laps can accommodate complex junctions, but excessive piece size or restraint causes fatigue damage (METAL2). METAL1 demonstrates underside corrosion aggravated by condensation and acidic substrates. Continuous support, separation and drying are coupled duties. | Lead-welded local patches or individual sheet renewal are established repair concepts (S3); competent execution and fire controls remain necessary. Reject an alleged long-life roof whose underside cannot be kept suitable or whose bay replacement destroys adjacent weathering. |
| Copper | METAL3 identifies fixed-cleat cycling, pan strength and seam lock-up as distinct problems. Fewer transverse joints can increase movement demands at ends and restraints. | Repair may require opening/re-forming seams or cutting and joining a patch. Require an actual replacement boundary and installation access; do not promise that an arbitrarily long pan is locally removable. Downstream runoff restrictions are independent of apparent visual compatibility. |
| Zinc | METAL4's backing/coating and substrate permissions distinguish warm and cold build-ups; correct clips, continuous support and keeping construction dry matter. One vent gap or membrane cannot be copied into a different assembly. | A damaged pan and inaccessible clip are different renewal tasks. Reject a bare-zinc/plywood substitution or a hybrid build-up unsupported by the actual system, even if its visible roof matches the preferred silhouette. |
| Stainless steel | Named grade/finish and joint process matter. METAL5 requires surface preparation, controlled joining and flux removal; a soldered joint has different strength from a welded one. | Access for tooling, heat and cleaning must survive the design. Do not infer maintenance-free performance or universal mixed-metal compatibility from the word stainless. Full product literature is needed before selecting folded/soldered/welded roof details. |

Coated aluminium and coated-steel sheet systems remain related alternatives, not silently interchangeable versions of these four. Their coating, cut-edge protection, fixing/seal arrangements, underside exposure and replacement system need a named candidate investigation if advanced. No such product is selected here.

The **[shared roof/rainwater compatibility record](roof-rainwater-material-compatibility.md)** owns directional material cases, including runoff, direct contact, substrate contamination, fixings and guards. ROF must add actual covering/flashings/valley products to that record when known; it must not create a conflicting generic galvanic chart. The present study supplies no new mixed-material permission.

## Study C — flexible waterproofing and low-slope families

These families remain research comparators under the no-flat-roof constraint. A membrane can be used in a pitched detail, but neither the material name nor a low minimum slope earns a flat-roof rewind. Conversely, admitting a product to a low-slope application does not establish safe ponding, drainage, outlet or threshold behaviour.

| Family | Seam, substrate and installation dependency | Local repair/renewal question |
|---|---|---|
| PVC/FPO single ply | Thermoplastic seams can be welded; F1 distinguishes chemistries and compatibility, while F2 shows product-specific hot-air preparation. Attachment, perimeter restraint and penetrations remain separate from field-seam quality. | Can the aged surface be identified, prepared and joined to a compatible patch? Where is moisture below it located and removed? A neat weld cannot prove a dry or stable supporting build-up. |
| EPDM | F3 uses specific primers, seam tapes and formed accessories. Large factory sheets can reduce field seams but leave edges, corners, tie-ins and outlets dependent on preparation and accessory installation. | Demonstrate a compatible patch and the working area needed around a drain/penetration. Replacing a body beneath an adhered flashing is more than adding tape over the visible symptom. |
| Reinforced bitumen | F4 distinguishes heat-applied and self-adhesive products. Multiple layers add joint/bonding operations; they are not automatically independent backups where one movement or wet substrate defeats both. | Define whether a repair is a sound local bond or an overlay concealing a wet assembly. Renewal requires condition evidence, removal extent and a viable treatment of edges/outlets. Installation and repair fire risk depend on the actual method. |
| Reinforced liquid-applied | Eliminating sheet laps transfers sensitivity to substrate preparation, adhesion, reinforcement, application conditions and achieved film. F5 requires specific reinforcement in some cases and excludes steep slopes and permanent immersion for that product. | Can a sound repair perimeter and compatible recoat be established, including thickness and cure? A seamless-looking surface does not prove intact reinforcement at a moving joint or make every resin suitable for every slope. |

A local puncture in an exposed, accessible membrane may be repairable. Concealed migration, trapped wet insulation or inaccessible terminations can enlarge that intervention. Those are candidate challenge cases, not asserted failure rates. Attachment, insulation/deck compatibility and fire evidence concern the complete specified assembly; a membrane's product classification cannot approve the whole roof.

## Candidate handoff and falsification

Carry forward conventional pitched slate or suitable tile and supported sheet-metal alternatives as **conditional families**, alongside the bounded flexible-system comparison. There is no winner without ROF-04 assembly work and ROF-05 junction design. A material essential to fitting the roof cannot be selected on the assumption that its missing proof will appear later.

Each actual candidate must supply: face and valley pitch; product/finish; seam/lap orientation and dimensions; substrate and secondary drainage; fixed/moving points; fixing/wind evidence; full normal and failed runoff chain; critical junctions; one credible local repair and major renewal sequence; installation/inspection controls; and unresolved evidence that could defeat selection. Use the thirteen-dimension ROF-01 record rather than a joint-count score.

For the house, test at least a broken unit or puncture near an inner valley, a blocked court receiver wetting the roof edge, differential movement at the cornice, and concealed wetting beneath an apparently sound covering. Establish whether the repair can be performed from an actual working position without creating a new route into the courtyard. Do not treat a valley lining, collector and roof pan as one replaceable object unless that sequence has been shown.

ROF-04 owns the plausible supporting/drying topology; ROF-05 the located junctions; ROF-06 the access/renewal sequences; RWT the collectors and shared compatibility record; HAD the resulting height, silhouette and visible material; BCR/EPR/PHY/DEL the technical/workmanship evidence as appropriate. Competent structural/building-physics judgement, current applicable system evidence and actual site exposure remain outstanding. This comparison changes no geometry, maturity, constraint or construction-readiness claim.
