# P03 — Structural Floor Edge Test Protocol

**Status:** pre-test protocol; blocked on competent engineering geometry  
**Date:** 2026-10-12  
**Engineering brief:** [P03 Structural Floor Edge Engineering Brief](p03-floor-edge-engineering-brief.md)  
**Programme:** [Tectonic Prototype and Engineering Programme](../development/tectonic-prototype-programme.md)

P03 does not begin by loading an invented floor edge until it breaks. It begins by asking what evidence a physical specimen can legitimately add after engineering has selected credible connection families.

The protocol therefore has two stages:

- **P03-A — engineered option reduction**;
- **P03-B — full-scale junction evidence**.

P03-B remains blocked until P03-A is signed off by a competent structural engineer.

---

## 1. P03-A — engineered option reduction

Use the engineering brief to compare:

- conventional certified restraint-type hanger — control;
- direct/seated bearing + separate restraint — comparator;
- shoe/ledge or other bespoke family — challenger only if earned.

Required P03-A record:

```text
engineer / competence
→ assumptions and applicable design basis
→ selected joist and masonry families
→ connection options analysed
→ option(s) rejected and reason
→ option(s) released to specimen
→ structural properties the specimen can test
→ structural properties that remain calculation/certification only
→ any architectural rewind
```

### P03-A stop conditions

Do not build the structural specimen if:

- load path is not defined;
- the joist depth/family is still arbitrary;
- wall support geometry is unresolved;
- the engineer cannot state what the specimen would prove;
- a candidate already loses clearly to the conventional control on engineering grounds.

A paper rejection is a valid result.

---

## 2. P03-B specimen geometry

Once released by engineering, construct a **representative full-depth floor-edge bay** rather than an isolated connector coupon.

Minimum assembly should include:

- representative loadbearing masonry inner-leaf analogue or real masonry test wall as specified by engineer;
- **at least two adjacent joists** so blocking/restraint/edge behaviour is represented;
- full joist depth and selected centres;
- representative structural deck over a sufficient length to engage edge/diaphragm detailing;
- selected support/hanger/bearing connection;
- wall-restraint components relevant to the option;
- representative ceiling/fire/acoustic edge closure where safe/practical;
- representative internal wall/ceiling finish edge sufficient to coordinate with P04;
- declared service-free protected structural zone.

Use the Reference House **4.2 m gross-span edge condition** as the governing project geometry where the engineer judges it representative. A shortened specimen may be used for connection/assembly evidence, but it must not be described as reproducing full-span floor behaviour.

---

## 3. Control and challenger discipline

Every non-standard family must be compared with a strong conventional control under as similar conditions as practicable.

Record for each:

- component count;
- permanent masonry fixings;
- installation sequence;
- adjustment/tolerance operations;
- specialist tooling;
- time to inspect the completed junction;
- components realistically repairable from occupied side / edge access;
- components trapped permanently;
- fire/acoustic closure complexity;
- predicted future reproducibility.

The challenger fails the whole-life test if its only advantage is that one metal piece can theoretically be unscrewed while the primary floor remains impossible to access or remove.

---

## 4. Dimensional / tolerance trial

Before closure, impose one or more **engineer-approved representative construction deviations** within the declared tolerance range.

Examples may include:

- support datum slightly high/low;
- wall face locally out of true;
- joist-end length variation;
- normal masonry-joint dimensional variation.

The installer should receive only the intended drawings/instructions and permitted adjustment method.

Record:

- whether the assembly still reaches datum;
- any improvised packers/fillers/holes;
- whether the deviation becomes visible before closure;
- whether correction preserves structural and boundary duties.

Do not invent deviation magnitudes in the workshop. P03-A must define them.

---

## 5. Installation / comprehension trial

Where practicable, have an **ordinary competent installer who did not design the detail** assemble at least the selected connection or closure sequence.

Record:

- questions asked;
- misunderstood sequence;
- rework;
- hidden judgement calls;
- additional materials introduced;
- whether the installation can be inspected before concealment.

A connection that works only when its designer personally supervises every step fails the House Kernel robustness test even if the calculation is correct.

---

## 6. Structural loading

### Default rule

**No workshop proof load is interpreted as structural validation unless the competent structural engineer defines the load case, rig, instrumentation, acceptance limits and safety method.**

A non-engineered load test can be useful only as an assembly demonstration and must be labelled as such.

### If engineer-defined loading is included

Record at minimum:

- loading arrangement;
- load increments / duration;
- deflection measurements;
- slip / rotation at connection;
- distress / cracking / connector deformation;
- unload recovery;
- test limitations.

The engineer, not the project narrative, decides what conclusion follows.

---

## 7. Inspection / local-repair trial

After assembly and any permitted testing, inspect the junction as a future building professional would.

Ask:

1. Can the load path be understood without destructive opening?
2. Which steel/timber/masonry interfaces can actually be checked?
3. Can a damaged local connector/blocking/closure component be repaired without masonry demolition?
4. Does repair require removing unrelated finishes or services?
5. Does the detail preserve a clear no-drill zone?

Where a local component is claimed replaceable, perform **one real replacement/reinstatement cycle** and record collateral removal.

Do not claim primary joist replaceability unless the specimen demonstrates a credible whole-assembly withdrawal route.

---

## 8. Fire/acoustic/interface review

The prototype is not fire or acoustic certification.

Nevertheless document the physical edge condition sufficiently for later specialists to review:

- cavities and closure materials;
- exposed/protected steel;
- ceiling continuity;
- potential flanking paths;
- gaps created by intended movement/tolerance;
- sequence for reinstating closure after any local repair.

A structurally elegant connection that creates an obviously disproportionate fire/acoustic problem should be rejected before further optimisation.

---

## 9. Evidence record

Photograph and measure before closure and after any intervention.

Minimum record:

- as-built dimensions;
- product/component identifiers;
- engineer detail revision;
- installer identity/role;
- deviations from design;
- installation time by operation;
- issue log;
- photographs of support/restraint/closure before concealment;
- inspection/replacement trial result;
- any engineer-defined load-test data;
- conventional-control comparison.

---

## 10. P03 decision

Choose one of:

### `SELECT CONVENTIONAL`

The certified hanger/restraint family provides the best total answer. Record the explicit function decomposition and carry it into technical design.

### `SELECT BEARING FAMILY`

A direct/seated arrangement demonstrates a material advantage with acceptable engineering and boundary consequences.

### `SELECT CHALLENGER`

A non-standard shoe/ledge genuinely earns project-specific engineering.

### `QUALIFY / RETEST`

The family remains plausible but an important boundary, tolerance or repair question is unresolved.

### `REJECT CHALLENGER`

Complexity exceeds demonstrated value. Retain the stronger conventional family.

### `REWIND RH-05 / RH-04`

No credible solution fits the controlled structural/section geometry without architectural change.

---

## 11. What P03 can and cannot prove

A successful P03 can support a **Reference House structural-interface selection** and can strengthen evidence for the analytical strategy of decomposing connection functions.

It does **not** by itself:

- certify structural capacity;
- establish generic code compliance;
- prove fire resistance or acoustic performance;
- prove full joist replaceability;
- promote a bespoke seated connection into House Kernel doctrine.

The most successful P03 outcome may be confirmation that an ordinary certified hanger, used with clearer separation of duties and a better finish interface, is the right answer.
