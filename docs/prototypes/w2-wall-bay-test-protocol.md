# P01-W2 Replaceable Wall — Evidence Protocol v0.1

**Status:** pre-build test protocol  
**Prototype:** `P01-W2`  
**Companion build pack:** [W2 Replaceable Wall — Prototype Build Pack](w2-wall-bay-build-pack.md)  
**Decision target:** `HSA-P-022 — Controlled Attachment Plane` + held candidate **Replaceable Architectural Lining**

This document controls how P01-W2 is judged. The build pack says **what to make**; this protocol says **what evidence must be captured before anyone is allowed to call the result promising**.

The protocol is intentionally harsher than a design review. A beautiful mock-up assembled by its designer on a perfect background proves very little.

---

# 1. Question under test

Can a removable room-facing lining deliver a domestic wall that is simultaneously:

- visually calm and architecturally convincing;
- tactilely solid and free of rattle;
- tolerant of realistic background error;
- understandable to an unfamiliar competent installer;
- repeatedly removable without wet making-good;
- capable of ordinary domestic fixing loads;
- independent of irreplaceable proprietary hardware;
- measurably maintainable without transferring damage into permanent fabric?

The benchmark is not a bad plasterboard partition. It is a **first-rate conventional wall finish**.

A negative result is useful. If the candidate cannot compete with excellent plaster on normal-use quality, it should not be rescued merely because it is reversible.

---

# 2. What this test can and cannot establish

## P01 can establish

- comparative visual/tactile quality at full scale;
- geometric repeatability;
- basic impact/handling robustness;
- ordinary fixing behaviour;
- practical removal and reinstatement;
- installer comprehension and tolerance recovery;
- whether the candidate deserves specialist engineering/testing.

## P01 cannot establish

- a BS 5234 duty grade;
- fire resistance;
- airborne/impact acoustic classification;
- airtightness certification;
- hygrothermal safety;
- structural anchor capacity for the house;
- electrical compliance;
- product/system warranty.

If P01 survives, formal strength/robustness testing can escalate to BS 5234-2 or an equivalent competent laboratory route. BS 5234-2 remains the relevant UK performance/test framework for partitions and matching linings, including stiffness, hard/soft body impact, door slamming and anchorage tests. A workshop screening test must never be described as compliance with that standard.

# 3. Freeze the experiment before building

Before fabrication begins, issue a frozen **P01-W2-A** test definition containing:

1. drawing/detail revision;
2. panel material, thickness and measured sheet mass;
3. panel dimensions;
4. rail/backplane section and spacing;
5. permanent-anchor type and count;
6. adjustment method and available range;
7. lower-seat detail;
8. top-restraint detail;
9. resilient-strip material;
10. joint family for each field;
11. skirting/picture-rail/cornice attachment;
12. socket/interface detail;
13. intended fixing-load classes;
14. incoming background tolerance envelope;
15. target finished datum;
16. build instructions issued to the independent installer.

Any change after freeze is a **deviation**, not an invisible design improvement.

For every deviation record:

- what failed or became ambiguous;
- who proposed the change;
- whether the change was required for safety or merely convenience;
- what material/tool/fixing was added;
- whether drawings/instructions would have predicted it;
- whether the test must restart or the result can remain comparable.

Do not silently tune the prototype during the test and then report the tuned result as though it were the frozen design.

# 4. Specimens

Build two directly comparable full-height wall conditions in the same environment where practical.

## C0 — conventional control

A first-rate conventional domestic wall finish appropriate to the background, for example well-executed plaster on masonry or a high-quality conventional lining finished to the same visual standard.

The control exists to answer:

> What quality are we paying complexity to preserve?

Record:

- wall build-up;
- total depth;
- materials;
- labour time;
- finished flatness;
- surface finish;
- fixing strategy;
- repair/access method.

## W2 — candidate wall

The complete P01-W2 assembly, including:

- representative imperfect background;
- continuous slow boundary/background treatment;
- adjustable attachment plane;
- cavity/absorption condition;
- removable panels;
- skirting;
- picture rail or equivalent release datum;
- ceiling/cornice interface;
- socket/control interface;
- at least one ordinary fixing zone;
- one explicit heavier-load ground or explicit exclusion.

The candidate must not be given a better background, better lighting or more forgiving finishing standard than the control.

# 5. Material facts and handling

## Board mass

Do not calculate handling from a remembered catalogue value.

The current British Gypsum product data for Rigidur H 12.5 mm states a **minimum mass of 12.3 kg/m²**. A nominal 600 × 2400 mm field would therefore be at least about **17.7 kg before finish and hardware**. Historical test specimens have reported materially different actual surface densities, so the prototype panel must be weighed after finishing rather than treated as a nominal 17.7 kg object.

Record:

- raw board mass;
- finished panel mass;
- centre-of-gravity/handling observation;
- available grip points;
- required lifting height and reach;
- one-person or team handling decision;
- any aid used.

HSE guidance does not define a universal safe manual-handling weight. Awkward geometry, reach, posture, repetition and team handling matter alongside mass. Use the HSE MAC/manual-handling approach where the operation is not plainly low-risk.

The prototype does **not** pass by forcing one-person removal of an awkward full-height panel.

# 6. Background-tolerance trial

A perfect wall is not an acceptable sole test substrate.

Before installing the backplane:

1. establish the reference plane with laser/straightedge;
2. measure the background at a regular grid;
3. record local high/low points and overall plumb;
4. declare which deviations are inside the system's claimed correction range;
5. identify at least one rail/fixing location that materially exercises the adjustment mechanism;
6. photograph and retain the raw survey.

The candidate fails this gate if:

- the claimed in-range background cannot be corrected;
- correction requires undocumented filler, foam, improvised packing or additional masonry fixings;
- adjustment consumes all reserve such that ordinary construction variation would exceed the system;
- out-of-range conditions can be concealed rather than triggering a visible stop/remediation decision.

## Finished geometry measurements

Record after installation and after the 10-cycle test:

- maximum deviation under a 2 m straightedge;
- face step across every vertical panel joint;
- joint width at top/middle/bottom;
- plumb over full panel height;
- skirting/picture-rail alignment;
- socket/faceplate alignment.

Project screening targets, to be frozen before build:

- designed open joint remains within its declared **2–4 mm** family;
- face step at a nominally flush joint: **≤ 1 mm**;
- cycle-induced change in joint width or face step: **≤ 1 mm** from the post-install datum;
- no progressive drift across cycles.

These are P01 design thresholds, not general construction standards.

---

# 7. Installer-robustness trial

At least one complete assembly cycle must be performed by a competent installer/fabricator who did not design the system.

Give them only:

- the frozen drawings/instructions;
- scheduled components;
- ordinary tools listed by the build pack;
- the measured background condition.

Do not coach except for safety or to prevent irreversible destruction.

Record:

- start/finish time by operation;
- every clarification question;
- every instruction interpreted incorrectly;
- every unlisted tool;
- every improvised packer/fixing/adhesive/filler;
- every operation attempted in the wrong sequence;
- every hidden defect that could have been closed over;
- any designer intervention.

### Installer gate

**Immediate fail** if correct assembly depends on undocumented material, designer memory, hidden improvisation or an unrecorded critical sequence.

A clarification question is not itself failure. The evidence target is whether the system and information are robust enough to become ordinary work.

---

# 8. Removal and reinstatement cycling

Choose one representative removable field and run **10 complete cycles**.

Freeze the removal procedure before cycle 1.

For each cycle record:

| Measure | Record |
|---|---|
| people required | count |
| tools required | list/count |
| release access time | min:s |
| panel release time | min:s |
| reinstatement time | min:s |
| alignment adjustment required | yes/no + description |
| damaged finish/edge/fixing | location + severity |
| wet making-good | yes/no |
| rattle after reinstatement | yes/no |
| joint-width change | mm |
| face-step change | mm |

Also photograph the seat, restraint, panel edges and background after cycles **1, 5 and 10**.

### Non-negotiable cycle gate

Fail if normal access:

- requires wet filler/plaster/redecoration to restore the joint;
- damages the permanent background/boundary;
- progressively loosens the support/restraint;
- produces audible rattle;
- chips/damages edges faster than a replaceable component can reasonably tolerate;
- loses the declared joint/face datum beyond the frozen screening tolerance.

Time is evidence, but P01 does not impose an arbitrary universal minute target. The important comparison is repeatability, ordinary tools and absence of destructive making-good.

---

# 9. Solidity and rattle screening

P01 is not an acoustic laboratory test. It should nevertheless make subjective claims harder to hand-wave.

## Fixed-point contact map

Mark repeatable locations:

- panel centre;
- 100 mm from vertical joint;
- support/rail line;
- socket interface;
- skirting zone;
- picture-rail zone.

At each point perform the same sequence on **C0** and **W2**:

1. knuckle/tap comparison;
2. firm palm strike;
3. steady hand/shoulder pressure;
4. release and listen for rattle/ring;
5. repeat after cycle 10.

Where simple measurement equipment is available, record comparative impulse audio and panel deflection. Treat these as relative data only; do not invent an acoustic rating.

### Immediate fail

- normal hand contact produces rattle or looseness;
- hardware rings conspicuously in ordinary contact;
- panel movement can be felt as a loose access panel;
- cycle 10 is materially worse than cycle 1.

# 10. Repeatable impact screen

Use a simple documented impact screen before considering formal laboratory testing.

Suggested workshop method:

- pendulum/controlled striker of known mass;
- calculate nominal impact energy from mass and release height;
- test panel centre and a location near a joint/support;
- begin at approximately **3 J**;
- if undamaged, repeat at approximately **6 J**;
- photograph and measure any indent, crack, edge damage, fixing movement or residual looseness.

These energies are only **project screening levels**. The geometry/apparatus does not reproduce BS 5234-2, so do not report a duty grade from this test.

Fail P01 if modest repeatable impact causes:

- perforation/fracture;
- panel unseating;
- permanent fixing looseness;
- joint disengagement;
- damage that makes routine replacement/reinstatement implausible.

If P01 otherwise passes and robust-wall claims matter, commission the relevant BS 5234-2 tests through a competent/appropriately accredited laboratory.

---

# 11. Domestic fixing screen

Predeclare three project use cases rather than claiming a generic fixing capacity.

Suggested first screen:

| Class | Representative object | Support route |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | picture / light decoration | panel where evidence permits |
| L2 | coat hook / modest domestic fitting | panel or known ground as designed |
| L3 | shelf / medium object with eccentric load | dedicated backplane ground |

Before testing record actual mass, lever arm, fixing and substrate. Apply the intended load for at least 24 hours and inspect:

- local crushing;
- screw/insert movement;
- permanent set;
- cracking;
- panel/backplane movement;
- effect on neighbouring joint alignment.

Do not convert these project use cases into general safe-working-load claims. Engineered/heavy loads remain a separate design problem.

---

# 12. Socket and service-interface trial

The back box remains fixed to the backplane/service carrier unless the test deliberately challenges that assumption.

Demonstrate, in sequence:

1. isolate/remove faceplate as appropriate;
2. release surrounding architectural trim;
3. remove panel without pulling the fixed wiring infrastructure out with it;
4. inspect cable/service zone;
5. replace panel;
6. reproduce alignment around the fixed service interface.

Record whether replacement of the panel would require specialist machining/templates and whether those data are sufficient for future remanufacture.

Fail if routine panel access requires destructive rewiring or if the decorative panel becomes the structural support for permanent electrical infrastructure.

---

# 13. Independent room-quality assessment

The largest unresolved question is **repose / permanence**, so record it separately from designer enthusiasm.

Use at least **five assessors who were not involved in designing the wall** where practical. Do not tell them which condition is the experimental one until after their first assessment. Randomise whether they inspect C0 or W2 first.

Ask each assessor to score each wall from 1–7 for:

- perceived solidity;
- perceived permanence;
- visual calm;
- quality of joints/detailing;
- likelihood that they would notice it as an access system;
- desirability in a principal domestic room.

Also require one short free-text observation before revealing the construction method.

This is a small comparative design test, not statistical science.

### Strong-pass screen

A strong result should show:

- median W2 scores no more than **one point below C0** for solidity, permanence, visual calm and desirability;
- no consistent free-text description of rattly, temporary, office-like or access-panel character;
- joints interpreted as deliberate architecture rather than technical necessity.

If W2 scores poorly, do not erase the result by switching evaluators after the fact.

---

# 14. Cost, depth, material and labour account

Record both C0 and W2:

- wall depth consumed;
- material mass per m²;
- material cost;
- fabrication labour;
- site installation labour;
- specialist tools/processes;
- number of permanent-wall anchors;
- number/types of unique hardware;
- likely replaceable components;
- expected destructive work for one future service intervention.

P01 does not need a speculative 50-year NPV model. It does need enough data to expose a system that buys £10 of future access with £1,000 of present complexity.

# 15. Decision matrix

## Reject

Reject the current implementation if any non-negotiable gate fails, including:

- access requires wet making-good;
- permanent background is damaged by routine access;
- normal contact rattles or feels loose;
- declared background tolerance cannot be recovered;
- independent installer requires undocumented improvisation/design coaching;
- cycling produces progressive loss of datum;
- ordinary room quality is materially inferior to C0;
- proprietary/unique hardware creates an obvious replacement dependency without a credible remanufacture route.

## Hold / redesign

Hold if the principle remains plausible but one or more of these dominate:

- handling is awkward;
- cavity depth/material intensity is excessive;
- joint family is visually weak;
- restraint is too fiddly;
- impact/fixing performance is marginal;
- cost/labour premium appears disproportionate.

## Reference-house experimental

Allow controlled project use only if the implementation passes the non-negotiable gates but still lacks enough repeatability, engineering or whole-system evidence for general promotion.

## Promote candidate toward pattern status

Promotion of **Replaceable Architectural Lining** should require, at minimum:

- P01 strong pass on normal-use quality and cycling;
- credible installer robustness;
- credible conventional-wall comparator;
- engineering/fire/acoustic/building-physics review appropriate to the intended use;
- evidence that the extra layer is proportionate in the contexts where the pattern would apply.

P01 alone cannot promote the candidate.

# 17. Evidence storage

For every test retain:

- frozen drawings and issued instructions;
- materials/products and batch where relevant;
- background survey;
- installation photographs;
- deviation log;
- raw dimensional measurements;
- timing sheets;
- load/impact setup photographs and calculations;
- assessor sheets before aggregation;
- cycle photographs;
- final teardown photographs;
- cost/material/labour record;
- final signed result sheet.

Do not retain only a polished summary.

# 19. Core rule

> **Do not ask whether the prototype is clever. Ask whether, after being built by someone else, used, struck, loaded, opened ten times and compared directly with excellent plaster, anyone would still want it in their house.**
