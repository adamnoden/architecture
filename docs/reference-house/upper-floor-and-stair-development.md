# Reference House — RH-03 Stair, Upper Floor and Courtyard Circulation

**Status:** RH-03 design gate record  
**Date:** 2026-10-12  
**Question:** can the stair, upper-floor rooms, bathrooms and courtyard circulation form one coherent domestic composition without damaging the controlled ground floor?  
**Ground-floor authority:** [RH-02 Ground-Floor Development](ground-floor-development.md)  
**Upper-floor drawing:** [upper-floor-and-stair-development.svg](upper-floor-and-stair-development.svg)  
**Section:** [stair-courtyard-section.svg](stair-courtyard-section.svg)

RH-03 is the first Reference House gate that must work simultaneously in plan and section. It fixes a **coordination stair**, upper-floor room order and circulation topology. It does not yet fix final storey heights, structural framing, façade openings, fire strategy, drainage pipe sizes or detailed bathroom layouts.

The stage makes one local correction to RH-02: the entrance/stair hall is widened by approximately **200 mm into the front edge of the drawing-room reservation**. The outer shell, courtyard, principal ground-floor room order and service flank remain unchanged.

---

## 1. Regulatory and safety baseline for the stair

The current England statutory-guidance baseline remains Approved Document K, 2013 edition. For a private stair it gives:

- rise: **150–220 mm**;
- going: **220–300 mm**;
- maximum pitch: **42°**;
- normal `2R + G` relationship: **550–700 mm**;
- minimum headroom on the access between levels: **2.0 m**.

Government published research in 2025 on possible future changes to domestic-stair guidance, but that report explicitly does **not** constitute government policy or current guidance. RH-03 therefore uses the current Approved Document K as the compliance reference and chooses a materially more comfortable stair than its minima.

References:

- GOV.UK, [Protection from falling, collision and impact: Approved Document K](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/protection-from-falling-collision-and-impact-approved-document-k)
- GOV.UK, [Evidence to support potential changes to Approved Document K guidance on domestic stairs](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/evidence-to-support-potential-changes-to-approved-document-k-guidance-on-domestic-stairs)

### RH-03 target geometry

Use this as a **coordination target**, not final technical design:

- **18 risers total**;
- **three flights of six risers**;
- **five 280 mm goings per flight**;
- **full landings, no winders**;
- target clear flight width: **approximately 1000 mm**;
- planning floor-to-floor datum for the section: **3240 mm**;
- resulting planning rise at that datum: **180 mm**;
- `2R + G = 640 mm`;
- planning pitch approximately **33°**;
- clear headroom target: **not less than 2000 mm**.

The 3240 mm floor-to-floor dimension exists only to test the stair. RH-04 owns final storey and opening datums. With 18 risers, a later floor-to-floor value in roughly the 3150–3300 mm range remains compatible with a comfortable rise of approximately 175–183 mm without changing the stair topology.

The project preference for deep, secure treads is therefore carried explicitly: **going is treated as a design quality, not merely a regulatory minimum**.

## 2. Why the obvious two-flight stair does not survive

RH-02's front centre is only **3.4 m gross deep** between the front façade and courtyard.

Once the temporary planning allowances for substantial external/courtyard walls are acknowledged, the clear front-wing depth is approximately **2.7 m**. A comfortable two-flight return stair using circa 280 mm goings needs roughly:

- about **2.24 m flight run** for eight goings in a nine-rise flight;
- plus a landing approximately the stair width;
- therefore well over **3.2 m** in the flight direction.

That stair can fit only by:

- displacing the east service block;
- consuming a principal room;
- entering the courtyard reservation;
- or steepening/shrinking the stair simply to protect a diagram.

RH-03 rejects all four responses.

A three-flight stair reduces each flight run to **1.40 m** while retaining the 280 mm going. The additional landing is an acceptable cost because it makes the stair geometrically compatible with the compact house **without sacrificing tread quality**.

## 3. Stair-position comparison

### S1 — Three-flight stair in the entrance hall — **SELECTED**

The stair sits in the western part of `G-HALL-02`, immediately beside the all-weather passage that continues into `G-GAL-01`.

A coordination reservation of approximately **2.4–2.5 m square** is sufficient to develop three 6-rise flights around a small open well with full landings. The exact strings, balustrades, well and landing construction remain later work.

To preserve a credible passage beside it, the ground-floor partition between the drawing room and entrance hall moves approximately **200 mm west** for the front-wing depth only:

- former front drawing/hall boundary: X ≈ **4.20 m**;
- RH-03 front boundary: X ≈ **4.00 m**;
- courtyard edge remains X = **4.20 m**;
- gross stair-hall/front-arrival field therefore becomes approximately **3.80 m wide**;
- a target **1.05–1.15 m** clear passage remains on the east side of the stair after detailed wall/string coordination.

The entrance door moves into that eastern arrival field rather than being forced to the abstract centreline. This is not treated as a defect: G-01 evidence already shows that coherent Georgian-derived order does not require every entrance to coincide with a whole-building centreline.

**Strengths**

- principal stair remains part of the principal arrival, not the technical route;
- deep going survives;
- no winders are required;
- service hub and kitchen geography survive unchanged;
- ground all-weather route remains legible beside the stair;
- upper stair arrival naturally feeds the south landing and east courtyard gallery;
- stair can receive light from front / courtyard-side openings once RH-04 couples section and elevation;
- the stair becomes an architectural room rather than a leftover corridor object.

**Costs / obligations**

- front entrance is no longer assumed to be dead-centre;
- drawing-room front width loses approximately 200 mm;
- exact 1000 mm clear-flight / balustrade / passage fit must be checked at technical resolution;
- the three-flight stair has two intermediate landings rather than one.

These costs are proportionate and visible.

### S2 — Two-flight stair replacing the east service block — **REJECT**

A conventional return stair fits well in the 2.9 × 4.0 m east-middle outer zone.

It is rejected because that geometric neatness is purchased by destroying one of RH-02's strongest integrations:

- `G-SVC-02` loses its dedicated service-hub geography;
- utility / plant / WC must then colonise the study, kitchen or residual cabinetry;
- the principal stair becomes spatially entangled with the technical flank;
- a downstream stair convenience rewrites the ground-floor service strategy.

This is exactly the sort of local optimisation the Reference House is intended to expose.

### S3 — Stair inserted between the west principal rooms — **REJECT**

The west-middle flank can physically accommodate a conventional stair.

It is rejected because it breaks the drawing/family room pair into undersized front/rear cells and turns the stair into a divider between the two strongest occupied rooms. The damage to domestic hierarchy is greater than the compact-stair complexity saved.

### S4 — Helical / elliptical / winder-led stair — **HELD, not selected**

G-01 precedent can support geometrically expressive stairs. A curved or tapered-tread stair could also solve the shallow front-wing problem.

RH-03 does not select one because:

- the project explicitly values secure, generous ordinary treads;
- the geometry would introduce a new craft/engineering problem before it earns architectural value;
- a full-landing stair already solves the coordination problem.

Historical plausibility alone is not a reason to prefer complexity.

## 4. Selected upper-floor circulation topology

The upper floor deliberately avoids a full gallery around the courtyard.

Circulation forms a **three-sided U**:

1. **south:** stair hall / south landing above the entrance;
2. **east:** a narrow courtyard-side gallery through the middle service band;
3. **north:** a short cross-landing connecting the rear rooms.

There is **no west gallery**.

This matters because a full 1.1–1.2 m gallery around all four court edges would consume the useful width of the west principal bedrooms. The U-shaped route preserves the courtyard as an orienting centre without paying for an equal circulation ring.

### Movement sequence

**stair → south landing → east courtyard gallery → north cross-landing**

Doors from those three circulation fields serve the four bedrooms and wet/service rooms.

The route is deliberately easy to understand: the courtyard remains visible as an orientation device, while bedroom doors do not open into a single hotel-like corridor.

## 6. Room-scale adequacy

Using the same conservative planning allowances as RH-02:

### Principal bedroom — `U-BED-P01`

Approximate useful clear field before local en-suite/dressing deductions: **3.5 m × 5.3 m**.

This supports:

- super-king / king bed with meaningful side clearances;
- two bedside tables;
- proper wardrobe run;
- chair / chest / dressing furniture;
- a route that does not require walking through the bed zone.

The principal bedroom earns the longest west-wing field rather than obtaining status through a gratuitous increase in building width.

### West-front bedroom — `U-BED-02`

Approximate clear field after the local 200 mm stair-hall shift: roughly **3.3 × 4.5 m**.

This remains a credible double bedroom with wardrobe and desk.

### East-front and east-rear bedrooms — `U-BED-03`, `U-BED-04`

Each occupies approximately **4.2 × 3.4 m gross** at the corner fields.

After wall allowances the useful field is around **3.7 × 2.9 m**. These are compact doubles / generous single rooms rather than principal rooms.

The project therefore selects a **four-bedroom upper floor** at this gate. The five-bedroom ceiling remains a ceiling, not a target. A fifth bedroom would require subdividing one of the rooms or colonising the north/south circulation fields and would weaken the current hierarchy.

### Family bathroom — `U-BATH-01`

The east-middle outer strip is approximately **2.9 × 4.0 m gross**. This is ample at coordination scale for a proper family bathroom plus linen/service allowance while retaining a dry-side access relationship to `U-GAL-02`.

The bathroom has an external east wall available for real daylight and purge opening if RH-04/RH-07 support it.

### Principal en-suite — `U-ENS-01`

The current reservation is deliberately compact. It is intended to prove whether a naturally lit shower-room / dressing condition can be given to the principal bedroom without scattering the main bathroom/service geography.

Its soil/waste route is **not yet proven**. RH-06 must either:

- demonstrate a short, maintainable route toward the east/north service geography;
- modify the en-suite position;
- or reject the en-suite rather than force an ugly route through permanent fabric.

The Reference House does not require an en-suite strongly enough to corrupt the topology for one.

## 7. Wet-room and service geography

RH-03 produces a much stronger service condition than the F0 upper floor because the main family bathroom is directly stacked over the current ground-floor service hub.

### Main stacked wet/service zone

`G-SVC-02` below → `U-BATH-01` above

This creates a credible project location for:

- soil/waste stack;
- bathroom supply isolation / accessible joints;
- extract route;
- vertical electrical/data/service distribution where compatible;
- dry-side access from the east courtyard gallery.

A small explicit vertical-service reservation should be carried within this stack rather than treating the whole bathroom wall as an undifferentiated service cavity.

### Kitchen relationship

`G-KIT-02` remains immediately north of the ground service block. The upper east-rear bedroom deliberately prevents the project from assuming every wet room must sit over the kitchen. The important demand is **coherent route geography**, not literal fixture-over-fixture stacking everywhere.

### En-suite caution

`U-ENS-01` is the only wet-room condition not directly inside the primary east stack. It is therefore a deliberate RH-06 challenge rather than a solved fact.

## 8. Courtyard section and daylight logic

The courtyard remains genuinely open to sky through both principal storeys.

RH-03 establishes these sectional intentions:

- the stair hall sits immediately south of the courtyard and should receive borrowed/direct light from its relationship to the court and front elevation;
- the east upper gallery overlooks or addresses the court, providing orientation and daylight;
- the north cross-landing addresses the courtyard rather than becoming a blind corridor;
- the main family bathroom is capable of real external daylight from the outer east elevation;
- the principal en-suite is only retained if it can obtain credible daylight/privacy and service routing;
- upper bedroom windows remain outer-elevation openings; courtyard openings are secondary outlook/light opportunities, not their only source of daylight.

This avoids making bedrooms dependent on a small internal court while still letting the court animate circulation.

RH-07 will quantify environmental consequences later. RH-03 claims only a credible spatial opportunity.

## 9. G-01 implications

RH-03 does not claim that the selected stair is a validated Georgian stair type.

It does establish a strong G-01-compatible order:

- stair is explicitly principal circulation rather than technical circulation;
- hierarchy is expressed in route, room size and section rather than one universal ratio;
- the stair is capable of becoming a major sectional event without requiring a double-height spectacle;
- south stair hall, east gallery and north landing form related but non-identical circulation spaces;
- the courtyard remains a scoped ordering centre rather than proof that the whole building must be bilaterally symmetric;
- off-centre entrance geometry is left for plan/section/elevation negotiation in RH-04 rather than corrected reflexively back to symmetry.

The stair is therefore an architectural proposition to be tested by G-01, not a Georgian label pasted onto a circulation diagram.

## 10. Pattern-occurrence consequences

RH-03 creates enough project geometry to establish two further coordination occurrences:

1. **High-Service-Room Service Wall** — the main family bathroom has a real maintainable edge adjoining `U-GAL-02`, allowing dry-side access to selected concealed components;
2. **Accessible Vertical Service Zone** — `G-SVC-02` and `U-BATH-01` now form an actual stacked geography within which a bounded accessible vertical zone can be reserved.

The exact clear riser size, segregation, fire/acoustic closure and service content remain RH-05/RH-06 obligations.

`U-ENS-01` does **not** yet earn a pattern occurrence merely because an en-suite is drawn. Its service route is deliberately unresolved.

---

## 11. Rejected moves that should remain rejected unless evidence changes

- **Do not steepen the principal stair merely to keep a simpler two-flight shape.**
- **Do not use winders simply because they make the plan easier.**
- **Do not displace the dedicated service hub to make the stair conventional.**
- **Do not build a full upper gallery around all four courtyard sides merely for symmetry.**
- **Do not add a fifth bedroom merely because the brief allows five.**
- **Do not force the principal en-suite to survive if RH-06 shows its drainage/service route is disproportionate.**

---

## 12. What remains deliberately unresolved

RH-03 does not fix:

- final ground/first-floor ceiling heights;
- exact stair strings, well size, balustrade or handrail profile;
- exact front-door position within the eastern arrival field;
- detailed upper partition thicknesses;
- exact bedroom door positions;
- final en-suite geometry;
- structural support/trimming around the stair and courtyard;
- fire-resistance / escape-window strategy;
- bathroom fixture layout and drainage falls;
- riser clear dimensions;
- façade window centres, sill/head heights or hierarchy;
- roof geometry.

These are intentionally routed to RH-04 through RH-08.

# RH-03 gate record

**Result:** **PASS.**

The house now has:

- a principal stair that fits the compact front wing without degrading tread quality;
- a four-bedroom upper-floor hierarchy;
- a three-sided courtyard circulation topology rather than an area-hungry full ring;
- a main family bathroom directly stacked over the ground service hub;
- outer-wall daylight opportunity for the main bathroom;
- a bounded principal en-suite hypothesis whose survival is explicitly conditional on RH-06;
- a coherent relationship between stair arrival, courtyard orientation and bedroom privacy;
- no need to reopen the 12.0 × 10.8 m outer shell.

**No maturity promotion is claimed.**
