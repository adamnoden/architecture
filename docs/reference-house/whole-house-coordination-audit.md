# Reference House — RH-10 Whole-House Coordination Audit

**Status:** RH-10 whole-house gate record — **PASS after corrective rewinds / additions**  
**Date:** 2026-10-12  
**Purpose:** attack the Reference House as one building rather than continue improving isolated subsystems.  
**Corrective addenda:** [Energy / Heat / Renewable Coordination](energy-heat-coordination-rh10.md), [Envelope Coordination](envelope-coordination-rh10.md), [Outer Elevation Coordination](outer-elevation-coordination-rh10.md)  
**Summary drawing:** [RH-10 Whole-House Coordination Map](whole-house-coordination-rh10.svg)

RH-10 is the first gate allowed to ask whether the Reference House has earned the word **coordinated**.

The standard is not technical completion. The standard is stricter in another way:

> **Have the major architectural, structural, service, environmental, drainage, maintenance and occupied-interface decisions been reconciled strongly enough that the remaining uncertainty belongs to engineering, prototype, real-site or external-review work rather than to an unfinished house?**

The answer is **yes, after RH-10 exposed and repaired several real omissions and one authority conflict**.

No claim is made that the house is buildable from these documents. RH-10 moves the Reference House from a developed virtual design into a coordinated virtual design.

# 1. Step back before auditing details

Before reviewing clashes, RH-10 asks whether the project has moved in the right direction at all.

## 1.1 Has House Kernel made the house more technical than domestic?

**No, on current evidence.**

The later design sequence has repeatedly removed infrastructure when it failed a proportionality test:

- RH-06 deleted the remote principal en-suite rather than adding another soil stack / gravity route;
- RH-07 kept openable windows and rejected cooling reservation rather than making MVHR solve summer comfort;
- RH-08 rejected inner roof gutters, internal roof outlets and a symbolic roof hatch;
- RH-09 rejected house-wide removable lining and house-wide access flooring;
- RH-10 rejects default underfloor heating and uses visible/replaceable low-temperature emitters as the first heating family.

The result is not a conventional house with an invisible machine hidden inside it. It is a relatively conventional house whose **few unusual interfaces are concentrated where they buy something measurable**.

## 1.2 Has the courtyard survived because it was emotionally protected?

The courtyard is a selected brief choice, so RH-10 does not pretend it had no incumbency. It still has to earn its downstream cost.

Its current value is real but bounded:

- gives the entrance / gallery / landing a strong orienting centre;
- keeps occupied bands shallow and multi-aspect;
- creates a front → court → garden spatial sequence;
- supports purge / cross ventilation;
- gives diffuse light to circulation and adjacent rooms;
- provides a genuine local architectural dialect zone.

Its costs are also explicit:

- more external wall per unit floor area;
- shaded ground plane rather than sunny patio;
- extra external interfaces and maintenance surface;
- dedicated courtyard drainage and overflow;
- tighter front/rear wings.

RH-07/RH-08 have stopped pretending it is a sunny garden or maintenance-free void. On the current brief it still earns its place. A real site / energy / daylight model may later prove otherwise; removing it would then be an explicit brief change, not a doctrine failure.

## 1.3 Has G-01 leaked into House Kernel doctrine?

**No material upstream leakage found.**

The four-bay front, three-axis garden elevation, masonry family, courtyard and low roof remain Reference House / G-01 / project choices. The reasons extracted upstream remain style-neutral: scoped order, legibility, maintainable interfaces, passive hierarchy, failure geography and proportional serviceability.

## 1.4 Has technical asymmetry damaged architectural order?

No. The house now has several scoped orders:

- principal front — RH-04 centred four-bay field with door in bay 3;
- courtyard — separately centred internal order;
- garden — RH-10 three-axis composition;
- service side — three broad room/service fields with technical expression concentrated in the middle;
- west side — quieter room-owned order.

Technical asymmetry is local rather than allowed to dissolve the whole composition.

## 1.5 Is the house actually compact?

It is **not a small house** and should not be described as one.

The controlled gross enclosed plate proxy is **115.2 m² per storey / 230.4 m² across two storeys**, before subtracting substantial outer/courtyard walls, internal partitions and stair effects.

Using the RH-10 450 mm envelope stress test gives a rough pre-partition internal floor field of about **94 m² per storey**, or roughly **188 m² across two storeys** before internal partitions/stair voids. That is a generous four-bedroom family house with study, real utility/plant space and courtyard—not a mansion.

The current plan contains no cinema, gym, spare formal dining room or fifth bedroom invented to consume symmetry. The area is primarily spent on real rooms, stair, service geography and the selected courtyard morphology.

**Verdict:** "relatively compact" remains defensible only in relation to the selected detached courtyard-house brief. Avoid using "compact" as an absolute marketing adjective.

# 3. Ordinary domestic life

## Arrival and daily movement

The house retains two useful circulation modes:

- architectural route: entrance → re-centred courtyard → garden dining → garden;
- all-weather route: entrance hall → east gallery → kitchen / rear.

The stair remains part of the entrance architecture rather than the service flank.

No normal daily route requires crossing the open courtyard.

## Living rooms

Drawing and family rooms remain distinct, with controlled connections rather than a compulsory great room. That gives acoustic retreat and different patterns of occupation without multiplying formal rooms.

## Kitchen / utility

Kitchen remains directly related to garden/dining and short wet/service geography. Utility remains real programme, not kitchen cabinetry.

## Bedrooms / bathing

Four bedrooms remain the selected upper programme. RH-10 finds no reason to restore the fifth-bedroom possibility.

One family bathroom plus an entrance-storey WC is modest for a four-bedroom house but not a coordination failure. The deleted principal en-suite stays deleted.

The south service band is one of the tightest remaining plan areas. Final partitioning must prove that the WC satisfies applicable M4(1) access geometry **before** utility storage/appliance convenience is protected. If those two uses conflict, utility yields area first; the external shell does not expand automatically.

## Storage

The rejected en-suite has become principal dressing / linen / storage. This is a genuine domestic improvement rather than dead residual space.

**Domestic-use verdict:** pass.

# 5. Fire and escape geometry

RH-03 deliberately left fire strategy open. RH-10 now checks whether later geometry has boxed the house into an implausible escape strategy.

It has not.

The first-floor FFL is **+3300 mm**, below the 4.5 m threshold used in Approved Document B's dwellinghouse guidance for the upper-storey escape options.

The current strategy is therefore:

- each of the four bedrooms retains an outer-façade emergency-escape window;
- the stair is **not required at this coordination stage to become a protected stair enclosure merely to rescue bedrooms**;
- front bedrooms use front bays 1 and 4;
- principal and rear-east bedrooms use the new RH-10 garden openings;
- at least one side route from rear garden toward the street must remain viable so rear escape does not terminate in an inescapable enclosed yard.

Current upper opening planning family:

- width about 900–950 mm;
- sill about 800 mm above first FFL;
- height about 1550 mm.

That geometry is compatible with current guidance requiring an emergency escape opening with at least **0.33 m² unobstructed area**, minimum **450 mm** height and width, and bottom of openable area no more than **1100 mm** above the floor. Final sash/opening stays/restrictors must prove the real free opening.

Canonical source: GOV.UK, *Fire safety: Approved Document B, Volume 1 — Dwellings* — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fire-safety-approved-document-b

Smoke/heat detection, final fire-door requirements, cavity barriers, service-shaft closure, PV/roof electrical fire detail and material reaction/resistance remain building-control / fire-review work.

**Fire-geometry verdict:** pass with explicit technical obligations.

# 6. Structure

The RH-05 short-span ring remains one of the strongest whole-house decisions.

It still carries later design without major contradiction:

- external + courtyard masonry support lines remain stable;
- side floor spans ≈4.2 m gross;
- front/rear central spans ≈3.4 m gross;
- four repeated edge beams remain local floor-frame conditions rather than whole-house transfer structure;
- stair trimmers remain bounded;
- service crossings remain registered rather than discovered on site.

### Roof

RH-08/RH-10 now require the low-slope roof and PV to use the same basic short-span support logic where practical: roof framing spans between outer and courtyard masonry rather than inventing a large transfer frame.

PV mounting loads, parapet/coping loads, roof diaphragm behaviour and the exact low-slope deck remain structural-engineer questions.

### Envelope depth

RH-10's 400–450 mm wall family does not move the support-line concept but does affect bearing/opening details. The inner loadbearing leaf remains the structural datum; outer facing masonry is not to be treated as gravity structure merely because the elevation reads as masonry.

**Structural verdict:** pass at pre-engineering level; P03 + engineer review remain mandatory.

# 7. Services, heating and plant

The service strategy remains concentrated rather than distributed:

- controlled utility entry;
- `G-PLANT-01` equipment room;
- bounded `SV-01` riser;
- single primary foul stack;
- short kitchen/bath wet routes;
- central MVHR with explicit MEV fallback;
- separate cooker source capture;
- RH-10 air-to-water heat-pump / DHW baseline;
- bounded low-temperature hydronic routes;
- PV electrical route into accessible service geography.

The plant room is now asked to carry **MVHR + DHW/hydraulics + dry controls/distribution + riser access**. Its 2.9 × 2.2 m gross reservation is plausible but no longer generous.

This becomes one of the main RH-11 MEP attack questions. Product-level layout must prove filter changes, cylinder replacement, valve access and working clearances simultaneously.

The rule is important:

> **If the room is too small, simplify plant before enlarging the house.**

No battery, cooling plant, speculative buffer vessel or redundant manifold is reserved by default.

**Services verdict:** coordinated topology; sizing/packing remains specialist validation.

No current water route depends on an electronic sensor to prevent fabric damage.

**Water/moisture verdict:** pass at coordination level; hydraulic/building-physics design remains external.

# 10. Acoustics and repose

The plan does not force obvious high-noise adjacencies into principal retreat spaces:

- plant is beneath the family-bath/service geography rather than beneath the principal bedroom;
- the external heat pump is held at the service side / rear and not beneath a selected bedroom opening;
- kitchen and family room can be closed from the drawing room;
- W2 panelised lining is not required for the house to succeed;
- principal floors remain conventional continuous fields;
- fan-coil room terminals are not the heating baseline.

Current Building Regulations Part E requires reasonable internal sound resistance for floors and walls between bedrooms/WCs and other rooms. Geometry does not obstruct that requirement, but actual constructions remain unproved.

MVHR breakout/crosstalk, ASHP neighbour/bedroom noise, soil-stack noise and the plant-room enclosure are high-priority RH-11 MEP/acoustic questions.

**Repose/acoustic verdict:** spatially sound; technical ratings unproved.

- windows/doors use replaceable assembly logic;

# 17. Next stage

`RH-11 — External-review package` becomes active.

The review package should not ask general questions such as "does this house work?" It should give each reviewer the relevant coordinated drawings, assumptions, clash history and **specific failure conditions that would force a rewind**.

The most valuable early attacks are:

1. **structural:** masonry/courtyard ring, floor edge, roof/PV loads, openings and service penetrations;
2. **MEP/acoustic:** plant-room packing, MVHR + ASHP + DHW topology, service-side terminal/noise geography;
3. **building control/fire:** escape-window strategy, M4(1) ground-floor access/WC, service-zone and interface fire closure;
4. **building physics/energy:** 400–450 mm envelope family, low-slope roof/parapet, overheating/daylight and Future Homes compliance.
