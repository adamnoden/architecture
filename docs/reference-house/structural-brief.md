# Reference House — structural brief

**Authority:** the owner's direction of 30 October 2026, under the [owner-house charter](../../project/owner-house-charter.md). This page owns the structural preferences for this house. It does not select assemblies, establish capacities or revise the accepted plan. The [current house basis](current-house-basis.md) owns adopted geometry; the [structural architecture programme](../../project/str/structural-architecture-programme.md) owns the work needed to test and coordinate this brief.

## The desired house

The owner wants a house whose weight appears to rest convincingly on what is below it: substantial walls, generous bearings, understandable support and openings formed through solid fabric. Compression, arches and direct support contribute to the owner's sense of repose. That personal response is a legitimate design reason; it is not evidence of a general neurological effect or superior structural safety.

The preference extends indoors. Internal walls should normally have real substance, rather than acquiring an appearance of mass from hollow linings. Rooms and routes should be composed with that substance from the outset. A solid wall can be worthwhile without being assigned responsibility for the floor above.

## Design order

1. **Prefer solid internal masonry.** Give every wall an explicit role: enclosure, gravity support, lateral restraint/stability, or a stated combination. A nonloadbearing masonry wall still needs support for its own weight and adequate restraint. Do not promise unspecified future load capacity.
2. **Align support between storeys.** Prefer upper bearing walls and concentrated reactions over lower bearing walls or piers, continuing to a credible foundation concept. Compare the structural order and the inhabited plan together.
3. **Explore real masonry arches at external and internal openings.** Assess curved and flat arches where appropriate. State which leaf and load each arch carries, and how its thrust, abutments and construction sequence work. A decorative arch over a hidden beam does not satisfy the preference by itself.
4. **Redesign awkward support before introducing exceptional transfers.** An arch can bridge an opening within an appropriately supported wall; it does not automatically solve a wall displaced sideways on the floor above. Reconsider the opening, wall, room arrangement and span before adopting a transfer beam, hung support or cantilever. Where an exception earns its place, make its reason and consequences visible in the decision record.
5. **Investigate non-timber primary structure first.** The owner's developing preference against structural timber belongs in the floor and roof comparison, alongside the compression preference. It is not a prohibition on timber furniture, joinery or replaceable finishes. Any proposed primary timber solution needs an explicit return to the owner before adoption.

The preferences are strong design directions, not permission to omit ties, reinforcement, diaphragms, anchors or other necessary resistance to tension, movement and lateral action. The scheme must explain where tension remains and why. Replacing timber with steel or reinforced/prestressed concrete does not make a structure tension-free.

## What “solid” must mean in the design

No universal wall thickness is selected. The earlier phrase “two bricks wide” expressed a desire for substantial fabric, not a dimensioned specification. State actual units, bond, leaves, cavities, finishes and total thickness in millimetres. Distinguish brick, block and other solid mineral candidates through their actual construction and architectural consequences; labels alone do not establish equal substance or performance.

For each representative wall, establish bearing/restraint assumptions, slenderness and opening effects, foundations or supporting floor, and any movement joint. Assess finished room size, acoustic separation, fire performance, moisture behaviour, material quantity and construction burden at assembly level. Mass alone does not settle these questions. More bearing walls can improve a particular support arrangement while making alteration, services and circulation harder.

Keep replaceable services accessible and separate from permanent masonry. Do not solve the plan by chasing service networks into structural fabric. Coordinate service fronts, doors and opening heads without treating linings as free space or a decorative cover as a performance boundary. Reconcile material constituents with the [whole-house material policy](material-policy.md).

## Decision and evidence boundary

The current 125 mm partition and 350 mm floor reservations are starting facts, not solutions to this brief. The work may propose changes through the existing shared source-rewind procedure. A change is adopted only when its geometric, spatial and technical consequences are recorded and the owning sources are updated together.

A good result must be both a credible structural proposition and a house the owner wants to inhabit. Reject or revise a candidate that depends on unexplained reactions, destroys necessary room or circulation space, makes ordinary service renewal destructive, or conceals a material exception. Retain the reasons for rejected options.

For the book, distinguish the owner's experience of solidity, the architectural argument for legible support and the engineering evidence for an actual assembly. The enquiry may support a broader discussion of repose and permanence; this brief alone cannot establish a universal rule against tension, lintels, timber or framed buildings.
