# Reference House — RH-11 Building-Physics & Energy Review Brief

**Status:** ready for competent building-physics / energy review  
**Parent pack:** [RH-11 External Review Brief](external-review-brief.md)  
**Current project maturity:** M3 coordinated virtual design; **no thermal, daylight, overheating, hygrothermal or Future Homes compliance claim**

## Review question

> **Can the selected courtyard form, 400–425 mm masonry-envelope family, deep openings, low-slope roof/parapet and passive/MVHR/ASHP strategy deliver a credible high-performance new dwelling without requiring thicker walls, materially different glazing, mechanical cooling or roof changes that reopen the architecture?**

The project wants the environmental costs of the courtyard and Georgian-derived opening order quantified rather than defended rhetorically.

## 1. Controlled environmental / envelope assumptions

### Envelope geometry

RH-10 replaces the obsolete 350 mm planning wall with:

- **400–425 mm** normal external/courtyard-wall planning family;
- **450 mm** whole-house geometry stress limit;
- anything materially thicker than 450 mm triggers an architectural rewind rather than silently reducing rooms/stair.

Working family only:

```text
facing masonry outer leaf
→ drained / insulated cavity
→ substantial loadbearing masonry inner leaf
→ continuous internal air / finish boundary
→ W0 direct mineral/plaster finish by default
```

Indicative geometric comparator:

- ~102.5 mm facing brick;
- ~150–175 mm insulated/drained cavity;
- ~140 mm structural inner masonry;
- ~15 mm direct finish / tolerance.

No insulation product, U-value or hygrothermal build-up is selected.

### Roof

Selected project geometry:

- four very-low-slope fields falling **outward from the courtyard**;
- outer cornice/eaves architectural control around **+6.25 m**;
- no inner courtyard roof gutter;
- no internal roof outlet;
- low-slope weathering/insulation/parapet detail unresolved;
- approximately **50 m² PV-capable roof reserve**;
- no permanent roof hatch.

### Windows / openings

`OW-01` target sequence:

**permanent masonry opening → mounting/tolerance zone → mechanically fixed replaceable frame → removable internal trim**.

The primary air/water/thermal boundary must survive removal of faster-changing trim.

The selected G-01 opening order is relatively restrained; actual frame material, glazing, g-value, U-value, reveal position, airtightness and solar-control strategy are open.

### Courtyard

- open to sky: **3.6 × 4.0 m**;
- wall/cornice height around **6.25 m**;
- height/width about **1.56–1.74**;
- retained deliberately as a **shaded light court**, not a sunny primary amenity;
- principal rooms must retain useful outer-façade daylight responsibility;
- court also functions as purge/cross-ventilation opportunity and maintenance space.

### Environmental hierarchy

Current project order:

1. shallow occupied bands / multi-aspect opportunity;
2. disciplined glazing;
3. orientation-specific external shading / solar control;
4. secure openable-window purge/cross ventilation;
5. MVHR for background IAQ / winter heat recovery;
6. ASHP for low-carbon heat;
7. no mechanical-cooling reservation.

The service side remains a surrogate and the house may mirror on a real site.

## 4. Questions requiring building-physics / energy review

### Wall / floor / roof performance

1. Is a **400–425 mm overall masonry cavity family** a credible starting envelope for the expected Future Homes-level performance, or is the project likely to need >450 mm generally?
2. What wall U-value / insulation family should be used as a realistic design comparator rather than merely the regulatory limiting value?
3. Is a parge/direct-plaster internal air-control strategy compatible with the W0/W1 hierarchy and the expected airtightness target?
4. What thermal-bridge details are most likely to threaten the 450 mm geometry or G-01 opening order: floor edge, courtyard wall, window reveal, parapet/cornice, threshold, service penetrations?
5. Does the masonry inner leaf deliver useful thermal mass in practice once insulation position, finishes and night purge are considered, or should that benefit be removed from project language?

### Window / opening family

6. Where should the window frame sit through the deep masonry/cavity build-up to balance thermal continuity, drainage, fixing and reveal proportion?
7. Can `OW-01` remain genuinely replaceable without breaking the primary airtight/waterproof layers during frame replacement?
8. What frame/glazing U-value and g-value ranges should be tested first for the G-01 opening areas?
9. Are courtyard-facing openings at risk of condensation / low surface temperature because of reduced solar exposure or deep reveals?

### Low-slope roof / parapet

10. Is the very-low-slope outward-draining roof physically robust as an insulated airtight assembly within the RH-04 cornice/parapet geometry?
11. Can wall/roof insulation and air-control layers remain continuous at the parapet without an unacceptable thermal bridge?
12. Does the selected drainage fall / tapered-insulation family materially increase inner courtyard roof-edge height or structure depth beyond RH-08's architectural assumption?
13. Can PV mounting be integrated without creating unacceptable thermal bridging, waterproofing risk or inaccessible membrane repair?
14. Does keeping all primary roof drainage outside the court improve moisture robustness enough to justify the low-slope family compared with a more conventional pitched alternative?

### Courtyard / daylight

15. Quantify the daylight penalty/benefit of the **3.6 × 4.0 m / ~6.25 m high** courtyard with realistic 400–425 mm walls, reveals and glazing.
16. Do any principal rooms rely too heavily on court-facing daylight after outer-façade openings are modelled?
17. Is the current court likely to create persistent cold/damp surface or drying problems at paving/lower façades despite the explicit drainage strategy?
18. Does courtyard orientation materially change winter heat loss / solar benefit enough to challenge its current geometry?
19. What daylight metric / method should the project use for a real site rather than relying on historical ADF shorthand?

### Overheating / purge

20. Is RH-07 right to treat **dynamic thermal modelling** as the probable Part O route for this G-01/courtyard house rather than sizing architecture around the simplified tables before a site exists?
21. Across the four broad orientation stress cases, which rooms / elevations are the likely overheating drivers?
22. Are the current outer + courtyard multi-aspect openings likely to provide meaningful secure purge, or could noise/security/rain make the apparent cross-ventilation benefit unusable?
23. What external shading families can work with the restrained Georgian-derived openings without becoming cosmetic historicism or permanent technical clutter?
24. Under what plausible London case would mechanical cooling become genuinely necessary after fabric, glazing, shading and purge are optimised?

### Ventilation / airtightness interaction

25. What airtightness range makes central MVHR technically worthwhile for this house, and does that target create special workmanship risk at the courtyard / service penetrations / replaceable openings?
26. Does MVHR interact materially with the thermal-mass / night-purge concept, or are those correctly treated as separate seasonal mechanisms?
27. Is the continuous-MEV fallback still environmentally credible under Future Homes-level fabric/energy expectations if MVHR fails its proportionality gate?

### Heating / energy / PV

28. What design heat-loss range should be expected for a house of this internal area and envelope family, before a real site is known?
29. Does that range support the project's low-temperature passive-emitter baseline without excessive radiator area?
30. Is the approximately **50 m² PV-capable reservation** likely to be enough geometrically under the 2026 L3 methodology after roof offsets, courtyard void, soil vent and maintenance routes are considered?
31. Does the distributed four-field roof create a meaningful annual-output disadvantage that should affect architectural roof geometry before a real orientation exists?
32. What Home Energy Model / SAP transition assumptions should be stated when the real project date is unknown?

## 5. Required red-team cases

### BP-RT1 — wall exceeds 450 mm

Assume the preferred robust wall/junction solution needs **>450 mm** generally.

- Is that genuinely necessary for performance, or can insulation / inner-leaf / detailing change?
- If necessary, identify which RH-02/RH-03 rooms/stair fail first.
- Do not silently reduce clear dimensions.

### BP-RT2 — courtyard daylight failure

Assume simulation shows one or more principal rooms materially underperform because of the court geometry even with credible outer openings.

- Can opening/reveal/glazing changes solve it without overheating?
- If not, state whether the courtyard size/room order must reopen.

### BP-RT3 — west-London overheating case

Assume an east/west real orientation, realistic internal gains and security/noise-constrained night ventilation.

- Can shading/glazing/opening control solve overheating without cooling?
- If not, which architectural coordinate changes before mechanical cooling is reserved?

### BP-RT4 — parapet / low-roof thermal failure

Assume the roof/parapet junction cannot achieve robust thermal/air/water continuity within the current shallow edge.

- Can the edge detail deepen inward/upward without damaging the cornice/court daylight?
- At what point should the roof family reopen?

### BP-RT5 — Future Homes PV geometry fails

Assume the usable roof area after required offsets / maintenance / penetrations cannot deliver the required renewable output.

- Can array layout / efficiency / curtilage generation solve it?
- If not, does roof geometry need architectural change?

## 6. Rewind conditions

Treat the following as likely RH rewinds if confirmed:

1. wall build-up materially exceeds **450 mm** generally;
2. floor/roof thermal build-up materially alters controlled room heights / cornice geometry;
3. principal-room daylight cannot be made credible without changing court/room/opening geometry;
4. Part O / comfort requires mechanical cooling because current opening/shading architecture is intrinsically inadequate;
5. the low-slope roof/parapet cannot be made robust without materially different roof geometry;
6. the selected window family cannot deliver thermal/air/water continuity and replacement within the current masonry opening;
7. Future Homes renewable requirements cannot be met within the available roof/curtilage strategy without material architectural change;
8. courtyard moisture/drying consequences prove materially worse than RH-07/RH-08 assume.

Changing insulation products, glazing specification, membrane family, local thermal-break details or modelling inputs within the same architecture is **not** automatically a rewind.
