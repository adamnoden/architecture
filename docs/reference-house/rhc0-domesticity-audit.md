# Reference House — RH-C0 Domesticity Audit

**Status:** RH-C0.5 — **PASS**  
**Date:** 2026-10-12  
**Baseline:** [`model-baseline-rhc0.md`](model-baseline-rhc0.md)  
**Programme:** [`../computational/reference-house-model-freeze-rhc0.md`](../computational/reference-house-model-freeze-rhc0.md)

This audit deliberately reads the current Reference House as an ordinary home rather than as a demonstration of House Kernel. It gives no credit for serviceability, courtyard form, G-01 composition or technical legibility unless those things improve the house enough to justify their area, cost and complexity.

The comparator is excellent ordinary domestic architecture: rooms that furnish naturally, routes that do not demand explanation, useful storage, daylight from sensible places, and technical systems that stay subordinate to daily life.

## Disposition vocabulary

- **PASS** — convincing at current architectural resolution.
- **PASS WITH CONDITION** — credible, but only if the stated constraint is protected.
- **PROVISIONAL** — current geometry is plausible but cannot be honestly closed without later technical/product/site work.
- **REWIND** — a current design decision is architecturally defective and must return to its owning RH gate before freeze.

## Executive finding

**The current house survives the domestic red-team without a morphology rewind.** It is not a small or efficiency-maximised four-bedroom house; at **230.4 m² enclosed gross** it is a generous four-bedroom courtyard house whose court, circulation and maintainability consume real area. That is acceptable only because the resulting principal rooms, study, two living territories, dry storage and service geography remain useful rather than residual.

Three conditions matter most before freeze:

1. the **450 mm wall case is a stress boundary, not a comfortable domestic baseline**—especially around the stair/front-centre wing;
2. the **garden-dining room works as a linked everyday dining/circulation room**, not as a grand isolated formal dining room;
3. the **utility/WC split is not proven** and must not be frozen as though the current schematic subdivision were an accessible, appliance-resolved plan.

No House Kernel feature currently forces enlargement of the shell. If later technical work asks for more area, simplification or local rearrangement must be tried before principal-room area or shell size is surrendered.

## 1. Area and proportionality

| Subject | Current condition | Domestic judgement |
|---|---|---|
| enclosed gross area | 115.2 m²/storey; 230.4 m² over two storeys | **PASS WITH CONDITION** — generous for four bedrooms, not conventionally compact |
| open court | 14.4 m² footprint removed from each enclosed plate | expensive spatial choice; must continue to earn orientation/light/sequence value |
| ground service suite | 11.6 m² gross | proportionate at ~5% of whole-house enclosed gross, provided plant does not expand |
| two living rooms + study | drawing ~20.3 m² gross; family ~24.4 m²; study ~9.9 m² | useful programme rather than symmetry filler |
| four bedrooms | ~14.3–24.4 m² gross reservation range | credible family-house hierarchy, not a forced five-bedroom plan |

The phrase **“relatively compact”** remains defensible only against the selected courtyard/serviceability brief, not against ordinary UK four-bedroom houses. RH-C0 therefore rejects any future argument that another technical zone, extra corridor, larger plant room or ceremonial space is harmless because the house is “compact.” The current area is already generous enough that further growth requires a specific programme reason.

This is a judgement, not a compiler maximum-area rule.

## 2. Furniture and usable clearances

### Drawing room — `G-DRAW-02`

**PASS.** The gross reservation of about 20.3 m² is large enough for an ordinary sofa/chair grouping without furniture becoming circulation. The RH-03 200 mm hall correction locally notches the room but does not destroy its useful centre. Its value is domestic only if it remains a genuinely retreat-capable living room rather than a front-facing ceremonial room used to justify the façade.

No technical route currently crosses its principal wall field. That is a real benefit, not infrastructure theatre.

### Family room — `G-FAM-02`

**PASS.** At about 24.4 m² gross it is the strongest everyday living room. It has enough depth to take seating, media/books/storage and ordinary family clutter without making the route toward the garden/dining territory pass through the furniture group.

The room should not be subdivided later merely to recover symmetry or add another technical zone.

### Garden dining — `G-GARDEN-02`

**PASS WITH CONDITION.** The gross reservation is only 3.6 × 3.4 m (12.24 m²). Under the conservative 425–450 mm envelope tests, its north↔court clear depth is roughly **2.55–2.50 m** before local finish/interface effects. A normal six-seat table still fits at planning level, but this is the least forgiving principal social room.

It works because it is a **linked garden dining room / passage between family, kitchen, court and garden**. It would be weak if treated as a self-contained formal dining room requiring large circulation margins, sideboards and multiple inward door swings.

Protect:

- a simple furniture-free route through or beside the dining setting;
- a generous garden opening;
- connections to kitchen and family that do not consume the room with door-leaf arcs;
- no service cabinetry or technical access panels on its principal furniture walls unless later coordination proves they are harmless.

Do **not** enlarge the shell to make this room grander. If later doors/joinery make it fail, simplify those interfaces first.

### Kitchen — `G-KIT-02`

**PASS WITH CONDITION.** The 14.28 m² gross reservation is a credible family kitchen, but not an oversized show kitchen. Its value is the adjacency to garden dining, family territory and the service flank. The older cabinetry sketch proves only that a plausible run/island study existed; RH-C0 does not treat that furniture as current geometric proof under the RH-10 wall family.

A later kitchen layout should preserve ordinary work triangles, appliance-door clearance and access to the garden/dining route without requiring the room to grow into the court or plant zone.

### Study — `G-STUDY-02`

**PASS.** About 9.9 m² gross is sufficient for a real home office/study and potentially occasional guest use, while remaining small enough not to distort the house programme. It does not need to become a fifth bedroom to justify itself.

## 3. Bedrooms

**PASS WITH CONDITION.** All four bedrooms are architecturally credible as bedrooms rather than labels placed into leftover ring segments.

- `U-BED-P01` (~24.4 m² gross) is comfortably principal-bedroom scale and now benefits from the RH-06 decision to replace the remote en-suite with dry dressing/storage rather than forcing long wet routes.
- `U-BED-02` (~20.3 m² gross) is a generous secondary double.
- `U-BED-03` and `U-BED-04` (~14.3 m² gross each) are ordinary double-bedroom scale. They are the rooms most sensitive to later wall-face, wardrobe and door decisions, but their current gross proportions are not inherently defective.

The important domestic rule is that **wardrobe capacity and a normal bed must coexist with the room door and outer escape/window zone**. RH-C0 does not invent a numeric wardrobe rule; exact joinery and door positions are not yet controlled geometry.

All bedrooms retain an outer façade. The courtyard is therefore not being used to excuse bedrooms that depend solely on a deep shaded light court.

---

## 4. Door swings and circulation

**PROVISIONAL, no topology defect found.** Current RH drawings contain connection/opening hints but do not author a complete internal door schedule, leaf width or swing geometry. It would be false precision to “audit” exact clashes from those pixels.

At topology level there is no unavoidable conflict:

- drawing and family rooms can be entered without crossing the stair;
- kitchen/garden-dining/family can form a connected daily-use cluster;
- study and service rooms can be reached from the hall/gallery side;
- upper bedrooms can be reached from the south/east/north three-sided circulation without a west corridor;
- the family bathroom remains on the gallery/service side rather than opening through a bedroom.

The absence of exact door geometry is **not** permission for RH-C1 to invent it. Internal door leaf positions/swings remain outside the first slice unless an owning architectural gate deliberately authors them. RH-C1 can encode room topology and major controlled openings without pretending a final joinery plan exists.

Domestic red flag for later detail: the compact `G-GARDEN-02`, south utility/WC band and upper east bedrooms have the least tolerance for multiple inward swings. Pocket/sliding doors should not be adopted merely to rescue a poor layout; first choose sensible hinged-door positions and directions.

---

## 5. Stair, arrival and upper circulation

### Ground arrival / stair

**PASS WITH CONDITION.** The RH-03 stair correction was worthwhile: moving the front drawing/hall boundary west by ~200 mm creates a usable arrival passage beside the stair without moving the shell or court.

The current 2.4–2.5 m stair reservation remains close to the available front-centre clear depth under the wall stress cases:

- 425 mm envelope test → about 2.55 m clear front↔court field;
- 450 mm stress case → about 2.50 m.

Therefore **450 mm is the edge of the current planning idea, not evidence of generous fit**. Exact strings, guarding, handrails, landing edges, finishes and headroom remain technical/architectural detail. If those make the 450 mm case fail, RH-10/RH-03 must reopen rather than shaving the 1.0 m stair target or lowering ceilings.

### Upper circulation

**PASS.** The south landing → east court gallery → north cross-landing route is longer than a simple central landing, but it is not dead circulation. It provides:

- orientation to the courtyard;
- access to the bathroom/service edge;
- a clear route to the rear bedrooms/principal suite;
- separation between bedrooms without taking a west-side gallery from the principal rooms.

The design made the correct simplification at RH-03 by **not** completing a four-sided upper gallery. Adding one later for symmetry would be a domestic regression.

## 6. Storage, laundry and mundane domestic work

### Upper dry storage

**PASS.** Replacing the unproven principal en-suite with `U-DRESS-01` plus `U-LIN-01` is a strong domestic improvement. It gives the house useful dry storage and reduces wet-route complexity simultaneously.

### Entrance / coats / shoes / cleaning

**PASS WITH CONDITION.** The current model does not author a dedicated coat/boot cupboard or cleaner's cupboard. That is a real domestic obligation, but not a reason to add another room or compiler rule.

The hall/service joinery must eventually accommodate:

- everyday coats and shoes near arrival;
- vacuum/cleaning equipment;
- household consumables that do not belong in kitchen cabinetry;
- linen overflow only if upper linen proves insufficient.

If later detailed planning cannot provide these within the existing hall/service geography, RH-02/RH-06 should be revisited before any shell growth.

### Utility / WC band

**PROVISIONAL — highest mundane-use risk.** RH-06 controls only a **2.9 × 1.8 m gross** south service band shared between laundry/cleaning utility, entrance-storey WC and local storage. The current split is explicitly adjustable. RH-11 already asks whether M4(1) WC geometry can coexist with the utility function.

RH-C0 therefore refuses to freeze the current schematic utility/WC subdivision. The priority if space tightens is:

1. preserve a credible entrance-storey WC/access route;
2. preserve actual laundry/cleaning capability;
3. simplify cabinetry/ancillary equipment;
4. use adjacent service/plant geography intelligently if technically possible;
5. enlarge the architectural service block only after those options fail.

A beautiful service diagram that cannot fit a washing machine door, laundry handling and an ordinary WC is not a successful House Kernel house.

---

## 7. Daylight, court dependence and ordinary outlook

**PASS WITH CONDITION.** The environmental work correctly defines the 3.6 × 4.0 m, ~6.25 m-high court as a **deep shaded light court**, with H/W roughly 1.56–1.74. It is not a sunny garden.

The plan remains domestically credible because principal habitable rooms and all bedrooms retain meaningful outer-façade potential. The court contributes diffuse light, orientation, cross/purge opportunities and a secondary outlook; it is not asked to rescue deep principal rooms with no external aspect.

Protect this hierarchy. If a later elevation iteration reduces outer-façade openings because “the room also has the courtyard,” that is a design regression.

Exact daylight adequacy remains outside RH-C1 and requires real opening geometry, orientation/site and simulation.

---

## 8. Courtyard usefulness, quality and cost

**PASS, but as a project-specific luxury rather than doctrine.** The court survives the adversarial review for four reasons:

1. it gives the otherwise compact ring an immediate orientation device;
2. it strengthens the front → court → garden sequence;
3. it gives circulation/service-side rooms useful diffuse light and purge potential;
4. it creates an intimate outdoor/light space distinct from the main garden.

It also imposes real penalties:

- 14.4 m² of footprint is removed from each enclosed storey;
- roughly 15.2 m of additional internal façade perimeter exists at each level;
- four extra corners, more glazing/interfaces, drainage and maintenance access must be built and kept dry;
- its depth means shade and damp risk are structurally part of the idea, not exceptions.

It therefore receives **no House Kernel credit** merely for being legible or maintainable. It is retained because the brief explicitly selects a real courtyard and the current plan makes it do several architectural jobs.

Do not add arcades, fountains, elaborate service access, retractable roofs or other machinery merely to “activate” it. Shade-tolerant planting, durable paving/drainage and simple openings are enough unless later architecture earns more.

If future cost/compactness pressure requires one major project-specific sacrifice, the courtyard should be openly re-questioned as a brief choice rather than protected as doctrine.

---

## 9. Service / plant / riser proportionality

**PASS WITH CONDITION.** The service geography is substantial but not yet bullying the house.

- gross service suite = 11.6 m², ~5% of two-storey enclosed gross;
- plant room = ~6.4 m² gross reservation within it;
- `SV-01` = ~0.63 m² clear reservation, technically unproved;
- galleries/circulation also carry dry-route value but are not dedicated technical corridors.

The key success is that plant, wet services and replacement access are concentrated on one flank without pushing primary wet routes through drawing/family/bedroom walls. The key risk is that the plant/riser concept may later ask for more room once real equipment, fire/acoustic closure and working clearances are known.

The domestic answer is already established by RH-06/RH-10 and is reaffirmed here: **simplify services before enlarging permanent service geography**. MVHR may fall back to continuous MEV; ancillary equipment may be omitted; local routes may split. The principal rooms do not owe the plant room future expansion.

---

## 10. Dead circulation and technical geography

**PASS.** The house has more circulation than a compact solid-plan four-bedroom home, but little of it is purposeless:

- the ground east gallery is both ordinary circulation and dry-service route;
- the upper east gallery/north landing are circulation plus court orientation and service/bath access;
- the hall/stair is a real arrival sequence rather than a corridor-only field;
- no west gallery is retained for symmetry;
- the court itself is not counted as an internal circulation shortcut or emergency-exit fiction.

What would fail the test later:

- adding a west gallery merely to complete the ring;
- widening circulation because services are awkward rather than fixing the service route;
- treating maintenance access as a second household circulation system;
- inserting visible access panels or grilles throughout principal rooms just to demonstrate serviceability.

---

## 11. “Simplification” check

Several later decisions are genuine simplifications in lived use and should be protected:

- **principal en-suite deleted** → fewer wet routes, more useful dressing/storage;
- **continuous ordinary principal-room floors** → no raised-floor theatre in living rooms;
- **W0 direct-finished masonry baseline** → ordinary walls remain ordinary;
- **W1 selective, W2 experimental only** → serviceability does not require removable lining everywhere;
- **no permanent roof hatch** → routine roof maintenance uses ordinary external access rather than sacrificing internal space to a rarely used technical route;
- **outward roof drainage** → failures remain at the external perimeter rather than overloading the court;
- **central MVHR with MEV fallback** → ventilation ideology is allowed to yield if the duct topology becomes disproportionate.

None should be reversed merely because a more elaborate option expresses House Kernel more visibly.

---

## 12. G-01 / Georgian leakage test

**PASS.** G-01 currently influences the Reference House where it belongs: façade bay/order, hierarchy, opening families and architectural character. It does not currently determine House Kernel doctrine, service topology, courtyard necessity, structural system or universal compiler rules.

The four-bay front, entrance on bay 3 and Georgian-derived window families are **fixture-specific authored facts**. RH-C1 may encode them because this Reference House uses G-01; it must not encode “Georgian four-bay house” as a House Kernel invariant.

Generalise the reason; localise the taste.

---

## 13. House Kernel theatre test

**PASS.** After RH-06→RH-10 simplifications, the present house would still be intelligible if the reader knew nothing about House Kernel:

- the rooms have ordinary names and uses;
- plant is in a plant room rather than dispersed as spectacle;
- walls/floors are mostly conventional to occupy;
- the service gallery is also a normal route;
- the riser is hidden but accessible;
- the roof drains outward for ordinary failure reasons;
- the court is an architectural choice rather than a technical machine.

The remaining high-risk theatrical move would be allowing future serviceability ambition to enlarge `SV-01`, plant, access zones or visible interfaces at the expense of domestic space. RH-C0 explicitly forbids treating that as automatic progress.

## RH-C0.5 gate result

**PASS.** A fresh domestic reading finds a credible house rather than a technical diagram in search of occupants. No current defect is strong enough to justify moving rooms, enlarging the shell or deleting the selected courtyard before freeze. The important uncertainties—door leaf geometry, utility/WC fit, final stair construction, exact clear dimensions and technical plant/riser packing—are now explicitly bounded rather than quietly claimed as solved.

The audit makes no claim that the house is construction-ready, M4-compliant, daylight-compliant or technically validated. Those matters remain outside the evidence available at M3.
