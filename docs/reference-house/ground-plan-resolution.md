# Ground-floor coordination and stair resolution

This is a provisional architectural successor to the deliberately incomplete
earlier coordination plan. The numeric choice register is
[`compiler/reference-house/plan-resolution.ts`](../../compiler/reference-house/plan-resolution.ts).
Its source factory passes through the existing whole-house plan, structure,
services, boundary and selected stair-geometry checks. Its explicit opening
and wall choices are then adapted through the architectural drawing engine.

The historical coordination fixture and released drawings remain recoverable. They do
not establish the missing decisions below. The current proposal changes real
architecture; it is not a cosmetic completion of an old drawing. Physical
acceptance, structural design, fire strategy and construction adequacy remain
outside this coordination decision.

the former drawing/family partition and the upper part of the drawing/hall

The WC's represented clear footprint is 2170 × 1625 mm. A provisional 150 mm

The former study's east window moves north and becomes a 900 mm high-sill WC

Guest WC water, foul and extract obligations now belong to this room in the

The courtyard, stair, front facade axes, overall shell and upper-floor rooms
stay at the preceding coordination positions. The 1032.5 mm passage behind the

The following earlier decisions remain applicable except where this room
programme explicitly supersedes the former drawing room and eastern study.

## Rear living rooms

Family, garden dining and kitchen become three occupied zones of one open-plan
rear living field. The two non-loadbearing partitions extending north from
the courtyard corners are removed. Their centred positions previously made
small offsets at the thick courtyard walls, but adjusting those offsets would
preserve partitions that have no compelling spatial purpose here.

The drawing room remains separately closable, connected to the family area by
provisional double doors. This gives the house a quieter front room as well
as the open rear field. The kitchen remains beside the service suite. The
project still needs its dedicated review of cooking extraction and fire/escape
relationships; an open-plan choice does not prove either.

## Service suite and circulation

The study, service suite and kitchen are separate uses. Their shared planning
edges were not all represented by physical walls in the earlier source. Add the service suite's
north and south enclosure walls; retain a doorway from the gallery. These
walls are provisional service-free dry partitions, not loadbearing masonry.

The suite continues to own utility, plant and WC geography from the service-topology study. This
plan resolves its enclosure, not its internal sanitary/appliance layout. A
plant replacement door is explicitly selected on the east wall. Its nominal
aperture is not a certified clear replacement width.

Remove the unnecessary ground-floor division between entrance hall and the
front continuation of the gallery. They are one arrival/circulation field,
which also avoids a partition meeting the entrance aperture. The service-side
partition moves 250 mm east: the represented east gallery is then 1070 mm
clear between the inner envelope face and the 125 mm partition. The upper
south turn is deepened 300 mm so its clear turning field is
1120 mm. These are coordination clearances at the represented wall thickness,
not a completed accessibility or fire assessment.

The change reduces the service suite's gross planning width from 2900 to
2650 mm. Equipment, utility and WC subdivision must fit that actual width;
the former larger gross room must not be used in later capacity claims.

positions are no longer represented as faint host bands; unknown widths no
longer appear as diamonds. Technical uncertainty about frames, seals, lintels,
thresholds and performance remains, but does not require uncertainty about
whether a room has a window or where its door belongs.

central garden door, kitchen window. The courtyard receives paired doors on

Add a 1200 mm east window to the study and a 900 mm east window to the

## Stair: replace an unsupported fit claim

The former three-flight, six-rise-per-flight stair promised five 300 mm goings
per flight, approximately 1000 mm clear flights and full landings inside a
2400–2500 mm square. For the ordinary three-sided U arrangement, the transverse
middle flight and two corner landings require at least
1500 + 1000 + 1000 = 3500 mm. That arrangement cannot fit the stated square.
This falsifies that particular fit claim; it is not a theorem excluding every
possible stair arrangement.

Select a provisional two-flight return stair with 9 + 9 rises instead. Retain
all 18 rises over 3300 mm, the 300 mm going, approximately 1000 mm clear flight
width and full intermediate landing. Eight goings now produce a 2400 mm run;
with the 1000 mm turn landing, the stair runs 3400 mm across the house. Two
parallel 1000 mm flights and a 100 mm well occupy 2100 mm in the other direction.
A 3500 × 2200 mm coordination envelope allows a bounded allowance around the
clear-flight diagram, without selecting strings or guarding construction.

The front drawing/hall boundary moves from X 4000 to X 3100 mm on both floors.
This costs the front drawing-room and west-front-bedroom planning reservations
approximately 3.06 m² each. It is a real cost of retaining generous treads and
full landings. The outer shell and four-bay front order do not move. The courtyard is shortened as described below.

The stair is located west of the entrance arrival. A previous arrangement fit
its bounding box but left only 32.5 mm between its coordination envelope and
the courtyard wall. It blocked the drawing-room approach and did not establish
usable access to the courtyard. That arrangement is rejected; box fit was an
insufficient test of circulation.

Move the stair 50 mm west and 300 mm south, to X 3250 / Y 500 mm. Move the
courtyard's south edge 700 mm north, keeping its other three edges fixed. Its
new planning size is **3600 × 3300 mm**, formerly 3600 × 4000 mm: area falls
from 14.40 to 11.88 m², a loss of 2.52 m² (17.5%). This buys a **1032.5 mm clear
cross passage** behind the entire stair envelope. Both the courtyard door and
the relocated drawing-room doorway are reached through that passage, without
assuming passage under a flight. Ground-route ceiling datum remains 2950 mm;
headroom along the stair itself and its floor-opening construction remain
unproved. A smaller courtyard also requires renewed daylight and courtyard
elevation review; no previous environmental result is transferred automatically.

The drawing-room doorway moves to Y 3150 mm. A short horizontal return joins
the hall partition to the courtyard's south-west corner, ending the formerly
floating wall. Retain the drawing/family division at Y 5000 mm: pushing it
south would take further useful area from the drawing room without providing
the missing passage. Against the immediately previous proposal, the drawing
room loses another 0.77 m² gross planning area; the family area is unchanged.

The study/service boundary stays at Y 3400 mm, independent of the moved
courtyard edge. Study and service-suite areas therefore remain 9.01 and
10.60 m² gross, respectively. Keep the corresponding upper bedroom/bathroom
boundary at that datum. The first-floor arrival has a full 1000 mm field
east of the stair and connects north into the same cross-passage geometry,
then east above the upper south-turn partition. The front door is hinged on
its eastern jamb so its open leaf does not divide the stair arrival field.
These are coordination fields, not certified clear doorset widths.

The source and tests explicitly reject the previous passage, a door-only move,
and the wrong entrance handing. The bounded route check reports its measured
fields and does not claim a general navigation or accessibility solver.

The ordinary private-stair rise/going calculation passes on this successor,
as do the existing bounded structural/service/boundary topology checks. They
do not prove stair support, guarding, floor-opening framing, actual headroom,
fire/escape performance or finished-room furnishing. Those remain technical
obligations. The ground plan shows a 1200 mm representation cut, dashed
above-cut treads and an UP arrow; the companion first-floor drawing shows DN.

## Wall representation

Keep the joined topology, white cavity and quiet distinction between masonry
leaves. Add restrained fine boundaries at the leaf/cavity interfaces. Do not
introduce brick-by-brick lines: they would select an unowned bond and produce
noise at 1:100. No masonry hatch is selected for this whole-house candidate;
the lighter interface outlines earn the needed distinction with fewer marks.
