# Reference House — architectural design brief

This brief selects the architectural direction for the current house. It is a
project design decision within G-01 v0.1, not a new grammar rule or a technical
approval. The current plan and section remain the field of design.

## Character

A settled English masonry house around a lighter, quieter court. The exterior
should have the presence of a small classical domestic building; the interior
should feel comfortable in ordinary occupation rather than ceremonial. Depth,
daylight, true joinery and a few good materials carry the character. Elaborate
mechanisms, ornamental abundance and imitation age do not.

The front is the public address, the garden side the shared domestic address,
the court the intimate outdoor room. They belong to one house but need not have
identical material or ornamental intensity. The court's warmer inflection is
local: light mineral wall surfaces, stone underfoot and a limited opportunity
for green glazed ceramic. An arcade, fountain, decorative arch, water feature
or tiled court is not required. Do not distort the accepted rectangular openings
to manufacture a historical allusion.

## Current field and its limits

The controlling house is `RH-LAYOUT-REVISION-02`, coordinated by
`RH-LAYOUT-FAMILY-02`. Read [the accepted layout](layout-revision-02.md) and
[the coordinated drawing family](../publication/architectural-plate-layout-family.md),
not the older square-house or mansard description as though it were current.

The field includes the 12 m square shell, centred 3.5 m courtyard, ground/first
floors at 0/3450 mm, 3100 mm clear ceilings, west stair and gallery, eastern
service geography, four bedrooms, entrance void/bridge and two court doors on
the front-to-garden axis. Opening occurrences come from
`revisionOpenings()`; finished dimensions and boundary duties are not inferred
from a window symbol.

The current roof is a **twin-pitched ring**, falling inward to the court and
outward to the perimeter. The earlier outward-only roof narrative is
superseded for this design field. No flat roof, dormer, loft stair or roof window
is introduced. Collection, overflow and safe maintenance of the inner eaves
remain unresolved. Calling this an outward-only roof would conceal a material
moisture obligation.

The nominal 417.5 mm envelope, 125 mm partitions and fully allocated 350 mm
intermediate floor zone are planning/representation constraints. They do not
contain free space for extra panelling, grounds, acoustic layers, frames or
service cavities. Visible articulation must be developed with real depth;
where the depth cannot fit, propose reallocation or an explicit source rewind.

## Spatial hierarchy

| Place | Rank and intended experience | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Front approach and entrance | Principal public threshold; recognisable without spectacle | Concentrate exterior doorcase detail here; preserve the accepted opening and entrance axis |
| Entrance hall, void and bridge | Arrival and orientation; the court is discovered beyond | Give the vertical volume a coherent wall and guard composition; do not add a pendant, chandelier or gallery furniture before clearances are resolved |
| Court | Intimate outdoor room and daylight focus | Let light and wall depth dominate; keep the two-ended route clear; do not fill the small court with permanent objects |
| Family/dining and garden threshold | Principal everyday shared interior | Compose the whole open rear space before subdividing it with finishes, lighting or ceilings; distinguish uses without rebuilding the accepted plan |
| Stair and west gallery | A legible, calm daily route | Borne handrail/guard expression, clear arrivals and useful light; architectural rank does not author a larger stair |
| Kitchen and pantry | Working domestic architecture contiguous with family/dining | A quieter cabinetry field within the shared rear composition; useful work surfaces, reachable storage and a clear pantry approach outrank a display island |
| Study | Small private retreat with two front windows | A coherent desk/storage wall and restrained articulation; do not borrow circulation space or obstruct a window to create a library image |
| Bedrooms and walk-in wardrobe | Private rooms of equal dignity, differing capacity | Privacy, bed approach and usable storage first; the principal suite earns richer joinery only where the actual wall field permits |
| Bathrooms and guest WC | Calm rooms with privacy and clear wet/dry duties | Make the service face architectural on the maintainable side; do not hide unresolved access in tiled walls |
| Utility and plant | Explicit working rooms | Access, robust surfaces, local isolation and legible identification; no fake domestic panelling over plant |
| General circulation | Subordinate connective space with real service duties | Repeated quiet trim; preserve walking width and dry-side access rather than turning galleries into display corridors |

Equal floor heights are retained. Hierarchy comes from route, room role,
openings, outlook and concentrated articulation; it does not require unequal
ceilings or a universal room ratio. The entrance void is already selected
geometry, not permission to add further theatrical voids.

## Composition rules for this house

1. **Wall before module.** Compose corners, openings, furniture and usable wall
   fields first. A removable field may use those divisions; a manufacturer's
   grid does not command the room.
2. **Few real datums.** Skirting, door heads and a selected upper termination
   give order. A dado or picture rail needs a real architectural or hanging
   role. Do not add all of them to every room.
3. **Concentrate detail.** The entrance and principal shared rooms may carry
   richer joinery; private rooms are quieter; service rooms remain carefully
   resolved. Ornament is not a proxy for dignity.
4. **Continuous floor as control.** Principal dry rooms begin with a continuous
   conventional floor. No universal raised floor, visible grid or mandatory
   threshold strip is selected.
5. **Actual depth.** Architraves, skirtings and cornices may cover adjustment or
   movement interfaces; they cannot make an unallocated construction depth
   disappear or become the primary water/air boundary.
6. **Room-wide lighting.** Daylight, shade, local task light and a restrained
   ambient family precede decorative fittings. Avoid a uniform ceiling grid
   and obligatory coves. Actual positions follow furniture, access, structure
   and the selected service routes.
7. **Functional metal.** Brass/bronze is appropriate at touch, wear, indexing
   and selected working covers. No brass grid or compulsory metal seam
   announces every removable part.
8. **Quiet technology.** Real controls and grilles join a wall/joinery field.
   Concealment is legitimate only with a reachable release, a viable working
   side and a credible operation after ordinary furnishing.
9. **Material truth.** Timber, stone, masonry, plaster/mineral finish, paint and
   legitimate veneer are available. No false stone lintel, counterfeit
   structural joint or decorative access outline implies a role it lacks.
10. **Water remains physical.** Sills, drips, eaves, thresholds and wall bases
    retain real water duties. Calm appearance cannot erase a visible overflow,
    drying route or maintainable rainwater component.

These select a design direction, not final moulding sections, product sizes,
hardware, appliance arrangements, lighting levels or a performance class.

## Candidate palette and comparison boundary

The leading exterior direction is warm red/brown brick with restrained pale
mineral/stone accents, quiet painted opening joinery and a dark pitched roof.
The court may use a lighter mineral field and limited ceramic colour. The
leading interior direction is warm mineral/plaster walls, painted true joinery,
timber in principal dry rooms and mineral surfaces at wet/wearing interfaces.

These are **candidates** to develop against the real rooms and IAS/OAS
assemblies, then reconcile at material synthesis. No species, stone product,
roof covering, coating, exact colour or universal room palette is frozen here.
Compare a simpler mineral-wall solution before adding a removable face; compare
quiet painted joinery before adding elaborate panelling. Red brick and timber
frames are not G-01 invariants. The earlier aluminium window comparator remains
eligible until OAS and HAD have compared actual frame/sightline consequences.

## Repose and rejection tests

Review a candidate in ordinary furnished use as well as in an empty drawing.

- Does the occupant understand arrival, privacy, route, shade and controls?
- Do visible masses and guard/handrail elements read as supported and settled?
- Is a small room made materially less usable by joinery or ornament?
- Does a panel, fitting, grille or seam demand attention without earning it?
- Can doors, storage, service access and local controls operate after furnishing?
- Does removal sacrifice brittle finish or an independent safety/environmental
  boundary that the image pretends is untouched?
- Does weathering, cleaning, wear or patina improve legibility or expose a
  misleading material claim?
- Does the court remain an outdoor room when drainage and maintenance are
  represented honestly?

Reject a design that needs invisible thickness, obstructed access, false
historical construction, an impossible maintenance reach or an unacknowledged
plan change. Technical conflict can require a bounded architectural rewind;
aesthetic preference cannot dismiss that conflict.

## Ownership and onward design

HAD owns intended visible composition. IAS owns interior assembly mechanics,
service/access fronts and finish terminations; OAS owns opening mounting,
replacement and environmental transitions. The shared interface contract must
reconcile them before candidate fan-out. The selected-house gate follows actual
junction and material synthesis, not merely three attractive independent views.

MRA's [moisture questions](../../project/mra/mra-00-moisture-constitution-threat-model.md)
are carried into candidate reviews. HAD identifies form, concentration,
maintenance and visible failure consequences; it does not certify drainage,
structure, acoustics, fire, energy or moisture performance.

HRS later absorbs construction-resolution and moisture-assurance findings.
PLT represents decisions that have acquired design authority. Neither is asked
to invent unresolved architecture from this brief.
