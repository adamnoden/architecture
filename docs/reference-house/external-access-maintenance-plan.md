# Reference House — External Access & Maintenance Plan

**Status:** project design-control document; RH-08 spatial coordination + RH-10 energy/maintenance additions incorporated  
**Purpose:** translate Principle 5 and the external-maintenance research into project-specific tasks while preserving the distinction between architectural access conditions and later contractor methods.

**Current authorities:** [RH-08 Roof, Drainage and External Maintenance](roof-drainage-access-rh08.md), [roof/drainage plan](roof-drainage-plan-rh08.svg), [maintenance-access diagram](external-maintenance-access-rh08.svg), and [RH-10 Energy, Heating and Renewable Coordination](energy-heat-coordination-rh10.md).

## 1. Objective

The Reference House should be inspectable, cleanable, repairable and renewable using credible ordinary methods, without avoidable damage to permanent fabric or avoidable dependence on specialist access.

This is not a future contractor's method statement. It defines the architectural conditions within which safe maintenance must remain possible.

The central test is:

> **Can a competent future trade perform the foreseeable task using ordinary appropriate equipment without first improvising around the architecture?**

The canonical patterns most directly implicated are [`PAT-013 — Ground-Supported Façade Access`](../patterns/ground-supported-facade-access.md), [`PAT-011 — Roof Maintenance Route`](../patterns/roof-maintenance-route.md), [`PAT-009 — Accessible Rainwater Route`](../patterns/accessible-rainwater-route.md) and [`PAT-007 — Permanent Opening / Replaceable Window`](../patterns/permanent-opening-replaceable-window.md).

RH-08 created project occurrences for the first three. RH-09/RH-10 now also establish the representative replaceable-window family and later energy equipment whose maintenance must use the same geography.

## 2. Required project deliverable

By developed design, maintain one coordinated **External Access & Maintenance Plan** covering:

- external and courtyard elevations;
- roof slopes, ridges/watersheds, penetrations, PV fields and rainwater goods;
- external heat-source equipment;
- site/service entrance and side/rear access;
- perimeter support conditions;
- gates, passages and changes in level;
- significant permanent planting and mature tree envelopes;
- drains and chambers affecting access;
- neighbouring boundaries and foreseeable highway dependency.

Read the plan with the elevations and roof drawings, not as a facilities appendix produced after the geometry is fixed.

## 3. Access hierarchy for the house

For each task, test options in this order:

1. perform it from inside or at ground level where practical;
2. use an existing safe architectural position;
3. use ordinary collective access such as scaffold, tower or suitable mobile equipment;
4. use a ladder only where the task genuinely suits one;
5. use specialist access only where architecture and site constraints justify it.

Different elevations may use different methods.

## 4. Required maintenance scenarios

At minimum, draw and review the following.

### A. Upper-storey masonry repair

Assume periodic repointing or local brick/stone repair. Check scaffold or tower support positions, projections, ground conditions, material route, neighbour/highway dependence and whether the task remains ordinary rather than becoming specialist by accident.

### B. Upper-storey window cleaning and routine service

Test whether glass cleaning and ironmongery service can occur from inside where appropriate. Do not confuse cleanability with full maintainability.

### C. Full upper-storey window replacement

For a representative `OW-01` window family, draw the complete route:

**room / opening → release of removable internal trim/fixings → frame withdrawal → handling route → site entrance / delivery route**, with external scaffold/tower assistance where the frame/product requires it.

Coordinate external lifting with the façade-access strategy.

### D. Gutter and eaves renewal

Test inspection, clearing, local repair and full replacement separately. Confirm that planting, PV and projections do not consume the selected access position.

RH-08 fixes the primary roof collection at the **outer perimeter**, with no courtyard roof gutter.

### E. High-roof / penetration repair

The current roof has no selected chimney, rooflight or rooftop plant. The soil-vent penetration and RH-10 PV mounting/cable interfaces are the current roof service conditions. Any later chimney, rooflight or rooftop plant reopens the access review.

### F. Blocked roof outlet / rainwater failure

Assume an urgent inspection after abnormal discharge or blockage. RH-08 requires visible outward overflow behaviour at outer gutters and an independent courtyard emergency overflow. PV layouts must not obstruct the inspection line or conceal overflow evidence.

### G. Courtyard upper façade

Assume repointing, coping work and full-frame window replacement. The court itself remains the working area for a dismantled mobile tower brought through the north garden-dining logistics route.

### H. Mature-landscape replay

Repeat the review using the intended mature size of major trees and permanent planting. Identify what can be cut back or removed and which Category A positions must remain substantially open.

### I. `ASHP-01` service and replacement — RH-10

Assume routine service, coil/terminal cleaning, component replacement and eventual whole-unit replacement.

The current working unit is in the **middle-to-rear east/service-side field beside `G-PLANT-01`**, subject to real-site acoustic and airflow design.

Require:

- ground-level working access without entering a principal room;
- direct route to the side/rear equipment path;
- replaceable local `PEN-HS-01` service connections;
- no permanent landscape, store or screen that prevents withdrawal;
- any acoustic screen to remain removable and to preserve the manufacturer's airflow/working clearances;
- a useful human/equipment passage along the east side after the unit is installed.

If the real site cannot provide both the unit and useful side passage, move the unit toward a rear service position before sacrificing the sole maintenance route.

### J. PV inspection / module replacement — RH-10

The roof now reserves approximately **50 m² of PV-capable field** as a forward-design condition.

Test:

- array-edge and drainage inspection;
- access to the soil-vent penetration;
- module removal / replacement route;
- mounting / waterproofing inspection;
- cable-route and roof-crossing inspection;
- safe material handling from the selected external scaffold/tower access.

PV does **not** by itself reverse the no-hatch decision. Current external access already provides roof arrival, edge protection, working platform and material handling; a hatch would add only another permanent roof interface unless future task analysis proves otherwise.

## 5. Ground plane, wall base and temporary support

Do **not** assume one universal “perimeter dry zone”. Wall-base drying, drainage, inspection and maintenance-support territory are distinct functions that can conflict.

Instead coordinate them deliberately:

- identify where ordinary scaffold/tower support positions are valuable;
- identify wall-base inspection and splashback/moisture concerns separately;
- design drainage from actual levels, soils, foundations and discharge routes rather than assuming gravel or a French drain is beneficial;
- allow one piece of ground geometry to serve several functions only where those functions are technically compatible;
- keep critical maintenance support territory free of uniquely difficult permanent obstructions.

Record where temporary support would bear over drains/chambers; where landscape must remain removable; and where temporary loads require structural coordination.

RH-08 identifies the east/service side, west representative access positions, rear working line and courtyard tower position as **Category A** maintenance geography. RH-10 adds the ASHP working/withdrawal footprint to the east-side coordination without allowing it to consume that strip.

## 6. Bays, porticos and projections

The Reference House may retain Georgian-derived projections. Review each against its intended maintenance method before freezing the geometry.

Record what work the projection complicates, whether conventional temporary works can pass or bridge it, whether a modest dimensional change materially improves access, and whether any remaining complexity is worth the architectural value gained.

This is not a veto on projections. It is a whole-life consequence check.

No large front portico is currently required by the controlled Reference House baseline.

## 7. Roof strategy — current controlled resolution

RH-08 worked backwards from actual roof tasks; RH-10 adds PV tasks without changing the access hierarchy.

### Selected roof-access position

- no permanent roof hatch in the current baseline;
- no speculative permanent anchor/fall-arrest system;
- primary roof access by planned ground-supported scaffold/tower from outer elevations;
- courtyard tower for rare court-side coping/façade work;
- roof entry, where required, remains a task-specific temporary-works decision with edge protection.

This decision remains proportionate because the selected roof has:

- no inner courtyard gutter;
- no internal roof outlet;
- no rooftop mechanical plant;
- no rooflight;
- no chimney;
- one soil/vent penetration;
- PV modules/mountings whose relatively infrequent maintenance can use the same planned external access.

A hatch would add weather/air/thermal/security debt without providing the edge protection and working platform that those roof tasks require.

### Selected roof drainage relation

Four low-slope roof fields fall outward from the courtyard toward external collection edges. This deliberately removes primary roof drainage from the building centre.

PV array geometry must preserve the drainage/overflow and inspection logic rather than forcing concealed transfers around modules.

Reconsider the access strategy if later design adds genuinely frequent rooftop maintenance or equipment.

## 8. Courtyard logistics route — controlled resolution

Treat the garden-to-courtyard connection as both architectural circulation and latent maintenance logistics.

The preferred logistics path is:

**rear garden → garden-dining room → courtyard**.

At technical design, preserve a **1200 mm clear-opening coordination target** at the controlling door/opening where the selected architectural door family can provide it. A larger fully opening pair is preferable where compatible with RH-04/RH-10 composition.

Test the path with:

- dismantled mobile-tower components;
- full opening replacements;
- coping/rainwater components;
- masonry materials;
- temporary protection;
- waste removal.

The **3.6 × 4.0 m courtyard** remains the working area. Do not install a permanent fountain, fixed raised planter or other central object that consumes the only credible tower position unless an alternative working geometry is demonstrated.

The route should remain domestic and attractive. Its maintenance role should disappear in ordinary use.

## 9. Landscape controls — current project geometry

Classify the immediate perimeter as:

### A. Critical access positions

Preserve substantial future capability at:

- east/service-side plant, `ASHP-01`, utility, façade and roof-access strip;
- west-side representative façade / roof-edge support positions;
- rear representative scaffold/tower working line;
- north garden-to-courtyard logistics route;
- at least one viable courtyard tower position;
- external courtyard-drain / overflow inspection points.

On the current surrogate 15.5 m-wide plot the 12.0 m house leaves approximately **1.75 m gross side clearance per side**. Treat the east/service-side residual width as particularly valuable; do not consume it with fixed stores, immovable screening or uniquely difficult landscape before a real site provides another route.

### B. Removable landscape positions

Planting or furniture may occupy them if removal is straightforward and does not require demolition or excavation of permanent work.

### C. Unconstrained landscape

Maintenance does not materially depend on these areas.

Consider mature crown and root geometry where trees affect sole access positions, gutters, roof edges or façade inspection.

## 10. Neighbour and highway dependency

Map work likely to require neighbour access, oversailing beyond the legal boundary, pavement/highway occupation or specialist lifting from the street.

Zero dependency is not the objective. Avoidable dependency should not be discovered after completion.

The principal front may legitimately need temporary highway-facing scaffold for front-façade work. Do not make it the only route to service plant or routine roof components.

A real site must separately test `ASHP-01` noise and boundary clearances; the virtual east-side placement is not permission to externalise acoustic burden onto a neighbour.

## 11. Handover / stewardship information

The building record should preserve:

- the as-built external-access plan;
- critical support and access positions;
- below-ground drainage constraints;
- any later designed scaffold ties, anchors or guard sockets;
- landscape zones whose obstruction would impair access;
- roof access strategy and restrictions;
- PV array / cable / isolation / mounting replacement information;
- `ASHP-01` service/replacement route and clear working geography;
- courtyard logistics route;
- representative replacement routes for major opening families;
- rainwater overflow/failure routes.

This information should survive landscape redesign and ownership change.

- permanent roof anchors / guard sockets, currently **not selected**;
- exact internal versus external full-frame window replacement sequence;
- wall-base/drainage detail at critical access positions;
- landscape species/positions and mature-tree replay;
- final roof waterproofing/gutter/coping detail;
- final rainwater discharge destination from real-site ground/sewer evidence;
- exact MVHR intake/exhaust and soil-vent terminal clearances;

- final ASHP position, working clearance, acoustic treatment and neighbour effect.

The roof-hatch question is not currently unresolved: **the current Reference House does not include one.** Reopen only if later tasks justify it.

The doctrine requirement remains fixed: maintenance access must be deliberate and demonstrated rather than left to future improvisation.
