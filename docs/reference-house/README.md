# Reference House

The Reference House is one coordinated interpretation of House Kernel. It brings architectural and technical propositions into the same building so that conflicts hidden by isolated principles or patterns become visible in plan, section, structure, envelope, services and maintenance.

It is an integration test, not proof of the doctrine.

The active staged execution plan is the [Reference House Design Programme](../development/reference-house-design-programme.md). It is the restart-safe control for current design work and should be read before resuming a partially completed Reference House stage.

The canonical project brief is the [Brief and Constraint Ledger](brief-and-constraint-ledger.md). RH-00 closed that ledger on 2026-10-12.

RH-01 selected the compact central-court morphology. RH-02 tested it at furniture scale, widened the side wings by 300 mm each and selected the ground-floor order. RH-03 has now fixed the principal stair topology, four-bedroom upper-floor order and three-sided upper courtyard circulation.

## Architectural scope

The house selects a **Georgian-derived architectural grammar**, provisionally represented by G-01, together with a courtyard morphology and a project-specific tectonic dialect. These are choices made for this house, not House Kernel doctrine.

House Kernel propositions should remain intelligible under a materially different architectural language. G-01, by contrast, is allowed to be strongly opinionated about composition, hierarchy, proportion and element families. Keeping those authorities separate lets the Reference House be architecturally specific without allowing its style to leak upstream into the doctrine.

That separation is governed by the [Architectural Specificity Boundary](../development/architectural-specificity-boundary.md), while the [G-01 Grammar Charter](../grammar/g01-grammar-charter.md) and [G-01 Research Brief](../grammar/g01-research-brief.md) define the current grammar programme.

## Composition and authority

The house combines several independently owned layers:

```text
House Kernel doctrine
+ selected House Kernel patterns / strategies
+ supported technical families where used
+ G-01 Georgian-derived grammar
+ site + contemporary family programme
+ courtyard project morphology
+ local courtyard dialect where selected
+ Reference House tectonic dialect
= Reference House
```

The layers constrain one another without becoming interchangeable.

- **House Kernel doctrine** owns the general long-life propositions: serviceability, lifespan separation, designed interfaces, failure tolerance, maintenance geography, repose, passive-first environmental strategy and architectural resolution of technology.
- **House Kernel patterns / strategies** own reusable responses selected for the project. Their use here does not increase their general evidence or maturity.
- **Supported technical families** describe construction or system routes being evaluated. A masonry, floor or roof family does not become a House Kernel preference merely because this house uses it.
- **G-01** owns Georgian-derived spatial and compositional constraints: lineage, hierarchy, ordering, proportions, opening relationships and element families that survive the grammar research.
- **Project morphology** owns the courtyard and other house-specific geometric decisions that are not part of G-01 by default.
- **Local dialect** may modify a bounded part of the house—for example the slight Andalusian influence explored for the courtyard—without redefining G-01.
- **Reference House tectonic dialect** owns project-specific visible resolutions such as traditional joinery and restrained brass or bronze where those choices are architecturally and functionally justified.
- **Site / programme** owns orientation, planning response, household needs, external relationships and other facts of the project.

Where one decision touches two layers, record the dependency rather than promoting the project choice upstream. A cornice may realise a House Kernel movement-interface principle in this house without becoming a House Kernel requirement. A courtyard may satisfy G-01 ordering without becoming a G-01 invariant.

The current house studies develop this composition through the [Tectonic Architectural Language](tectonic-architectural-language.md), [External Access & Maintenance Plan](external-access-maintenance-plan.md), the staged Reference House gate records and later physical prototypes.

## Current architectural baseline

The current controlled shell remains:

- two principal storeys as the working configuration;
- outer reservation: **12.0 × 10.8 m**;
- open courtyard: **3.6 × 4.0 m**;
- gross side wings: **4.2 m**;
- gross front/rear wings: **3.4 m**;
- gross enclosed plate proxy: **115.2 m²/storey**, **230.4 m²** across two principal storeys before wall thickness, stairs and local voids/projections.

The width is a deliberate RH-02 correction to RH-01's 11.4 m shell. RH-03 does not reopen the outer shell.

### Ground floor

The selected ground-floor order remains **G1 — one-sided service gallery + linked rear rooms**. Use the [RH-02 Ground-Floor Development](ground-floor-development.md) and [drawing](ground-floor-development.svg) together with the later [RH-03 stair overlay](stair-ground-floor-overlay.svg).

Current ground-floor roles are:

- west/front — principal drawing room;
- west/rear — informal family room;
- south/centre — entrance / principal stair hall addressing the courtyard;
- one inner flank — narrow all-weather gallery;
- outer side of that flank — compact study and service-hub reservation;
- north/centre — garden dining hall;
- north/service-side corner — kitchen with direct garden relationship.

RH-03 moves the front drawing/hall partition approximately **200 mm west** for the front-wing depth only. This creates enough stair-hall width to retain a clear arrival passage beside the stair. The shell, courtyard, study and service block do not move.

The principal stair is now a **three-flight full-landing stair** rather than a conventional two-flight return:

- 18 risers total;
- three flights of six risers;
- 280 mm planning going;
- approximately 1000 mm clear-flight target;
- no winders;
- 3240 mm floor-to-floor used only as the RH-03 coordination datum, giving 180 mm rise.

Use the [RH-03 stair/courtyard section](stair-courtyard-section.svg) for current sectional intent. RH-04 owns final storey heights.

### Upper floor

Use [RH-03 Stair, Upper Floor and Courtyard Circulation](upper-floor-and-stair-development.md) and the [upper-floor drawing](upper-floor-and-stair-development.svg) as the current upper-floor authority.

The upper floor selects **four bedrooms** rather than treating the five-bedroom ceiling as a target:

- west/rear — principal bedroom;
- west/front — secondary double bedroom;
- east/front — compact double / secondary bedroom;
- east/rear — compact double / secondary bedroom.

Circulation forms a deliberate **three-sided U around the courtyard**:

**south stair hall → east courtyard gallery → north cross-landing**.

There is no west gallery, so the west bedrooms retain their useful width.

The main family bathroom occupies the east-middle outer strip and stacks directly over `G-SVC-02`. A compact principal en-suite remains a **conditional project hypothesis**: RH-06 must prove its service/drainage route or remove it.

The main bathroom and upper gallery now create a direct dry-side service-access relationship, and the stacked `G-SVC-02 → U-BATH-01` geography creates the current accessible vertical-service-zone reservation.

The service/gallery flank is drawn east for continuity only. The scheme remains mirrorable until a real site gives one side different solar, utility, neighbour or access value.

The earlier [Whole-House Coordination Fixture 01](whole-house-coordination-fixture.md) and its [drawing](whole-house-coordination-fixture.svg) are historical F0 coordination provenance. The [RH-01 morphology record](morphology-options.md) and RH-02 remain earlier gates where later RH-03 decisions explicitly supersede local geometry.
