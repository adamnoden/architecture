# Reference House — RH-11 Human Factors & Inclusive-Design Review Brief

**Status:** ready for competent human-factors / inclusive-design review  
**Parent pack:** [RH-11 External Review Brief](external-review-brief.md)  
**Current project maturity:** M3 coordinated virtual design; **no accessibility, inclusive-design or compliance certification claim**

## Review question

> **Does the current Reference House provide a credible, proportionate basis for ordinary domestic use across foreseeable variation in bodies, capability and temporary states, or has the architecture normalised avoidable repeated-use burdens that should be corrected before technical design?**

The reviewer is asked to challenge the architecture, not to certify a virtual house or force every specialist scenario into every room.

---

## 1. Evidence and authority boundary

Please keep the following classes distinct:

- legal / regulatory duties applicable to an eventual real project;
- Approved Document or other statutory-guidance routes;
- standards and professional inclusive-design guidance;
- research evidence;
- project-specific human-factors preferences;
- product-specific evidence;
- architectural judgement.

The present Reference House assumes only an ordinary England dwelling baseline where no real planning condition or user brief has imposed a stronger category. Human-factors preferences stronger than that baseline are **project targets, not rewritten law**.

In particular, the selected **300 mm principal-stair going** is an Ovid’s House human-factors preference adopted after internal comparison. It is not presented as a statutory minimum.

---

## 2. Current architectural propositions

The review should take the following as current propositions to test rather than conclusions to endorse:

- the principal entrance and internal ground-floor route are intended to be substantially level;
- the entrance-storey plan preserves habitable-room capacity that could support a later sleeping arrangement;
- the principal stair uses 18 risers, three flights with full landings, no winders and a selected **300 mm going**;
- upper sleeping and main bathing remain upstairs;
- wet utility / laundry geography remains on the ground floor;
- the house uses deliberate principal circulation rather than a separate second-class “accessible” route;
- repeated-use controls and interfaces should minimise unnecessary bodily or cognitive compensation;
- specialist equipment remains demand-led unless the actual brief requires it.

Several finished-interface facts are intentionally not yet modelled. Missing product, lining, threshold, sanitary, hardware and real-site facts are not to be treated as failures merely because they do not exist at virtual-design scope.

## 4. Questions requiring review

### Principal approach / entrance / ground-floor continuity

1. At current architectural resolution, is the intended substantially level principal route credible without creating obvious weathering, drainage or door-operation contradictions?
2. Which approach, threshold, door-clear-opening and manoeuvre facts must be frozen architecturally before ordinary technical build-ups can erode them?
3. Which questions are impossible to close before a real site's levels, gradients, boundary and set-down conditions exist?
4. Is the entrance-storey fallback-sleeping proposition meaningful, or does it rely on missing sanitary/shower/support geometry that should be reserved earlier?

### Principal stair / vertical movement

5. Does the selected 300 mm going materially improve ordinary ascent/descent and reduced-attention/carrying conditions without introducing another human-use burden?
6. Which finished stair facts must be demonstrated at technical design: net clear width, handrails/guards, headroom, landing transitions, lighting/conspicuity and door conflicts?
7. Are there foreseeable users/states for whom the current stair topology is weak enough that a modest architectural enabling provision should be considered now?
8. Is any current wording implying stairlift compatibility without enough product-specific parking, transfer, rail/chair, residual-route, power and door-conflict geometry? If so, narrow it rather than assuming compatibility.
9. Does the absence of a through-floor-lift reservation represent a real architectural defect at this brief, or would reserving one now be disproportionate speculation?

### Doors, thresholds, corners and controls

10. Where are the most likely causal bottlenecks once frames, stops, ironmongery, seals, radiators, joinery and other fixed projections become real?
11. Which door/control operations should be assessed as complete actions rather than component ratings or isolated dimensions?
12. Do any threshold ambitions risk solving wheelability/trip concerns by creating a moisture, drainage, acoustic or envelope defect elsewhere?
13. Are important controls located so occupants can alter a condition before entering the condition where that is reasonably achievable—for example shower, bath, ventilation, shade or lighting control?
14. Which operating-force / reach questions are genuinely architectural now and which are properly product-specific later?

### Sanitary / bathing / care scenarios

15. What minimum spatial reservations, if any, are worth protecting before final sanitaryware and support details exist?
16. Does the entrance-storey sanitary arrangement preserve credible ordinary use if an occupant temporarily cannot use the principal stair?
17. Which assistance/support scenarios are sufficiently foreseeable to justify durable enabling capacity, and which would overfit the generic house to specialist needs not in the brief?

### Domestic-task routes

18. Does the kitchen / entrance / garden topology avoid obvious repeated carrying and turning burdens once doors and appliance open states are added?
19. Does the ground-floor utility location impose a material repeated laundry burden because bedrooms, main bathing and linen geography are upstairs?
20. If so, test the **smallest credible interventions first**—collection, clean-linen/storage coordination, set-down/staging and reduced-trip workflows—before recommending relocation or duplicated wet appliances.
21. If relocation is recommended, account explicitly for moisture/leak containment, ventilation/drying, noise/vibration, services, structural build-up, appliance replacement and garden/dirty-work relationships.
22. Are any large-object/furniture/replacement paths likely to fail because of a particular corner or opening rather than a globally narrow plan?

## 5. Required red-team cases

### HFI-RT1 — nominal clearance disappears in the finished assembly

Assume a route that looks adequate in the planning model loses usable width to linings, frames, handrails, guards, radiators or joinery.

- What fails first?
- Is the smallest correction local detail/product choice or architectural geometry?
- At what point should the relevant RH gate reopen?

### HFI-RT2 — level entrance creates an envelope contradiction

Assume drainage, weathering and door construction cannot deliver the intended transition within the current threshold reservation.

- Can the interface be resolved without a material step or awkward operating sequence?
- If not, what architectural reservation should have been larger/different?

### HFI-RT3 — stair works numerically but not as an occupied sequence

Assume finished handrails/guards, lighting, landing doors and ordinary carrying conditions make the stair materially less usable than its rise/going arithmetic suggests.

- What is the causal bottleneck?
- Can it be fixed without changing the stair family?
- Which finding would justify reopening RH-03?

### HFI-RT4 — temporary ground-floor living is required

Assume an occupant cannot use the principal stair for several weeks or months.

- Can an entrance-storey habitable room function meaningfully as sleeping space?
- Is sanitary/washing access credible at the actual level of geometry represented?
- Which missing facts are technical-design inputs and which expose an architectural omission?

### HFI-RT5 — laundry burden proves material

Assume repeated bedroom/bath → stair → ground utility → stair return journeys are onerous in ordinary family use.

- Compare local workflow/storage interventions against utility relocation.
- Include service, moisture, noise, structural and replacement consequences.
- Do not treat “upstairs laundry” as a universal inclusive-design rule.

## 6. Rewind conditions

Treat the following as candidate architectural rewinds only if substantiated:

1. ordinary finished build-ups consume a critical route/opening/stair clearance and no proportionate local correction exists;
2. the principal entrance cannot remain credibly low-friction once weathering/drainage and door operation are resolved;
3. finished stair design creates a material repeated-use problem requiring stair/landing topology change;
4. a modest life-course enabling provision is shown to be cheap now, destructive later and broadly useful enough to justify permanent reservation;
5. entrance-storey fallback use is impossible without moving primary room/service boundaries despite that capability being retained as an architectural proposition;
6. a domestic-task burden such as laundry is shown by balanced comparison to justify a topology change rather than a local workflow correction.

Do **not** declare a rewind merely because final product, site, sanitary or technical-detail evidence is not yet available.

- explicit separation of mandatory baseline, good-practice guidance and stronger project preference;
- the most important repeated-use burdens you believe the current architecture is normalising;
- the smallest credible correction for each material burden;
- any durable enabling provisions worth protecting before technical design;
- any current provision you believe is disproportionate or overfit;
- at least two ordinary counterexamples that materially stress the current assumptions;
- which questions must remain open until a real site, product or user brief exists.

The project does **not** need a certificate or generic accessibility checklist at RH-11. It needs a competent adversarial answer to whether the architecture has embedded avoidable friction, and what should actually change if it has.
