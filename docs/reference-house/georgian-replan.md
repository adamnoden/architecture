# Georgian house re-plan

This architectural coordination proposal uses a 12 m square shell, a centred
3.6 m open courtyard, a centred entrance and a west stair continuing from ground
to first floor and attic. The owner accepts this floor plan as the working basis for continued drawing
development; personal layout refinements are deferred. It succeeds the square-house plan at that scope.
The earlier plans remain historical comparison evidence. This proposal is not
yet the complete structural, environmental or service-model successor.

## Subsequent exterior decision

The owner replaces the mansard and dormers with a consistent 25-degree outward
pitch and no roof windows. Ground/first plans stay held; standing attic use and
the full-height upper stair are withdrawn. The earlier roof/attic descriptions
below are retained historical evidence, not current design.

## Roof constraint

**No flat roofs are permitted in newly authored project designs.** This applies
to the main roof, dormers, porches, extensions and ancillary covered elements.
A very-low-slope roof is not an acceptable substitute. Dormers have pitched
gables; no flat-topped dormer or concealed flat terrace is admitted.

This is a firm owner-imposed project constraint, recorded in the
project brief and `project/design-constraints.ts`.
It is not a universal claim that every flat roof fails. Historical fixtures and
proof identities remain reproducible; they no longer prescribe the new roof.

## Stair and entrance

The five front axes are 1800 / 3900 / 6000 / 8100 / 10200 mm. The entrance is a
1800 mm paired opening exactly on the centre. Four further openings per outer
side use equally spaced 1800 / 4600 / 7400 / 10200 mm axes. Window apertures
are 1000 mm on both floors, with common sill/head datums; garden doors and the
plant replacement door occupy the same axes with explicitly different widths.

The west return stair has 1200 mm clear flights, a 300 mm open well and a
1750 mm half-landing. Each storey has twenty 172.5 mm rises and 300 mm goings;
the 4450 × 2700 mm flight/landing footprint repeats through both storeys.
The half-landing is the feature space, with daylight from adjacent west/court
openings. The full ground approach landing is reserved ahead of the first tread.
The same source generates the developed stair diagram and both plan flights.

The west band cannot hold this stair and a separate full-width longitudinal
gallery side by side. North and south landings therefore connect through the
east gallery, which also supplies service access. No route through a bedroom or
under a flight is assumed. The western front bedroom combines the space above the study and cloakroom
into an L-shaped room, preserving the stair approach. It is represented with a
1200 × 2000 mm bed; the rear-west bedroom uses a 1350 mm double-bed template.
This is not a claim that every bedroom is a generous double room. The rear-west bedroom is broad but relatively shallow.

Furniture footprints fit their rooms. The stair has more generous clear width
and turning space than the previous candidate, but a continuous three-dimensional
sofa/cylinder carrying simulation has not passed. The displayed developed stair
is not a physical headroom, guarding, fire enclosure or structural framing proof.

## Ground programme and service geography

The study grows from 6.07 to approximately **6.48 m² clear**, extending northwards.
It is narrower and deeper than before. The cloakroom sits beside it with 600 mm
hanging and a nominal 1475 mm clear aisle. Its lower north boundary preserves
the approach to the west stair; aligning that boundary with the study would
constrict the route. Other service partitions share continuous spine faces and
real T junctions rather than floating line ends.

The utility and plant are separate rooms. The plant reservation gives
approximately **9.66 m² clear**, with a nominal 920 mm working/replacement strip,
700 mm cylinder reserve, segregated dry controls and an explicit riser. A
1400 mm external replacement opening avoids carrying plant through the house.
These are equipment envelopes, not a selected or sized installation. The pantry
is entered directly from the kitchen. The guest WC retains an east-side window.
Family/garden dining sacrifices space to the stair core as instructed.

The principal en suite shares a transverse boundary with the family bathroom
in the eastern wet band. The north-east principal suite includes its own
walk-in wardrobe. The south-east en suite occupies the front bay beside its
bedroom, preserving a real window and uninterrupted gallery. Its short branch
runs through the accessible gallery service zone to the main riser, never
through a bedroom; it is not falsely described as directly back-to-back with
the family bathroom. The south-east bedroom is compact, about 7.53 m² clear,
with a double-bed template and one main access side. The 550 × 700 mm riser envelope is inside both the ground
plant and upper bathroom reservation. Removable dry-side service lining and
900 mm access-panel envelopes face the public gallery/landing. Panel/riser access and
the family-bathroom door do not overlap. A conservative 100 mm walking grid
with a 600 mm person envelope connects every room to the entrance/stair arrival
without using the courtyard or stair footprint as a shortcut; this does not
establish furniture carrying or escape compliance. Wet-room fixture selection, extract,
drainage gradients, acoustic/fire boundaries and actual withdrawal clearances
remain to be resolved before technical adoption. No route is hidden inside
permanent masonry merely to make the plan fit.

## Roof, attic and height cost

Retain 3100 mm clear principal ceilings, FFLs 0 / 3450 / 6900 mm and 350 mm
intermediate floor zones. The new roof is a steep lower mansard with 30-degree
upper fields, all falling outward to external eaves. It avoids inner courtyard
gutters and internal roof outlets. A nominal 300 mm roof build zone is reserved.
Three pitched dormers centre on the middle three front bays; they are not
arbitrarily offset between lower windows.

The roof break is +8800 mm at a 600 mm setback. With a 30-degree upper slope
across the remaining 3600 mm to the courtyard edge, the high edge reaches
**+10878 mm**. This is a substantial massing change from the former +6800 mm
roof edge. The roof section shows it explicitly. The attic arrival has about
**2437 mm nominal vertical clear headroom** at the lowest edge of its upper
flight/landing, after the 300 mm build zone. Dashed attic contours distinguish
nominal 2100 mm headroom from low storage zones; floor footprint is not all
full-height usable area.

The courtyard remains open, but its taller enclosing walls reduce sky exposure.
Daylight, overshadowing, ventilation, roof span/support, outward valley/hip
junctions, externally accessible gutters and PV provision require explicit
reconciliation. An attic roof-access opening is provisional: a secure landing,
working platform, edge protection and a roof-work route remain unresolved.
No flat platform is to be disguised as a roof surface to evade the roof rule.

## Drawing and evidence boundary

`compiler/reference-house/georgian/` owns the new source, geometric audit, ADE
plan adaptation and deterministic scenes. The proof pack contains ground, first,
attic, front, rear, transverse roof section, developed stair and service overlay.
B5/A4 are 1:100; A2 is 1:50. Every finished page is checked for size, geometric
scale, embedded Source Sans and Unicode. It is an architectural proposal, not a
construction drawing set or an accepted physical print release.

The historical RH compiler still compiles its recorded two-floor source and roof.
Its passing tests must not be cited as whole-system validation of this new attic,
stair, en suites, roof or service geometry. Technical migration follows review
of this coordinated proposal, with an explicit delta rather than substituted
historical fingerprints.
