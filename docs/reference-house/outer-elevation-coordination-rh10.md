# Reference House — RH-10 Outer Elevation Coordination

**Status:** RH-10 corrective architectural coordination  
**Date:** 2026-10-12  
**Parent architecture:** [RH-04 Plan / Section / Elevation Gate](plan-section-elevation-gate.md)  
**Service / access authorities:** [RH-06 Services](service-topology-rh06.md), [RH-08 Roof / Access](roof-drainage-access-rh08.md), [RH-10 Energy / Heat](energy-heat-coordination-rh10.md)  
**Drawing:** [rear and service elevation study](outer-elevations-rh10.svg)

RH-04 deliberately concentrated on the principal front and courtyard. That was sufficient for the early G-01 coupling question, but it is no longer sufficient for a whole-house coordination claim.

By RH-10 the east/service elevation has accumulated real obligations: utility entry, plant replacement, MVHR intake/exhaust, heat-pump geography, cooker source capture and rainwater routes. The rear/garden elevation also carries the principal garden threshold and structural obligation to preserve useful masonry piers.

RH-10 therefore gives both elevations an explicit **scoped order**. This is not a new universal G-01 rule.

---

## 1. North / garden elevation

Use a simple three-axis composition centred on the house:

- west axis: approximately **X = 2.1 m**;
- centre axis: **X = 6.0 m**;
- east axis: approximately **X = 9.9 m**.

The spacing is approximately 3.9 m. Exact opening widths remain technical / G-01-detail work.

### Ground floor

- west: family-room garden window / controlled opening;
- centre: principal garden-dining door family, with a **1200 mm clear-opening coordination target** retained from RH-08 logistics;
- east: kitchen garden window / door family as final furniture and service-wall design permits.

The rear does **not** become one wall of undifferentiated glass. The three fields preserve meaningful masonry piers at the rear structural line and keep the room hierarchy legible.

### Upper floor

- west: principal-bedroom window;
- centre: north cross-landing window;
- east: rear secondary-bedroom window.

The upper openings align broadly with the three ground fields where room use permits. The centre landing opening may be narrower/lighter than bedroom openings.

### Fire consequence

The principal-bedroom and east-rear bedroom windows are part of the first-floor emergency-egress strategy at current coordination level. Their opening geometry must later satisfy the applicable Approved Document B emergency-escape-window requirements.

The current circa-800 mm upper sill family is compatible with the guidance maximum of 1100 mm to the bottom of the openable area, but final frame/sash geometry must prove the required unobstructed openable area and minimum dimensions.

---

## 2. East / service elevation

The service side is allowed to be more asymmetrical than the principal front, but it is **not** allowed to become an equipment dump.

Use three broad room-owned vertical fields corresponding to the plan:

- south/front field — study / east-front bedroom;
- middle field — utility/plant / family bathroom;
- north/rear field — kitchen / east-rear bedroom.

### South field

- ground study window belongs to the ordinary occupied-window family;
- upper east-front bedroom obtains its principal egress/daylight opening from the front elevation, so the side opening is optional rather than required to rescue the room.

### Middle service field

Concentrate most unavoidable technical expression here:

- robust plant replacement door / opening at `G-PLANT-01`;
- low utility-entry group;
- MVHR intake/exhaust terminals high on the plant-room wall where MEP separation permits;
- `ASHP-01` at ground level beside the plant room, not beneath a bedroom window;
- family-bathroom daylight window above, kept visually independent of the technical terminals.

The plant opening is treated as a real secondary architectural door, not a blank industrial hatch.

### North field

- ground kitchen window belongs to the occupied opening family;
- cooker source-capture terminal stays short and local but is positioned as a subordinate service object rather than centred as an architectural feature;
- upper east-rear bedroom uses the garden/rear elevation for its primary window/egress condition.

### Rainwater

`RWP-SE` and `RWP-NE` stay near the front/rear returns rather than occupying the centre of the elevation. This visually brackets the service side and keeps the middle working zone clear.

### Terminal hierarchy

From most to least visually controlling:

1. occupied windows / doors;
2. plant replacement door as secondary architecture;
3. downpipes at return edges;
4. high-level ventilation/extract terminals grouped in service geography;
5. small utility penetrations low and subordinate.

No decorative screen is introduced merely to pretend the equipment does not exist. If an acoustic/weather screen is technically required for `ASHP-01`, it must be designed as real secondary architecture with full airflow and maintenance obligations.

---

## 3. West elevation

The west elevation remains technically quiet.

Its order follows the two principal west room fields:

- front drawing / west-front bedroom;
- rear family / principal bedroom.

Use vertically related room-owned openings where daylight, furniture and the final G-01 family permit. No primary services, plant, roof drainage stacks or technical panels should migrate west merely to make the east elevation prettier.

---

## 4. Front elevation fire check

The current RH-04 upper front family gives:

- planning opening width about 900–950 mm;
- sill about 800 mm above first FFL;
- opening height about 1550 mm.

The west-front bedroom already sits on front bay 1. The east-front bedroom sits on front bay 4. RH-10 therefore assigns those two outer windows the additional job of emergency escape, subject to final opening mechanism.

This does not change the four-bay composition. It changes the **performance obligation of two members of the family**.

---

## 5. Roof-family authority correction

RH-04 said a pitched visible roof remained preferred and warned against a parapet-concealed technical lid. RH-08 subsequently selected the outward-draining very-low-slope four-field roof because that geometry removes inner courtyard gutters, valleys and internal rainwater outlets.

RH-10 resolves the contradiction explicitly:

> **RH-08 supersedes RH-04 on roof family and roof visibility. RH-04 remains authoritative for storey/cornice datum and opening order.**

The low-slope roof is not justified because technical roofs should disappear. It is justified because this particular courtyard house gains materially safer, simpler water geography from the outward fall.

The parapet/cornice edge must therefore be designed as genuine architecture and weathering construction at RH-technical resolution; it may not become a cosmetic screen hiding unresolved roofing.

The new RH-10 PV reserve sits behind that same controlled outer order and does not change the front bay composition.

# RH-10 elevation result

**PASS at architectural coordination level.**

The whole external envelope now has an explicit order:

- **south/front:** RH-04 four-bay principal composition;
- **north/garden:** RH-10 three-axis garden composition;
- **east/service:** three broad room/service fields with concentrated technical expression;
- **west:** quiet two-room-field elevation;
- **courtyard:** RH-04 separately scoped internal order.

The building therefore no longer has an architecturally resolved front attached to an administratively serviced back.
