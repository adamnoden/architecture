# Reference House — RH-04 Plan / Section / Elevation Gate

**Status:** RH-04 design gate record — HFI-08B stair recheck passed  
**Date:** 2026-10-12; HFI-08B recheck 2026-10-19  
**Question:** does the RH-03 spatial arrangement generate one convincing Georgian-derived architecture when plan, section and elevation are negotiated together?  
**Plan authority:** [RH-02 Ground-Floor Development](ground-floor-development.md), as modified by [RH-03 Stair / Upper-Floor Development](upper-floor-and-stair-development.md)  
**G-01 control:** [G-01 D5 / D6 Evidence Gate v0.3](../grammar/g01-d5-d6-web-pass-v03.md)  
**Drawings:** [front elevation](front-elevation-study.svg), [courtyard elevations](courtyard-elevations-study.svg), [principal section](principal-section-rh04.svg)

RH-04 does not attempt to invent a complete Georgian rulebook. G-01 has not earned one. It uses the evidence that *is* mature enough: explicit hierarchy, scoped order, first-class section, attribute-specific coupling, and the rejection of one universal room/proportion rule.

The result is a coupled architectural baseline, not a façade style study.

HFI-08B later rechecked this gate after RH-03 selected a 300 mm principal-stair going. RH-04's storey, opening and façade datums survive unchanged; only the derived stair arithmetic changes.

## 1. What RH-04 inherits

The controlled house remains:

- outer shell: **12.0 × 10.8 m**;
- courtyard: **3.6 × 4.0 m**;
- four-bedroom upper floor;
- three-flight principal stair in the south entrance hall;
- ground-floor service/gallery flank shown east but still mirrorable at real-site stage;
- family bathroom stacked over the service hub;
- principal en-suite conditional on RH-06 route proof.

RH-04 is allowed to move opening coordinates and sectional datums. It is not allowed to rescue an incoherent façade by silently changing the room order.

## 2. Sectional comparison

Three height families were tested.

### H0 — flat / equalised hierarchy

Approximate intent:

- retain the RH-03 **3240 mm** ground-to-first datum then under consideration;
- give both occupied storeys near-equal perceived height;
- differentiate hierarchy mainly through opening treatment.

**Advantage:** compact and easy to coordinate.

**Failure:** the house reads too uniformly stacked. The principal ground-floor rooms, entrance and garden sequence gain little sectional rank over the bedroom storey. G-01 evidence does not require a taller principal floor, but it clearly shows that section is a legitimate carrier of hierarchy. There is no reason to suppress it here.

**Decision:** reject as the controlled baseline.

### H1 — restrained ground-floor primacy — **SELECTED**

Controlled planning datums:

- ground finished floor: **±0**;
- ground clear ceiling datum: **+2950 mm**;
- first-floor finished floor: **+3300 mm**;
- planning floor/service/ceiling zone between ground ceiling and first FFL: **350 mm**;
- upper clear ceiling datum: **+6000 mm**;
- outer cornice / eaves-control datum: approximately **+6250 mm**;
- roof above: morphology only at RH-04; exact pitches, valleys, drainage and access belong to RH-08.

Current stair consequence after HFI-08B:

- 18 rises over 3300 mm = **183.3 mm planning rise**;
- **300 mm going**;
- `2R + G ≈ 666.7 mm`;
- planning pitch ≈ **31.4°**.

This keeps RH-03's comfortable stair topology while giving the ground floor a modest but real sectional primacy. HFI-08B changed the going from 280 to 300 mm without changing any RH-04 datum, opening head or façade axis.

The difference is intentionally restrained. This is a compact contemporary family house, not a piano-nobile villa enlarged for spectacle.

### H2 — emphatic principal storey

Approximate test:

- ground-to-first around **3420 mm**;
- ground clear ceiling around 3050 mm;
- upper storey around 2750 mm clear;
- larger cornice/eaves datum.

The stair remains technically plausible at about 190 mm rise, but the house becomes more vertically assertive and the front elevation starts to acquire a grandeur inconsistent with the compact London-house brief.

**Decision:** reject.

### Section decision

**H1 is selected.**

The controlling architectural hierarchy is therefore:

> **principal ground floor > private upper floor**, expressed through room height, opening height and entrance scale rather than through excessive monumentality.

These are planning datums, not technical build-ups. RH-05 may alter floor depth locally if structure requires it; a material change to the visible storey hierarchy must return to this gate.

HFI-08B recheck: **PASS.** The 300 mm-going stair retains the same 18-riser / three-flight / full-landing topology at +3300 mm FFL. Detailed string, guard, headroom and finished-clear-width geometry remains technical work.

## 3. Front elevation: the entrance conflict

RH-03 puts the front entrance toward the east side of the stair hall because the principal stair occupies the hall's western portion. Forcing a centred entrance at X = 6.0 m would either collide with the stair or require a contrived internal vestibule purely to obtain abstract symmetry.

RH-04 rejects that move.

G-01 evidence already contains a useful counterexample: 76 Dean Street has an ordered four-window-wide front with an off-centre entrance. More importantly, the current G-01 modelling correction is stronger than the historical example:

> **symmetry and alignment have scope; whole-building centred entrance is not a universal requirement.**

### Front ordering options

#### F1 — centred five-bay front / centred door

This would make the elevation easy to read but would require the plan to move for the façade rather than through a defensible architectural reason.

**Reject.**

#### F2 — openings follow internal partitions independently

This preserves every local plan convenience but produces no stable façade order.

**Reject.** G-01 is not a licence for arbitrary fenestration.

#### F3 — centred four-bay field / door in bay three — **SELECTED**

Use a regular four-bay field centred on the 12.0 m façade:

| Bay | Axis from west outer face | Ground role | Upper role |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **1.95 m** | drawing-room window | west-front bedroom window |
| 2 | **4.65 m** | stair/hall window | south stair-landing window |
| 3 | **7.35 m** | **principal entrance** | south landing window |
| 4 | **10.05 m** | study window | east-front bedroom window |

Bay spacing is **2.70 m**. The field itself is centred on X = 6.0 m; the entrance occupies the third bay rather than pretending the third/fourth internal condition does not exist.

This is the key RH-04 move:

> **the façade is ordered; the entrance is off-centre; neither fact cancels the other.**

No ground-floor room boundary needs to move materially to obtain this order.

## 4. Front opening hierarchy

The numbers below are coordination dimensions for the RH-04 elevation, not final window schedules.

### Ground principal windows

For bays 1 and 4:

- planning opening width: approximately **1050 mm**;
- sill: approximately **+650 to +700 mm**;
- head: approximately **+2550 mm**;
- opening height: approximately **1850–1900 mm**.

They read as the larger occupied-ground-floor family.

### Ground stair window — bay 2

The stair, not exterior regularity, controls the lower part of this opening.

Use:

- width around **900 mm**;
- common head around **+2550 mm**;
- higher sill around **+1050 mm**;
- height around **1500 mm**.

The centreline remains in the four-bay order while the sill responds to the stair. This is deliberate attribute-specific coupling:

- **centreline:** elevation-order led / mutually accepted by plan;
- **sill:** stair/section led;
- **head:** shared façade datum.

### Principal entrance — bay 3

Planning opening:

- door leaf/clear geometry to be developed later;
- architectural opening width around **950–1050 mm**;
- head approximately **+2550 mm**;
- restrained surround / entrance hierarchy rather than portico-scale grandeur.

The exact surround, fanlight and material treatment remain RH-09 / G-01-detail questions.

### Upper windows

Planning family:

- width approximately **900–950 mm**;
- sill around **+800 mm above first FFL**;
- head around **+2350 mm above first FFL**;
- opening height approximately **1550 mm**.

This creates a clear ground > upper opening hierarchy while preserving the same four vertical axes.

No claim is made that these dimensions are a canonical Georgian family. They are a project-level composition consistent with the current G-01 evidence and the house's own hierarchy.

## 5. Courtyard order

The courtyard does **not** inherit the front façade's four-bay field. It has its own architectural owner and scale.

This is important: the house now has multiple explicit orders rather than one global grid masquerading as architecture.

### South courtyard elevation — stair / entrance side

The court-facing south wall belongs to the hall/stair sequence.

Selected order:

- one broad ground opening centred on the courtyard axis **X = 6.0 m**;
- one upper stair/landing opening on the same centreline;
- vertical relation intentionally direct.

The front door is therefore **not** on the courtyard axis. Arrival moves from the off-centre front entrance into a hall that re-centres on the court.

That compression/recentring is treated as an architectural sequence, not as a defect to disguise.

### North courtyard elevation — garden side

The north court wall belongs to the garden-dining / upper cross-landing sequence.

Selected order:

- central ground opening toward `G-GARDEN-02`;
- central upper landing window above;
- same X = 6.0 m courtyard axis;
- garden relationship continues beyond the ground room.

The **courtyard axis is therefore stronger than the front-door axis** inside the house.

### East courtyard elevation — gallery side

Ground and upper galleries occupy the same court edge.

Use a **two-bay vertical rhythm** over the 4.0 m court length:

- two aligned ground gallery openings;
- two aligned upper gallery openings;
- lighter/narrower family than principal-room windows;
- exact sash/door type later.

Because the architectural owner is the gallery on both floors, this is the most regular courtyard face.

### West courtyard elevation — occupied-room side

The west edge is split between front and rear occupied rooms on both floors.

Use two opening axes, each owned by its adjacent room field, and align upper/lower openings where the plan supports it.

This face may be slightly less mathematically regular than the east gallery face. That is accepted if the room compositions are better. The objective is a coherent court, not four copied elevations.

## 7. Architectural reading

The Reference House now has three nested orders:

1. **outer front order** — centred four-bay field, off-centre entrance;
2. **internal/courtyard order** — re-centred X = 6.0 m court and garden sequence;
3. **service order** — deliberately subordinate east-side geography, still mirrorable until a real site fixes handedness.

They are related but not identical.

This avoids two weak outcomes:

- forcing every internal wall onto an exterior grid;
- allowing every room to punch openings independently through an otherwise Georgian-derived exterior.

The house remains calm because the exceptions are bounded and legible.

---

## 8. Roof and cornice zone

RH-04 fixes only the **wall/cornice control**, not the roof drainage design.

Selected intent:

- substantial but restrained outer cornice/eaves line around **+6250 mm**;
- pitched roof remains the preferred Reference House family;
- roof should read as a real load-bearing/weathering assembly, not as a parapet-concealed technical lid;
- courtyard requires its own inner eaves/rainwater geometry;
- ridge heights, hips, valleys, gutters, overflow paths, roof hatch and safe access remain RH-08 decisions.

A notional pitched envelope may appear in RH-04 drawings only to test silhouette.

---

## 9. Material / tectonic scope

RH-04 does not freeze finishes.

The current architectural direction remains compatible with:

- red facing brick as the leading outer-wall candidate;
- genuine stone / cast-stone or painted joinery used selectively at sills, lintels and entrance hierarchy;
- substantial timber or aluminium-window assemblies resolved honestly within G-01 proportions;
- a quieter courtyard material dialect later, potentially including the previously explored warmer/Andalusian influence.

No material is allowed to repair weak geometry cosmetically.

---

## 10. Rewinds and rejected moves

RH-04 makes **no major rewind** to RH-02/RH-03.

It records one fine adjustment:

- the front entrance centreline is controlled at **X = 7.35 m** as part of the four-bay field; this remains within the RH-03 arrival passage and does not require a room-order change.

HFI-08B later reopens RH-03 only for stair going. Its 300 mm selection is rechecked here and requires **no RH-04 architectural rewind**: H1, the bay field, window hierarchy and visible storey proportions remain unchanged.

Rejected:

1. moving the stair to obtain a centred door;
2. creating a fake central door/blank entrance composition;
3. making all four front ground openings identical despite the stair;
4. applying the front bay grid to all courtyard faces;
5. enlarging ground-floor height merely to make the house feel more "Georgian";
6. using symmetry as a whole-building boolean.

---

## 11. What RH-05 now inherits

The structural stage must treat the architectural coordinates as real obligations rather than approximate decoration.

At minimum it must test:

- the four front opening axes and resulting masonry piers;
- the stair opening / trimming implied by the three-flight stair;
- courtyard-wall openings and their vertical alignment;
- room/opening conditions around the west court edge;
- the 350 mm planning floor-depth zone against plausible engineered-floor structure;
- whether loadbearing wall lines can remain simple around the central court;
- whether any required beam/transfer begins to dominate room or façade order.

RH-05 may change member depths and structural families. If it requires moving the front bay order, changing the courtyard opening order or materially changing visible floor/ceiling datums, it must explicitly rewind RH-04.

# RH-04 gate record

**Result:** **PASS; HFI-08B stair recheck PASS.**

The house now has a coupled plan/section/elevation baseline in which:

- the storey hierarchy is explicit;
- the principal stair still fits at the selected datum with 300 mm goings;
- the front is ordered without falsifying the off-centre entrance condition;
- courtyard elevations derive from their actual architectural owners;
- the front and courtyard use different scoped orders deliberately;
- opening hierarchy reflects room/storey hierarchy without pretending to be a universal Georgian ratio system;
- roof/cornice control is sufficient for architecture while drainage remains correctly downstream;
- no technical feature has been cosmetically hidden to achieve the composition.

**No maturity promotion is claimed.** Structural, service, environmental and physical coordination remain open.

The HFI-08B recheck changes no RH-04 datum or opening order. It records only the updated stair arithmetic derived from the corrected RH-03 source.
