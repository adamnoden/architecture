# Whole-house material policy

**Owner requirement, established 30 October 2026.** This policy applies to Ovid's House under the [owner-house charter](../../project/owner-house-charter.md). It governs the whole installed house, not just its appearance. It is a deliberate preference and design constraint; it is not a claim that every natural material is safe or every synthetic material is technically inferior.

## Governing rule

**Design out plastic wherever a suitable non-plastic solution can be established. Visible and touchable plastic is particularly unacceptable; concealment is not an exemption.** Do not select a synthetic-polymer solution merely because it is customary, cheap, readily available or already drawn. First challenge the need for the component, then the assembly, then the material. The owner may prefer to delete or redesign a feature rather than accept its polymer dependency.

Treat this as a strong default prohibition with a controlled, owner-approved exception process, not a percentage aspiration. No polymer exception is approved by this document. Safety, structural adequacy, fire protection, electrical insulation, hygiene, moisture control and lawful performance must not be sacrificed to achieve a misleading material-purity claim. Where a necessary function has no demonstrated acceptable alternative, leave that selection unresolved and present the actual trade-off to the owner; never silently install the polymer or omit the function.

## Scope: inspect the assembly, not the label

The target includes synthetic plastics, foams, resins and elastomers whether visible, concealed, structural, a coating or a small accessory. Audit PVC/uPVC, PE, PP, ABS, polyurethane/PIR, EPS/XPS, acrylics, epoxies, polyester composites, synthetic rubbers, silicone sealants and polymer-modified products. These examples are a search scope, not an exhaustive chemical blacklist.

Inspect pipes and sleeves; gutters and channels; roof underlays and vapour-control layers; insulation and its binder/facing; wet-room membranes; damp-proofing; drainage geotextiles; timber glues and board resins; paint, lacquer and floor finishes; adhesives, sealants and tapes; glazing seals and thermal breaks; cable insulation and terminations; sockets and controls; pumps, valves, tanks and appliance internals. Permanently installed equipment remains in scope even where its internal material cannot practically be altered. Record its residuals rather than claiming that a metal enclosure makes it plastic-free. Temporary packaging and later loose possessions are separate questions, not a loophole for installed products.

Wood, cellulose and other naturally occurring polymers are not excluded merely because they are chemically polymeric. Conversely, 'natural', 'bio-based', 'mineral', 'metal', 'low-VOC', 'recyclable' or 'traditional' does not prove an installed assembly free of synthetic constituents. Natural rubber, waxes, oils, bitumen, preservatives and modified natural materials need their own suitability and composition review; their names confer no automatic approval. A polymer coating on metal or an epoxy backing on stone still counts. A thin brass face over a plastic body is not the intended material expression.

## Selection order

1. Remove an unnecessary component or water trap through geometry and simpler construction.
2. Develop an appropriate mineral, metallic, glass, ceramic, solid-timber or other evidenced non-plastic assembly, including all joints, finishes and fixings.
3. Prefer accessible mechanical, folded, lapped, soldered or other suitable joints where they genuinely perform; do not replace a necessary flexible seal with a brittle joint solely to avoid a polymer.
4. Only after the alternatives are properly challenged, propose a narrowly bounded residual polymer exception for explicit owner decision. Make it as small, inspectable, isolated and replaceable as its safety and performance permit.

'Conventional' does not mean 'plastic by default'. Equally, avoiding plastic does not authorise an elaborate bespoke mechanism when a simpler sound non-plastic assembly exists. Compare whole-life access, craft availability, repair, fire/moisture performance, cost and the burden placed on ordinary occupation. No universal lifespan or health advantage is inferred from this preference.

## Starting material directions, not product approvals

Exposed rainwater collection is to develop in **copper**, with brass/bronze channel and grate details investigated where suitable. Other non-plastic materials can be compared for a stated technical or architectural reason; they do not replace the copper direction by habit. Prefer genuine exposed material and an intentional ageing strategy over an undisclosed polymer clearcoat. The [rainwater design brief](../../project/rwt/rainwater-elegance-brief.md) owns water-handling choices.

For the rest of the house, investigate solid timber and mechanically fixed joinery, mineral plasters and appropriate finishes, masonry and ceramics, metallic service routes, and inorganic insulation or other verified alternatives. These are candidate families, not a claim that every version, glue, backing, gasket or coating complies. Investigate mineral-insulated electrical routes where useful, but assess the entire certified circuit and its terminations; a metal conduit around polymer-insulated wiring is not polymer elimination.

Do not introduce hazardous legacy materials, deficient insulation, unsuitable lead-containing water-contact components or untested wet assemblies to satisfy this policy. Do not remove manufacturer-required seals, fire-stopping, insulation or compatible accessories without an engineered alternative.

## Evidence and exceptions

Each selected assembly must disclose its constituent materials and installed accessories with recoverable product revision and technical evidence. Supplier marketing alone is insufficient; missing constituent information is **unknown**, not zero plastic. Seek composition declarations, technical data and installation instructions; no single safety data sheet or environmental declaration is presumed exhaustive.

A proposed exception must state: exact product/constituent and function; location and visibility; approximate installed quantity and uncertainty; exposure and expected replacement interval; alternatives actually considered, including redesign/deletion; why each alternative failed; the safety/performance evidence; access and replacement method; and the owner's dated approval or rejection. Proposed, approved, rejected and unknown are distinct. Approval applies only to that location, function and revision, and must be reconsidered after substitution. Silence or agent judgement does not count as owner approval.

No generic exception exists for 'hidden', 'small', 'necessary', 'industry standard' or 'inside an appliance'. Review priority is visible/touchable surfaces first, then substantial or inaccessible concealed assemblies, then remaining functional residues; all remain in scope. Do not game compliance with a mass percentage dominated by masonry. Report the unresolved assemblies and the count, location and extent of approved exceptions. Do not advertise a plastic-free house while undisclosed or approved polymer contents remain.

## Applicability and design change

Earlier PVC/PE drainage and polymer-dependent wet-room studies remain recoverable evidence of their stated geometry and failure tests. Their material preferences are superseded by this policy, **including hidden carriers, sleeves and spacers**. Their successful arithmetic is not approval for the current house. Reusing a route with copper, another metal or clay requires new product, support, joint, movement, corrosion and renewal checks; do not substitute a material name into the old calculation.

The research and alternative-selection programme is [Material integrity](../../project/mat/material-integrity-programme.md). Its compliance review must be reconciled with the owning roof, opening, interior, service and house-design sources before selected-house integration. The transferable architectural lesson to test is reducing concealed dependency and making residual materials inspectable; this owner's stronger material prohibition is not silently promoted to a universal pattern.
