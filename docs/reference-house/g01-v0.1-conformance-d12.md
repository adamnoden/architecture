# Reference House — G-01 v0.1 Conformance

**Result:** **PASS at coordinated virtual-design scope**  
**Grammar authority:** [G-01 v0.1](../grammar/g01-v0.1.md)  
**Evidence context:** [G-01 Validation and Limits](../grammar/g01-validation.md)

The Reference House conforms to the six relationships defined by G-01 v0.1 without requiring the grammar to absorb the house’s courtyard morphology, technical systems or construction choices.

This is an architectural-language result only. It does not establish technical adequacy, regulatory compliance, historical authenticity or architectural quality in general.

## Result

| Rule | Reference House disposition |
|---|---|
| G01-R01 — ordered classical architectural field | **PASS** |
| G01-R02 — related and ranked opening family | **PASS** |
| G01-R03 — architecturally ranked principal entrance | **PASS** |
| G01-R04 — integrated upper termination / roof order | **PASS** |
| G01-R05 — relational spatial + sectional hierarchy | **PASS** |
| G01-R06 — concentrated classical articulation | **PASS** |

No grammar change is needed to accommodate the house, and no project-specific feature has been promoted into G-01 merely because the house uses it.

## Ordered architectural fields

The principal front uses a centred four-bay field at approximately X = **1.95 / 4.65 / 7.35 / 10.05 m**, while the principal entrance occupies bay 3 rather than the geometric centre.

The other elevations use related but separately scoped orders:

- the garden elevation uses a three-axis composition;
- the service elevation groups its openings and technical expression into three broad fields;
- the west elevation follows a quieter room-owned order;
- the courtyard elevations use their own internal order rather than inheriting the front grid.

The house therefore demonstrates the distinction at the core of G01-R01: classical order can be explicit without requiring one whole-building symmetry rule.

The courtyard remains **project morphology**, not a G-01 requirement. Applying an ordered field to its elevations does not make courtyard planning part of the grammar.

See [Reference House — Technical Coordination](structural-coordination.md) and [Vertical Interface Coordination](vertical-bay-coordination.md).

## Related and ranked openings

The front elevation uses a related opening family rather than a collection of unrelated apertures:

- principal ground-floor windows are larger and taller;
- upper windows are smaller while retaining the principal vertical axes;
- the stair window remains within the field while its sill height responds to the stair and section;
- the principal entrance is a ranked exception within the same composition.

Other elevations use their own related families. Technical terminals on the service side remain subordinate to occupied openings rather than becoming equal compositional events.

G-01’s sash-derived preference is architectural rather than technological. The Reference House can therefore retain a contemporary high-performance window family provided its visible proportions and subdivision continue to belong to the selected opening language. Final product, frame and egress mechanisms remain technical/detail decisions.

## Ranked principal entrance

The entrance is deliberately off the geometric centre of the façade but remains architecturally primary.

Its rank comes from its dedicated bay condition, surround, threshold role and relationship to the arrival sequence. The approach moves from the off-centre entrance into the stair hall and then re-centres on the courtyard/garden axis.

This satisfies G01-R03 without relying on a portico, grand steps or a centred doorway. The substantially level threshold intent is a contemporary requirement and does not weaken the architectural hierarchy.

## Upper termination and roof

The Reference House uses a recessive low-slope roof family behind a controlled cornice/parapet condition. The roof form is driven partly by the water and maintenance geography of the courtyard house: the roof fields drain outward rather than concentrating water at the inner court.

That solution conforms because G01-R04 requires **integration**, not roof suppression. The roof, cornice/parapet, drainage edges and visible service/PV consequences must resolve as one deliberate upper condition rather than as an unrelated technical cap.

A more visibly expressed hipped or pitched roof could also conform to G-01; the selected low-slope family remains a Reference House decision rather than a grammar rule.

The current roof, drainage and maintenance relationship is recorded in [Reference House — Technical Coordination](structural-coordination.md).

## Spatial and sectional hierarchy

The plan establishes explicit architectural rank rather than treating every room and route as equivalent.

The ground floor distinguishes drawing room, family room, entrance/stair hall, study, dining hall, kitchen and concentrated service geography. The upper floor distinguishes bedrooms, stair hall, courtyard gallery, cross-landing and stacked bathroom/service conditions.

The section also carries rank:

- ground FFL: ±0;
- ground clear ceiling: **+2950 mm**;
- first-floor FFL: **+3300 mm**;
- upper clear ceiling: **+6000 mm**;
- cornice/eaves control: approximately **+6250 mm**.

This gives restrained ground-floor primacy without requiring a piano nobile, double-height principal room or fixed historical room ratio.

The service geography is subordinate because of the contemporary plan and maintenance strategy, not because G-01 requires historical servant circulation.

## Concentrated classical articulation

The house uses classical emphasis selectively rather than coating every surface with equal decorative intensity.

The principal entrance, opening hierarchy and upper termination carry the strongest architectural emphasis. Quieter wall fields remain comparatively restrained. The exact moulding, joinery and metalwork vocabulary can develop at detail stage without changing the underlying conformance result, provided it continues to express rather than obscure the established hierarchy.

## What this result does not mean

Conformance to G-01 v0.1 does not prove that:

- the courtyard belongs to Georgian grammar;
- the low-slope roof is a preferred G-01 roof type;
- masonry, aluminium windows or any selected technical family are grammar requirements;
- the house is structurally, environmentally or regulatorily adequate;
- every unresolved product/detail decision will automatically preserve conformance;
- a conforming design is necessarily good architecture.

The result is narrower: **the coordinated architectural order of the Reference House satisfies G-01 v0.1 without special pleading or project-specific additions to the grammar.**
