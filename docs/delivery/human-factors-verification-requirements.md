# Human-Factors Verification — Implementation Brief Module

**Status:** delivery module for the architect-facing RIBA implementation brief  
**Parent principles:** P1 — Build for time; P8 — Design for repose  
**Source programme:** Human Factors / Low-Friction Inhabitation (HFI)

## Project requirement

The design team shall demonstrate that high-frequency or high-consequence domestic routes, controls and interfaces work as **complete actions in the foreseeable states in which they are used**.

A nominal dimension, room adjacency or product data sheet is not sufficient evidence where the actual task also depends on approach, turning, opening, carrying, reaching, force, set-down, feedback, withdrawal or another occupied condition.

The requirement is proportional. It does not demand specialist-access geometry everywhere, universal equipment provision or bespoke testing for ordinary well-evidenced construction.

## Proposed non-negotiable

| ID | Requirement | Principle | Target / acceptance test | Owner | RIBA stage locked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| N17 | Principal repeated-use routes, controls and interfaces are verified as complete occupied actions | P1/P8 | Human-Factors Verification Schedule covering selected scenarios, finished/net geometry, action sequence, evidence authority and unresolved conditions; no material repeated-use burden accepted solely because an isolated nominal dimension passes | Architect + relevant specialists | 3/4 |

## Verification schedule

Create one schedule for the project rather than scattering ergonomic claims through drawings and specifications.

For each selected condition record:

- location / interface ID;
- ordinary task or action sequence;
- why it is frequent, consequential or difficult to retrofit;
- relevant foreseeable occupied state;
- governing statutory or guidance constraints;
- project target, if stronger than the external minimum;
- target class and evidence authority;
- modelled / measured / product-specific inputs;
- remaining `NOT-MODELLED` facts;
- acceptance method and stage;
- responsible designer;
- result and any accepted residual burden.

Project targets must retain their provenance. A stronger project preference does not rewrite the external guidance method from which it was compared.

## What must be measured as finished geometry

Where the action depends on actual usable space, verify the **net installed condition**, not only the planning reservation.

Examples include:

- stair going, rise, pitch and headroom;
- clear stair width after strings, guards and handrails;
- landing and turning space after finishes and fixed elements;
- door clear opening and usable approach after frame, ironmongery and stops;
- threshold upstand/transition after weathering, drainage and floor finishes;
- corridor/corner clear width after radiators, joinery, controls and other fixed projections;
- sanitary, shower and bath approach/transfer/assistance space where selected by the brief;
- kitchen/appliance open-state clearances where the sequence depends on them;
- fixed-storage reach and bending geometry where material to ordinary use;
- large-object or replacement-component swept paths where relevant.

Planning-model geometry may establish feasibility. It does not automatically discharge the finished-interface test.

## Complete-action test

For each selected route or interface, test the relevant sequence rather than one frozen pose.

A sequence may include:

1. approach;
2. turn / align;
3. reach or grip;
4. operate / open;
5. carry, support or assist where foreseeable;
6. pass / transfer / step;
7. set down or regrip where needed;
8. receive intelligible feedback;
9. close / reset / leave.

Use only occupied states that are foreseeable and material to the project. Typical prompts include occupied hands, wet hands, low light, fatigue, temporary injury, reduced mobility, assisting another person and carrying a bulky ordinary object.

Do not turn the prompt list into a universal requirement that every interface simultaneously serves every scenario.

## Controls, doors and hardware

Where operation quality matters, test the installed assembly rather than an isolated component.

Relevant evidence can include:

- actual operating force or manufacturer evidence appropriate to the complete assembly;
- grip/reach geometry;
- one-handed operation where the task commonly occupies the other hand;
- control location relative to the condition being controlled;
- direction/state feedback;
- manual fallback where automation affects ordinary comfort;
- approach and withdrawal space;
- interaction between closers, seals, latches, thresholds and opening force.

A low-force handle does not prove an easy door if the closer, seals or threshold create the real burden.

## Thresholds and environmental transitions

Threshold verification shall treat the transition as one interface, coordinating:

- level change / trip and wheelability consequence;
- weather and water exclusion;
- drainage and falls;
- air / thermal / acoustic duties where applicable;
- door operation;
- durability, cleaning and debris tolerance;
- movement and replacement;
- interior/exterior approach geometry.

Do not flatten a threshold by quietly creating a moisture or envelope defect elsewhere.

## Stairs

For a principal domestic stair, technical design shall confirm the selected architectural geometry in the finished assembly, including as applicable:

- finished rise and going consistency;
- pitch and applicable numeric relationship;
- net clear width after strings/guards/handrails;
- headroom;
- landing geometry;
- handrail/guard continuity and graspability;
- lighting and conspicuity of changes in level where material;
- door conflicts at landings;
- carrying/reduced-attention sequence.

A project may adopt a stronger stair target than minimum guidance. Record it as a **project target with its own authority**, not as a fictional statutory requirement.

A nominal width alone shall not be used to claim stairlift compatibility. Any later stairlift proposition requires product/family-specific parking, transfer, rail/chair, residual-route, power and door-conflict checks.

## Object and domestic-task routes

Use swept-path or sequence checks only where the object/task is material enough to earn them.

Candidate cases include:

- routine groceries;
- waste/recycling;
- laundry/linen;
- garden-serving routes;
- cleaning equipment;
- care tasks;
- principal furniture moves;
- replacement of large appliances or building components.

The test should first seek the narrow causal bottleneck. Do not respond to one difficult turn by enlarging every corridor in the house.

## RIBA stage requirements

### Stage 1 — Preparation and Briefing

Identify:

- occupants/scenarios explicitly in the brief;
- high-frequency and high-consequence domestic actions;
- foreseeable life-course or temporary states worth preserving capacity for;
- any specialist equipment that is required now versus merely conceivable later.

**Gate:** distinguish a present user requirement, a durable enabling provision and a speculative future feature.

### Stage 2 — Concept Design

Prepare an initial Human-Factors Verification Schedule.

Use it to identify geometry that is expensive to change later, including principal stairs, circulation bottlenecks, important thresholds, wet-room approaches and replacement/object routes where relevant.

Apply the proportionality test before allocating extra permanent area.

**Gate:** concept geometry should not freeze a known high-frequency/high-consequence burden merely because minimum guidance can be met later by detail.

### Stage 3 — Spatial Coordination

Coordinate selected complete actions in the actual plan/section model.

At minimum where relevant:

- principal route continuity;
- stair/landing geometry;
- door approaches and opening zones;
- corner manoeuvres;
- object swept paths;
- sanitary/wet-room clearances;
- appliance open states;
- control positions;
- set-down/staging positions.

Record facts that remain `NOT-MODELLED`; do not invent detailed geometry to make the schedule look complete.

**Gate:** planning reservations must demonstrate credible feasibility for the selected scenarios before technical design.

### Stage 4 — Technical Design

Replace nominal/planning assumptions with finished-interface evidence.

Resolve:

- final build-ups affecting net dimensions;
- frames, strings, guards, handrails, ironmongery and fixed projections;
- thresholds with drainage/weathering;
- installed operating-force dependencies;
- control reach, sequence and feedback;
- product-specific equipment assumptions where any are actually selected;
- construction tolerances capable of eroding a critical net clearance.

The Human-Factors Verification Schedule shall identify the evidence that will be checked at Stage 5/6.

**Gate:** a critical human-use target is not closed by a nominal drawing dimension if ordinary construction or product interfaces can materially reduce the usable result.

### Stage 5 — Manufacturing and Construction

Protect human-use geometry from substitution and site drift.

Use hold points where a later finish, frame, fitting or product choice could consume a critical clearance or alter an operating sequence.

Verify representative installed conditions by measurement/operation where the requirement cannot be established reliably from records alone.

### Stage 6 — Handover

Confirm selected as-built human-use conditions and demonstrate occupant-facing controls where specified.

Record material deviations between design target and installed condition. Do not mark a requirement complete merely because the drawing intended it.

### Stage 7 — Use

Post-occupancy review should ask where occupants have developed recurring workarounds, careful choreography or avoidable compensations that were not predicted by the design review.

Feed material findings back to the project record and HFI evidence base without treating anecdote alone as a universal rule.

## Prototype gate

**HFI does not create a general human-factors prototype programme.**

Do **not** build a mock-up where established evidence, ordinary coordinated drawings, product data and conventional inspection adequately answer the question.

A physical prototype / first article is earned only when one or more of the following applies:

- the interface is genuinely non-standard or bespoke;
- several coupled actions cannot be resolved credibly in drawings/model alone;
- operating force/feedback depends on a novel complete assembly;
- tolerances or movement may materially change the usable condition;
- repeated fabrication/installation introduces workmanship uncertainty;
- the project makes a stronger performance claim that cannot otherwise be demonstrated proportionately.

Where a prototype is earned, test the **complete interface/action**, not a decorative fragment.

Conventional stairs, ordinary compliant door assemblies and established threshold systems do not become prototype candidates merely because HFI reviewed them.

## Acceptance questions

For each selected condition ask:

1. What complete action is being performed?
2. Why is it frequent or consequential enough to deserve design attention?
3. Which occupied state materially changes the action, if any?
4. What is the causal bottleneck?
5. Is the relevant dimension nominal or genuinely net/finished?
6. What external rule/guidance applies, and what is only a project preference?
7. Could a local change solve the problem without blanket area or complexity?
8. What structure, fire, moisture, services, security, privacy, acoustic or architectural duty constrains the response?
9. What remains `NOT-MODELLED` and at what stage will it become knowable?
10. Is conventional evidence sufficient, or has a physical prototype genuinely earned itself?

## Evidence boundary

Distinguish:

- **law / statutory guidance:** applicable regulatory duties and Approved Document methods;
- **standards / professional guidance:** scenario-specific ergonomic, inclusive-design and product guidance;
- **research evidence:** empirical evidence about falls, reach, force, perception or task performance;
- **project target:** a deliberately stronger value or sequence chosen for this commission;
- **product-specific evidence:** geometry, force, parking, transfer and installation facts tied to a selected product/family;
- **architectural judgement:** proportionality, domestic character and trade-off decisions not reducible to one numeric threshold.

Passing one layer does not silently prove the others.
