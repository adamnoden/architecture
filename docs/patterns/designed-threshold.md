# HSA-P-020 — Designed Threshold

## Context

Two floor/space conditions meet at a door or opening. Their finishes, substrates, movement, wear, levels and—at external doors—weathering duties may differ.

## Problem

Thresholds are often treated as leftover strips between independently designed floors and doors. The final trade then resolves level changes, exposed edges, movement and water with sealant, filler or fragile trim.

## Pattern

**Treat the threshold as a deliberate interface element that resolves the change between adjacent floor/door conditions: datum, wear, movement, edge protection and, where external, drainage/weathering and accessibility.**

The threshold may be visually quiet. Its importance lies in making the change of system explicit rather than hiding it in brittle finish.

## Forces

- level access and trip avoidance constrain projection;
- floor assemblies may move or be replaced independently;
- thresholds receive concentrated wear and impact;
- external thresholds must resist or drain weather;
- visually heavy threshold pieces can fragment rooms;
- some openings benefit from seamless continuity.

## Variants

- durable timber/stone/metal wear strip;
- flush movement/interface piece;
- external sill/threshold with drainage and door-seal duties;
- removable perimeter/edge element protecting a changeable floor system.

## Proportionality

Use a distinct threshold where there is a real constructional or performance transition. Do not insert one merely to decorate an opening that is genuinely continuous.

## Boundary debt

At external or protected doors, resolve water, air, thermal, fire/smoke, acoustic, security and accessibility duties as applicable. A visible metal or stone strip does not itself discharge those obligations.

## Permanent-fabric impact

A durable threshold may become part of the long-lived opening/floor interface. Fixings, drainage channels and edge supports should be accessible or replaceable where practical.

## Workmanship and tolerance

Use the threshold to accommodate a defined interface tolerance—not to conceal arbitrary floor-height errors. Establish finished-floor datums before adjacent assemblies are fixed.

## Occupation / repose impact

The threshold should feel natural underfoot, remain quiet, avoid avoidable trip hazards and belong to the room/door composition. Technical interfaces should not announce themselves unnecessarily.

## Failure modes

- threshold becomes a trip edge;
- finish movement cracks adjacent brittle material;
- external water tracks beneath it;
- replaceable floor cannot be removed without destroying the threshold;
- threshold hides a gross datum error;
- metal interface rattles or becomes visually gratuitous.

## Evidence

Thresholds as wear, movement, weathering and accessibility interfaces are mature construction practice. These duties recur at transitions between independently resolved assemblies rather than belonging solely to the door or floor.

## Does not prove

The pattern does not require a visible raised strip at every doorway, nor one material or geometry across internal and external conditions.

## Reference House application

The Reference House uses thresholds only at real changes of construction or wear condition. Stone, timber or metal is selected for the actual duty, not as technical decoration.

## Formalisation boundary

Adjacent assembly identities, level relationships, replacement dependencies and applicable boundary roles can be represented formally. Tactile quality, visual weight and detailed weathering performance remain design/evidence questions.
