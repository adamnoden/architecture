# Strategy — Separate Structural Floor from Changeable Layers Where Proportionate

**Status:** canonical strategy  
**Evidence:** Supported direction; domestic implementation value remains context-dependent

## Proposition

Where future change or service access justifies it, keep the primary structural floor conceptually and physically distinct from shorter-lived finish, levelling, acoustic and service layers.

The aim is not maximal removability. It is to avoid consuming the primary structure whenever a faster-changing layer needs replacement or access.

## Why this is a strategy rather than a pattern

The valid physical responses vary materially:

- ordinary layered floors with no routine access;
- shallow service zones;
- local access bands;
- removable carriers in selected areas;
- raised-access or platform systems in unusual cases.

Those are not one recurring spatial relationship. The stronger reusable knowledge is the lifecycle separation between durable structure and changeable layers, subject to proportionality.

The earlier **Finish-Agnostic Floor Platform** remains an implementation challenger, not the strategy itself.

## Use

Ask, for each room/zone:

1. what actually changes faster than the structural floor;
2. whether services genuinely need future access here;
3. whether access can be local rather than whole-room;
4. whether acoustic, tactile and fire performance are weakened by separation;
5. whether the added depth, material and interfaces earn themselves over the building life.

Prefer the simplest conventional floor where the change/access case is weak.

## Guardrails

- do not make the removable layer the sole source of critical diaphragm action without explicit engineering;
- a floor that feels hollow, rocks, squeaks or advertises its access system has failed the project's repose requirement;
- do not generalise commercial raised-floor precedent into domestic rooms without physical comparison;
- underfloor heating, waterproofing and wet-room layers may justify different relationships from ordinary dry rooms.

## Evidence

External precedent supports layered construction and deliberate service-zone separation. The project's **Finish-Agnostic Floor Platform** research tests the harder domestic question: whether that separation can produce a low-profile, maintainable floor without sacrificing acoustic, tactile or architectural quality.

Relevant project work includes [Reversible Assembly Candidates](../reversible-assembly-candidates.md) and [Finish-Agnostic Floor Platform Options](../../research/finish-agnostic-floor-platform-options.md).

## Does not prove

This strategy does not establish that every room should have an accessible floor, that a common platform interface is desirable, or that a raised/removable floor is better than a conventional durable floor.
