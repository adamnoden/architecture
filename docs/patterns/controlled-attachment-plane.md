# HSA-P-022 — Controlled Attachment Plane

## Context

Long-lived walls repeatedly receive shorter-lived fit-out: pictures, mirrors, cabinets, radiators, controls, lining systems and joinery.

## Problem

Treating permanent fabric as an unlimited fixing surface causes repeated drilling, chasing and patching over the life of the house. A proprietary technical subframe can solve that problem while creating another one: depth, acoustic hollowness, dependence on unique hardware and an industrial room character.

## Pattern

**Provide a durable, load-classified attachment plane between permanent fabric and faster-changing fit-out where repeated fixing or replaceable layers justify it. Concentrate permanent anchors into that plane and keep later ordinary attachment on the replaceable side where practical.**

The invariant is controlled attachment, not a particular rail, frame or backplane product.

## Forces

- rooms must retain convincing solidity and ordinary fixing behaviour;
- ordinary loads differ from engineered heavy loads;
- continuous rails/frames can create acoustic or thermal bridges;
- proprietary clips may outlive their supply chain;
- site-built permanent walls and manufactured fit-out need tolerance adjustment;
- extra depth and material must earn themselves;
- hidden services need protection from later fasteners.

## Variants / implementation families

- timber grounds;
- metal rails or slotted sections;
- local heavy-load plates;
- joinery-integrated fixing zones;
- real picture/dado rails carrying suitable loads;
- sparse backplane frames supporting replaceable linings.

These are implementations, not separate patterns merely because their hardware differs.

## Design requirements

- establish load classes and distinguish ordinary from engineered loads;
- define safe fixing and no-fix zones;
- prefer non-proprietary or remanufacturable geometry where practical;
- coordinate service routes and later fastener depth;
- provide bounded tolerance adjustment;
- record permanent anchors and allowable loads;
- allow the attachment plane itself to be inspected/replaced where its life is shorter than the permanent wall.

## Proportionality

The pattern is strongest where repeated fixing, major joinery, replaceable lining or service access already justifies an intermediate layer. It should not add a technical subframe to every wall by default.

## Boundary debt

Check fire, acoustic, air/vapour and moisture consequences of cavities, rails and repeated penetrations. An attachment plane must not become an uncontrolled service void or flanking path.

## Permanent-fabric impact

Permanent masonry/structure still receives fixings; the point is to make them sparse, engineered and documented rather than repeated unpredictably through occupation and renewal.

## Workmanship and tolerance

Define the controlling wall datum, expected incoming variation, adjustment capacity and remediation threshold. The plane should absorb ordinary variation without becoming a licence to conceal grossly inaccurate permanent construction.

## Occupation / repose impact

The attachment system should normally be invisible. A wall that rattles, sounds hollow, reveals an intrusive module or forces occupants to understand a technical rack has not earned the lifecycle benefit.

## Failure modes

- proprietary hardware becomes unavailable;
- users bypass the intended fixing plane;
- heavy loads are attached as ordinary loads;
- hidden services conflict with later fasteners;
- continuous rails create acoustic/fire problems;
- depth becomes disproportionate;
- tolerance adjustment is exhausted during initial installation.

## Evidence

Independent lining, mechanical fixing and robust sheet/rail systems are mature. The distinctive HSA proposition is the sparse, load-classified intermediate attachment plane serving multiple faster-changing uses. Phase 6 merged the former Architectural Backplane and Fixing Infrastructure ideas around this stronger invariant.

## Does not prove

The pattern does not justify a whole-room subframe, one proprietary rail system or replaceable linings everywhere. Whether the intermediate plane earns its material/depth remains project-specific.

## Reference House application

The Reference House develops the attachment plane with the Georgian lining grammar so selected skirting, dado, picture-rail or panel divisions can coincide with genuine fixing/adjustment zones without turning the room into a technical grid.

## Formalisation boundary

Anchor occurrences, load classes, safe/no-fix zones, supported fit-out and replacement dependencies can be represented formally. Perceived solidity, visual integration and system proportion remain architectural/prototype judgements.
