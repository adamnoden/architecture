# HSA-P-002 — Plant Room as Service Hub

**Pilot language record.** The full developed pattern prose remains in [`../core-12.md`](../core-12.md) during the migration pilot.

## Context

A house contains replaceable plant, controls, isolation, distribution equipment and recurring maintenance tasks that otherwise tend to disperse into lofts, cupboards, facades and occupied rooms.

## Invariant

Concentrate major distribution, controls and replaceable plant into a service hub where system relationships, working space, isolation and replacement paths are explicit.

## Why this is a pattern

The invariant is spatial and operational rather than product-specific. The hub may be a room, enlarged cupboard, utility zone or other proportionate architectural space. What matters is the concentration of maintenance-intensive system relationships and the geometry needed to work on them.

## Language relationships

### Completes `HSA-P-001 — Controlled Utility Entry`

The utility entry establishes a deliberate transition from external networks into the building. The service hub commonly develops that transition into understandable internal distribution, isolation and plant organisation.

This is deliberately **not** expressed as a hard requirement. A service hub can exist where utility entries are separated, inherited or supplier-constrained.

## Sequence note

Establish the service hub while the plan can still respond to replacement paths, acoustics, heat rejection, drainage and external access. A late “plant cupboard” chosen after room planning is not equivalent.

## Formalisation boundary

Potentially formalisable consequences include equipment occurrences, required working volumes, replacement paths, access relationships, drainage obligations and system connectivity.

Whether the hub is architecturally proportionate, quiet enough and appropriately placed remains partly architectural and engineering judgement.
