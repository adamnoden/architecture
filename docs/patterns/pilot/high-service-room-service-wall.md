# HSA-P-004 — High-Service-Room Service Wall

**Pilot language record.** The full developed pattern prose remains in [`../core-12.md`](../core-12.md) during the migration pilot.

## Context

Bathrooms, kitchens and utilities concentrate water, drainage, ventilation, power, controls and replaceable fittings. Conventional fit-out often places the highest-maintenance components behind brittle or wet finishes.

## Invariant

Where geometry permits, arrange high-service fixtures against an accessible service zone so shorter-lived components can be reached from a dry or otherwise maintainable side without dismantling the occupied finish unnecessarily.

## Why this is a pattern

The invariant is the relationship between a high-service room, a maintainable adjacent zone and the boundary between them. It can be realised through several wall, joinery or cupboard constructions.

## Language relationships

### Completes `HSA-P-003 — Horizontal Service Spine`

The horizontal spine provides coherent primary distribution. The service wall localises that topology at the room scale and creates an orderly place for short branches, isolation and maintainable components.

The relation is not exclusive: a service wall may be useful even without a formal building-wide spine.

## Sequence note

Test service-wall opportunity while wet-room and kitchen adjacencies are still movable. If the plan scatters high-service fixtures across unrelated walls, later detailing should not be expected to recover the lost topology cheaply.

## Formalisation boundary

Potentially formalisable consequences include adjacency, access-side relationships, working/withdrawal volumes, component locations and boundary obligations.

Whether the extra wall thickness is proportionate and whether the resulting room remains acoustically private, visually settled and spatially good remain architectural judgements.
