# HSA-P-007 — Permanent Opening / Replaceable Window

## Context

The opening and surrounding wall may last much longer than frames, glazing units, seals, ironmongery and operating hardware.

## Problem

Ordinary installation can fuse the shorter-lived window into plaster, foam, sealant and masonry so that full-frame replacement damages sound long-lived fabric and primary environmental layers.

## Pattern

**Treat the opening as enduring architecture and the window as a replaceable assembly within a deliberate interface that manages fixing, drainage, thermal continuity, airtightness and movement.**

The required outcome is replaceability without reconstructing the opening; no proprietary removable subframe is implied.

## Forces

- high-performance installation needs robust air, water and thermal continuity;
- subframes and extra interfaces can add cost and thermal bridging;
- concealed fixings should remain reachable;
- external seals and hardware remain maintenance items;
- repeated replacement must not erode masonry edges or primary boundaries.

## Design requirements

- distinguish frame weathering from building-side drainage;
- define a real mounting/fixing zone;
- avoid brittle finish bridging between moving frame and wall;
- draw full-frame replacement, not only glazing replacement;
- keep primary air/water layers intact during internal trim removal where practical;
- protect permanent opening edges from repeated work.

## Proportionality

The case is strongest where the wall/opening is genuinely longer lived and window replacement is foreseeable within the building's life. Extra complexity that merely relocates ordinary seal renewal is not automatically valuable.

## Boundary debt

The interface must resolve rain, drainage, air, thermal continuity, vapour/moisture as relevant, acoustics, security and movement. Replaceability is not permission to weaken any of them.

## Permanent-fabric impact

Use deliberate fixing locations and interfaces rather than repeated destructive trimming/chasing. Any permanent subframe or anchor must justify its own service life and thermal consequences.

## Workmanship and tolerance

Define the tolerance zone between masonry/opening and manufactured frame, including adjustment and remediation limits. Architectural trims may cover the designed tolerance but should not conceal grossly incorrect openings.

## Occupation and repose

The window must still support daylight, outlook, privacy, ventilation, glare control, thermal comfort and external-noise moderation. A technically removable frame that produces a weak-looking or noisy opening has failed architecturally.

## Failure modes

- subframe creates an unacceptable thermal bridge;
- sill drainage blocks or falls incorrectly;
- sealant becomes the sole weather defence;
- reveal cannot be removed cleanly;
- future frame cannot fit the retained opening;
- primary airtight/water layer is destroyed during routine replacement.

## Evidence

Conservation guidance supports reversibility and minimising damage to enduring fabric. Design-for-disassembly guidance supports reversible connections between layers with different service lives. The specific HSA interface remains subject to building-physics and prototype testing.

## Does not prove

The evidence does not establish that every window needs a secondary frame or custom demountable detail. The invariant is the permanent-opening/replaceable-window relationship.

## Reference House application

The Reference House will prototype one jamb/head/sill condition at full scale before repeating a non-standard interface across the house.

## Formalisation boundary

Opening identity, frame occurrence, mounting zone, replacement sequence and boundary transitions can be represented formally. Architectural quality, weathering robustness and actual product applicability remain evidence/judgement questions.
