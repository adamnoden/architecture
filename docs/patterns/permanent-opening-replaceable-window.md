# HSA-P-007 — Permanent Opening / Replaceable Window

## Context

Use where a long-lived wall/opening is expected to outlast the window frame, glazing unit, seals, ironmongery and other shorter-lived components.

The pattern is especially relevant where replacement would otherwise require breaking sound masonry, plaster, render or primary envelope layers.

## Problem

The opening and surrounding wall can survive several generations of windows, yet ordinary installation often fuses the frame into plaster, foam, sealant and reveal finishes. When the window eventually fails or performance expectations change, replacement damages material that did not need replacing.

At the same time, a high-performance window cannot simply be made “loose”: fixing, drainage, airtightness, thermal continuity and movement still have to work.

## Pattern

> **Treat the opening as enduring architecture and the window as a replaceable assembly within a deliberate interface that manages fixing, weathering, drainage, air/thermal continuity, movement and future removal.**

The invariant is lifespan separation at the opening, not a proprietary removable subframe.

## Forces

- high-performance installation requires robust air, water and thermal continuity;
- the building-side opening may be far longer-lived than frames, seals and glazing;
- subframes and added interface layers can introduce thermal bridges, cost and complexity;
- concealed fixings must remain sufficiently reachable for full-frame replacement;
- external seals and weathering details still need renewal;
- repeated replacement can damage masonry/reveal edges;
- the finished opening must still feel solid, quiet and architecturally complete.

## Relationships

[Movement / Slip Junction](movement-slip-junction.md) often informs the room-side frame/reveal relationship where the frame and building move independently.

[Designed Structural Penetration](designed-structural-penetration.md) may apply to related service or fixing interfaces, but the window opening itself has its own structural and boundary design.

A future `Permanent Opening / Replaceable Door` pattern shares the lifespan principle but remains a separate identity because doors have materially different wear, security and repeated-use forces.

## Variants / implementation families

- mechanically fixed frame with removable internal trims;
- replaceable subframe or mounting frame;
- masonry opening with durable fixing zones and sacrificial/removable reveals;
- clip/screw-fixed liners concealing the mounting interface;
- window assemblies designed for internal removal where external access is difficult.

The pattern does not require one universal interface detail.

## Design requirements

- distinguish frame weathering from building-side drainage;
- define the mounting/fixing zone mechanically;
- avoid brittle finish bridging moving frame and masonry;
- draw full-frame replacement, not only glazing replacement;
- keep primary air/water layers intact during trim removal where practical;
- protect masonry/reveal edges during repeated work;
- show access to fixings and the frame withdrawal sequence;
- coordinate sill/head/jamb as one boundary system rather than independent cosmetic details.

## Proportionality

The interface earns additional components only where they materially reduce damage, improve boundary robustness or make realistic full-frame replacement possible. A complex proprietary subframe is not justified merely because it is theoretically reversible.

## Boundary debt

Resolve as applicable:

- weather/bulk-water management;
- drainage and sill falls;
- airtightness;
- thermal continuity/thermal bridging;
- vapour/moisture behaviour;
- acoustic performance;
- fire where the opening participates in a relevant boundary;
- security;
- movement/tolerance.

## Permanent-fabric impact

Permanent masonry or structure may receive deliberate anchors, bearing/fixing zones and durable building-side membranes. Those interventions should be sparse, documented and capable of surviving replacement of the shorter-lived frame without repeated enlargement or reconstruction.

## Workmanship / tolerance

The opening and manufactured frame arrive with different achievable accuracies. Define:

- the controlling datum;
- credible opening variation;
- fitting/packing/adjustment range;
- minimum seal/membrane geometry;
- remediation threshold when masonry is outside tolerance.

Do not ask foam, sealant or plaster to hide an opening that cannot physically accept the frame within its designed range.

## Occupation / repose impact

The window should support daylight and outlook while allowing glare, solar gain, privacy, ventilation and external noise to be moderated intelligibly. Technical interface depth, trims and joints are acceptable only if the completed opening remains solid, quiet and composed.

## Architectural resolution

The permanent opening should read as real architecture. Removable liners, architraves, sills or trims can conceal the serviceable interface where they are genuine architectural elements rather than cosmetic filler over unresolved construction.

## Assembly / replacement sequence

Record:

1. how the opening is prepared and datum established;
2. how the frame is fixed and supported;
3. how air/water/thermal layers connect;
4. which architectural trims complete the opening;
5. which trims are removed first at replacement;
6. how fixings and seals are reached;
7. how the complete frame is withdrawn;
8. what building-side layers remain undisturbed;
9. how the new frame is installed and boundaries recommissioned.

## Failure modes

- subframe creates a significant thermal bridge;
- sill drainage blocks or falls inward;
- sealant becomes the sole weather defence;
- reveal/architrave cannot be removed cleanly;
- hidden fixings are inaccessible;
- future frame cannot fit the enduring opening geometry;
- primary air/water layer is destroyed during ordinary frame replacement;
- repeated replacement spalls or enlarges masonry fixing zones.

## Evidence

Conservation guidance supports reversibility and minimising unnecessary damage to enduring fabric. Design-for-disassembly guidance supports reversible connections between layers with different service lives. Conventional window detailing also establishes the need to coordinate structural fixing, drainage, thermal continuity and airtightness as one interface.

## Does not prove

Every window does not need a proprietary removable subframe, and reversibility does not override weather, thermal, acoustic, structural or security performance. The required outcome is replacement of the shorter-lived assembly without needless reconstruction of the opening.

## Reference House application

The Reference House should develop one 1:1 jamb/head/sill prototype and test water, air, thermal bridging and replacement sequence before multiplying a non-standard interface across the building.

## Formalisation boundary

Potentially formal consequences include:

- stable opening/window identities;
- fixing/interface relationships;
- replacement dependencies;
- clearance/withdrawal geometry;
- boundary transitions and evidence applicability;
- prohibition on replacement sequences that destroy selected longer-lived elements.

Whether the opening remains architecturally convincing, tactilely solid and proportionate to its whole-life benefit remains design judgement.
