# Candidate — Selective Floor Access

**Status:** held; no HSA pattern ID  
**Evidence:** Proposed / supported by adjacent precedent  
**Primary unresolved gate:** prove domestic tactile/acoustic quality and a repeatable geometry that is more useful than ordinary local access

## Candidate proposition

Provide deliberately limited accessible floor geography only where real service/change demand justifies it, rather than making an entire room or storey routinely removable.

Possible forms include an access band, service edge, corridor strip or other bounded zone integrated with the room/floor architecture.

## Why it remains held

Selective access is more proportionate than assuming a full-room removable platform, but the proposition remains too unresolved for pattern admission. “Access where needed” is not enough: the project needs a repeatable spatial relationship that survives real acoustic, tactile and architectural constraints.

## Admission gate

Promote only after physical and worked-design comparison shows that a selective zone can:

1. provide meaningful access to the services/components actually expected to change;
2. remain visually and tactilely part of a convincing domestic floor;
3. avoid rocking, rattle, hollow footfall and acoustic bypass;
4. resolve thresholds, edges and furniture without trapping the access path;
5. outperform conventional local access panels or fully inaccessible layered construction on whole-life value;
6. demonstrate at least two worked room/route geometries with the same underlying invariant.

## Relationship to floor strategy

This candidate sits beneath the strategy [Separate Structural Floor from Changeable Layers Where Proportionate](../strategies/separate-structural-floor-changeable-layers.md).

The strategy is canonical. This candidate is not.

A full-room **Finish-Agnostic Floor Platform** remains an implementation challenger and comparator, not the default expression of this candidate.

## Risks

- the access geometry drives room composition rather than serving it;
- visible seams make the floor read as temporary/technical;
- acoustic continuity is weakened;
- the accessible strip is blocked by fixed furniture;
- the zone is provided speculatively and never carries services worth accessing;
- local access adds interfaces without reducing destructive work elsewhere.

## Evidence

Adjacent access-floor precedent and the project's floor-platform studies support selective access as a plausible direction, but not yet as a proven domestic pattern. The unresolved test is whether a bounded access zone can remain physically convincing while providing materially better serviceability than ordinary local panels or inaccessible layered construction.

Relevant work includes [Reversible Assembly Candidates](../reversible-assembly-candidates.md), [Finish-Agnostic Floor Platform Options](../../research/finish-agnostic-floor-platform-options.md) and the [Targeted Pattern-Language Evidence Review](../../research/pattern-language-phase6-targeted-evidence.md).

## Does not prove

This candidate does not establish that accessible flooring belongs in ordinary domestic rooms. A conventional durable floor remains the baseline unless actual service/change geography justifies the additional interfaces.
