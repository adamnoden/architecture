# Candidate — Individually Isolatable Manifold Distribution

**Status:** held; no HSA pattern ID  
**Evidence:** Supported  
**Primary unresolved gate:** demonstrate that the invariant belongs in the architectural language rather than only the services implementation layer

## Candidate proposition

Where the system and scale justify it, distribute a service from an accessible manifold so individual branches can be identified and isolated without disabling unrelated parts of the house.

The invariant under review is **network topology + granular isolation**, not a proprietary manifold product.

## Why it remains held

Manifold distribution is technically mature and has credible domestic precedent. What remains unresolved is whether it creates sufficiently architectural, cross-project design knowledge to warrant a canonical HSA pattern rather than being a strong services implementation family.

Different systems also behave differently. Heating, potable water and other networks should not be collapsed into one doctrine-level preference merely because manifolds exist for each.

## Admission gate

Promote only after worked domestic cases show that manifold topology repeatedly and materially improves HSA-level concerns such as:

1. maintenance geography and legibility;
2. granular isolation during failure/repair;
3. reduction of concealed joints or destructive access;
4. coherent relationship to plant hubs/service walls;
5. lifecycle flexibility without disproportionate pipe length, heat loss, space or material cost.

The comparison must include conventional branch/tee distribution.

## Forces to resolve

- increased pipe/cable length versus simpler branches;
- manifold space and access;
- heat loss / dead-leg implications where relevant;
- commissioning and labelling;
- isolation reliability;
- branch replacement/access strategy;
- cost and embodied material;
- whether a centralized topology actually suits room geography.

## Evidence

Domestic patternbooks and CIBSE precedent support manifold distribution as a mature services option. The unresolved question is not whether manifolds work, but whether their topology repeatedly creates architectural value strong enough to justify a canonical HSA pattern.

The current evidence review is recorded in [Targeted Pattern-Language Evidence Review](../../research/pattern-language-phase6-targeted-evidence.md).

## Does not prove

This candidate does not establish that manifolds are universally superior, that every branch should be home-run, or that all domestic service networks should share one distribution philosophy.
