# Candidate — Replaceable Architectural Lining

**Status:** held; no HSA pattern ID  
**Evidence:** Supported direction  
**Primary unresolved gate:** physical repose / solidity

## Candidate proposition

Where service access, movement or lifecycle conditions justify it, separate the room-facing architectural surface from slower primary boundaries so the lining can be removed/replaced without destructive work to permanent fabric.

The proposition is intentionally broader than “wall panels”. Material and fixing system are implementation choices.

## Why it remains held

The conceptual admission test is strong, but this is an occupied-surface proposition. If the resulting wall feels hollow, rattles, advertises module joints or produces an office-fit-out character, it fails a central HSA requirement even if it is technically serviceable.

High-quality direct plaster therefore remains the benchmark, not a legacy system to be displaced automatically.

## Admission gate

Promote only if the full wall-bay programme demonstrates a repeatable implementation that:

1. preserves convincing visual and tactile solidity;
2. handles corners, reveals, sockets and ordinary decoration without destructive filler;
3. can be removed and reinstalled repeatedly;
4. keeps primary fire/acoustic/air/moisture duties in appropriate durable layers;
5. tolerates credible background/site variation;
6. compares favourably with first-rate plaster and ordinary independent drylining on whole-life value.

## Implementation directions under test

Potential implementations include:

- robust mineral/manufactured panels;
- timber or other architectural panel systems;
- factory-finished mineral/plaster surfaces;
- wet-room-specific removable panels;
- attachment through [`HSA-P-022 — Controlled Attachment Plane`](../controlled-attachment-plane.md) where proportionate.

These are not themselves the candidate identity.

## Failure modes

- hollow or temporary feel;
- visible module pressure where a continuous field was intended;
- excessive joint count;
- rattle or impact weakness;
- critical boundary duties accidentally assigned to removable decoration;
- corner/reveal details that require wet making-good after every intervention;
- proprietary clips or finishes that undermine long-life repairability.

## Evidence

The direction is supported by the project's reversible-assembly and replaceable-wall research, but the central question remains physical: whether a removable lining can match the repose, solidity and ordinary decorative freedom of a first-rate conventional wall.

Relevant work includes [Reversible Assembly Candidates](../reversible-assembly-candidates.md), [Replaceable Wall System Options](../../research/replaceable-wall-system-options.md) and the W2 wall-bay prototype programme.

## Does not prove

This candidate does not imply removable lining is desirable on low-service walls or in every room. Conventional plaster may remain the superior answer in many conditions.
