# HSA-P-019 — Permanent Opening / Replaceable Door

## Context

Door leaves, frames, seals, closers, hinges, locks and thresholds are repeatedly used and commonly have shorter service lives than the surrounding wall and opening.

## Problem

A door assembly can become fused to plaster, flooring, trims and adjacent finishes so that adjustment, frame replacement or hardware renewal damages sound long-lived fabric. Unlike windows, doors also carry repeated impact, privacy, security, acoustic and accessibility duties.

## Pattern

**Treat the wall opening as enduring architecture and the doorset as a replaceable, adjustable assembly within a deliberate interface. Let trims and thresholds expose or protect that interface without becoming the only thing holding it together.**

## Forces

- doors need repeated adjustment under wear and building movement;
- fire, acoustic, privacy and security duties vary by location;
- frames need robust fixing while remaining replaceable in principle;
- thresholds wear and interact with floor build-ups/accessibility;
- architraves can conceal useful adjustment zones but can also hide poor work.

## Design requirements

- distinguish permanent opening geometry from replaceable doorset geometry;
- provide a defined fixing/adjustment zone;
- keep critical fixings and hardware serviceable;
- avoid relying on brittle plaster continuity across expected movement;
- coordinate full frame replacement, not only leaf/hardware replacement;
- resolve architraves and thresholds as real interface components where used.

## Proportionality

The pattern is strongest at doors likely to outlive multiple leaves/ironmongery sets or where frame replacement would otherwise destroy valuable wall/floor fabric. It should not generate unnecessary demountable complexity around trivial lightweight partitions.

## Boundary debt

Where applicable, maintain fire/smoke, acoustic, security, air/weather and accessibility performance through the interface and replacement sequence.

## Permanent-fabric impact

Frame anchors and opening-edge preparation should be deliberate and documented. The aim is not zero fixing; it is to avoid routine reconstruction of the opening when the shorter-lived doorset changes.

## Workmanship and tolerance

The interface must absorb realistic wall-opening variation while preserving plumb, squareness and required gaps. Architectural trim may cover the adjustment zone but should not substitute for correcting an opening outside tolerance.

## Occupation / repose impact

A good door should feel solid, quiet and predictable in daily use. Replaceability does not excuse rattle, excessive gaps, weak-looking frames or hardware clutter.

## Failure modes

- architrave conceals inaccessible or inadequate fixings;
- frame replacement destroys plaster/flooring unnecessarily;
- adjustment range is consumed during initial installation;
- seals and ironmongery cannot be renewed independently;
- threshold and frame interfaces fight the adjacent floor build-up;
- fire/acoustic/security duties are lost during replacement.

## Evidence

Replaceable doorsets, mechanical frame fixing, adjustable hardware and removable trims are mature practice. Phase 6 retained a distinct door pattern because repeated use, wear, privacy/security and threshold forces differ materially from the window condition.

## Does not prove

The pattern does not require proprietary demountable frames or identical detailing for internal, external and fire-resisting doorsets. Each condition retains its own technical duties.

## Reference House application

The Reference House uses the sequence **permanent opening → replaceable frame/doorset → removable architectural trim**, with architraves performing real tolerance/protection work rather than merely simulating traditional construction.

## Formalisation boundary

Opening/doorset identity, replacement dependencies, clearances and applicable boundary roles can be represented formally. Hardware durability, fire/security performance and architectural solidity remain evidence and judgement questions.
