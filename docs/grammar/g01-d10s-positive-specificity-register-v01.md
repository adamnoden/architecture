# G-01 D10-S — Positive Specificity Register v0.1

**Status:** frozen pre-validation candidate set  
**Validation set:** frozen separately in [D10-S Programme](g01-d10s-positive-specificity-programme.md) and **not yet inspected for this pass when these candidates were frozen**  
**Purpose:** identify the smallest positive relationships that make G-01 more than generic architectural order.  
**Authority:** candidate register only; no G-01 v0.1 rule is promoted here.

## 1. Derivation result in one sentence

The strongest positive signal is not a sacred proportion or a fixed Georgian plan. It is a **coordinated classical domestic field**:

> **a legible wall body is ordered into bays and storeys; openings belong to related families; base/top/edge conditions bound the composition; entrance or principal emphasis is concentrated rather than smeared across the whole; roof and appendages remain subordinate to the primary architectural reading.**

Each part remains scoped and capable of exception. Membership is expected to come from the **bundle**, not from any single motif.

## 2. Evidence used for extraction

D10-S uses only the predeclared derivation/theory pool plus facts already frozen before D10-S.

### Built derivation anchors

**Warbrook House**  
Historic England describes the surviving John James house as a prime example of a small Palladian villa or “box”, placed at the intersection of major designed axes. The original entrance forecourt, raised entrance reached by steps and through-house east–west landscape axis reinforce the reading of one compact primary architectural body with a ranked arrival/frontage relation.

Source: Historic England, Warbrook House RPG entry 1000249 and Research Report 46/2024.

**Combe Bank**  
Historic England describes the original Roger Morris house as a small five-bay Palladian villa: two-and-a-half-storey side bays under low pyramidal roofs, a two-storey centre with pyramidal roof/cupola, sash openings in moulded architraves, stone quoins/dressings, ground-floor rustication and selected pedimented openings.

Sources: Historic England entries 1000365 and 1243769.

**Clandon House**  
Historic England records a square red-brick house with four different elevations, roof obscured by parapets and a nine-bay entrance front whose centre three bays are stone-faced and pedimented. This is useful precisely because strong order does not require four identical elevations.

Sources: Historic England entries 1001171 and 1294591.

**Ditchley House**  
Historic England records a principal stone block with lower flanking service pavilions linked by quadrant corridors. The main front has two principal storeys plus attic/semi-basement, a basement plinth, rusticated quoins, heavy modillion cornice, a projecting central three-bay group and projecting outer bay pairs.

Sources: Historic England entries 1000463 and 1251422.

**70 Dean Street**  
Historic England's early-Georgian townhouse study identifies an explicit façade-order example: on the three-bay front the first- and second-floor window field forms a square between pilasters and below the cornice. The source is useful as morphology-specific evidence of the wall/opening field, not as authority for a universal square rule.

Source: Historic England, Early Georgian Townhouse — 69 and 70 Dean Street.

### Existing trial/counterpressure retained from pre-D10-S work

- 76 Dean Street rejects a universal centred entrance, odd-bay or whole-building symmetry rule.
- Danson rejects one plan/elevation authority direction and one room-ratio family.
- Marble Hill and Danson together show that sectional order can operate through different mechanisms.

No D10-S candidate may silently undo those results.

### Theory / transferability evidence

The primary literature establishes that architectural knowledge was consciously published as transferable plans, elevations, element systems and design examples rather than existing only as one-off buildings:

- Palladio, *Four Books on Architecture*, English edition 1715;
- Gibbs, *A Book of Architecture* (1728), 150 plates of the architect's own buildings/designs, explicitly influential as a pattern source;
- Ware, *A Complete Body of Architecture* (1756), plans and elevations with a broad architectural treatise;
- Morris, *Select Architecture* (1755), explicitly presenting regular plans/elevations from plain town house and farmhouse through grander types.

These sources establish historical codification/transfer intent. They do **not** count as independent built confirmations of any candidate below.

# 3. G01-SPEC-01 — Bounded classical wall field

**Class:** `CORE RELATION`  
**Scope:** principal/public elevation or explicitly named subordinate elevation  
**Confidence before validation:** medium/high

## Statement

A principal G-01 elevation should read as a **bounded architectural wall field**, not as an accidental aggregation of openings.

The field coordinates at least:

- repeated or deliberately related vertical bays;
- storey datums / vertical ordering;
- a legible upper termination such as cornice, parapet or disciplined eaves condition;
- defined edge or centre conditions where the composition requires them;
- local departures that remain subordinate to the larger field.

The rule does **not** require:

- bilateral symmetry;
- equal bay widths everywhere;
- odd bay count;
- four identical elevations;
- an applied classical order across the entire façade.

## Why this is more specific than generic order

The candidate concerns a **classically bounded domestic wall/opening composition**: bay field + storey order + architectural termination working as one façade object.

It is not satisfied merely because a modern façade has a grid.

## Supporting derivation evidence

- Clandon: nine-bay entrance field, centre-three emphasis, roof hidden by parapet; four elevations can differ.
- Ditchley: plinth, quoins, bay projections and heavy cornice form one bounded front.
- Combe Bank: five-bay Palladian body with differentiated centre/flanks and controlled roof conditions.
- 70 Dean Street: upper window field explicitly bounded by pilasters/cornice in the Historic England analysis.

## Counterpressure

- 76 Dean Street shows that the entrance can sit off the abstract centre while the façade remains ordered.
- 2 New Street from D9 showed that unequal bay intervals and corner morphology can still be coherent Georgian architecture; exact repetition is therefore not the point.

## Contemporary transfer

Preserve the compositional relation. Materials, insulation build-up, window technology and technical wall construction remain separate decisions.

## Does not prove

- beauty;
- structural adequacy;
- any exact ratio;
- that every elevation deserves equal formality.

# 4. G01-SPEC-02 — Related opening family within ranked storeys/bays

**Class:** `CORE RELATION` + optional `ELEMENT FAMILY` implementation  
**Scope:** principal/public elevation  
**Confidence before validation:** high for relation; medium for sash-derived element preference

## Statement

Openings on a principal G-01 elevation should belong to **recognisably related families** and participate in the bay/storey order.

The relation may use:

- recurring vertical axes;
- repeated width/height families;
- storey-specific height or treatment;
- a principal opening/entrance exception inside the larger family;
- blind/simulated states where deliberately selected.

Opening hierarchy may vary by storey or architectural role, but variation should read as **ranked differentiation**, not arbitrary catalogue mixing.

## Element-family preference

The English Georgian lineage strongly supports a **vertically proportioned sash-derived opening language** for the primary domestic field.

For contemporary work, `sash-derived` describes architectural type/rhythm rather than requiring eighteenth-century timber construction.

Do not require:

- timber frames;
- six-over-six glazing;
- one pane size;
- one width:height ratio.

The Georgian Group's window guidance explicitly notes that there was no standard Georgian pane/window size, although six-over-six arrangements were widespread and later eighteenth-century examples often became more vertically emphatic.

## Supporting derivation evidence

- Combe Bank: sash family across five-bay Palladian elevations with selected ground-floor/pediment differentiation.
- Clandon and Ditchley: repeated window fields coordinated with ranked central/projection conditions.
- 70 Dean Street: upper-storey window field treated as one proportional composition.
- prior 76 Dean evidence: storey/opening hierarchy can vary inside an ordered field.

## Counterpressure

- Exact sash subdivision changes by date and scale.
- Blind openings show that façade state need not equal spatial aperture.
- A contemporary grammar may use modern window assemblies while retaining the architectural family.

## Contemporary transfer

Preserve family/rank/verticality; allow modern material and performance systems.

## Does not prove

- exact pane count;
- exact proportion;
- that every room must receive an opening;
- daylight, thermal or ventilation adequacy.

# 5. G01-SPEC-03 — Ranked entrance within frontage order

**Class:** `CORE RELATION`  
**Scope:** principal arrival/frontage condition  
**Confidence before validation:** high

## Statement

The principal entrance should be **architecturally ranked above an ordinary opening** and intelligible within the order of its frontage/approach.

Ranking may be produced through one or more of:

- doorcase;
- steps / raised threshold;
- porch or portico;
- pediment / entablature;
- differentiated bay;
- axis/forecourt/approach relation;
- controlled opening hierarchy.

The entrance does **not** need to coincide with:

- geometric façade centre;
- whole-building centre;
- dominant garden-front centre;
- an odd-numbered bay.

## Supporting derivation evidence

- Warbrook: raised front door approached from a forecourt and aligned to the major designed axis.
- Clandon: centre-three entrance-front emphasis, though later porch work must be separated from original Leoni order.
- Ditchley: strong centre-three projection within the main front.
- prior 76 Dean counterexample: an off-centre entrance remains valid inside an ordered four-bay urban frontage.

## Why this is membership-bearing

The candidate converts `door exists` into a classical domestic **threshold hierarchy** while retaining enough scope to admit the urban counterexample.

## Contemporary transfer

A contemporary accessible entrance may transform steps/threshold geometry; it should preserve architectural rank through other means rather than using inaccessible historic level change as the identity carrier.

## Does not prove

- centred entrance;
- portico requirement;
- column order;
- accessibility adequacy.

# 6. G01-SPEC-04 — Deliberate base and top termination; roof subordinate to façade order

**Class:** `CORE RELATION` / `LANGUAGE PREFERENCE` depending morphology  
**Scope:** principal body/elevation  
**Confidence before validation:** medium/high

## Statement

The principal architectural body should have a **deliberate lower and upper termination**. The roof belongs to that composition rather than reading as an unrelated object placed above it.

Typical mechanisms include:

- basement/plinth/rusticated lower condition;
- cornice;
- parapet concealing or reducing roof visibility;
- disciplined eaves;
- low hipped/pyramidal roof aligned to the body;
- attic/mansard condition subordinated to the principal storeys.

No single roof family is invariant.

## Supporting derivation evidence

- Clandon: roof obscured by parapets over a square red-brick body.
- Ditchley: semi-basement/plinth to heavy cornice; roof/stacks above a strongly terminated main range.
- Combe Bank: low pyramidal roofs and central cupola tied to a differentiated five-bay body.
- urban Georgian evidence retained from the existing corpus: parapet/cornice can turn fire-regulation roof concealment into a classical termination rather than a separate roof expression.

## Counterpressure

Visible pitched, hipped, pyramidal and mansard roofs all occur. The rule therefore concerns **subordination/termination**, not concealment.

## Contemporary transfer

Roof drainage, insulation, PV, ventilation and maintainability remain technical/project obligations. G-01 controls the architectural reading, not the roof technology.

## Does not prove

- parapet requirement;
- flat roof;
- roof pitch;
- hidden gutters;
- environmental adequacy.

# 7. G01-SPEC-05 — Concentrated classical articulation

**Class:** `LANGUAGE PREFERENCE`  
**Scope:** principal elevations and thresholds  
**Confidence before validation:** medium/high

## Statement

Classical articulation in G-01 should be **hierarchically concentrated** at architectural loci that carry rank—entrance, centre or end bay, base, principal storey, cornice/termination—rather than applied at equal intensity everywhere.

Possible devices include:

- quoins/pilasters;
- pediment;
- rustication;
- architrave/doorcase;
- cornice/entablature;
- differentiated central or flanking bay.

The device is subordinate to the ordering relation. G-01 is not a catalogue requiring all of them.

## Supporting derivation evidence

- Clandon: centre three stone-faced and pedimented inside a larger red-brick nine-bay front.
- Ditchley: plinth, quoins, heavy cornice and projecting bay groups organise rather than decorate every surface equally.
- Combe Bank: stone quoins/dressings, rusticated ground-floor openings and selected pediments articulate hierarchy inside a simpler wall field.
- 70 Dean Street: pilasters/cornice bound the upper window composition.

## Counterpressure

Restrained/astylar Georgian work can carry order with little applied ornament. Therefore the candidate is a preference about **where articulation goes when selected**, not a requirement for decorative abundance.

## Contemporary transfer

The grammar can be materially restrained and still satisfy the relation. Contemporary construction should not fake structural truth merely to reproduce historical ornament.

## Does not prove

- a specific classical order;
- moulding profile/dimension;
- applied ornament requirement;
- tectonic or structural adequacy.

# 8. Proposed but NOT admitted

## Primary-body / subordinate-wing invariant

**Not admitted to the core register.**

Ditchley strongly demonstrates a principal block with lower pavilions and quadrant links, while Warbrook/Clandon support legible primary bodies. The cross-case evidence is not strong enough to require subordinate wings generally, and a courtyard morphology would make the statement dangerously project-shaped.

Retain only as a possible `COMPACT_VILLA` morphology-profile question.

## Defined-room axial fireplace / door / window rule

**Not admitted.**

The current D10-S evidence is insufficient to distinguish a real Georgian-derived invariant from generic classical room composition or selective famous-room examples.

## Universal square / harmonic façade ratio

**Rejected.**

70 Dean Street provides one explicit square-based townhouse composition. It is morphology-specific evidence, not authority for a cross-G-01 exact ratio.

# 9. Pre-validation bundle test

The candidate set is intentionally read as a bundle:

```text
SPEC-01  bounded classical wall field
   +
SPEC-02  related ranked opening family
   +
SPEC-03  architecturally ranked entrance
   +
SPEC-04  deliberate base/top + subordinate roof reading
   +
SPEC-05  concentrated classical articulation (preference)
```

A materially different architectural grammar could share one or two of these. It should be substantially less likely to satisfy the coordinated bundle accidentally.

That is the working answer to D10's specificity failure.
