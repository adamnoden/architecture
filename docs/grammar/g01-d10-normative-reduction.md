# G-01 D10 — Descriptive → Normative Reduction

**Status:** D10 PASS; D11 RELEASE BLOCKED pending targeted specificity recovery  
**Programme:** [G-01 Completion Programme — D8 to D13](g01-completion-programme.md)  
**Upstream:** [D8 Controlled Falsification](g01-d8-falsification-result.md), [D9 Held-Out Validation](g01-d9-held-out-validation.md)  
**Issue:** #78  
**Date:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Executive result

D10 resolves the surviving D8/D9 material into contemporary normative decisions.

The result is deliberately uncomfortable:

> **The surviving candidates are defensible, but they are not yet sufficient to define membership in a Georgian-derived architectural language.**

D8 and D9 successfully removed overfit and historically contingent mechanisms. What remains is mostly:

- explicit architectural hierarchy;
- scoped order;
- section/vertical order;
- optional opening hierarchy;
- route/sequence and coupling semantics;
- a negative prohibition against one universal ratio.

Those are valuable, but many are either style-neutral grammar-framework capabilities or high-order architectural relationships that could occur in Arts-and-Crafts, Beaux-Arts, classical-modern or other coherent languages.

Freezing them as `G-01 v0.1` would therefore violate the G-01 Charter's governing test: G-01 must remain recognisably and unapologetically Georgian-derived even when House Kernel is removed from the comparison.

D10 consequently **passes as a normative reduction gate but blocks D11 release** until a small, targeted positive-specificity loop earns enough Georgian-derived membership content.

This is not a return to broad corpus accumulation.

---

# 2. Normative decision contract

For every surviving candidate D10 records:

```text
historical/descriptive evidence
→ architectural function
→ historical/social dependency
→ contemporary usefulness
→ present-day conflict
→ PRESERVE / TRANSFORM / REJECT
→ destination
```

`REJECT` can mean **reject from G-01 release**, not reject the modelling concept as false.

# 3. C01 — explicit architectural hierarchy

## Descriptive evidence

Hierarchy recurs across very different cases:

- Marble Hill — central/double-height Great Room and differentiated stairs;
- Danson — principal rooms, primary stair and route hierarchy;
- 76 Dean Street — vertical/storey and stair hierarchy;
- Kings Nympton — stairhall, piano nobile, principal/service conditions;
- 29 Storey's Way — central stair hall and differentiated room roles;
- Tiverton House — principal garden rooms and dramatic stair while bedrooms are deliberately equal-sized.

## Architectural function

Hierarchy makes architectural roles legible and allows order to be carried by different variables:

- position;
- section;
- route;
- opening treatment;
- entrance/stair relation;
- room sequence.

## Historical/social dependency

Specific historical rank can encode class, servants and prescribed entertaining customs.

Those implementations are not contemporary authority.

## Contemporary decision

**PRESERVE — but relationally.**

A G-01 project should be capable of declaring principal and subordinate architectural roles where the design uses hierarchy.

Do not infer rank mechanically from:

- room area;
- centrality;
- floor number;
- opening size;
- servant-era use.

## Destination

Candidate for G-01 core **only if paired with Georgian-specific mechanisms elsewhere**.

By itself C01 is too style-neutral to establish membership.

# 4. C02 — connectivity / route / sequence distinction

## Descriptive evidence

Danson's historical shortcut proves that connectivity and architectural sequence can diverge.

29 Storey's Way supplies a materially different distributor-hall condition, while Tiverton uses interconnected garden-side rooms and a strong stair/gallery sequence.

## Architectural function

The model must distinguish:

- direct connection;
- route role;
- privileged/required sequence;
- visual/axial relation.

## Historical/social dependency

Specific ceremonial or servant-era sequences may be socially contingent.

## Contemporary decision

**TRANSFORM.**

Preserve the ability to declare and protect a project/profile-specific architectural sequence.

Reject any universal G-01 sequence such as:

- `Hall → principal room must pass through another principal room`;
- one fixed distributor/circuit topology.

## Destination

- modelling distinction → grammar framework / representation;
- actual sequence preference → morphology/profile-specific G-01 material if independently earned.

Not a standalone G-01 v0.1 membership rule.

# 5. C03 — differentiated route roles

## Descriptive evidence

Primary/service route distinctions recur historically at Marble Hill, Danson, 76 Dean Street and Kings Nympton.

D9 also produced a direct counterpressure: 29 Storey's Way distributes drawing room, study, dining room and kitchen from one central staircase hall.

## Architectural function

Movement systems can carry different architectural rank even where technically equivalent.

## Historical/social dependency

Strong.

Separate servant/service routes are entangled with historical domestic labour and class organisation.

## Contemporary usefulness

Contemporary projects may still benefit from differentiated:

- principal arrival;
- family/secondary movement;
- technical/service access;
- maintenance movement;

but those needs must come from current programme and architecture.

## Contemporary decision

**TRANSFORM.**

The grammar may distinguish route roles where they exist.

It must **not require creation of separate principal/service networks** merely to resemble historical Georgian planning.

## Destination

Profile/project preference, not G-01 invariant.

# 6. C04 — scoped symmetry / alignment / order

## Descriptive evidence

This survives every major challenge:

- strong but local symmetry domains at Marble Hill;
- controlled plan/elevation conflict at Danson;
- ordered four-bay façade with off-centre entrance at 76 Dean Street;
- multiple differently ordered fronts at Kings Nympton;
- L-shaped corner composition with unequal bay intervals at 2 New Street;
- different entrance/frontage roles at 29 Storey's Way.

## Architectural function

Order is real, but it belongs to named architectural domains:

- façade;
- room field;
- bay group;
- storey;
- axis;
- range;
- row/block;
- element family.

## Historical/social dependency

Low.

## Contemporary decision

**PRESERVE strongly.**

G-01 should require explicit ordering relationships rather than a global symmetry boolean.

It should be possible for several ordering systems to coexist and negotiate.

## Destination

Strong candidate for G-01 core.

### Specificity warning

Scoped order is not uniquely Georgian. It requires positive Georgian-derived content—what kinds of bay/opening/massing/element order are characteristic—before it can act as a membership discriminator.

# 7. C05 — opening hierarchy can express architectural rank

## Descriptive evidence

Opening/storey differentiation appears at:

- 76 Dean Street;
- Kings Nympton;
- 2 New Street;
- 29 Storey's Way in different elevation/role families;
- Tiverton House's large garden-side sashes associated with principal interconnected rooms.

## Architectural function

Fenestration can communicate:

- storey rank;
- room role;
- façade role;
- entrance/principal-field hierarchy.

## Historical/social dependency

Moderate where tied to piano-nobile occupation conventions; low for the broader architectural function.

## Contemporary decision

**PRESERVE AS PREFERENCE.**

Where a G-01 design declares spatial/storey hierarchy, opening treatment should be available to reinforce it.

Do not require:

- a piano nobile;
- one window-size delta;
- one sash count;
- principal room = largest opening.

## Destination

Potential G-01 preference, but it needs a more specific opening/bay family to become recognisably Georgian-derived.

# 8. C06 — attribute-specific plan / section / elevation coupling

## Descriptive evidence

Danson decisively shows that opening existence and centreline can have different authorities.

The hold-outs did not establish another equally strong per-attribute authority case, but they also did not contradict the modelling need.

## Architectural function

Allows plan, section and elevation to negotiate one architectural order without forcing one projection to dominate.

## Historical/social dependency

None material.

## Contemporary decision

**REJECT FROM G-01 RELEASE; PRESERVE IN THE GRAMMAR FRAMEWORK.**

C06 is now clearly a representation capability, not a Georgian design rule.

G-01 may use this capability to state specific relationships later, but `attribute-specific coupling` itself does not distinguish G-01 from another grammar.

## Destination

Style-neutral grammar framework / modelling corrections.

---

# 9. C07 — proportion is role/family-specific, not universal

## Descriptive evidence

The direct corpus contains multiple plan families and sectional relationships inside single coherent buildings.

Kings Nympton additionally demonstrates that a cube-room proposition could be omitted while a close Marble Hill lineage remained intact.

## Architectural function

Prevents false precision and ratio monism.

## Historical/social dependency

Low.

## Contemporary decision

**PRESERVE AS NEGATIVE AUTHORING GUARDRAIL.**

Do not encode one universal good Georgian room ratio.

Positive dimensional families remain `HOLD` until better comparable evidence establishes:

- role;
- absolute dimension;
- morphology;
- section;
- uncertainty;
- admissible range.

## Destination

G-01 derivation/release boundary, not a positive membership rule.

A house does not become Georgian because it lacks a universal ratio rule.

---

# 10. C08 — section / vertical hierarchy is first-class

## Descriptive evidence

Different mechanisms survive across the corpus and hold-outs:

- Marble Hill — cubic/double-height Great Room;
- Danson — common principal-floor datum across differing plan families;
- 76 Dean Street — vertical/piano-nobile hierarchy;
- Kings Nympton — piano nobile without executing the cube-room proposal;
- 29 Storey's Way — mansard upper floor and domed stair landing;
- Tiverton House — high-ceilinged rooms, dramatic stair and top-lit gallery.

## Architectural function

Section can carry hierarchy, unify rooms or alter upper-floor topology independently of plan area.

## Historical/social dependency

Moderate for specific piano-nobile customs; low for sectional ordering generally.

## Contemporary decision

**PRESERVE strongly, mechanism-neutral.**

A multi-storey G-01 design should treat section as part of architectural order rather than a late extrusion of plan.

Do not require:

- double-height room;
- piano nobile;
- fixed floor-to-floor ratios;
- one principal-storey location.

## Destination

Strong G-01 preference / possible high-order rule, but again not uniquely Georgian without positive language-specific content.

---

# 11. C09 — intentional absence as architectural data

## Descriptive evidence

Blind/simulated openings are directly evidenced at Danson and Kings Nympton.

## Architectural function

A façade/order system can depend on:

- simulated opening;
- retained solid field;
- prohibited opening;
- protected emptiness.

## Historical/social dependency

Low.

## Contemporary decision

**REJECT FROM G-01 CORE; PRESERVE AS OPTIONAL ELEMENT/SEMANTIC CAPABILITY.**

Blind windows are not necessary for Georgian-derived membership.

The grammar should be able to represent them where selected, but should not generate them merely because historical examples contain them.

## Destination

Element grammar / general representation, optional motif.

# 13. D11 release-readiness test

Before freezing G-01 v0.1, apply a stricter question than “did the candidates survive?”

> **Would the surviving rule set distinguish G-01 from a materially different coherent architectural language?**

Current answer:

**NO — not strongly enough.**

A house from several other architectural traditions could plausibly satisfy:

- explicit hierarchy;
- scoped order;
- intentional section;
- opening hierarchy;
- route/sequence semantics;

without being Georgian-derived.

This means the D8/D9 process has successfully found **architectural grammar substrate**, but has not yet earned enough **positive G-01 specificity**.

Freezing now would create a category error:

```text
GENERAL ARCHITECTURAL ORDER
        ↓ mislabeled as
GEORGIAN-DERIVED GRAMMAR
```

That would violate both the Charter and the project's anti-contamination rule.

# 14. D10 decision — trigger specificity recovery before D11

D11 is **BLOCKED**, not failed permanently.

Insert one bounded repair loop before release:

## D10-S — Positive Specificity Recovery

Purpose:

> derive the smallest positive relationships that make G-01 recognisably Georgian-derived **without** undoing the anti-overfit results of D8/D9.

This is not permission for broad corpus growth.

### Target domains

Investigate only the missing membership discriminators most likely to carry Georgian/classical identity:

1. **primary mass + roof/base order**  
   What relationships distinguish the compact Georgian-derived domestic body without requiring one footprint or roof type?

2. **bay / opening-field grammar**  
   What makes an elevation an ordered Georgian-derived field rather than merely “having windows in rows”?

3. **entrance / frontage hierarchy**  
   What relationships make an entrance legible within different centred, off-centred, urban and villa conditions?

4. **opening family / vertical order**  
   What sash/opening relationships are genuinely characteristic across morphology without becoming one product/style pack?

5. **classical architectural element relationships**  
   Which cornice/plinth/string/doorcase/order relationships carry membership at high level, and which are optional decoration?

6. **room enclosure / axial-element relationships**  
   Only if evidence shows that defined-room, fireplace/door/opening relationships carry distinctive architectural load beyond generic hierarchy.

### Derivation discipline

- use the existing theory corpus and cases already assigned `DERIVE` before adding buildings;
- do not tune new rules against the already-consumed D9 hold-outs and then call that validation;
- predeclare a **new validation subset** before extracting new positive rules;
- every new candidate still needs counterexamples and a does-not-prove boundary;
- exact ratios remain prohibited unless new evidence earns them;
- element/detail evidence must not reduce G-01 to a moulding catalogue.

### D10-S output

A short `positive-specificity register` containing only candidates that answer:

> **What would make a competent architect say “this is not merely ordered architecture; this belongs to this Georgian-derived language”?**

Then run a small second hold-out pass before D11.
