# G-01 Corpus and Source Quality

G-01 is derived from a deliberately mixed body of normative texts, measured or researched buildings, urban counterexamples and later adaptations. The purpose of the corpus is not to collect admired Georgian work. It is to make the evidential basis of the grammar inspectable and to prevent a few remembered precedents from hardening into rules by taste alone.

> **Do not derive a rule from a building merely because the building is famous. Derive only what the available evidence can actually support.**

The current normative grammar is [G-01 v0.1](g01-v0.1.md). [Scope, Evidence and Derivation Method](g01-research-brief.md) explains how the corpus is used; [Precedent Annotation Method](g01-annotation-schema.md) explains how individual buildings are read.

## Evidence-quality scale

The grades below describe fitness for grammar research, not architectural merit.

### P — primary normative source

Original treatise, pattern book or rules text. Useful for intended rules, explicit proportional systems, element families and what contemporary authors believed could be codified and transmitted. It is not direct evidence that built work consistently followed those rules.

### A1 — measured / primary geometric evidence

Reliable measured drawings, original drawings with scale/dimensions or equivalent high-quality survey material. Suitable for metric extraction, alignment, room ratios, bay geometry and sectional relationships, subject to phase control.

### A2 — authoritative analytical reconstruction

Detailed conservation or scholarly study with plans/reconstruction drawings and strong fabric/documentary research. Exact dimensions still require an adequate source drawing.

### B — authoritative descriptive evidence

High-quality listing, monograph or archival record describing plan, bays, storeys, hierarchy or construction without enough verified metric material for precise measurement. Useful for topology, qualitative comparison and counterexamples.

### C — contextual control

Architecturally useful comparison that should not drive rule extraction because of exceptional scale, major alteration, different typology, later reinterpretation or incomplete evidence.

### X — insufficient evidence

A potentially relevant case for which the available evidence cannot yet support analysis.

## Urban and ordinary domestic evidence

Urban cases prevent freestanding-villa relationships from becoming universal. Narrow plots, party walls, fire constraints and different social/service arrangements provide a materially different morphology against which deeper relationships can be tested.

| ID | Building / set | Date | Evidential use | Available evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| G01-U01 | **70 Dean Street, Soho** | early Georgian | three-bay townhouse proportional ordering at façade scale | authoritative annotated façade explanation | B |
| G01-U02 | **76 Dean Street, Soho** | 1732–33; Thomas Richmond | double-pile urban planning, storey hierarchy and a strong counterexample to villa assumptions | listing, Georgian-house study and historic survey-plan evidence | B |
| G01-U03 | **90 Dean Street, Soho** | 1756–67 | later-Georgian townhouse control | authoritative townhouse study | B |
| G01-U04 | **2 New Street, Leicester** | c.1750 | ordinary professional-class house; restrained classical detail and plan form | enhanced listing with plan/bay description | B |
| G01-U05 | **13 New Street, Leicester** | Georgian development; exact phase uncertain | companion ordinary urban case | useful documentary evidence but weaker drawing basis | B/X |
| G01-U06 | **20 Castle Street, Chester — The Georgian House** | mid/late C18 | regional townhouse control away from London | authoritative listing | B |
| G01-U07 | **Bedford Square, London** | later C18 | measured urban family; demonstrates order operating at terrace/block scale beyond the individual dwelling | public-domain measured plans, parcel dimensions and block-composition evidence | A1/A2 |

The relevant question is not whether urban houses reproduce villa form, but which relationships survive despite radically different site morphology.

## Later and contemporary adaptation controls

These cases do not tell us what Georgian architecture historically was. They test whether Georgian-derived ordering principles can survive later modes of life rather than merely reproduce period appearance.

| ID | Building | Date / author | Evidential use | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G01-L01 | **29 Storey’s Way, Cambridge** | 1922; M. H. Baillie Scott | high-quality neo-Georgian adaptation by an architect with an independent planning philosophy | B → potential A2 |
| G01-L02 | **Ridgehanger, Ealing** | 1913–15; Robert Atkinson | restrained neo-Georgian detached house at ordinary suburban scale | B |
| G01-L03 | **Tiverton House, Aldeburgh** | contemporary; Quinlan Terry Architects | modern English-Palladian family house; contemporary adaptation test | C/B |
| G01-L04 | **Davey’s Farm, Northamptonshire** | contemporary; Yiangou Architects | boundary case for how far “Georgian-derived” can stretch | C |

## What the corpus is allowed to establish

Used with phase-controlled drawings and source-qualified interpretation, the corpus can support claims that:

- certain topological structures recur;
- some forms of hierarchy persist across scale and morphology;
- dimensional/proportional families may recur within bounded contexts;
- certain plan/elevation/section relationships are characteristic;
- some theoretical rules are common, weak or contradicted in built work;
- some apparently Georgian features disappear under urban or later-life conditions while deeper ordering relationships survive.

It cannot establish universal beauty, universal Georgian practice, an objective aesthetic, that every future house should use G-01, or that historical social arrangements deserve contemporary preservation.

## Measurement discipline

Where measurements are used, the source drawing identity, source type, units, scale, raw dimensions, inferred dimensions, wall/clear-room basis, phase and uncertainty remain explicit.

Conflicting sources are not silently averaged. Ratios are downstream interpretations of raw measurements rather than substitutes for them.

Topology can be analysed from adequate plans or descriptions before complete metric evidence exists, but topological evidence is never presented as dimensional evidence.

## Social and occupational context

Spatial form can encode household structure, servant/service arrangements, entertaining conventions, privacy practices and later changes in domestic life. Where evidenced, those conditions are recorded because they may explain why a recurring relationship exists.

The contemporary grammar can then preserve, transform or reject the architectural relationship consciously. Historical servant circulation, for example, does not become a twenty-first-century invariant merely because it appears repeatedly.

## Current precedent cases

The cases used most directly in the inspectable comparative record are [Marble Hill House, Danson House, 76 Dean Street and Bedford Square](g01-cases/README.md).

Together they have been sufficient to reject several tempting simplifications: one universal room ratio, one whole-building symmetry condition, a centred principal entrance, one room sequence, one sectional mechanism and a direct equivalence between compositional and physical aperture.

Those conclusions are consolidated in [G-01 Validation and Limits](g01-validation.md) and represented normatively only where justified in [G-01 v0.1](g01-v0.1.md).
