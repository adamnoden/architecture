# Architectural Grammar

Architectural grammar is the project layer that makes a **selected architectural language** explicit without promoting that language into Ovid’s House doctrine.

The grammar layer may describe topology, hierarchy, ordering, dimensions and proportions, plan/section/elevation relationships, element families and tectonic rules. It remains independent from the computational supported domain: a computational model may represent or check selected grammar relationships, but it does not own the architectural language being represented.

The first developed grammar, **G-01 v0.1**, is a bounded Georgian-derived domestic grammar. Its rules do not become Ovid’s House doctrine merely because the Reference House uses them.

## Framework

[Architectural Grammar and Proportion](architectural-grammar-and-proportion.md) defines the style-neutral framework within which a project may select a concrete grammar.

The framework remains less strongly demonstrated than G-01 itself because one grammar cannot establish neutrality. A materially different second grammar would be the strongest test of whether concepts such as scoped order, hierarchy and plan/section/elevation coupling genuinely generalise.

## G-01 v0.1

The current specification is [G-01 v0.1 — Georgian-Derived Domestic Grammar](g01-v0.1.md).

Its six surviving relationships concern:

- coherent classical architectural fields;
- related and ranked opening families;
- architecturally ranked entrances;
- integrated upper termination and roof order;
- relational spatial and sectional hierarchy;
- concentrated classical articulation.

The grammar deliberately does **not** require universal room ratios, a centred entrance, whole-building bilateral symmetry, one circulation sequence, a double-height principal room, one roof type, fixed sash pane counts or historical servant circulation.

[Validation and limits](g01-validation.md) records why those distinctions survived comparative precedent, counterexample testing and held-out cases. It preserves the useful evidence without requiring the reader to follow the chronology of the research process.

## Relationship to the Reference House

The Reference House selects G-01 as one architectural input alongside Ovid’s House doctrine, patterns, site and programme, technical families and project-specific morphology.

Its courtyard, service strategy, construction family and tectonic choices therefore remain project decisions rather than hidden extensions of G-01.

The current limitation is explicit: G-01 is stronger at architectural body, elevation and threshold scale than at positive Georgian-specific internal plan grammar. Historical service hierarchy and fixed ceremonial sequences were rejected rather than converted into contemporary rules without evidence.
