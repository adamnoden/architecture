## 1. Executive result

D10-S succeeds, but one candidate was materially corrected by validation.

The fresh validation set confirms that the missing G-01 specificity is not a ratio table or a fixed historical plan. It is a coordinated **classical domestic façade/body language** built from:

- a bounded and storeyed wall/opening field;
- related vertically ordered opening families;
- an architecturally ranked entrance within that field;
- a deliberate relationship between façade termination and roof rather than one required roof shape;
- concentrated classical articulation at high-order loci rather than indiscriminate ornament.

The important negative result is equally clear:

> **“roof subordinate to façade order” was too strong.**

Ridgehanger is an authoritative Neo-Georgian adaptation with a deliberately prominent hipped roof, upswept eaves, dormers, small gables and tall end stacks. Validation therefore rejects the original `SPEC-04` wording and replaces it with a narrower relation about **integrated upper termination / roof composition**.

That rewritten relation is then independently challenged by the two remaining validators, 20 Castle Street and Basildon Park, rather than inheriting Ridgehanger's validation automatically.

No positive dimensional ratio is promoted.

# 3. VAL-S1 — Ridgehanger

## Evidence relevant to D10-S

Historic England records:

- detached 1913–15 house by Robert Atkinson;
- L-shaped plan;
- symmetrical three-window front elevation;
- two storeys and attic;
- contrasting brick panels and horizontal bands;
- central entrance with early-C19-style doors and a moulded doorcase;
- bracketed hood forming a balcony to a tall architraved first-floor sash with scrolled pediment;
- related sashes, oculi and shallow round-arched recesses around the entrance field;
- a garden front with a diagonally set canted entrance bay and an enriched pedimented door/French-window condition;
- pantiled hipped roof with upswept eaves, dormers, small gables and tall end stacks;
- a brick parapet on the garden front, rising over the entrance.

The selection guide explicitly treats its wide/sweeping roof and prominent stacks as compatible with Neo-Georgian design rather than as contamination from another language.

## SPEC-01 — bounded classical wall field

**PASS.**

The main front is a clearly bounded three-window/storey composition with a ranked central field, while the garden front uses a materially different but still ordered entrance condition.

This validates the rule's **scope**: an elevation can own a coherent field without requiring every elevation of the house to be identical.

## SPEC-02 — related opening family

**PASS.**

Sashes, oculi, recessed fields and the exceptional tall first-floor central sash form a related but differentiated opening system.

The case supports `family + rank`, not a single window proportion.

## SPEC-03 — ranked entrance

**PASS strongly.**

The entrance is not merely a hole in the wall. Doorcase, hood/balcony, vertical relation to the tall upper sash and surrounding recesses create a clear threshold hierarchy.

The garden entrance is also separately ranked through a canted bay and pedimented treatment.

## SPEC-04 — base/top termination + subordinate roof

**COUNTEREXAMPLE TO ORIGINAL WORDING.**

The roof is not merely subordinate or visually suppressed. Its hipped mass, upswept eaves, dormers, gables and end stacks are active parts of the house's Neo-Georgian character.

The original candidate confused one important Georgian/Palladian solution—concealed or low/subordinate roofs—with the deeper relationship.

### Validation-forced replacement: G01-SPEC-04R

**Integrated upper termination and roof order**

> The principal elevation/body must have a deliberate upper termination, and visible roof geometry must participate coherently in the same architectural hierarchy. The roof may be concealed, low/subordinate, or positively expressed through hipped/mansard/pitched form, dormers and stacks; it must not read as an unrelated residual cap.

The lower termination/base remains a preference where the morphology uses basement/plinth/rustication rather than a universal requirement.

`SPEC-04R` is a material rewrite. Ridgehanger does **not** count as an independent validation of the rewritten statement; VAL-S2 and VAL-S3 must challenge it afresh.

## SPEC-05 — concentrated classical articulation

**PASS.**

The strongest articulation is concentrated around entrances, the tall central sash, pediment/hood conditions and selected bands/recesses. The house is formal without coating every surface in classical ornament.

## VAL-S1 verdict

```text
SPEC-01 bounded wall field               PASS
SPEC-02 related opening family           PASS
SPEC-03 ranked entrance                  PASS
SPEC-04 subordinate roof wording         COUNTEREXAMPLE
SPEC-04R integrated roof/termination     NEW — requires fresh challenge
SPEC-05 concentrated articulation        PASS
```

Ridgehanger is therefore both a validator and a useful falsifier. It prevents D10-S from quietly turning Palladian roof restraint into all Neo-Georgian roof identity.

# 4. VAL-S2 — 20 Castle Street, Chester

## Evidence relevant to D10-S

Historic England records a mid/late-C18 townhouse with:

- three storeys, three bays;
- brown-brick Flemish-bond principal front;
- a stone step to the entrance;
- panelled door with overlight in a painted-stone bolection case;
- two flush twelve-pane sashes beside the door;
- three twelve-pane sashes on each upper storey;
- painted stone floorbands;
- painted stone sills, gauged flat arches and projecting keystones;
- painted stone frieze and cornice;
- hipped grey-slate roof with lateral chimney on each side.

The front is especially useful because the entrance is **not the centre bay** of a perfectly symmetric villa composition.

## SPEC-01 — bounded classical wall field

**PASS.**

The three-bay/storeyed brick front is coherently bounded by floorbands and a strong frieze/cornice termination. The regular upper opening field survives an asymmetrical ground-floor entrance condition.

## SPEC-02 — related opening family

**PASS strongly.**

The repeated twelve-pane flush sashes form an unusually clean family across the upper storeys, while the side/stair conditions demonstrate that secondary elevations can respond differently.

## SPEC-03 — ranked entrance

**PASS strongly and usefully.**

The entrance is architecturally legible through step, panelled door, overlight and stone bolection case even though it is not centred in the three-bay front.

This is direct validation that `ranked entrance` is the correct relation and `centred entrance` is not.

## SPEC-04R — integrated upper termination and roof order

**PASS — first independent validation after rewrite.**

The cornice provides a deliberate façade termination while the hipped slate roof and lateral chimneys form a controlled roof condition above it. The roof is visible and real, but belongs to the same architectural reading.

## SPEC-05 — concentrated classical articulation

**PASS.**

The front is austere. Classical emphasis is carried by the entrance case, floorbands, keystones, frieze/cornice and disciplined sash field rather than an applied order across the whole surface.

## VAL-S2 verdict

```text
SPEC-01 bounded wall field               PASS
SPEC-02 related opening family           PASS
SPEC-03 ranked entrance                  PASS
SPEC-04R integrated roof/termination     PASS
SPEC-05 concentrated articulation        PASS
```

This is the most useful ordinary/regional validation because it satisfies the bundle without relying on Palladian-villa monumentality.

# 5. VAL-S3 — Basildon Park

## Evidence relevant to D10-S

Historic England records John Carr's 1776–83 Palladian mansion with:

- explicit `3:3:3:7:3:3:3` bay rhythm;
- centre block with plinth, rusticated basement, raised band, sill/string courses, dentil cornice and parapet;
- central tetrastyle Ionic portico and triangular pediment;
- three-storey centre comprising basement, piano nobile and attic;
- glazing-bar sash families with differentiated first-floor/attic treatment;
- hipped slate roof with stacks and central light well;
- pavilions with related plinth/bands/cornice/pediment/opening systems;
- rear centre with canted bay, Venetian window and balustraded parapet.

Basildon is deliberately a **scale stress-test**, not a claim that G-01 houses should become mansions.

## SPEC-01 — bounded classical wall field

**PASS strongly.**

The front is an explicit hierarchy of bay groups, storey divisions, base, cornice and parapet. Its size makes the field logic more visible but does not alter its type.

## SPEC-02 — related opening family

**PASS strongly.**

Glazing-bar sashes repeat across the principal composition while hierarchy is expressed through architraves, balustrades, arched lower openings, Venetian window and pavilion/main-block differences.

## SPEC-03 — ranked entrance

**PASS strongly.**

The central portico and approach condition represent the monumental end of the same entrance-ranking relation. This validates the relation while showing that its implementation scale must remain project/morphology dependent.

## SPEC-04R — integrated upper termination and roof order

**PASS — second independent validation after rewrite.**

The centre block and pavilions terminate through dentil cornices/parapets/pediments while hipped slate roofs and stacks remain architecturally integrated above. Concealment is partial rather than the essence of the rule.

## SPEC-05 — concentrated classical articulation

**PASS.**

Articulation is explicitly hierarchical: portico/centre, rusticated base, pavilions, selected first-floor treatments, cornice and rear Venetian/canted centre carry more architectural weight than ordinary openings.

## Scale boundary

Basildon's pass does **not** promote:

- giant order/portico;
- 3:3:3:7:3:3:3 rhythm;
- pavilion composition;
- piano nobile;
- mansion scale

into G-01 domestic invariants.

Its role is to test whether the candidate relations survive at a scale where their implementation becomes much more elaborate.

## VAL-S3 verdict

```text
SPEC-01 bounded wall field               PASS
SPEC-02 related opening family           PASS
SPEC-03 ranked entrance                  PASS
SPEC-04R integrated roof/termination     PASS
SPEC-05 concentrated articulation        PASS
```

# 7. Does the recovered bundle solve D10's specificity failure?

**Yes, at the modest scope required for G-01 v0.1.**

The D10 survivors alone—hierarchy, scoped order, section—could accidentally describe many coherent architectural languages.

The D10-S bundle adds a recognisably Georgian/classical domestic syntax:

```text
BOUNDED CLASSICAL WALL FIELD
        +
RELATED VERTICAL / STOREY-ORDERED OPENING FAMILY
        +
RANKED ARCHITECTURAL ENTRANCE
        +
DELIBERATE UPPER TERMINATION + INTEGRATED ROOF ORDER
        +
CONCENTRATED CLASSICAL ARTICULATION
```

A modernist free façade, Arts-and-Crafts picturesque composition or other materially different grammar could share hierarchy or good section while failing this bundle without being “bad architecture”.

That is the membership distinction D10 was missing.

The bundle is still broad enough to admit:

- compact Palladian villas;
- ordinary Georgian townhouses;
- later Neo-Georgian adaptation;
- centred and off-centred entrances;
- concealed and expressed roofs;
- restrained and more elaborate classical articulation.

That is the intended level for v0.1.
