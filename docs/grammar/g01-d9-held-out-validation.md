# G-01 D9 — Held-Out Validation

**Status:** D9 PASS — hold-out challenge complete at current public-evidence scope  
**Programme:** [G-01 Completion Programme — D8 to D13](g01-completion-programme.md)  
**Upstream result:** [G-01 D8 Controlled Falsification](g01-d8-falsification-result.md)  
**Issue:** #78  
**Date:** 2026-10-14  
**Rule:** the hold-outs were selected before D8 closure and were not used to rewrite the candidates before testing.

## 1. Executive result

The four pre-declared hold-outs do not support a larger G-01 rule set. They make it smaller and clearer.

The strongest result is that **high-order hierarchy, scoped order and vertical/sectional expression generalise better than any one historical mechanism**.

The hold-outs reject or further narrow several tempting rules:

- Marble Hill's cubic/double-height Great Room cannot become a required G-01 principal-room mechanism: Kings Nympton was explicitly based on Marble Hill, yet its referenced cube room was not executed.
- distinct principal/service movement cannot become a universal contemporary invariant: 29 Storey's Way distributes drawing room, study, dining room and kitchen from one central staircase hall despite being an authoritative Neo-Georgian adaptation.
- one global symmetry or one relationship between “front”, entrance and plan cannot define the language: 2 New Street and 29 Storey's Way both retain coherent Georgian-derived order under different frontage and morphology conditions.
- opening hierarchy is a real expressive device, but not a single numeric formula.
- positive room-ratio bands remain unearned.

D9 therefore validates a **relational core**, not a style recipe.

---

# 2. Evidence rule

The hold-outs are used only for claims that their available evidence can support.

Possible dispositions:

- `PASS` — the candidate survives an applicable held-out challenge;
- `COUNTEREXAMPLE` — the hold-out contradicts the candidate at its current scope/authority;
- `NARROW` — the underlying relation survives only after reducing authority/scope;
- `NOT APPLICABLE` — the hold-out does not instantiate the relevant condition;
- `INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE` — available public evidence cannot answer the question.

`INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE` is preferable to inferring plan/section/elevation coupling from photographs or listing prose that does not establish it.

# 3. H1 — Kings Nympton Park

**Role:** close-family anti-overfit test  
**Date / architect:** 1746–50, Francis Cartwright  
**Source authority:** Historic England Grade I listing + Registered Park/Garden record  
**Key source:** https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1152957

Historic England describes Kings Nympton as a Palladian villa **based on Marble Hill**, with a double-depth almost-square plan, central stairhall, servant's stair, principal rooms, first-floor piano nobile, 1:3:1 principal fronts, blind windows on the north elevation and largely surviving original fabric.

Crucially, the listing records references in the building accounts to a **cube room that was not carried out**.

That one fact makes H1 unusually valuable: it is close enough to Marble Hill to test transfer, while differing at exactly the point where D8 warned against turning Marble Hill's sectional mechanism into a rule.

## H1 candidate challenge

### C01 — explicit architectural hierarchy

**PASS.**

Hierarchy survives clearly through:

- central stairhall;
- larger hall / principal rooms;
- first-floor piano nobile;
- differentiated service and principal conditions;
- ordered principal façades.

No single room dimension is required to detect that hierarchy.

### C02 — connectivity / route / sequence distinction

**INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE for an architectural sequence rule.**

The listing supports room/stair relationships but not a sufficiently exact original doorway graph to test required traversal or privileged sequence.

This is not a failure of C02's modelling correction; it prevents D9 from inventing a sequence.

### C03 — differentiated route roles

**PASS descriptively; D10 normative scope remains open.**

The main stairhall and servant/back-stair conditions show route-role differentiation outside Danson and Marble Hill.

The historical labour dependency remains explicit. The hold-out supports the architectural fact that movement systems can carry different rank; it does not establish that a contemporary house requires servant/service separation.

### C04 — scoped symmetry / alignment

**PASS.**

The east and west fronts use strong 1:3:1 ordering while north/south fronts use their own three-bay conditions; the building therefore reinforces the need to name the architectural domain rather than mark the whole house simply `symmetric=true`.

### C05 — opening hierarchy can express rank

**PASS as an expressive mechanism, not a universal formula.**

The first-floor piano nobile receives differentiated window treatment and the storeys use different opening families. This supports the proposition that openings can express storey hierarchy without supplying one numeric hierarchy rule.

### C06 — attribute-specific coupling

**INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE for direction.**

The blind windows are useful evidence that simulated-opening state occurs beyond Danson, but the listing does not establish per-attribute plan/elevation authority or exact centrelines.

Correct D9 behaviour is to preserve those attributes as `UNKNOWN`, not infer Danson's coupling logic into Kings Nympton.

### C07 — no universal room ratio

**PASS as negative guardrail.**

The explicit Marble Hill lineage survives even though the referenced cube room was never executed. A Marble-Hill-derived grammar that required the cubic/double-height room would therefore reject a close historical relative for the wrong reason.

This does not establish positive alternative ratio bands.

### C08 — section / vertical hierarchy is first-class

**PASS and NARROW.**

Kings Nympton has a first-floor piano nobile and strong vertical hierarchy without executing the cube-room proposition. Section remains architecturally important; **double height is only one possible mechanism**.

### C09 — intentional absence is architectural data

**PASS as semantic capability.**

The north elevation includes blind outer windows. `BLIND/SIMULATED` therefore generalises beyond Danson as a meaningful architectural state.

It remains an optional compositional device, not a required G-01 motif.

## H1 verdict

Kings Nympton is a successful anti-overfit case.

It recognises the same architectural family while defeating any attempt to equate that family with Marble Hill's exact section.

# 4. H2 — 2 New Street, Leicester

**Role:** ordinary-scale / urban control  
**Date:** c.1750  
**Source authority:** Historic England Grade II listing, amended 2021  
**Key source:** https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1074003

The building is a relatively austere mid-C18 townhouse on a corner site. Historic England records:

- roughly L-shaped plan;
- three storeys;
- two-bay New Street elevation;
- five-bay Friar Lane elevation with a wider interval between the third and fourth bays;
- classical proportions and subtle detailing;
- differing sash families by storey;
- a small entrance hall, stair and several room/corridor relationships in the surviving state;
- material later stair/office alterations that limit original-phase topological inference.

H2 is valuable because it is ordinary urban domestic architecture rather than a villa-scale canonical object.

## H2 candidate challenge

### C01 — explicit architectural hierarchy

**PASS only at high order / limited evidence.**

The house distinguishes entrance, stair, rooms and storeys, but later alterations make exact original room hierarchy unsafe to reconstruct.

Nothing in H2 supports a geometry-specific hierarchy rule.

### C02 — sequence

**INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.**

The recorded stair and corridor state is materially altered. Do not back-project an original sequence.

### C03 — differentiated route roles

**INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE for the original house.**

Current corridor/service access cannot be treated automatically as c.1750 grammar.

### C04 — scoped symmetry / alignment

**PASS strongly.**

An L-shaped corner house with a two-bay front, a five-bay side elevation and a deliberate wider bay interval remains an authoritative example of mid-C18 Georgian domestic architecture.

This is strong held-out pressure against:

- whole-building bilateral symmetry;
- one universal bay count;
- equal bay spacing as an invariant;
- villa-centred ordering assumptions.

### C05 — opening hierarchy

**PASS weakly / retain as preference.**

The ground/first floors largely use six-over-six sashes while the second floor uses three-over-six sashes. That is enough to show storey differentiation can be carried through fenestration, but not enough to prove the architectural cause or one required hierarchy.

### C06 — attribute-specific coupling

**INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.**

No exact room/opening authority relation should be inferred from the listing.

### C07 — proportion

**NOT APPLICABLE to positive dimensional claims.**

The listing calls the composition well proportioned but supplies no room metric dataset suitable for G-01 bands.

### C08 — section / vertical hierarchy

**LIMITED PASS.**

The differentiated upper-storey opening family confirms that vertical differentiation is representable, but H2 does not provide enough room-height/section evidence to do more.

### C09 — intentional absence

**NOT APPLICABLE.**

No useful blind/protected-solid condition is established by the selected evidence.

## H2 verdict

H2 does its job precisely by **not behaving like a compact Palladian villa**.

It strengthens scoped order and prevents the G-01 core from requiring canonical villa morphology.

# 5. H3 — 29 Storey's Way, Cambridge

**Role:** twentieth-century adaptation control  
**Date / architect:** 1922, M. H. Baillie Scott  
**Source authority:** Historic England Grade II listing with explicit design assessment; published plans identified in Baillie Scott & Beresford, *Houses and Gardens* (1933)  
**Key source:** https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1331882

Historic England calls the house a finely crafted **Neo-Georgian** work from Baillie Scott's later career.

The source records:

- approximately square double-pile plan;
- small service projection;
- three-bay, two-storey composition with upper floor in mansard roof;
- several distinct façade conditions;
- central staircase hall;
- drawing room, study, dining room and kitchen occupying corners around the hall;
- elegant Georgian interior treatment;
- a service area that forms part of the surviving design.

The house is important because its architect brought a strong independent Arts-and-Crafts planning tradition into a Georgian language rather than simply reproducing an eighteenth-century plan.

## H3 candidate challenge

### C01 — explicit architectural hierarchy

**PASS, with an important qualification.**

The central staircase hall is a clear organiser, while reception rooms and kitchen occupy the corner field. The plan remains ordered without requiring that every room advertise rank through size or centrality.

C01 therefore survives only as a relational proposition: the grammar can declare rank and role; it must not infer them from one geometric proxy.

### C02 — connectivity / sequence

**PASS for the modelling distinction; no hard sequence.**

The hall distributes multiple rooms directly rather than reproducing Danson's principal-room circuit. This is an excellent held-out warning against turning the Danson sequence into G-01.

A grammar can represent both distributor and circuit morphologies; it cannot equate Georgian-derived identity with either one.

### C03 — differentiated route roles

**COUNTEREXAMPLE to a hard separation rule; NARROW.**

The central staircase hall provides access to drawing room, study, dining room **and kitchen**. The house also has a service projection, but the authoritative description does not support a rule that principal and service destinations require wholly separate circulation networks.

Therefore:

> route roles must be representable where they exist, but G-01 must not require historical principal/service separation as an invariant.

This is the strongest D9 narrowing of C03.

### C04 — scoped order

**PASS strongly.**

The south-west elevation is described as the frontage, yet the front door is on the north-west gable end in its own central recessed composition. Different elevations therefore own different ordering roles.

This is strong evidence against a model that assumes `principal facade centre = entrance centre = plan centre` as one global relation.

### C05 — opening hierarchy

**PASS as contextual preference, not invariant.**

Different elevations and service areas use sash/casement families differently. The evidence supports openings responding to architectural role and elevation scope, but does not justify one monotonic room-rank→window-size rule.

### C06 — attribute-specific coupling

**NO CONTRADICTION; INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE FOR DIRECTION.**

The source exposes several differently owned façade/room conditions but does not establish exact per-attribute coupling authority. D9 retains C06 as an M0 modelling capability rather than inventing a G-01 release rule.

### C07 — no universal room ratio

**PASS as guardrail / no positive data.**

The house's accepted Neo-Georgian identity does not depend on a published universal room-ratio test in the available evidence. No positive band is derivable here.

### C08 — section / vertical hierarchy

**PASS qualitatively.**

The upper floor is contained within a mansard roof, and the principal stair culminates in a domed landing condition originally top-lit. Section participates in the architectural order without copying Marble Hill's double-height room.

### C09 — intentional absence

**NOT APPLICABLE to the selected evidence.**

No held-out blind-opening claim is required here because H1 already supplies a direct case.

## H3 verdict

H3 is the most important adaptation result in D9.

It demonstrates that a Georgian-derived language can coexist with a materially different planning intelligence. That is evidence against historical-plan reconstruction as the definition of G-01.

# 6. H4 — Tiverton House, Aldeburgh

**Role:** contemporary adaptation control  
**Date / architect:** contemporary; Quinlan Terry Architects  
**Source authority:** Georgian Group 2024 Architectural Awards + architect project record  
**Key sources:**

- https://georgiangroup.org.uk/2024/10/03/architectural-awards-2024/
- https://qtarchitecture.com/projects/tiverton-house/

The Georgian Group selected Tiverton House as the 2024 **New Georgian Country House** winner and describes it as a compact contemporary house in the English Palladian tradition.

The published account records:

- intimate compact scale within a walled urban plot;
- all bedrooms deliberately the same size in response to the family brief;
- high-ceilinged rooms;
- interconnected south/garden-side rooms through large arched openings;
- large sash windows providing generous light;
- a visually dramatic main staircase;
- a top-lit first-floor stair well with arch-headed gallery spaces and long views through the house.

The architect records a central Palladian Doric entrance porch and three-storey composition.

## H4 candidate challenge

### C01 — explicit architectural hierarchy

**PASS, and dimensional simplification is challenged.**

The brief deliberately makes all bedrooms equal in size while the house still contains strong hierarchy through main stair, principal garden rooms, section, openings and sequence.

Therefore room rank cannot be inferred monotonically from area or size.

### C02 — connectivity / route / sequence

**PASS as a modelling need; hard sequence remains morphology-specific.**

Interconnected garden-side rooms and the gallery/stair system show that contemporary Palladian-derived architecture still uses spatial connection and sequence as architectural material.

The available evidence does not justify one mandatory route graph.

### C03 — differentiated route roles

**NOT APPLICABLE / no evidence of a required separate service network.**

That absence reinforces the D9 decision not to make servant-era circulation separation a G-01 invariant.

### C04 — scoped order

**PASS at broad level.**

The central Palladian entrance is a strong local ordering condition, but D9 does not use it to reverse the urban counterexamples and make centred entry universal.

### C05 — opening hierarchy

**PASS as an expressive relationship, not a formula.**

Large garden-side sashes are described in conjunction with the high-ceilinged interconnected rooms and garden orientation. Opening treatment therefore participates in room/elevation role, but no universal size delta follows.

### C06 — attribute-specific coupling

**INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE for exact direction.**

The public descriptions do not give the plan/elevation coordinate evidence required to assign `PLAN_LEADS`, `ELEVATION_LEADS` or `MUTUAL` per attribute.

### C07 — no universal room ratio

**PASS as negative guardrail.**

Equal bedroom size is a deliberate contemporary programme decision, while other spaces are differentiated by section, connection and orientation. The hold-out again warns against turning architectural hierarchy into one ratio/size rule.

No positive ratio family is extracted.

### C08 — section / vertical hierarchy

**PASS strongly.**

High ceilings, the dramatic principal stair, top-lit stair well and arch-headed first-floor gallery all make section part of the architectural order in a contemporary house.

This is important because it validates C08 outside historical reconstruction.

### C09 — intentional absence

**NOT APPLICABLE.**

No useful blind/simulated-opening evidence is required from this hold-out.

## H4 verdict

H4 is the strongest evidence that the surviving G-01 ideas can participate in a contemporary family house without reproducing eighteenth-century occupation literally.

It does **not** validate every design decision in Tiverton House or turn an award into proof. It demonstrates that the relational candidates are compatible with a serious contemporary Georgian/Palladian interpretation.

# 8. D9 rule changes forced by hold-outs

## 8.1 C03 loses any claim to hard route separation

This is the largest substantive change after D8.

The useful proposition is not:

> a Georgian-derived house has principal and service circulation that must remain separate.

It is closer to:

> where movement networks have different architectural roles, the grammar must be able to preserve and evaluate that distinction; creating separate networks is morphology/programme dependent.

29 Storey's Way is the key held-out counterexample because one central staircase hall distributes both reception rooms and kitchen while the house remains an authoritative Neo-Georgian work.

## 8.2 C08 becomes stronger while becoming less specific

Kings Nympton lacks the unexecuted cube room; 29 Storey's Way uses mansard/stair section; Tiverton uses high rooms, a dramatic stair and top-lit gallery.

Therefore:

> vertical/sectional order is durable; **no one sectional motif is**.

## 8.3 C05 survives only as a preference-capable relation

Opening hierarchy repeatedly participates in storey, room or façade role, but no hold-out supports a universal numeric opening-size rule.

## 8.4 C04 is the cleanest architectural survivor

Every hold-out reinforces the value of **scoped order** precisely because their ordering devices differ.

G-01 should be opinionated about declared architectural order while remaining suspicious of whole-building symmetry shorthand.

# 10. What D9 proves — and does not prove

D9 does **not** prove that G-01 is a complete or objectively correct account of Georgian architecture.

It does establish that the surviving high-order candidates are not merely accidental descriptions of Marble Hill, Danson and 76 Dean Street.

More importantly, the hold-outs expose which parts **do not** generalise.

The grammar that now deserves normative consideration is therefore substantially smaller than D7:

1. architectural hierarchy is explicit but not tied to one geometric proxy;
2. order/symmetry/alignment has named scope;
3. connectivity, route and sequence are distinct where relevant;
4. differentiated route roles are optional/contextual, not a universal social topology;
5. section/vertical order is first-class but mechanism-neutral;
6. opening treatment may express hierarchy as a preference relationship;
7. plan/section/elevation coupling needs attribute-aware representation but not one Georgian authority direction;
8. blind/simulated/solid states are representable but optional;
9. no universal room ratio is justified.

That is the input to D10.
