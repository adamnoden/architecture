# G-01 — Validation and limits

G-01 v0.1 is the surviving result of comparative precedent study, deliberate counterexample testing and held-out validation. This page records the evidence that matters to a reader without reproducing the chronology of the research programme.

The central result is simple:

> **Georgian-derived domestic identity survives more reliably in relational order than in fixed ratios, plan recipes or historical mechanisms.**

## What survived challenge

Across the precedent corpus and independent validation cases, six relationships remained useful enough to enter the controlled grammar:

1. a coherent classical architectural field rather than an accidental aggregation of openings;
2. related and ranked opening families;
3. a principal entrance that is architecturally ranked, but need not be centred;
4. a deliberate relationship between upper termination and roof rather than one required roof shape;
5. spatial and sectional hierarchy expressed relationally rather than by one geometric proxy;
6. classical articulation concentrated at places that carry architectural rank.

These are the rules stated formally in [G-01 v0.1](g01-v0.1.md).

## What did not survive

The evidence does **not** justify treating any of the following as universal G-01 rules:

- one preferred room ratio or golden-ratio family;
- a centred entrance;
- whole-building bilateral symmetry;
- equal bay spacing or a fixed bay count;
- one privileged circulation sequence;
- separate principal and servant/service routes in contemporary houses;
- a mandatory double-height principal room or piano nobile;
- one roof type or a requirement that the roof be visually suppressed;
- fixed sash pane counts or one window proportion;
- courtyard morphology;
- historical construction technology.

Several of these propositions initially looked plausible precisely because they occur strongly in famous precedents. Counterexamples showed that they were examples of a deeper relationship, not the relationship itself.

## Key falsifications

### Marble Hill and Kings Nympton: hierarchy is not a cube-room rule

Marble Hill makes sectional hierarchy unusually explicit through its celebrated cubic/double-height Great Room. Kings Nympton Park is a particularly strong counterpressure because Historic England records it as a Palladian villa based on Marble Hill while also recording a referenced cube room that was **not carried out**.

The family resemblance survives without the exact room mechanism. The robust conclusion is therefore that section and vertical hierarchy matter; a cubic or double-height principal room does not define the language.

### Danson House: composition and aperture are not identical

Danson’s blind or simulated openings show that an elevation can preserve compositional centrelines or opening states that do not correspond one-to-one with an internal aperture. This supports two modelling conclusions that also matter architecturally:

- plan, section and elevation relationships must be stated attribute by attribute rather than collapsed into one direction of authority;
- intentional absence, blindness or simulation can itself be architectural data.

The same broad phenomenon appears elsewhere in the corpus, including Kings Nympton.

### 76 Dean Street and 20 Castle Street: rank does not require centring

Authoritative urban Georgian examples preserve strong architectural order while allowing an off-centre principal entrance. The evidence therefore supports **entrance rank**, not a centred-entry rule.

A façade can remain ordered even when programme, plot or circulation makes bilateral centring inappropriate.

### 2 New Street: Georgian order survives ordinary urban irregularity

The mid-eighteenth-century corner house at 2 New Street, Leicester is roughly L-shaped, with materially different two-bay and five-bay fronts and a wider interval within the latter. It remains recognisably Georgian domestic architecture without satisfying villa-derived assumptions about global symmetry, equal bay spacing or one universal frontage logic.

This is important because the grammar must recognise ordinary and urban cases, not only canonical villas.

### 29 Storey’s Way: historical service separation is not a contemporary invariant

This Neo-Georgian adaptation distributes principal domestic rooms from one central staircase hall. It therefore blocks any attempt to turn historically differentiated principal/service circulation into a universal contemporary membership rule.

Route hierarchy remains a useful architectural concept; servant circulation does not become a requirement merely because it occurs in historical precedent.

### Ridgehanger: a prominent roof can still be Neo-Georgian

Ridgehanger in Ealing was the decisive counterexample to the proposition that the roof must be subordinate to the façade. Its hipped mass, upswept eaves, dormers, small gables and prominent stacks are active parts of its Neo-Georgian character.

The stronger surviving relation is **integrated upper termination and roof order**: the roof may be concealed, quiet or positively expressed, but it should belong to the same architectural hierarchy rather than read as an unrelated residual cap.

That corrected relation was then checked against materially different examples, including 20 Castle Street and Basildon Park.

## Held-out validation

Independent cases selected outside the main extraction set were used to test whether the proposed grammar generalised beyond the precedents that suggested it.

The useful result was not an expanded rule set. It was a smaller one.

- **Kings Nympton Park** supported hierarchy, scoped order and vertical differentiation while defeating any cube-room requirement.
- **2 New Street, Leicester** supported local/scoped façade order while resisting whole-building symmetry and equal-bay assumptions.
- **29 Storey’s Way** supported Georgian-derived order without requiring separate service circulation.
- the wider held-out set reinforced that opening hierarchy is real but is not captured by one numeric window formula.

Where public evidence could not establish an exact route, dimensional relation or direction of plan/elevation authority, the result was left unknown rather than inferred from photographs or stylistic resemblance.

## Positive specificity

A grammar can become so cautious that it ceases to distinguish anything. G-01 therefore also had to demonstrate positive Georgian-derived specificity rather than merely reject over-strong rules.

Fresh validation supported a coordinated classical domestic body/elevation language built from:

- bounded and storeyed wall/opening fields;
- related vertically ordered opening families;
- architecturally ranked entrances;
- deliberate upper termination and roof composition;
- concentrated classical articulation at high-order loci.

20 Castle Street is useful because this bundle survives at ordinary townhouse scale and with an off-centre entrance. Basildon Park tests the same relationships at the opposite, monumental end of the scale. Ridgehanger demonstrates that the language can survive twentieth-century Neo-Georgian adaptation without collapsing into literal eighteenth-century replication.

No positive room-ratio table was earned by this work.

## Evidence boundary

This validation establishes only a bounded architectural-language proposition.

It does not prove that G-01 is the best language for a project, that every conforming building is good architecture, or that the framework is neutral across all architectural traditions. One Georgian-derived grammar cannot establish that neutrality; a materially different second grammar would be the strongest future test.

The positive account of internal Georgian-specific plan grammar also remains thinner than the body/elevation/threshold account. Historical service hierarchy and ceremonial sequence were deliberately rejected where they could not be separated from obsolete social organisation. Future additions should require evidence that distinguishes a genuinely transferable architectural relationship from generic good planning or historical imitation.
