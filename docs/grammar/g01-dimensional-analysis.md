# G-01 Dimensional and Proportional Analysis

G-01 treats proportion as architectural evidence, not as a master generator. Raw dimensions, room role, geometry, hierarchy and section remain visible before any named proportional family is inferred.

The present evidence already supports one strong conclusion: a coherent Georgian-derived grammar needs plural, scoped dimensional relationships rather than one universal room ratio.

## Measurement discipline

Every usable value should retain:

- case and entity identity;
- source identity;
- raw value and unit;
- whether the value is directly stated, measured from a drawing or derived;
- historical phase;
- uncertainty;
- derivation where applicable.

Do not:

- round a measured dimension towards a canonical ratio;
- infer missing dimensions from a weak image where a better source exists;
- combine historical phases silently;
- turn one building’s elegant numbers into a style law.

### Evidence classes

**D — directly stated** — dimension stated by an authoritative source.  
**M — measured** — dimension taken from an adequate scaled drawing.  
**R — derived ratio** — computed from D or M values.  
**U — unknown / not adequately extracted**.

## Derived Danson ratios

### Entrance Hall

Raw: **26 ft 8 in × 20 ft × 16 ft high**.

Derived:

- length:width ≈ **1.333 : 1**;
- width:height = **1.250 : 1**;
- length:height ≈ **1.667 : 1**.

The source itself identifies the plan proportion as 4:3.

### Library

Raw: **36 ft × 18 ft × 16 ft high**.

Derived:

- length:width = **2.000 : 1**;
- width:height = **1.125 : 1**;
- length:height = **2.250 : 1**.

### Dining Room

Same stated dimensions as the Library:

- length:width = **2.000 : 1**;
- width:height = **1.125 : 1**;
- length:height = **2.250 : 1**.

### Saloon

Using the stated 26 ft × 26 ft principal measure and 16 ft height:

- plan principal-axis ratio = **1.000 : 1**;
- principal width:height = **1.625 : 1**.

The room is canted/octagonal, so 1:1 describes its principal axes rather than a simple square prism.

## One house already needs several proportion families

Danson’s principal floor contains at least:

- 4:3;
- 2:1;
- 1:1 principal axes within canted geometry;
- approximately 4:3 ellipse geometry in side bays;

while retaining a common 16 ft room height.

A credible grammar therefore needs something closer to:

```text
ROOM ROLE
   +
GEOMETRIC FAMILY
   +
DIMENSIONAL BAND
   +
SECTIONAL DATUM
   +
RELATION TO ADJACENT ROOMS
```

The ratio is one variable inside a larger architectural relation.

## Shared height can unify different plans

Danson’s principal rooms share a 16 ft height despite major variation in plan geometry.

That shows why three questions must remain distinct:

- within-room proportion;
- cross-room dimensional hierarchy;
- shared storey/ceiling datums.

A common sectional datum can create coherence without repeated plan ratios.

## Hierarchy does not map to one “better” ratio

Compare two dominant spaces.

### Marble Hill Great Room

- 24 ft cube;
- central;
- double height;
- highest architectural rank.

### Danson Saloon

- 26 × 26 ft principal plan measure;
- 16 ft high;
- canted/octagonal;
- dominant garden-front room in the principal circuit.

Both have square-like principal axes, but very different volumes. Their hierarchy also depends on location, sequence, section, openings, form and use.

A proportional family may support architectural rank; it does not generate rank by itself.

## Exactness needs source context

Danson’s clean historical numbers are especially useful because the research explicitly discusses proportion and foot measures. That does not imply that every Georgian house was built to equally exact canonical ratios.

A dimensional statement should distinguish:

- directly stated source dimension;
- inferred or measured value;
- original versus later finished condition;
- structural/grid dimension versus clear room dimension;
- exact versus approximate/reconstructed relationship.

Design module and measured finished room may not be identical.

## Named proportion families are comparison tools

Useful reference families include:

- 1:1;
- 4:3;
- √2:1;
- 3:2;
- 5:3;
- 2:1.

These are analytical reference points, not G-01 preferences by default. The evidence must also allow **no meaningful named family**.

A measured ratio of 1.47 remains 1.47; a later comparison may note that 1.50 is the nearest named family. It is not rewritten as “3:2”.

## Absolute dimension matters

A 2:1 room may be 6 × 12 ft, 18 × 36 ft or 30 × 60 ft. Those are not architecturally equivalent.

Any future dimensional rule therefore needs to remain capable of considering:

- absolute width and length;
- area;
- height;
- role/use;
- furniture and occupation envelope;
- adjacent hierarchy.

## Room form matters

The Danson Saloon also demonstrates that a bounding ratio does not fully describe room form.

Dimensional evidence may need to distinguish:

- rectangular bounding dimensions;
- effective principal axes;
- polygonal/curved room family;
- bay/recess geometry;
- clear usable rectangle where relevant.

## Cross-room relations can be more informative than isolated ratios

Architectural hierarchy may be better exposed by relationships such as:

- principal-room area / secondary-room area;
- principal-room width / secondary-room width;
- ceiling-height ratio across storeys;
- centre-bay / flank-bay widths;
- opening height / room height;
- opening width / wall field;
- pier width / opening width;
- stair width / circulation width;
- principal-route width hierarchy;
- moulding datum / room height;
- façade storey heights.

These relationships remain evidence questions rather than assumed laws.

## What the evidence does not support

The current analysis rejects:

- golden-ratio hunting;
- “Palladio says 2:1, therefore rule”;
- averaging unlike rooms into one target ratio;
- low-quality screen measurement where stronger source material exists;
- silent mixing of clear and structural-grid dimensions;
- treating restored measurements as original without phase evidence;
- converting dimensional correlation into aesthetic proof.

## Current G-01 consequence

The dimensional evidence supports the position already reflected in G-01 v0.1:

> **Proportion belongs to families of admissible, role- and scope-specific relationships embedded inside hierarchy, topology and section—not to one numerical generator of the house.**

No universal room ratio is therefore a G-01 invariant. Positive dimensional preferences should remain bounded by source, role, morphology and evidence.
