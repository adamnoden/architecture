# G-01 Precedent Cases

The precedent cases make the evidence behind G-01 inspectable without asking the reader to follow the chronology of the research programme.

Each case distinguishes source facts, derived relationships, interpretation, uncertainty and historical phase. The cases are intentionally unlike one another: the purpose is not to make several buildings look the same, but to discover which relationships survive variation in morphology, date and urban condition.

## Core cases

| Case | Morphology | Evidence position | Architectural use in G-01 |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Marble Hill House](marble-hill-house.md) | compact freestanding Palladian villa | strong measured/phase-coded public evidence | sectional hierarchy; strong centre; room-volume versus façade-module evidence |
| [Danson House](danson-house.md) | compact later-Georgian / neo-Palladian villa | fabric research + near-contemporary plan | plural room families; shared sectional datum; route sequence; blind-window and structural/opening coupling |
| [76 Dean Street](76-dean-street.md) | urban double-pile terrace | authoritative descriptive record + located historic survey plans | urban counterexample; topology and hierarchy where metric evidence is weaker |
| [Bedford Square](bedford-square.md) | later-Georgian planned square / terrace family | public-domain measured plans + parcel dimensions + block-composition evidence | urban measurement; order operating above the individual-house boundary |

Dean Street and Bedford Square have deliberately different jobs. Dean Street prevents villa-derived assumptions from becoming universal; Bedford Square provides stronger urban metric evidence and tests the relation between house and larger terrace composition.

## Source-qualified evidence packs

Two cases also retain source-qualified measurement/coupling packs because the provenance of individual dimensional claims matters:

- [Marble Hill — measurement and coupling evidence](marble-hill-d5-d6-web-evidence-v02.md)
- [Danson — measurement and coupling evidence](danson-d5-d6-web-evidence-v02.md)

The important distinction is not their historical stage name. It is that publicly inspectable evidence, located-but-unavailable archive material, historical design/publication intent and later measured/restoration states are not treated as interchangeable.

## What the cases establish

Across the current set, the evidence rejects several attractive simplifications:

- Georgian architecture is not one plan graph;
- symmetry is not a whole-building Boolean;
- hierarchy is not reducible to room area;
- proportion is not one preferred ratio;
- circulation is not merely leftover connective space;
- plan and elevation do not always share one centre or one direction of causality;
- an opening can have different compositional and physical roles, including blind/simulated conditions;
- roof form varies while still participating in a coherent upper order;
- historical phase cannot be stripped from geometry;
- urban order may operate at terrace/block scale rather than stop at the party wall.

Those findings are synthesised in [G-01 Validation and Limits](../g01-validation.md) and reflected selectively in the current [G-01 v0.1 specification](../g01-v0.1.md).

## Evidence boundary

A catalogue record showing that a measured drawing exists does not authorise measurement if the drawing itself cannot be inspected. Metric claims remain unknown unless an adequate source is available.

Likewise, a precise modern survey can be geometrically authoritative while describing the wrong historical phase for a particular grammar claim. Geometry quality and phase authority are separate.

## Why separate case records matter

A case record should preserve what is specific to that building: its sources, morphology, room identities, topology, hierarchy, dimensions, openings, section, alterations and uncertainties.

The grammar is derived only after comparison. Precedent-specific quirks are not allowed to redefine the general model merely because they are richly documented.

The useful result is therefore not that the four cases agree. It is that one evidence method can describe a canonical villa, a different villa organised by circulation sequence, a constrained urban townhouse and a terrace condition without forcing them into one type or inventing missing evidence.
