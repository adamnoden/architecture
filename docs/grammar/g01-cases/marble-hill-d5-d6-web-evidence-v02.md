# Marble Hill — Measurement and Coupling Evidence

**Case:** G01-MHH

Marble Hill combines unusually rich archival material with an important evidential complication: design/publication drawings, modern interpretive plans, conservation geometry and catalogue records do not all have the same authority.

This page records the source distinctions, measurements and coupling conclusions that can be defended directly.

## Source position

The evidence should be separated into:

- current or interpretive plans that can actually be inspected;
- historical design/publication images that can actually be inspected;
- measured-drawing catalogue records whose images may not be publicly available;
- specialist secondary statements that provide specific dimensions without being the underlying survey.

Those classes are complementary, not interchangeable.

## Publicly inspectable sources

### MHH-W01 — English Heritage all-floor plan, November 2024

URL: https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/siteassets/home/visit/places-to-visit/marble-hill-house/history-and-stories/marble-hill-plans-2024.pdf

**Authority:** English Heritage interpretive/current plan.  
**Useful for:** all-floor topology, current room names, Great Room/upper-Great-Room relationship, phase coding, lost service-wing location and metric orientation via the printed scale.

The plan distinguishes fabric dated 1724–9, 1739, the 1740s, 1950 and the 1990s and notes walls demolished in 1909.

**Limit:** it is a modern interpretive drawing of a building with later work and restoration history, not a raw 1729 measured plan.

### MHH-W02 — English Heritage / Clews Architects restoration plans

URL: https://www.clewsarchitects.co.uk/english-heritage-visitor-improvement-projects.html

**Authority:** modern conservation-project survey/design information.  
**Useful for:** detailed surviving room/wall/opening geometry, opening identity, stair/door relationships and cross-checking the interpretive plan.

**Limit:** high-quality current geometry is not automatically historical-phase evidence.

### MHH-W03 — Colen Campbell published design / early design image

English Heritage history image: https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places-to-visit/marble-hill/history-and-stories/history/

Public-domain copy: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MarbleHillHousePlans.JPG

**Authority:** historical design/publication evidence.  
**Useful for:** intended Palladian ordering, plan/elevation co-presentation and comparison between published intent and surviving geometry.

**Limit:** the as-built house must not be measured from the plate without first establishing that the relevant condition was executed as drawn.

### MHH-W04 — Michael Bidnell / Georgian Group, “Windows – Don’t Despair, Repair”, 2008

URL: https://ablakprofilok.hu/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Michael_Bidnell_The-Georgian-Group_eloadas.pdf

**Authority:** specialist secondary statement, not raw survey evidence.  
**Useful for:** a façade-module hypothesis.

Bidnell describes classic **1–3–1** Palladian fenestration, states that the windows are **40 in wide**, and gives lateral/vertical spaces of **40, 60, 80 or 120 in**.

These values remain expert-stated secondary metric evidence pending direct survey corroboration.

### MHH-W05 — Historic England measured-drawing records

Examples include:

- MP/MHH0016 — hand-tinted ground/first-floor measured plans, 1926;
- MP/MHH0116 — ground-floor publication measured drawing, 1962;
- MP/MHH0117 — first-floor publication measured drawing, 1962;
- MP/MHH0155 — plan/elevation of centre window of first-floor salon, 1964;
- PF/MHH — archive volume containing hundreds of measured drawings.

These records establish a strong A1 source universe. A catalogue record alone does not supply a dimension where the sheet itself cannot be inspected.

Numerical precision in a secondary source is not treated as A1 measured evidence merely because the number is exact.

## Withheld dimensional claims

A Friends of Marble Hill architectural essay describes rooms flanking the Great Room as “double cubes” and the two behind as “single cubes”. The statement is architecturally interesting but insufficiently sourced for quantitative use.

No exact dimensions are inferred from it and the rooms are not back-solved from the 24 ft Great Room.

Likewise, the 2024 plan contains a scale bar, but room dimensions are not promoted from casual screen measurement. Any later extraction should retain the exact source image, method and uncertainty.

## Multiple dimensional systems

The evidence suggests at least two different dimensional phenomena:

1. a **24 ft cubic dominant room**;
2. a reported **40 in façade opening module** with larger intervals expressed through related multiples.

That supports a scope-aware interpretation:

```text
ROOM / VOLUME FAMILY
        ≠
FAÇADE / OPENING MODULE
```

The architectural lesson is not that one number governs the house. Different dimensional systems may operate coherently at different scales.

## Sectional privilege

The 2024 English Heritage plan shows the Great Room on the first floor and the **upper part of the Great Room** occupying the corresponding second-floor position, with surrounding second-floor rooms/gallery continuing around the void.

This provides direct evidence that first-floor room hierarchy changes the topology of the floor above.

### MHH-CPL-02 — sectional privilege

- **scope owner:** dwelling / room;
- **subject:** MHH-1-GREAT;
- **attribute:** vertical occupation / room rank;
- **plan signal:** dominant central principal room;
- **section signal:** 24 ft cubic/double-height volume;
- **upper-plan consequence:** second-floor void / absence of an ordinary room above;
- **coupling:** mutual between room identity and section;
- **confidence:** high.

The significance is topological and sectional, not merely the visual effect of a tall room.

## Design intent and built evidence

Marble Hill makes the distinction between publication intent and executed building unusually clear.

```text
DESIGN / PUBLICATION INTENT
        ↕ compare
SURVIVING / MEASURED GEOMETRY
        ↕ phase reconcile
HISTORICAL INTERPRETATION
```

A relationship does not become a G-01 rule merely because it appears elegantly in Campbell’s published plate. It must survive comparison with the built case and the relevant historical phase.

## Architectural centre

High-confidence evidence supports:

- five-bay north and south fronts;
- projecting and pedimented centre three;
- a dominant central Great Room;
- the reported 1–3–1 principal opening rhythm.

Together these strongly support a shared broad architectural centre across room hierarchy and façade order.

What remains unproved is the exact coordinate relationship among room centreline, centre-window centreline, flanking window centres, primary wall centres and stair axis.

The appropriate conclusion is therefore:

**MHH-CPL-01 — mutual broad-order coupling at high confidence; exact geometric coupling remains open.**

## Evidential boundary

Marble Hill supports three durable G-01 conclusions:

1. strong architectural order can operate through more than one dimensional system;
2. room rank can be materially sectional and can change upper-floor topology;
3. published architectural intent and executed geometry must remain distinct evidence classes.

It does not yet justify exact façade-coordinate rules or a universal Palladian proportional system.
