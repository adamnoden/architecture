# Appendix B — Legacy Doctrine Register Crosswalk

The sixty-three statements below are preserved from **House Design Doctrine v7**, the last long-form doctrine snapshot before Ovid’s House was reduced to eleven governing principles and separated more cleanly into doctrine, strategies, patterns, project choices and technical constraints.

They are included here as provenance, not as a second current doctrine.

The historical source was deleted from the live repository after the migration but remains recoverable in git history at parent commit `8ea91d8ea3dff5ca04ee2cb263a8ee003a348547`, file `docs/source/house-design-doctrine-v7.md`. The wording in the first column is retained from that source.

The crosswalk records where each statement now belongs:

- **Retained** — substantially survives as a governing principle;
- **Transformed** — the durable intent survives, but the old wording was too absolute or implementation-specific;
- **Absorbed** — survives below doctrine as a pattern, strategy, technical requirement or governing constraint;
- **Project-specific** — useful for a particular house or system but no longer a general proposition;
- **Superseded** — the project has deliberately rejected or materially revised the old proposition.

A historical item may map to more than one current principle. The current eleven principles remain authoritative.

## What the crosswalk shows

The reduction from sixty-three statements to eleven principles was not primarily deletion. It was a separation of levels.

The v7 register mixed at least five kinds of proposition:

1. durable architectural principles;
2. technical or statutory constraints;
3. reusable patterns and strategies;
4. project/system implementation preferences;
5. delivery and commissioning requirements.

The eleven-principle form is stronger precisely because those levels are no longer presented as equivalent doctrine.

Several old statements also became less absolute. Services are no longer prohibited from every physical relationship with permanent fabric; sparse designed crossings and attachments are legitimate. Reversibility is pursued proportionately rather than universally. Passive architecture acts first without becoming an ideological ban on mechanical systems. Accessible infrastructure is not required to become visible or demountable everywhere.

This appendix therefore records continuity without pretending stasis.

The doctrine itself changed by learning which of its earlier rules were actually principles, which were implementations, and which belonged somewhere else.
