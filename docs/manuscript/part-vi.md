# Part VI — The House

This part is intentionally spare.

It is reserved for the resolved architectural object that follows the proposition, the platform, the reusable patterns, the worked design proposition and the ordeal of making and proving it. It should read as a short architectural monograph: the house itself, not another explanation of how the project arrived there.

The present page establishes that final place in the book. It does **not** claim that the Reference House is technically complete, construction-ready or finally resolved.

## What belongs here

When the house has passed through architectural synthesis and the required technical/evidential gates, this part may contain:

- a concise account of the final architectural idea;
- the resolved plan, section and elevation family;
- selected exterior, courtyard and interior views;
- the final material, opening, joinery and room language;
- a small number of details where they are necessary to understand the architecture;
- captions that state what is actually resolved and what remains project-specific.

The emphasis is visual and architectural. Repeated doctrine, development chronology and proof administration belong elsewhere.

## Admission rule

Nothing enters this part merely because it is attractive, drawn or plausible.

The architectural configuration must first be reconciled as one house. Material technical findings must already have been absorbed into that configuration, and the final publication drawings must represent the same controlled source.

Where a significant question remains open, either keep it in the proving/technical-closure layer or state the limitation directly. Part VI is a conclusion, not a place to hide unresolved design work.

## Relationship to Part IV

Part IV explains the Reference House as a design proposition: its type, order, systems and selected architectural decisions.

Part VI is different. It is the concise final presentation of the resolved house after those propositions have survived the proving process. The two parts should therefore never become duplicate case studies.
