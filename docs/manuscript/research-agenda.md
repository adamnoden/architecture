# Research Agenda

Ovid’s House is intentionally unfinished at several edges.

The purpose of a research agenda is not to keep the project permanently open. It is to distinguish questions that can change the architectural position from those that belong to a future project, specialist calculation or implementation choice.

The agenda below records the questions most capable of falsifying, narrowing or materially improving the work.

## 1. Can the long-life platform survive competent external criticism?

The Reference House has been coordinated internally and packaged for external review, but no competent discipline response has yet been processed.

Priority reviews are:

- structure;
- fire and Building Control;
- MEP and acoustics;
- building physics, energy, overheating and daylight.

The purpose is not endorsement. A useful review should identify which assumptions are wrong, under-evidenced, disproportionate or likely to force architectural change.

A material finding should reopen the smallest owning decision rather than being absorbed as a note at the end of the project.

## 2. Which serviceable interfaces remain desirable at full scale?

Drawings can make almost any joint appear calm.

The project still needs physical evidence at the interfaces where maintainability risks degrading solidity, acoustics, workmanship or visual repose.

The two most important Reference House questions are currently:

- the structural floor edge and its relationship to the architectural platform;
- the wall/ceiling movement junction, including comparison with an excellent conventional control.

Optional challengers such as replaceable full-height lining should be allowed to fail without threatening the house.

The benchmark is not whether a detail can be disassembled. It is whether anyone would still want the detail if its maintainability were invisible.

## 3. How much serviceability is proportionate?

Every permanent route, access zone, removable layer and spare capacity has an initial cost in space, carbon, labour, coordination and possible failure modes.

The project now has a fail-closed architectural screening method, but real projects still need stronger comparative evidence for where early provision is justified by:

- frequency of the future task;
- consequence of failure or obsolescence;
- destructiveness of retrofit;
- cost of provision at first construction;
- whole-life material and carbon effect;
- who pays the present premium and who is likely to receive the future benefit.

This question may narrow the project further. That is an acceptable outcome.

## 4. Can ordinary workmanship deliver the interfaces reliably?

A long-life detail that depends on continuous designer supervision is not robust.

Future prototypes and project work should deliberately include:

- imperfect but realistic masonry backgrounds;
- normal construction tolerances;
- representative competent installers for repeated non-standard work rather than only specialist fabricators;
- repeated removal and reinstatement where reversibility is claimed;
- inspection before closure where consequence warrants it;
- failure states caused by plausible site error.

The project should distinguish details that are merely difficult from details whose difficulty is structurally useful. Conventional work should not acquire exceptional process merely because it appears in Ovid’s House.

## 5. Does a second architectural grammar expose hidden bias in the framework?

G-01 has now reached an internally controlled state as a Georgian-derived grammar.

The architectural grammar framework itself remains less mature because one grammar cannot prove neutrality. The strongest next test would be a materially different domestic language whose ordering principles are not classical.

The purpose would not be to expand Ovid’s House stylistically for its own sake. It would test whether concepts such as scoped order, hierarchy, coupling and human judgement are genuinely framework-level—or whether assumptions from G-01 have leaked upward unnoticed.

There is no standing programme to do this before a concrete need exists.

## 6. What positive Georgian-specific internal grammar is still missing?

G-01 is strongest at architectural body, elevation and threshold scale.

Its positive account of internal plan grammar is deliberately thinner. Historical service hierarchy and fixed ceremonial sequences were rejected rather than laundered into contemporary rules.

Future work may ask whether there are transferable Georgian-derived relationships involving:

- room sequence;
- axial or enfilade relationships;
- fireplaces and room centres;
- stair arrival;
- principal/subordinate enclosure;
- plan/section coupling at ordinary domestic scale.

Such rules should be added only when evidence distinguishes them from generic good planning and from obsolete social organisation.

## 7. How does the Reference House change on a real site?

The current house has no actual plot.

A real project would expose questions that virtual coordination cannot settle:

- planning constraints and neighbours;
- orientation and overshadowing;
- external noise;
- utilities and drainage;
- ground conditions and trees;
- boundary access and temporary works;
- actual landscape;
- fire-service access;
- cost and procurement conditions.

The valuable research question is not whether the current geometry survives unchanged. It is which relationships prove robust enough to survive site reality and which were artefacts of the surrogate condition.

A compact-terrace counter-morphology has already shown that some Reference House devices should disappear rather than transfer literally: the dedicated service flank, generous plant room and side replacement opening are project expressions, not doctrine.

## 8. Can the courtyard earn its environmental and maintenance costs?

The Reference House court is a project morphology, not doctrine.

Its current role is primarily spatial, orienting and daylighting rather than solar. It also increases envelope area and introduces an internal external-space maintenance condition.

A real-site study should compare the courtyard house against plausible non-courtyard alternatives for:

- daylight quality;
- overheating;
- heat loss;
- embodied carbon;
- usable area;
- cost;
- maintenance access;
- domestic value.

The courtyard should survive because it is good architecture for the project, not because it has become part of the project’s mythology.

## 9. What does long-term occupation reveal that design cannot?

Many claims in Ovid’s House concern events that happen slowly:

- whether future trades actually understand the building;
- whether access routes remain clear;
- whether physical labels remain useful;
- whether interfaces are repaired rather than bypassed;
- whether removable elements are reinstated accurately;
- whether adaptable provisions are ever used;
- whether future owners preserve or destroy the maintenance geography;
- whether the house remains calm rather than technical in ordinary life.

These questions require buildings and time.

Any future built project should therefore treat post-occupancy observation and maintenance history as research evidence, not merely facilities-management data.

## 10. Which environmental claims survive measured performance and future conditions?

Passive-first is a design hierarchy, not a performance result.

Future project work should test actual:

- heat loss;
- overheating;
- daylight;
- ventilation effectiveness;
- airtightness;
- acoustic performance;
- heat-pump operation;
- solar generation;
- moisture behaviour;
- resilience during utility or equipment outages.

A long-life project should also test expensive-to-change geometry against a **reasonable range of future climate and hazard conditions appropriate to the real site**. The purpose is not to choose one speculative future-weather file and treat it as truth. Record instead:

- the scenario/source and time horizon;
- material uncertainty;
- which permanent decisions are sensitive to the scenario;
- what low-burden adaptation remains available;
- the threshold at which plan, opening, shading, envelope, roof/drainage, landscape or plant strategy should be reconsidered.

Measured or scenario failure should be allowed to change the architecture or the doctrine where necessary. The current site-free Reference House cannot close this question honestly.

## 11. Where should computation stop?

Metamorph Compiler is intentionally bounded.

Future executable work is justified only when another workstream produces a concrete relationship worth checking deterministically: a professional-review finding, a physical test, a real project, an authoritative method or a new proof family.

The research question is not how much of architecture can be encoded. It is where explicit representation makes architectural responsibility, evidence and contradiction clearer than prose and drawings alone.

A machine-checked weak assumption remains a weak assumption.

## 12. Can the project remain strange after professionalisation?

Publication itself is part of the research.

As the work encounters architects, engineers, critics, publishers and institutions, a final question is whether increased credibility forces unnecessary conventionalisation.

The project should become clearer, better evidenced and harder to dismiss. It should not become generic merely to resemble an established professional genre.

The test is whether external criticism can remove error while preserving the compact propositions, visual restraint and memorable architectural ideas that make the work transmit at all.
