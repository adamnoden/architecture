# Glossary

The terms below are used in specific senses throughout Ovid’s House. They are not proposed as universal architectural definitions.

**Architectural platform** — the enduring combination of fabric, interfaces, maintenance geography, boundary strategy and attachment/service provisions within which faster-changing systems can be maintained, repaired and replaced without repeatedly consuming slower construction.

**Boundary debt** — the performance obligation created when access, services, openings or disassembly interrupt another function of construction: structure, fire, smoke, acoustics, air, vapour, water, thermal continuity, pests or security as applicable.

**Building passport** — the transferable record of the as-built building: drawings, service routes, structural and boundary information, commissioning results, critical interfaces, maintenance information, replacement sequences and later alterations. Critical knowledge should remain readable without proprietary software or vendor accounts.

**Controlled attachment plane** — a known intermediate fixing datum that allows faster-changing fit-out to attach without repeated uncontrolled penetration of permanent fabric. It is used only where its capacity and future usefulness justify the added layer.

**Designed interface** — a deliberate junction between assemblies that manages the functions actually present: load transfer, restraint, movement, tolerance, fixing, sealing, drainage, fire/acoustic separation, inspection, disassembly or renewal.

**Evidence** — the strength of support for a proposition within its stated scope. In this project, evidence and project maturity are recorded separately.

**External maintenance envelope** — the task-specific combination of approach route, supporting ground, working position, clearance, handling space and temporary access needed to inspect, clean, repair or replace an external building element safely.

**G-01** — the project’s bounded Georgian-derived domestic architectural grammar. G-01 constrains selected aspects of architectural order without becoming Ovid’s House doctrine or a complete definition of Georgian architecture.

**Held candidate** — a plausible reusable proposition deliberately kept outside the canonical pattern language because evidence, transfer, proportionality or physical-quality questions remain unresolved.

**House Kernel** — the formal architectural/computational system within Ovid’s House. It organises the project’s doctrine, patterns, technical semantics and bounded executable work. It is not the public title of the monograph.

**Implementation family** — a particular construction or system family capable of realising a pattern. Several implementation families may satisfy the same pattern; their selection remains project-specific.

**Interface infrastructure** — a durable intermediary such as a bearing, rail, mounting frame, backplane, ground or edge condition that allows faster-changing work to attach to slower construction through a controlled relationship.

**Maintenance geography** — the deliberate spatial organisation of maintenance-intensive systems, approach routes, working spaces, isolation points and replacement paths. A component is not meaningfully accessible merely because it can be touched.

**Maturity** — what this project has actually done with a proposition or workstream: framing, development, coordination, internal validation or external validation at a stated scope. Maturity does not measure the general truth of the proposition.

**Metamorph Compiler** — the human-facing name for the bounded executable machinery within House Kernel. It represents and checks selected declared relationships; it does not compile an entire physically or legally adequate house.

**Occurrence** — one use of a pattern in one project. A Reference House occurrence demonstrates project coordination only; it does not validate the pattern generally.

**Ovidian frame** — the project’s conceptual account of permanence through change: architecture can remain enduring not by resisting every transformation, but by giving necessary transformations deliberate places to occur.

**Passive-first** — a design hierarchy in which form, envelope, orientation, shading, mass, controllable openings, drainage and gravity do useful work before active correction is added. It does not mean passive-only; measured performance and health outrank ideological simplicity.

**Pattern** — a recurring architectural response to a recurring context and set of forces, specific enough to shape design but general enough to admit materially different implementations. Patterns sit below doctrine and strategies and above project-specific occurrences.

**Permanent fabric** — the slower building layers intended not to be consumed at the renewal rate of services, fit-out or ordinary replaceable components. Permanent describes an intended lifespan relationship, not immortality.

**Physical Service Index** — a small durable in-building record that identifies critical service routes, isolations and system relationships so that essential knowledge survives the loss of digital records.

**Reference House** — one coordinated worked interpretation of Ovid’s House. It combines the doctrine, selected patterns, G-01, one courtyard morphology, one domestic programme and project-specific technical choices. It is an integration test, not proof of the general project.

**Replaceable system** — a service, plant item, control, finish, fitting or other component whose useful life is materially shorter than the slower fabric around it and whose renewal should therefore be anticipated.

**Repose** — the domestic condition sought when ordinary spaces minimise unnecessary vigilance through physical comfort, spatial comprehensibility, privacy and retreat, intelligible local control and perceptual settlement. Repose is not a claim that one historical style is neurologically privileged.

**Serviceability** — the ability to inspect, isolate, maintain, repair, remove and replace a system with disruption proportionate to the task. Ovid’s House does not seek maximum access; serviceability must justify its cost in space, material, complexity and carbon.

**Strategy** — a broad way of satisfying one or more principles that can be realised through materially different patterns or implementation families. Strategies are not counted as pattern nodes.

**Tolerance strategy** — the explicit account of how constructions of different achievable accuracy meet: controlling datum, expected incoming variation, adjustment capacity, permitted packers/shims or sacrificial elements, acceptance limits and remediation threshold.

**Workmanship robustness** — the ability of a design to tolerate credible construction variation and to make important errors visible and correctable rather than relying on continuous designer interpretation or site improvisation.
