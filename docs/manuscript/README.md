# Manuscript

The manuscript develops the public architectural argument for **House Systems Architecture**. It begins with the problem of designing a house for its life after completion, establishes the governing principles, then develops the architectural consequences through lifespan layers, interfaces, maintenance geography, repose, passive-first environmental design and testing.

Begin with the [Preface](preface.md) and [Governing Principles](governing-principles.md). The main argument continues through [Part I — The Proposition](part-i.md), [Part II — Architecture of the Platform](part-ii.md), [Repose](principle-08-repose.md), [External Maintenance Geography](maintenance-geography-external.md) and [Part V — Making and Testing the Platform](part-v.md). The intended book structure is recorded in [Publication Architecture](publication-architecture.md).

## Architectural specificity

Concrete architectural and construction examples are used where they make the argument clearer. Masonry, plaster, cornices, architraves, thresholds, timber, metalwork and the Reference House may therefore appear in general chapters without becoming HSA requirements.

Authority belongs to the claim, not to the example. A general proposition should survive a materially different architectural language and construction family. Where a particular form, material or system is itself required, the text should identify the narrower owner: pattern context, supported technical domain, selected grammar, Reference House, implementation family or precedent.

The Preface may state architectural preferences, and the Reference House may be strongly specific. Neither promotes those preferences into doctrine. The governing placement rule is the [Architectural Specificity Boundary](../development/architectural-specificity-boundary.md).
