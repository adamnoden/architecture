# Sources and further reading

Ovid’s House is not an academic literature review, but it sits within a substantial body of architectural, technical and cross-domain thought. This page gathers books, papers, standards and essays that are cited in the manuscript or materially illuminate its arguments.

Where a chapter uses numbered notes, those remain the evidence trail for specific claims. This is a reading list: inclusion means that a work is relevant to the project, not that Ovid’s House adopts the whole of its argument.

## Architecture, change and long life

- N. John Habraken, *Supports: An Alternative to Mass Housing*.
- Stephen Kendall & N. John Habraken, *Open Building for Architects* (2024).
- Stewart Brand, *How Buildings Learn: What Happens After They’re Built* (1994).
- Christopher Alexander, Sara Ishikawa & Murray Silverstein, *A Pattern Language: Towns, Buildings, Construction* (1977).
- David Pye, *The Nature and Art of Workmanship* (1968).
- Ignaz Strebel, “The living building: towards a geography of maintenance work”, *Social & Cultural Geography* 12(3), 2011. https://doi.org/10.1080/14649365.2011.564732

## Systems, resilience and coupling

- C. S. Holling, “Resilience and Stability of Ecological Systems”, *Annual Review of Ecology and Systematics* 4, 1973, 1–23. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.es.04.110173.000245
- Charles Perrow, *Normal Accidents: Living with High-Risk Technologies* (1984; updated ed. Princeton University Press).
- David D. Woods, “The theory of graceful extensibility: basic rules that govern adaptive systems”, *Environment Systems and Decisions* 38, 2018. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10669-018-9708-3
- Meir M. Lehman & Juan F. Ramil, “Rules and Tools for Software Evolution Planning and Management”, *Annals of Software Engineering* 11, 2001, 15–44. https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1012535017876
- Gwern Branwen, “Technology Holy Wars are Coordination Problems”, § “Bitrot” (2020). https://gwern.net/holy-war

## Maintenance, repair and stewardship

- ISO 15686-5:2017, *Buildings and constructed assets — Service life planning — Part 5: Life-cycle costing*. https://www.iso.org/standard/61148.html

This list should remain selective. A source belongs here when it is actually cited, materially shapes an argument, or provides a particularly useful route into the intellectual territory around the project. It should not become a dump of everything encountered during research.
