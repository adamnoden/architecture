# Ovid’s House — Governing Constitution

The numbered provisions remain stable because patterns, research and project records refer to them. They are not eleven claims of novelty and they do not all occupy the same conceptual level.

**Core doctrine** defines the proposition that would materially change if removed: build for time; preserve slower fabric; design interfaces; plan for failure; give maintenance geography; let permanence remain architectural; and preserve intergenerational legibility.

**Standing architectural disciplines** constrain how that doctrine is allowed to become a house: ordinary parts and understandable assembly; repose; passive-first environmental design; and the architectural resolution of technology.

The distinction matters. A standing discipline may be excellent established practice rather than an invention of Ovid’s House. It remains here because removing it would permit the long-life agenda to produce technically coherent but architecturally, environmentally or practically inferior buildings.

## Governing constraints

The constitution operates within non-negotiable requirements for life safety, legal compliance, structural integrity, health and environmental performance.

Longevity does not override whole-life cost, carbon, building physics or construction risk.

**Serviceability is pursued proportionately.** Permanent provision should reflect the frequency, consequence and retrofit difficulty of the future event it is intended to support.

---

## 1. Build for time — core doctrine

**Design the house as a building that will be maintained, repaired, altered and renewed repeatedly, not as an object frozen at practical completion.**

The relevant unit of design is not only the initial component but the maintenance, repair, replacement and adaptation that follow it. Cheap passive provision for foreseeable change is valuable; expensive provision for speculative futures must justify itself.

Foreseeable change includes change in occupants as well as systems. Treat foreseeable variation in bodies, capability and ordinary temporary states as normal design input rather than a late accessibility check; do not design around a fictional average person. Where a modest early decision can preserve ordinary use through reduced mobility, ageing, injury or care, test that option by the same proportionality rule. Preserve durable enabling capacity where it earns itself, but keep specialist equipment demand-led unless the brief requires it.

## 2. Preserve the permanent fabric — core doctrine

**Do not use long-lived fabric as the routine distribution or attachment zone for shorter-lived systems. Where services, fittings or replaceable assemblies must cross or attach to permanent construction, make those interfaces sparse, deliberate, concentrated where practical and documented.**

Mechanical fasteners are not the problem. Reversible screws, bolts and clips are often desirable. The objective is to stop slower layers being repeatedly perforated, chased or consumed at the renewal rate of faster ones.

## 3. Design the interface — core doctrine

**Treat junctions between systems, materials and lifespan layers as designed building elements.**

Required load paths and restraint should be deterministic; harmless movement should have bounded places to occur. Where useful, separate support, restraint, movement, sealing, protection and finish rather than asking one brittle junction to perform all of them.

An interface should manage the functions that actually apply: movement, construction tolerance, fixing, sealing, drainage, acoustic or fire separation, inspection, disassembly and renewal. Where constructions of different achievable accuracy meet, define the controlling datum, expected incoming variation, adjustment capacity and remediation threshold. The visual expression may be quiet; the technical relationship should not be accidental.

## 4. Make foreseeable failure detectable, containable and repairable — core doctrine

**Design credible failures to reveal themselves early, remain safely managed and locally containable where practical, and be reachable for repair before they consume long-lived fabric.**

Ask how the system fails, what that failure touches, how it is detected and where it is stopped.

## 5. Give maintenance a geography — core doctrine

**Organise maintenance-intensive systems, working spaces, access routes, supporting ground and replacement paths as a coherent spatial topology established during design.**

Access is more than a hatch or nominal clearance. A person needs an approach route, a safe working position, handling space and, where components are replaced, a credible withdrawal and delivery path.

The same rule applies outside the weather envelope. Façade and roof geometry, the ground plane, landscape, courtyards and routes from the site entrance should preserve ordinary safe means of inspection, cleaning, repair and replacement where the actual site permits it. A component is not meaningfully accessible merely because it can be reached.

## 6. Prefer ordinary parts in extraordinary arrangements — standing delivery discipline

**Prefer widely understood components, standard interfaces and ordinary fabrication methods; concentrate invention in the architectural arrangement and at interfaces that genuinely require it.**

Test for understandability, breadth of supply or remanufacturability, ordinary tools and interfaces, and independence from proprietary software, consumables or unique mechanisms.

Repeated construction should, where practical, be intelligible to an ordinary competent trade without continuous designer interpretation. Additional prototypes, installer trials, manufacturing controls or special tolerance procedures are justified only where the selected non-standard work or its consequences earn them; conventional construction should remain conventional.

The shorter-lived and more replaceable a layer is, the stronger the presumption for controlled manufacture and reversible site assembly. This remains a presumption, not a rule: site fabrication is appropriate where it gives the better whole-life result. Skilled site craft should be spent where judgement and workmanship create architectural value, not merely to rescue unresolved interfaces.

## 7. Let permanence be architectural — core doctrine

**Concentrate adaptability in layers that benefit from change; allow rooms, axes, stairs, structure and other defining architectural relationships to be deliberately enduring.**

Functional longevity does not require a generic shell. Well-proportioned rooms, useful dimensions, good light, generous circulation and compatible services can support many lives without making the architecture physically indeterminate.

Permanence carries the same burden of proof as adaptability: a permanent architectural relationship should earn the future constraints it creates rather than being protected solely because the architect likes it.

## 8. Design for repose — standing occupation criterion

**Treat the house as a place of repeated occupation and recovery. Its ordinary spaces should minimise unnecessary vigilance by being physically comfortable, spatially comprehensible, capable of privacy and retreat, locally controllable and perceptually settled.**

Repeated bodily or cognitive compensation can itself be a form of vigilance. Judge ordinary routes, controls and interfaces as complete actions in the states in which they are actually used rather than as isolated dimensions. Where recurring use demands avoidable contortion, choreography, force, foresight or careful workaround, test the building-side cause before normalising the compensation. The response remains proportional: trivial, rare or technically necessary inconvenience does not automatically justify permanent area, mechanism or complexity.

Repose does not require visual austerity. A room can be ornamented, strongly characterised and rich in ordinary possessions without becoming exhausting. The useful distinction is between richness and confusion.

Thermal conditions, air quality, acoustics, daylight, glare, privacy and effective local control carry stronger evidential weight than aesthetic inferences about structural expression.

Where structure is strongly expressed, the apparent relationship of support, mass and equilibrium may still be used as an **architectural review prompt**. Human perception is sensitive to physical stability, but the claim that one visual account of equilibrium improves long-duration domestic comfort remains a project hypothesis. Apparent precariousness is therefore neither prohibited nor treated as a health claim; it should simply be intentional where it dominates a principal room.

Occupants should have intelligible means to regulate privacy and ordinary environmental conditions such as shade, light, ventilation, temperature and acoustic separation where relevant. The house should not require continuous attention simply to remain comfortable.

## 9. Let passive architecture do the first work — standing environmental discipline

**Use form, envelope, orientation, shading, mass, controllable openings, drainage and gravity to reduce demands on active systems before adding mechanical or electronic correction.**

“First” does not mean “only”. Measured performance, health and resilience outrank ideological simplicity. Mechanical systems are the correct answer when the building, climate or use requires them.

A long-lived real project must also test permanent geometry against a reasonable range of **future climate and hazard conditions appropriate to its site**. Orientation, opening distribution, shading opportunity, purge paths, roof/drainage capacity, landscape and envelope choices can be expensive to change later. Do not select one speculative future-weather file as doctrine; state the scenarios, uncertainty and adaptation consequences, and allow the architecture to rewind where a robust passive response cannot be achieved proportionately.

## 10. Make the building legible across generations — core doctrine

**Preserve enough information—in records and, for critical systems, in the building itself—that future owners and trades can understand, isolate, maintain and alter it safely.**

The strongest form of legibility is physical: coherent service geography, discoverable isolation, ordinary fixings and interfaces that can be understood by inspection. Records should make that work faster and safer, not be the only thing making it possible.

A building passport, digital model and change-control history are valuable stewardship tools, but they may be lost or become stale. A restrained amount of critical information should survive the loss of software, accounts, archives or the original design team.

## 11. Resolve technology as architecture — standing architectural discipline

**Technical necessities encountered by occupants should be resolved through architectural composition, material and craft rather than treated as concealed accidents or gratuitous technical display.**

The building should be tectonically honest. Concealment is legitimate; falsification is not. Architecture may cover services, fixings and movement where the concealing element is itself real and coherent. Cosmetic continuity should not substitute for unresolved construction, and materials or structural relationships should not be counterfeited merely to create an appearance.

Joints, access, wear, movement and replacement should become part of the architectural resolution where they materially affect occupied space. Bespoke architectural enclosures should, where practical, contain ordinary replaceable technical components.

## From constitution to implementation

These provisions are not construction instructions. They sit above more specific layers of the project:

**Core doctrine** — durable proposition distinctive to the project’s long-life argument.  
**Standing discipline** — an architectural, environmental or delivery constraint that prevents the doctrine degrading into a narrower technical system.  
**Strategy** — general way to satisfy the constitution.  
**Pattern** — reusable architectural response with stated trade-offs and evidence.  
**Reference implementation** — one project-specific choice.

A pattern or implementation can fail, be rejected or prove disproportionate without invalidating the constitutional provision it was intended to serve.
