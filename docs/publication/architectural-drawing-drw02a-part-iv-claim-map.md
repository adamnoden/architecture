# DRW-02A — Part IV architectural claim-to-view map

**Programme:** DRW — Architectural Drawing Programme

**Status:** DONE pending branch validation  
**Issue:** #154  
**Source claim universe:** `docs/manuscript/part-iv.md`

## 1. Purpose

Map every material architectural claim in Part IV to the **minimum architectural drawing or analytical reading capable of making that claim inspectable** before DRW-02 freezes the actual drawing register.

This packet does not freeze the final view list. It deliberately precedes:

- DRW-02B — Parts I–III drawing-need audit;
- DRW-02C — detail-authority audit;
- DRW-02D — final `ESSENTIAL / SUPPORTING / OPTIONAL / DELETE` view register, source authority, scale and page class;
- DRW-02E — plate sequence and book rhythm.

It also does not harden Reference House geometry, implement the renderer, choose exact section cuts, assign page classes or replace current publication figures.

The governing rule is:

> **A Part IV claim either earns a minimum proving view, remains explicitly prose-bounded, or is prevented from borrowing authority from a drawing that cannot honestly prove it.**

## 2. Method and terminology

Part IV is treated as the claim universe. Closely linked sentences are grouped only where they make one architectural assertion and require the same evidence.

Each row is assigned one of three proof dispositions:

- **drawing required** — the claim is geometric, spatial, sectional, compositional or route-dependent and should be inspectable in architectural drawing;
- **drawing supports** — the drawing can make the proposition intelligible but cannot by itself prove the experiential, historical, performance or value judgement;
- **do not draw as proof** — the text is an authority boundary, unresolved technical obligation, site contingency, regulatory/performance claim or subjective judgement that a drawing must not accidentally upgrade into certainty.

Row numbers below are audit coordinates only. They are not publication identifiers and must not become reader-facing figure numbers.

## 3. Provisional minimum-view vocabulary

These names describe the minimum representational instruments exposed by the claim audit. They are **not yet the frozen DRW-02D register**.

### Base orthographic substrate

- ground-floor plan;
- first-floor plan;
- principal longitudinal section through the arrival / court / garden order;
- principal front elevation;
- roof / drainage plan;
- synthetic setting / type plan that is explicitly not a real site plan.

### Candidate secondary orthographic views exposed by specific prose claims

- garden elevation;
- service-side elevation;
- west elevation;
- courtyard elevation / section material;
- service / transverse section.

These are not pre-authorised merely because the manuscript currently mentions those faces. DRW-02D may instead decide that a low-value claim should be softened or removed rather than purchasing a whole extra view.

### Analytical readings over canonical geometry

- structural / span overlay;
- service / maintenance-geography overlay;
- external-maintenance / setting overlay;
- roof-water / failure overlay;
- entrance-storey future-adaptation overlay.

Analytical readings reuse the orthographic substrate. They do not get separately invented building geometry.

### Conditional details

- permanent opening / replaceable frame detail;
- service-wall / riser / dry-side-access detail;
- wall / floor / movement-junction detail only if DRW-02C finds adequate authority.

No detail is authorised by this packet. DRW-02C must remove any detail that would manufacture technical certainty.

## 5. Deduplicated minimum Part IV substrate

The claim map collapses to a relatively small architectural substrate. The important distinction is between **base geometry**, **analytical readings of that geometry**, and **extra views whose existence is driven by narrow prose claims**.

### 5.1 Base views strongly earned by multiple independent claims

The following are repeatedly required and should enter DRW-02D with a strong presumption of retention:

1. **Ground-floor plan** — programme, court morphology, arrival, service flank, maintenance approach and future adaptation substrate.
2. **First-floor plan** — bedroom/landing/gallery order, bathroom/service alignment and rejected en-suite consequence.
3. **Principal longitudinal section** — sectional hierarchy, court depth, front-to-court-to-garden relation, floor zone and key vertical datums.
4. **Principal front elevation** — four-bay order, off-centre entrance, ranked openings and stair-window consequence.
5. **Roof/drainage plan** — outward falls, watersheds, external collection, downpipes and water-failure substrate.
6. **Synthetic setting/type plan** — detached surrogate condition, external maintenance strips, service-side replacement approach and site contingency.

These six views are the minimum conventional architectural substrate exposed by Part IV itself.

### 5.2 Analytical readings strongly earned by Part IV

These reuse the base geometry rather than redraw it:

- **structural/span overlay** — short-span ring, edge beams and opening interruptions;
- **service/maintenance overlay** — service flank, wet concentration, vertical zone, plant/utility geography and protected principal rooms;
- **external-maintenance/setting overlay** — façade/roof/courtyard working positions, plant withdrawal and landscape constraints;
- **roof-water/failure overlay** — courtyard collector/overflow, failure visibility and wet-consequence geography;
- **entrance-storey future-adaptation overlay** — family-room sleeping option, step-free route and potential shower geography.

The adaptation reading is materially different from the service overlay because its reader task is human occupation under loss of stair use, not service topology. It should nevertheless reuse the same ground-plan geometry.

### 5.3 Secondary orthographic proof burdens created by current prose

Part IV currently makes claims that cannot honestly be proved by the six base views alone:

- **garden elevation** — three-field garden order and disciplined rather than wall-of-glass rear expression;
- **service-side elevation** — grouped technical expression / asymmetry and potentially plant replacement opening;
- **courtyard elevation/section material** — internal court order and local material dialect;
- **service/transverse section** — vertical service zone, bathroom/dry-side access and ventilation/service stacking;
- **west elevation** — only the narrow claim that the west side remains quiet and room-owned.

The first four have multiple potential reader tasks and may earn supporting status. The west elevation currently has a poor claim-to-view ratio. DRW-02D should not authorise it automatically merely to defend one sentence; either combine it efficiently with a broader elevation family or reconsider the prose claim.

### 5.4 Conditional details exposed by Part IV

Part IV creates three detail-level proof demands:

1. permanent opening / replaceable frame;
2. service-wall / riser / dry-side access;
3. layered wall / floor-edge / movement-junction families.

Only the first two align with already-anticipated high-value DRW-11 detail families. The third group contains explicitly unresolved technical questions and is therefore **not authorised by this map**. DRW-02C must fail closed where technical authority is insufficient.

## 6. What Part IV does **not** require

### 6.1 No unique claim requires the sectional axonometric

Every Part IV architectural claim can be proved or bounded using orthographic views, analytical overlays and conditional details.

Therefore the future sectional axonometric has **no unique claim-proof obligation** at DRW-02A.

This is not a decision to delete DRW-09. It means the axonometric must later earn its place as a synthesis / comprehension instrument rather than being classified `ESSENTIAL` simply because it is visually powerful.

That distinction protects the programme from designing the hero image first and then inventing a justification for it.

### 6.2 No generic service schematic is required

The Part IV service claims are spatial. Their minimum instrument is an analytical reading over canonical plans/section, not another independently authored block diagram.

### 6.3 No real site plan is authorised

The house has no commissioned site. The setting/type drawing must remain visibly synthetic and should avoid conventional cues that would make contingent orientation, boundaries, drainage or neighbours appear surveyed/fixed.

### 6.4 No drawing may prove unresolved performance

Part IV explicitly withholds authority for structural capacity, detailed building physics, regulatory compliance, plant sizing, hydraulic sizing, technical junction performance and site-specific environmental outcomes.

The future drawing system must therefore allow architectural specificity without visual overclaim. A detailed-looking line is not permission to manufacture engineering certainty.

## 8. Key editorial pressure exposed by the map

DRW-02A reveals a useful discipline problem in the manuscript itself.

Specific architectural prose is not free. A sentence such as “the service side groups technical expression into a controlled middle zone” creates a legitimate expectation that the reader can inspect that side. The same applies to the garden-front field, west-side quietness and courtyard order.

DRW-02D should therefore use a two-way test:

> **Does the claim earn the view, or does the view reveal that the claim is too minor to keep asserting?**

The programme should not accumulate elevations solely because prose happened to mention every façade. Nor should the prose retain inspectable geometric claims that the publication then declines to show.
