# DRW-01A — bounded architectural-drawing precedent sample

**Programme:** DRW — Architectural Drawing Programme  
**Packet:** DRW-01A  
**Status:** DONE pending branch validation  
**Issue:** #154

## 1. Purpose

Freeze a small precedent sample sufficient to derive the Ovid’s House drawing doctrine without turning DRW into a style-shopping exercise.

The sample is organised by **representational job**, not by admiration. Each family must answer a distinct question that later DRW packets actually face.

The governing rule is:

> **Borrow conventions that make architecture more inspectable. Do not borrow another architect’s visual personality.**

The sample is now bounded at five precedent families. Reopening it requires a later packet to expose a representational problem that none of these families can answer.

## 2. Precedent family A — restrained professional monograph orthographics

### Source sample

**Caruso St John Architects** — selected project drawing sets:

- Private House, Lincolnshire  
  https://carusostjohn.com/projects/house-lincolnshire/
- Brick House  
  https://carusostjohn.com/projects/brick-house/
- Private House, North London  
  https://carusostjohn.com/projects/private-house-north-london/
- Studio House, North London  
  https://carusostjohn.com/projects/studio-house/

The sample is deliberately domestic / building-scale rather than a survey of the whole practice. Across it, the published drawing families repeatedly use ordinary architectural instruments: site plan, floor plans, sections, elevations and selected details. The Brick House set, for example, moves from site and floor plans through several sections to glazing details; the Lincolnshire house publishes site, ground/first plans, elevations, sections and an external-wall detail.

### Problem it solves for DRW

What should the **primary visual grammar of the Reference House** look like before any analytical overlay is added?

### Transferable conventions

- let plan, section and elevation carry the building before explanatory diagrams carry the argument;
- use familiar architectural view families rather than inventing a project dialect for ordinary orthographics;
- publish only the details that materially extend understanding of the building;
- keep the drawing visually quiet enough that geometry and spatial order dominate;
- use a related set of orthographic views so the reader can cross-register one object rather than decode unrelated graphics;
- allow editorial restraint to come from selection, hierarchy and composition rather than from removing architectural information.

### Do not copy

- Caruso St John office-specific plotting, typography or line weights as style tokens;
- construction-level certainty merely because the precedent drawings possess it;
- project-specific material conventions unsupported by Ovid’s House;
- the exact number of views per project.

### DRW use

**Primary precedent for DRW-03 through DRW-08 orthographic drawings and DRW-02 view-register discipline.**

## 3. Precedent family B — small-format drawing monograph / source-to-detail continuity

### Source sample

**Glenn Murcutt — _Thinking Drawing / Working Drawing_**, TOTO Publishing, 2008.  
Official record: https://info.jp.toto.com/publishing/detail/A0294_e.htm

The book is **250 × 190 mm, modified B5, 248 pages** and presents thirteen works through drawings. TOTO describes the material as spanning early sketches through construction-stage detail drawings. The associated Gallery MA material frames drawing as part of Murcutt’s process of architectural discovery rather than as post-rationalised illustration.  
Exhibition record: https://info.jp.toto.com/gallerma/ex080612/index_e.htm

### Problem it solves for DRW

Can a serious architecture-first drawing publication remain legible and useful at approximately the smaller physical territory already authorised for PR-02?

### Transferable conventions

- judge line, type, hatch and annotation at **physical book scale**, not at SVG zoom scale;
- treat the drawing set as a continuous architectural information system from broad situation to fine assembly, while preserving the authority boundary of each scale;
- let drawings do intellectual work rather than merely illustrate prose after decisions are complete;
- make selective density acceptable when hierarchy remains clear;
- prove the final grammar at a B5-territory reduction, not only on the current A4 working PDF.

### Do not copy

- hand-drawn appearance as a proxy for authenticity;
- Murcutt’s personal notation, lettering or graphic manner;
- construction details where Ovid’s House source has not earned construction-detail authority;
- an assumption that every design process should be visible in the resolved publication.

### DRW use

**Primary physical-scale precedent for DRW-01B–01E and later print proofs; secondary precedent for source-to-drawing continuity.**

## 4. Precedent family C — editorial construction/detail grammar

### Source sample

**DETAIL magazine / DETAIL project documentation.**  
https://www.detail.de/en/magazine

DETAIL states that its issues focus on building-construction themes with project documentation and detail drawings. Its project archive explicitly distinguishes project documentation with detail drawings from projects without them.

### Problem it solves for DRW

How should a later bounded Ovid’s House detail family become technically dense without turning into a collection of inconsistent project sketches or decorative exploded diagrams?

### Transferable conventions

- use a consistent editorial technical grammar across different assemblies;
- make line hierarchy, material notation, annotation and scale carry information rather than atmosphere;
- use hatching/material distinction only where it helps the reader discriminate constructional relationships;
- make the scale and cut condition legible immediately;
- allow a detail page to be visually dense while keeping its semantics economical and conventional.

### Do not copy

- the implication that Ovid’s House currently owns construction-document certainty;
- build-ups, dimensions, specifications or material interfaces not present in controlled source/evidence;
- publication-specific graphic conventions merely because DETAIL uses them;
- technical density as a virtue in itself.

### DRW use

**Primary precedent for DRW-11 bounded details; informs DRW-01B line/poche/hatch discipline.**

## 5. Precedent family D — sectional perspective / synthesis drawing

### Source sample

**Atelier Bow-Wow — _Graphic Anatomy Atelier Bow-Wow_**, TOTO Publishing, 2007.  
https://www-s.jp.toto.com/publishing/detail/A0278_e.htm

TOTO describes the book as presenting 29 projects through detailed **sectional perspective drawings**. The format is 260 × 260 mm, 156 pages.

### Problem it solves for DRW

How can one drawing give a reader a strong three-dimensional understanding of the Reference House while remaining analytical architecture rather than photoreal rendering?

### Transferable conventions

- combine section, depth and visible building components in one spatially legible synthesis view;
- allow structure, enclosure, occupation and technical organisation to coexist where they are all source-supported;
- use one carefully chosen sectional cut to reveal relationships that are laborious to reconstruct from separate orthographics;
- reward close reading without using atmosphere as a substitute for information.

### Do not copy

- sectional perspective as the default for every argument;
- illustrative density for its own sake;
- people, furniture, vegetation or incidentals that the Reference House does not place with sufficient authority;
- a hand-illustrated aesthetic as project branding;
- any three-dimensional completion that silently fills gaps in source geometry.

### DRW use

**Primary precedent for the single earned DRW-09 sectional axonometric / synthesis plate.**

## 6. Precedent family E — projected section and analytical overlay

### Source sample

**Drawing Matter — “Projected Sections” by Laurent Stalder.**  
https://drawingmatter.org/stalder-projected-sections/

The essay describes the perspectival / axonometric section as a significant post-war British residential design and representation tool, citing work by Lasdun, the Smithsons, Neave Brown, Hodgkinson and Chamberlin, Powell & Bon.

**Cedric Price — Fun Palace archive, Canadian Centre for Architecture.**  
https://www.cca.qc.ca/en/archives/380477/cedric-price-fonds/396839/projects/399301/fun-palace-project

The CCA archive records site and circulation plans, diagrammatic sections, structural diagrams, service charts, axonometrics, service-tower drawings and publication drawings, including overlays of programmatic or servicing information on architectural/structural material.

### Problem it solves for DRW

How should structure, services, water/failure and maintenance arguments remain visibly attached to the architecture rather than reverting to detached flowcharts?

### Transferable conventions

- preserve an architectural substrate when adding analytical information;
- choose plan, section or axonometric according to the spatial question rather than according to graphic novelty;
- overlay routes, zones, structure or services on geometry that the reader can cross-register with the primary drawing set;
- separate analytical layers graphically without pretending they are separate buildings;
- use projected/axonometric section when the analytical relationship genuinely spans plan and vertical order.

### Do not copy

- Cedric Price’s historical graphic manner or annotation density;
- colour as the only carrier of layer identity;
- a diagrammatic language that requires archive-specific familiarity;
- analytical complexity unrelated to a frozen reader question;
- structural/service content not owned by the controlled Reference House state.

### DRW use

**Primary precedent family for DRW-10 analytical overlays; secondary precedent for DRW-09 synthesis.**

## 7. Cross-precedent rules now frozen

The five families agree strongly enough to freeze the following propositions before numerical style tokens are chosen in DRW-01B.

### 7.1 Architecture first

For a worked building, establish the object through ordinary architectural drawings before asking analytical overlays to explain it.

**Reference House reading order:**

`plan / section / elevation / roof → synthesis where useful → analytical overlays → bounded details`

This does not apply to inherently abstract doctrine figures retained by DRW-00D.

### 7.2 One geometry, multiple readings

Primary orthographics, synthesis views and analytical overlays may differ in what they emphasise, but they should project one controlled architectural state wherever the project owns that state.

### 7.3 Conventional view grammar, distinctive editorial discipline

Plans should look recognisably like plans; sections like sections; elevations like elevations; details like details. Ovid’s House identity should come from restraint, selection, proportion, page composition and consistent hierarchy — not from replacing ordinary architectural notation with bespoke symbols.

### 7.4 Physical scale governs graphics

The target is a sewn portrait book in B5 territory, even while automation currently proves A4. A line weight or label that only works on screen is invalid. DRW-01 must define paper-space tokens and prove them at conservative B5-territory reduction.

### 7.5 Density must be earned by information

A technically dense detail or synthesis view is legitimate when every layer carries owned architectural information. Density introduced merely to look “architectural” is rejected.

### 7.6 Analytical drawings stay architectural

Structure, services, water and maintenance should normally be drawn **on or through the building**, not beside a rectangle that stands in for it.

### 7.7 Drawing type follows authority

Professional-looking notation cannot promote provisional or absent source facts. When a view requires unsupported geometry, the renderer must refuse, mark uncertainty, or force an upstream source decision under the packet rules.

## 8. Explicitly rejected primary languages

The following do not belong to the DRW primary grammar:

- raw BIM / construction-document plots;
- presentation-deck “thought leadership” graphics as substitutes for architecture;
- post-digital collage and textured atmosphere;
- photoreal rendering;
- generic competition-board composition;
- ornamental historical drafting manner;
- hand-drawn affect used as authenticity theatre;
- exploded axonometrics used reflexively where plan/section is clearer;
- custom symbols where normal architectural conventions already solve the problem.

Any one of these may appear later only if a specific frozen reader task proves it superior to the default architectural instrument.
