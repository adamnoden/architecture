# DRW-02B — Parts I–III architectural drawing-need audit

## 1. Purpose

Audit the actual Working Edition content before Part IV and determine which claims, if any, genuinely require **architectural drawings** rather than prose, evidence, retained abstract diagrams or later Reference House occurrences.

The packet follows DRW-02A, which mapped Part IV claims to their minimum proving views. DRW-02B asks the complementary question:

> **What architectural drawing burden is created before the book reaches the worked house?**

The answer is intentionally allowed to be small. An architectural-looking figure is not automatically a better explanation of general doctrine.

This packet does **not**:

- implement or redraw a figure;
- authorise a construction detail;
- assign final `ESSENTIAL / SUPPORTING / OPTIONAL / DELETE` status;
- assign scale or page class;
- sequence plates;
- harden Reference House source geometry;
- build renderer capability;
- reopen the DRW-01 drawing doctrine.

Those decisions remain with DRW-02C→02E and the later vertical slices.

## 3. Decision rule

Each early-book claim family receives one of four dispositions.

### `PROSE / EVIDENCE`

The claim is conceptual, historical, methodological, experiential or performance-bounded. A drawing would add apparent specificity without increasing truthful architectural proof.

### `ABSTRACT FIGURE`

The reader task is relational, causal or organisational rather than building-specific. The existing retained diagram is the correct representational instrument.

### `REFERENCE-HOUSE OCCURRENCE`

The relationship is genuinely spatial, but a generic architectural drawing would falsely imply one universal geometry. The first proper architectural drawing should therefore be the worked occurrence in Part IV / later DRW overlays.

### `DETAIL CANDIDATE — 02C AUTHORITY REQUIRED`

The claim becomes materially clearer through an architectural/assembly detail, but the book may show that detail only if DRW-02C finds enough current source, prototype or conventional authority to avoid manufacturing certainty.

A candidate is not an authorisation.

## 4. Principal conclusion

**Parts I–III earn no additional whole-building plan, elevation or section.**

The book should cross a deliberate representational threshold at Part IV:

- Parts I–III establish proposition, doctrine, pattern relationships and evidence boundaries;
- Part IV is where those claims first become one specific house that can honestly be drawn in plan, section and elevation.

Pulling Reference House orthographics forward merely to decorate earlier doctrine would blur that boundary. Inventing generic houses for Parts I–III would be worse: the drawings would look architecturally authoritative while owning no project-specific geometry.

The early book does, however, expose a **small bounded detail queue** for DRW-02C. Those details are different from whole-building views because the relevant claim exists at an interface or assembly scale.

## 5. Part I audit

### 5.1 The house after completion

Claims about maintenance, replacement, obsolescence, coupling and proportional serviceability are temporal and causal.

**Disposition:** `PROSE / EVIDENCE`.

A generic cutaway house showing ageing components would be illustrative rather than probative and would compete with the stronger selective-permanence abstraction already present later in Part I.

### 5.2 Buildings change at different speeds

The key claim is not a literal layer stack; it is that shorter-lived systems should not consume slower layers unnecessarily.

**Disposition:** `ABSTRACT FIGURE` through the retained selective-permanence/platform figure.

A construction section would collapse a general lifecycle argument into one material implementation.

### 5.3 Selective permanence

The claim concerns where permanence is valuable and where change is probable, including the distinction between architectural identity and technical renewal.

**Disposition:** `ABSTRACT FIGURE` + `PROSE / EVIDENCE`.

No generic adaptable-house plan is justified. The worked Part IV plans will later show one occurrence without pretending to define the doctrine.

### 5.4 Repose

Repose concerns comfort, privacy, intelligible control, spatial comprehensibility and a bounded review of structural expression.

**Disposition:** `PROSE / EVIDENCE`, with later Part IV plans/sections as worked support only.

Do **not** add a generic “repose plan”, privacy gradient diagram, neuroscience infographic or equilibrium sketch merely to make the chapter visual. Those would risk converting an explicitly bounded architectural criterion into a pseudo-metric.

### 5.5 Architectural platform

The four-part relationship between permanent fabric, replaceable systems, designed interfaces and maintenance geography is conceptual.

**Disposition:** `ABSTRACT FIGURE` through the retained selective-permanence/platform figure.

The platform does not require one canonical wall section or exploded axonometric.

### 5.6 Governing constitution

The eleven provisions are constitutional constraints, not eleven drawable objects.

**Disposition:** `PROSE / EVIDENCE`.

Their architectural consequences are tested in patterns and the Reference House rather than illustrated one-by-one.

### Part I result

- new whole-building architectural views: **0**;
- new unconditional details: **0**;
- retained abstract figures doing necessary work: **1** principal figure;
- later Part IV orthographics provide worked architectural evidence without being duplicated here.

## 6. Part II audit

Part II is closer to physical construction and therefore exposes the strongest early-book detail candidates. It still does not earn generic whole-building orthographics.

### 6.1 Designed interface

The chapter separates support, restraint, movement, boundary continuity, tolerance, access and replacement.

**Disposition:** `ABSTRACT FIGURE` for the functional decomposition; `DETAIL CANDIDATE — 02C AUTHORITY REQUIRED` for any physical example.

The retained designed-interface figure is correct because no single junction should pretend to embody every interface duty.

A later physical detail may complement it, but should not replace the abstract figure merely because a detail looks more architectural.

### 6.2 Window as model interface

The manuscript explicitly makes the window concrete as an enduring opening with a replaceable assembly and requires the replacement sequence to be drawn before construction.

**Disposition:** **strong detail candidate**.

Candidate instrument:

- permanent opening / replaceable frame jamb-head-sill family, or one bounded representative section if that is all current authority supports.

Guardrail:

- DRW-02C must verify source/prototype/building-physics authority;
- the drawing must not imply validated air, water, thermal, fire, acoustic or product performance merely because it resembles a construction detail.

This is the strongest early-book architectural-detail need.

### 6.3 Movement / slip junction and workmanship robustness

The manuscript argues that expected differential movement, tolerance and adjustment need deliberate interfaces.

**Disposition:** `DETAIL CANDIDATE — 02C AUTHORITY REQUIRED`, but **not a required standalone drawing yet**.

A generic brass slip joint would be actively misleading because the pattern itself states that quiet conventional overlaps, gaps and replaceable seals may be better. The current Reference House wall/ceiling movement family is also explicitly unresolved.

If 02C finds no sufficiently authoritative junction, prose plus the retained functional interface diagram is the correct publication state.

### 6.4 Failure architecture

The claim is a causal sequence: defence → consequence → detection/isolation → access/recovery.

**Disposition:** `ABSTRACT FIGURE` through the retained failure-architecture figure.

A generic leaking-bathroom section would over-specialise the principle. Worked water/failure geometry belongs later on canonical Reference House plan/roof/section substrate.

### 6.5 Internal maintenance geography

The four geometries—approach, working space, disconnection and withdrawal—are transferable task logic.

**Disposition:** `ABSTRACT FIGURE` through the retained maintenance-geography route.

The chapter's instruction to draw replacement geometry is a project workflow requirement, not a demand for one universal publication plan.

Worked spatial proof belongs in Part IV service/maintenance overlays.

### 6.6 External maintenance geography

Ground, landscape, façade, courtyard and roof access are genuinely architectural and site-spatial.

**Disposition:** `REFERENCE-HOUSE OCCURRENCE` rather than an invented generic site plan/section.

The existing abstract maintenance-route figure states the transferable logic. DRW-02A has already earned a synthetic setting/type plan plus external-maintenance overlay for the Reference House. Those later drawings are the proper worked proof.

No second generic scaffold/house drawing is justified in Part II.

### 6.7 Environmental resilience

Passive-first form, shading, purge, local control, future climate and source capture are performance-bounded and site/climate dependent.

**Disposition:** `PROSE / EVIDENCE` + later worked occurrence.

Do not add generic sun arrows, prevailing-wind graphics or “passive house” sections that imply site, orientation or simulation authority the doctrine does not own.

Specific source-capture relationships can be explained by their pattern pages; no architectural drawing is required unless a later implementation detail earns itself separately.

### 6.8 Boundaries

Boundary debt is cross-disciplinary: fire, smoke, sound, air, vapour, water, thermal continuity, pests, structure and security.

**Disposition:** `PROSE / EVIDENCE` with **conditional detail support only**.

A single generic section cannot truthfully prove all boundary duties. Any later detail must state exactly which boundaries it owns and which remain evidence obligations.

### 6.9 Replaceable interior

The chapter contains several potentially drawable relationships:

- permanent wall → attachment plane → replaceable lining/joinery;
- dry-side service access to high-service rooms;
- removable floor access where proportionate;
- kitchen as fit-out;
- permanent opening / renewable doors and windows.

**Disposition:** mixed.

- house-wide replaceable-interior axonometric: **not required**;
- generic demountable-room section: **not required**;
- service-wall/riser or dry-side-access family: **strong detail candidate**;
- controlled attachment plane: **supporting detail candidate**, only if physical authority exists;
- removable lining / selective floor access: **do not draw as resolved** while they remain held candidates or unresolved physical-quality questions.

The text explicitly allows excellent conventional walls, ceilings and bonded wet finishes where they perform better. A polished generic demountable-interior drawing would falsely make experimentation look like doctrine.

### 6.10 Legibility and stewardship

As-built plans/sections, route records, isolation schedules and physical service information are operating records rather than a new publication architecture.

**Disposition:** `PROSE / EVIDENCE`.

A sample Physical Service Index may later be valuable as information-design evidence, but it is **not an architectural drawing need for DRW-02**. It should not enter the DRW drawing register merely because it contains lines and labels.

- Permanent Opening / Replaceable Window;
- Permanent Opening / Replaceable Door;
- Designed Threshold;
- Movement / Slip Junction;
- Controlled Attachment Plane;
- Designed Structural Penetration.

These claims occur at assembly/interface scale and are the main legitimate source of early-book architectural details.

**Disposition:** `DETAIL CANDIDATE — 02C AUTHORITY REQUIRED`.

Priority order for authority review:

1. **permanent opening / replaceable frame** — highest reader value and strongest explicit manuscript demand;
2. **service-wall / riser / dry-side access** — high reader value because spatial serviceability is otherwise hard to see;
3. **one further interface detail only if authority is strong** — choose among movement/tolerance, controlled attachment, threshold or structural penetration based on evidence and non-duplication.

The pattern catalogue must **not** acquire one detail per pattern by default. Similar interface patterns should share one high-value detail when one drawing can carry the reader task honestly.
