# Current sectional synthesis candidate

## Question and source

The earlier transparent anatomy was withheld because crossed lines made the
house difficult to read. It also predated the reviewed 3.5 m courtyard. This
packet asks whether an opaque cutaway can synthesise the current enclosure,
apertures, level order, stair opening and twin roof without inventing architecture
or claiming a complete building model.

`georgian/sectional-synthesis.ts` owns the represented mesh and composition.
It derives from `layout-revision.ts`, `layout-family.ts` and the nominal floor
package in `section-source.ts`. The cut is the current south courtyard edge,
retaining the north portion. The source includes the 417.5 mm nominal envelope,
actual aperture voids on retained external/courtyard faces, current first-floor
zone and stair opening, and the twin-pitch roof with its existing 200 mm overhang
and 300 mm representation zone. Nothing is recovered from the historical
technical compiler.

The mesh represents selected opaque spatial zones, not the material interiors
of every assembly. Internal partitions, joinery, floor layers/seats, roof-space
decking, foundations and selected service products are deliberately omitted.
Their absence here does not authorise deleting them from the house or its
orthographic source. The nominal floor zone is not a solid timber member or
checked structural span. Roof zone thickness is not a selected construction.

## Earned engine capability

`architectural-drawing/spatial-visibility.ts` clips explicit 3D line segments
against caller-owned convex planar faces in the existing parallel projection.
It intersects projected coverage and interpolated ray depth, unions concealed
intervals and preserves visible model-space fragments and line roles. It does
not discover surfaces, infer solids, design architecture or use mean-depth
painter ordering as an occlusion substitute. Edge-on faces have no projected
area; non-planar and non-convex occluders fail explicitly.

The house adapter removes exact internal tile faces and joins coplanar tile
edges before projection. Numerical coincidence here only cleans the authored
representation mesh; it establishes no architectural topology or engineering
connection. Cut edges retain stronger physical weight. Existing renderer and
historical proofs retain their default behaviour.

Independent asymmetric tests cover rear/front/coplanar edges, partial and
overlapping occlusion, order independence, a sloping depth plane, and invalid
faces. Current-house tests cover the cut, retained aperture heads, roof heights,
absence of floors through court/stair, hidden-edge subtraction and unchanged
geometry between physical book formats.
