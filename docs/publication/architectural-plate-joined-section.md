# Joined cavity-wall section exemplar

Owner feedback rejects separate boxed wall/floor regions and awkward roof
intersections. This successor holds the ground/first plan and adopts a ridge
between external and courtyard eaves, with 25-degree falls both inward and
outward. No flat roof, dormer or roof window is introduced.

`georgian/section-exemplar.ts` owns this coordinated section/roof successor.
Both eaves are +6800 mm; the ridge is 2100 mm from either eaves line and reaches
approximately +7779 mm. The courtyard wall is therefore lower and more sky is
visible. Storage remains low; a standing attic and upper stair are not restored.
The historical outward-only sources remain reproducible through the retained
`sections-roof-01.ts` renderer and PLT08 proof. Do not combine the old roof with
this section as a current set.

## Cut assembly and junctions

The same nominal envelope family used in the plan supplies 102.5 mm weather leaf,
160 mm cavity, 140 mm inner leaf and 15 mm finish. The cavity is white and the
leaves use differentiated restrained fills and outlines. No brick courses,
ties, insulation product or tested wall specification are invented.

Floor zones extend through the finish and nominally 75 mm into the inner leaf;
they stop before the cavity. This bearing is a coordination choice, not capacity
or a selected joist/support detail. The existing ADE rectilinear boundary tracer
joins inner-leaf, floor and partition material, eliminating internal rectangle
edges. Weather leaves remain separate across the cavity. Aperture head/sill
lines and cut glazing express openings without inventing cavity closures.

Wall tops follow the nominal roof underside instead of intersecting a separate
wall-top block. The roof is a continuous pitched region with a 300 mm vertical
coordination build zone and 200 mm nominal overhang at each eaves. These are
architectural assumptions; wall plates, ventilation, insulation continuity,
restraint, thermal bridges, fire stopping and waterproofing remain unresolved.
The 300 mm zone is not claimed as a normal-to-slope measured assembly thickness.

The first-floor ceiling remains a secondary line. Low-storage reserves at FFL
6900 are dashed rather than boxed as supported construction; decking, support
and hatch access have not been selected. Windows on the courtyard face beyond
the cut retain lighter sash strokes; cut fabric remains visually primary.

## Roof consequences and drainage

The roof plan now has a square ridge ring and eight fall directions. Collection
is required at external and courtyard eaves. Both are shown symbolically as
accessible perimeter routes. No concealed box gutter or flat terrace is admitted.
Inward collection adds maintenance and leak consequences: outlet/downpipe routes,
cleaning access, overflow away from fabric, connection to discharge and capacity
must be resolved. Courtyard surface drainage remains separately unresolved.
No courtyard downpipe location or maintenance platform is silently invented.

Front and courtyard consequence elevations reuse the existing authored facade
articulation and aperture source, changing the roof silhouette and courtyard wall
height. The full elevation family and service section must consume this accepted
exemplar before final whole-set release. No view is silently mixed into the
frozen v1 book or treated as a full technical compiler migration.
