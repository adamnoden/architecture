## 1. Question

Do the five accepted Parts I–III FLOW figures work as one restrained publication family without collapsing their different reader tasks into a visual template or reverting to presentation-deck information design?

## 2. Family under review

DRW-11E authorised five distinct reader tasks and DRW-11F-1→5 re-authored each independently:

1. **Selective permanence / architectural platform** — temporal hierarchy and the boundary at which ordinary renewal should stop.
2. **Designed interface** — functional allocation of support, restraint, movement, boundary continuity, tolerance and removal between assemblies.
3. **Failure architecture** — first defence → failure event → designed second consequence → recovery.
4. **Maintenance geography** — approach → working space → disconnection → withdrawal as four connected spatial tasks.
5. **Pattern language + generative sequence** — five editorial reading fields beside one service-topology sequence with explicit rewind.

DRW-11G does not ask whether these should look identical. It asks whether they read as members of the same book while retaining the form appropriate to each argument.

## 4. Shared family grammar

The family is coherent through a small set of physical and editorial invariants rather than a repeated diagram template:

- each reader-facing SVG has a fixed **128 × 78 mm** paper-space frame;
- principal figure labels use the proved **7.5 pt** role;
- supporting notes use the proved **6.5 pt** role;
- black-only linework carries all essential meaning;
- primary and secondary rules remain visibly hierarchical without colour;
- dashed linework is reserved for conditions such as unavoidable crossing, threshold, clearance, ghost state or rewind;
- figures remain inline `FLOW` instruments inside prose rather than dedicated plates;
- captions own qualifications that would otherwise force small explanatory prose back into the SVG;
- no figure claims architectural specificity that its abstract doctrine does not own.

This is enough common grammar. Additional homogenisation would make the family worse by replacing semantic form with house style.

## 5. Difference is intentional

The five figures use different structures because they answer different reader questions:

- **Selective permanence** uses horizontal bands because the claim is stratified by relative longevity.
- **Designed interface** uses a central schedule between two assemblies because the claim is functional allocation at a junction.
- **Failure architecture** uses a directed causal sequence because consequence and recovery are temporal/logical.
- **Maintenance geography** uses four plan-like fragments because the claim is spatial task geometry.
- **Pattern language** uses a ruled editorial register beside a causal spine because it must distinguish grouping from sequence.

The family therefore avoids the opposite failure mode: forcing every doctrine into boxes, arrows or one repeated infographic chassis merely for visual consistency.

## 6. Presentation-deck red team

The completed family was reviewed specifically for the legacy tendencies DRW-11E identified.

### Decorative cards / UI panels

**PASS.** There are no rounded card systems, drop shadows, floating dashboard panels or ornamental containers. Rectangles remain only where they carry semantic boundaries: layers, assemblies, causal states or plan-like extents.

### Giant internal headings

**PASS.** Internal headings stay in the same physical label hierarchy as the rest of the diagram. None competes with the manuscript heading or turns an inline figure into a slide.

### Colour-dependent meaning

**PASS.** None. The figures are intelligible in black linework alone.

### Dense embedded prose

**PASS.** The 11F redraws moved qualifications and explanatory sentences back to captions/prose. Internal copy is now labels, terse notes and only the minimum qualification needed to prevent misreading.

### Dashboard / systems-map grammar

**PASS.** The strongest former offender—the Part III pattern-language figure—now reads as an editorial register plus one causal spine rather than a stack of cards. The other four figures are similarly bounded by their actual explanatory task.

### False architectural authority

**PASS.** None of the figures has been upgraded into a generic plan, section, construction detail or pseudo-technical assembly. DRW-02B's representational threshold remains intact: abstract doctrine first; project-specific architectural proof in Part IV.

## 9. Relationship to Part IV

The family proof preserves DRW-02E's intended visual threshold.

Parts I–III now contain five serious, physically controlled explanatory figures, but they still read as **figures in prose**. They do not acquire scale bars, drawing titles, plate furniture, dedicated spreads or house-specific geometry merely to resemble Part IV.

Part IV therefore remains the visual climax where the publication changes register into plans, sections, elevations, roof drawings, axonometric and analytical overlays over controlled Reference House geometry.

That distinction is architectural and editorial, not merely stylistic, and it survives the redraw programme.
