## Purpose

Ovid’s House already argues for **repose**: a good home should minimise unnecessary vigilance. HFI develops the embodied and operational side of that proposition.

The programme asks:

> **How much compensatory attention, choreography, contortion or foresight does the building demand from an ordinary occupant performing ordinary actions?**

The working objective is **low-friction inhabitation**.

This is broader than statutory accessibility. It includes anthropometric variation, ageing, temporary impairment, carrying loads, furniture and appliance movement, level changes, controls, circulation geometry and the ordinary attentional burden created by badly resolved architecture.

The programme is triggered by recurring domestic conditions that are often treated as isolated annoyances:

- stair goings that demand careful foot placement;
- narrow high-traffic corridors and avoidable pinch points;
- awkward corners that complicate furniture movement;
- raised thresholds between otherwise continuous floor surfaces;
- avoidable steps between street and ground floor;
- wet-room and shower interfaces that require stepping over barriers;
- controls placed for service convenience rather than the sequence of human use;
- shower controls placed beneath the shower head they are supposed to control.

HFI tests whether these are manifestations of deeper design failures and, where justified, converts the findings into doctrine, patterns, Reference House decisions, delivery requirements and selected computational checks.

## Authority boundary

Opening HFI does **not** itself authorise:

- a new Ovid’s House principle;
- a stronger stair target;
- wider corridors;
- Reference House geometry changes;
- new compiler constraints;
- new publication claims.

The examples above are research triggers and hypotheses.

DRW is active separately. HFI must not silently change architecture merely to make drawings look better. If HFI later proves a Reference House defect, the defect must be recorded as an upstream architectural finding and reconciled explicitly with DRW.

## Working architectural formulations

Two formulations are useful enough to carry into research but are **not yet doctrine**:

> **If an ordinary repeated action requires conscious compensation for the building, first ask whether the building is wrong.**

> **Human variance is a normal design input.**

HFI-05 must either harden, qualify, replace or reject these.

## Research hypotheses

The following are deliberately provisional.

- Domestic stairs may deserve materially deeper goings and gentler geometry than legal minima.
- High-traffic circulation may deserve more spatial generosity than secondary/service routes.
- Principal routes should avoid gratuitous pinch points and awkward tight turns.
- Important internal floor transitions should normally be flush or effectively flush.
- A credible step-free or near-step-free route from public approach to principal ground-floor accommodation may have value beyond minimum accessibility compliance.
- Furniture, appliances, prams and mobility devices should be considered in circulation geometry rather than treated as exceptional moving events.
- Controls should be positioned according to sequence of use, reach, hazard and feedback rather than service convenience alone.
- Small irreversible geometric decisions may deserve additional space when they permanently remove disproportionate long-term friction.
- Spatial generosity has real cost, carbon, energy and architectural consequences and must therefore be selective rather than automatic.

## Scope

### Anthropometric variance

Investigate real variation in:

- foot length and stair interaction;
- stature;
- shoulder/body breadth;
- hand and reach envelopes;
- children and shorter occupants;
- taller and larger occupants;
- ageing;
- pregnancy;
- temporary injury;
- reduced balance, dexterity or vision;
- movement while carrying loads or with occupied hands.

Do not design only for a median body. Do not convert extreme percentile values automatically into universal minimum dimensions.

### Movement and fall risk

Investigate:

- rise, going and pitch;
- stair descent behaviour;
- landings;
- handrails;
- headroom;
- visual legibility and lighting where evidence is credible;
- slips, trips and changes in level;
- movement while tired, distracted or carrying something.

### Circulation generosity

Investigate:

- corridor widths by role and traffic;
- door clear openings;
- passing and overtaking;
- local pinch points;
- corner and turning geometry;
- conflicts with door swings, radiators, cabinetry and furniture;
- whether principal and secondary circulation deserve different target classes.

### Continuous ground plane

Investigate:

- entrance steps and gradients;
- internal finish transitions;
- external-door thresholds;
- wet-room and shower transitions;
- drainage and weathering required for honest level access;
- the circumstances in which a step is genuinely justified.

Level access does not override the moisture doctrine. Water must be managed by geometry and drainage rather than wished away.

### Furniture and object logistics

Treat the house as a route network for large objects as well as walking bodies.

Representative objects should include:

- sofas;
- mattresses;
- tables;
- white goods;
- large furniture or joinery;
- prams;
- wheelchairs or mobility devices;
- ladders and maintenance equipment.

Investigate whether movement can be tested through representative envelopes and swept paths from street/entrance to principal destinations, including upper floors where appropriate.

### Sequence-of-use controls

Develop a review method that begins with the action sequence rather than a list of control heights.

Examples include:

- shower controls reachable before entering the spray;
- bath controls reachable without hazardous leaning;
- light switches at natural entry/exit positions;
- windows, shutters and blinds reachable from plausible use positions;
- sockets and appliance controls where the appliance is actually used;
- understandable local heating, ventilation and shading controls;
- fault/isolation controls reachable when they matter;
- predictable mapping, feedback and fallback behaviour.

The core question is:

> **Where is the occupant, what are they trying to do next, what hazards exist, and what action sequence does the building force?**

### Life-course and temporary impairment

Distinguish between:

- irreversible geometric choices worth making early because later correction is expensive or impossible;
- replaceable/adaptable components better left demand-led;
- speculative overprovision that fails proportionality.

### Cognitive and attentional friction

Connect directly to Repose / Low Vigilance.

Candidate friction includes:

- careful foot placement;
- ducking and head avoidance;
- stepping over small thresholds;
- door/corner choreography;
- ambiguous controls;
- routes requiring repeated collision avoidance;
- visually or physically unclear changes in level;
- unnecessary interaction with building systems.

The claim is architectural, not clinical: repeated compensatory attention is a design cost.

## Required evidence families

HFI must triangulate across multiple evidence traditions.

At minimum:

- England/UK Building Regulations and Approved Documents relevant to access, stairs and guarding;
- BS and other inclusive-design guidance where accessible;
- accessibility, universal-design and inclusive-design literature;
- anthropometric datasets, including population and percentile limitations;
- falls and stair-biomechanics research;
- ergonomics and human-factors literature;
- occupational-therapy evidence where spatially relevant;
- ageing-in-place / lifetime-housing research;
- housing post-occupancy evidence;
- bathroom, kitchen and control ergonomics;
- furniture/logistics and swept-path methods;
- strong domestic precedents that achieve generosity without indiscriminate plan inflation;
- counter-evidence covering cost, area, energy, embodied carbon and architectural compromise.

Standards are baselines and evidence inputs. They are not automatically Ovid’s House targets.

## Execution programme

### HFI-00 — authority recovery, baseline and friction inventory

Recover current authority and create a no-solution baseline.

Required work:

- reload current `main`;
- identify every current project claim or dimension touching stairs, circulation, thresholds, entrances, bathrooms, controls, furniture movement, life-course adaptation and human variance;
- freeze relevant Reference House facts and source identities;
- map ownership across manuscript, research, patterns, Reference House, delivery and computation;
- compile the initial **friction register**;
- distinguish known friction from unmodelled questions and mere user anecdotes.

**No design correction in HFI-00.**

### HFI-01 — evidence map and terminology

Define the research language and source hierarchy.

Distinguish:

- accessibility;
- inclusive design;
- universal design;
- ergonomics;
- human factors;
- anthropometrics;
- life-course design;
- low-vigilance / repose.

Define at minimum:

- friction;
- compensation;
- sequence of use;
- movement generosity;
- continuous ground plane;
- principal route;
- furniture/object route.

Record jurisdiction, date, scope and evidence strength for normative sources.

### HFI-02 — anthropometry and movement research

Study:

- body, foot and reach distributions;
- stair use and falls;
- turning, passing and carrying;
- temporary impairment;
- ageing.

Output candidate ranges and trade-offs, not final project dimensions.

### HFI-03 — route, threshold and object-movement research

Study:

- corridor, door and pinch-point geometry;
- turning geometry;
- step-free approaches;
- internal and external thresholds;
- furniture/appliance movement;
- moisture/weathering conflicts;
- candidate swept-path methods.

### HFI-04 — sequence-of-use and control ergonomics

Study:

- showers and baths;
- kitchen controls where architecture fixes location;
- windows, lighting and shading;
- environmental controls;
- fault/isolation interactions;
- mapping, feedback and fallback.

Produce a scenario-based review method rather than a reach-height checklist alone.

### HFI-05 — synthesis and principle formation

Only after HFI-01 through HFI-04:

- determine which frictions genuinely share underlying causes;
- reject false unification;
- formulate the smallest defensible architectural principles;
- separate evidence-backed propositions from explicit project preference;
- reconcile with Repose, moisture, maintenance, proportionality, grammar and Reference House constraints.

### HFI-06 — quantitative target register

For propositions that genuinely support numbers, record:

- external/legal baseline;
- evidence-supported range;
- preferred Ovid’s House target where earned;
- acceptable band/minimum where useful;
- evidence strength;
- cost/area/carbon penalty;
- conditions and exceptions.

Candidate topics include stair geometry, corridor width, door clearances, turning zones, thresholds, gradients and control/reach zones. None is pre-authorised.

Avoid false millimetre precision.

### HFI-07 — Reference House adversarial audit

Audit the coordinated house against the HFI synthesis.

Inspect at minimum:

- approach and entrance;
- principal circulation;
- stair;
- doors and local pinch points;
- tight corners;
- furniture routes;
- ground-floor step-free usability;
- bathrooms and wet rooms;
- shower/bath control locations where resolved;
- thresholds and floor build-ups;
- windows/controls where geometry exists.

Use an explicit status vocabulary such as:

`PASS / IMPROVE / CONFLICT / UNRESOLVED / NOT-MODELLED`

Freeze the vocabulary before the audit.

Do not protect existing geometry from a material HFI finding merely because it has already been drawn or compiled.

### HFI-08 — architectural integration

Route every accepted conclusion to the narrowest correct owner:

- manuscript/doctrine;
- strategy or pattern;
- Reference House project decision;
- research record;
- delivery requirement;
- prototype/test requirement;
- computation where genuinely checkable.

Cross-link instead of duplicating.

### HFI-09 — computational feasibility and selected checks

Only formalise constraints whose semantics and inputs are sufficiently controlled.

Candidate checks may include:

- selected stair target geometry;
- route clear widths;
- turning/clearance zones;
- threshold continuity;
- representative furniture/object path feasibility;
- control reach/sequence relationships where the source actually represents them.

The compiler must not invent geometry to manufacture an HFI pass. Unsupported inputs remain unsupported.

### HFI-10 — adversarial closeout

Attack the integrated result for:

- spatial bloat;
- median-body assumptions;
- false universality;
- standards worship;
- ergonomic pseudo-precision;
- clinical/institutional character;
- conflicts with moisture, fire, structure, services or architectural coherence;
- cost/carbon disproportion;
- Reference House overfit;
- implementation rules promoted into doctrine.

Close only when each surviving proposition has clear evidence, ownership and proof boundaries.

## Output architecture

HFI should leave, at minimum:

1. this programme-control document;
2. an evidence/standards register;
3. an initial and final friction register/taxonomy;
4. bounded research syntheses under `docs/research/`;
5. a small defensible set of principles rather than an ergonomic checklist;
6. a quantitative target register where numerical targets are genuinely justified;
7. a Reference House audit with explicit rewinds, improvements and unresolved gaps;
8. integrations into manuscript/patterns/delivery/computation only where earned;
9. regression checks where an accepted stronger target could otherwise silently regress;
10. a closeout record stating what HFI does not claim.

## Boundaries

HFI must not become:

- “Part M plus nicer numbers”;
- a wheelchair-only programme;
- a justification for gratuitous floor area;
- a universal rule that bigger is always better;
- an attempt to erase compression, intimacy or deliberately tight secondary spaces;
- a clinical or institutional aesthetic agenda;
- a substitute for fire, structure, moisture or building-control reasoning;
- unsupported ergonomic absolutism;
- Reference-House-shaped doctrine;
- a compiler-expansion programme before architectural conclusions exist.

The intended discipline is:

> **Spend geometry where geometry permanently removes meaningful friction.**

## Packet discipline

HFI should be restart-safe in the same sense as the other major project programmes.

Each packet must record:

- baseline `main` SHA;
- exact question;
- evidence or sources examined;
- findings and rejected hypotheses;
- artefacts created or modified;
- checks run;
- architectural/proof-boundary consequences;
- exact next authorised packet.

If a packet exposes several independent questions, split it. Do not allow “human factors” to become an open-ended excuse for general house redesign.
