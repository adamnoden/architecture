**Governing principle:** Principle 8 — **Design for repose**

## Canonical proposition

> **A good home should minimise unnecessary vigilance.**

In HSA, repose denotes a domestic environment that is physically comfortable, spatially comprehensible, capable of privacy and retreat, locally controllable and perceptually settled.

It is not a synonym for minimalism, low visual complexity, symmetry, historical style or sensory deprivation.

## Evidence discipline

The integration deliberately separates:

1. **Established or comparatively robust environmental evidence** — thermal comfort, noise, ventilation / air quality, daylight and glare, adequate space, usable controls.
2. **Supported but context-sensitive housing / environmental-psychology evidence** — privacy regulation, perceived control, coherence, hominess, restoration.
3. **Qualified architectural hypotheses** — especially the proposal that visually legible equilibrium and apparent structural settlement contribute to domestic repose.
4. **Claims not admitted as doctrine** — deterministic prospect-refuge, “curves are safe”, universal sacred proportions, style-specific neurological claims, or isolated biometric findings translated directly into design rules.

Any future text that collapses those levels should be corrected.

## What this principle can reject

Principle 8 is not merely explanatory. It should be capable of rejecting otherwise competent work.

Examples include:

- a structurally safe principal room whose main architectural effect depends on persistent apparent precariousness without a compelling reason;
- an open-plan arrangement that removes meaningful privacy or retreat from the dwelling;
- glazing that produces impressive transparency at the cost of uncontrollable glare, exposure or overheating;
- environmental automation that leaves no intelligible local control or fallback for ordinary comfort;
- an access / service strategy whose visible seams, grilles, labels or hardware become the dominant visual field of a room;
- a technically demountable floor or lining that rattles, feels fragile or sounds hollow enough to make the room feel provisional;
- a “calm” minimalist interior that achieves low visual content while remaining acoustically, thermally or spatially uncomfortable;
- decorative complexity that lacks hierarchy and becomes unresolved visual competition.

The principle does not automatically reject:

- asymmetry;
- ornament;
- strong colour;
- complex material;
- exposed construction;
- open planning;
- large windows;
- cantilevers;
- automation;
- architectural drama.

Those choices acquire a burden of architectural justification in relation to domestic occupation rather than being prohibited categorically.

## Design-review shorthand

For principal spaces, ask:

1. **Gravity** — does the architecture appear settled?
2. **Comprehension** — is the spatial order readily grasped?
3. **Exposure** — can privacy and retreat be regulated?
4. **Agency** — can important immediate conditions be altered intelligibly?
5. **Sensory load** — are noise, glare, heat and visual competition proportionate?
6. **Richness** — is there fascination within larger order?
7. **Recovery** — where can a tired, ill or overstimulated occupant withdraw?
8. **Technology** — does it serve without continually advertising itself?
9. **Intensity** — where are surprise or tension intentional, and where should they subside?

Measured items should use the appropriate building-science method. Architectural judgements should remain explicit judgements.

## Open work

The remaining items require a more mature Reference House, post-occupancy evidence, or new primary research.

### R1 — structural-legibility experiment

A controlled research study could compare otherwise matched domestic scenes with different apparent support conditions. Separate architects from lay participants. Measure perceived stability, tension/ease, hominess, liking and willingness to remain before considering biometric instrumentation.

This is optional research, not a prerequisite for continuing the house design.

### R2 — Reference House repose schedule

Once the Reference House plan is sufficiently resolved, create a room-by-room schedule covering:

- recovery / quiet priority;
- privacy requirement;
- acoustic relationship;
- daylight / glare / shading intent;
- outlook and exposure;
- local environmental controls;
- apparent structural settlement;
- intended level of fascination / drama.

Do not create this before the plan and section are mature enough for the schedule to test real architecture.

### R3 — post-occupancy protocol

Develop a compact Stage 7 review that distinguishes measured performance from occupant experience. Avoid turning subjective comfort into a pseudo-objective score.

### R4 — computational semantics

Do **not** encode “repose” as a compiler rule. Some independent technical inputs may later become formal constraints—noise limits, glare, daylight, control reachability, privacy graph relationships—but the overall architectural quality is not presently reducible to a valid deterministic metric.

The computational programme should not convert a carefully qualified human principle into false precision.

## Anti-drift rules

- Do not use “neuroarchitecture” as an authority word.
- Do not turn preference data into health claims.
- Do not turn short-exposure image or VR studies into long-duration residential claims without justification.
- Do not imply that Georgian or traditional architecture is biologically correct.
- Do not confuse a real structural load path with its visible expression.
- Do not confuse repose with absence of interest.
- Do not equate repose with low visual complexity or sensory impoverishment.
- Prefer concrete architectural language over aphorisms; retain the canonical proposition and do not multiply slogans around it.
