## D-F06 — Type and orientation

**File:** `docs/manuscript/figures/reference-house-type-orientation.svg`  
**Class:** ESSENTIAL ORIENTATION  
**Primary placement:** Part IV, Chapter 13 — Site and type.

**Purpose**

Establish the selected two-storey courtyard type, ground / upper programme fields, north-up model orientation and A–A sectional registration without reproducing the compiler inspection surface wholesale.

**Publication caption**

*The Reference House is a compact two-storey courtyard type organised around a 3.6 × 4.0 m open court within an approximately 12.0 × 10.8 m outer planning reservation. Ground and upper planning fields are shown north-up in the virtual model and cross-registered to section A–A. Solid outer and courtyard edges are controlled planning geometry; dashed internal divisions remain provisional. There is no commissioned site: north here is model orientation, not a claim about a future plot.*

**Does not establish:** wall-face construction geometry, exact internal door positions, a real-site orientation, planning acceptability, technical design, or that the courtyard is an Ovid’s House / G-01 requirement.

## D-F07 — Spatial and sectional order

**File:** `docs/manuscript/figures/reference-house-spatial-sectional-order.svg`  
**Class:** ESSENTIAL ARGUMENT  
**Primary placement:** Part IV, Chapter 14 — Architectural order, repose and tectonic language.

**Purpose**

Explain two architectural relationships that are more important than literal plan reproduction: the ordered but off-centre arrival re-centring internally on the court, and restrained sectional primacy of the shared ground floor.

**Publication caption**

*The principal façade is ordered without requiring a centred entrance. Arrival enters through the third bay, then re-centres on the open court and makes the garden legible beyond. In section, the shared ground floor carries restrained primacy over the private upper storey. G-01 requires relational spatial and sectional hierarchy; the four-bay front, courtyard and exact entrance position remain Reference House decisions.*

**Does not establish:** a universal Georgian circulation sequence, a centred or off-centre entrance rule, a mandatory courtyard, or one required storey-height ratio.

## D-F08 — Courtyard structural concept

**File:** `docs/manuscript/figures/reference-house-structural-concept.svg`  
**Class:** USEFUL ORIENTATION  
**Primary placement:** Part IV, Chapter 15 — Material, structural and assembly system.

**Purpose**

Show why the selected courtyard morphology has a consequential structural effect before engineering: it creates four comparatively short gross floor-span fields between outer and courtyard support lines, with short edge-beam continuations through the front and rear centre strips.

**Publication caption**

*The courtyard is also a structural organiser. At the current coordination level, outer and courtyard masonry support lines create two approximately 4.2 m side strips and two approximately 3.4 m centre strips, with four short edge beams continuing the court support lines through the front and rear wings. Span arrows show axis only. Stair and service openings are known obligations but are not positioned here because their detailed geometry remains unresolved.*

**Does not establish:** joist or beam sizes, capacities, deflection, vibration, robustness, lintel / bearing adequacy, connection design, diaphragm adequacy, foundation design, or structural compliance.

## Publication rule carried forward

These three figures intentionally simplify the COA inspection outputs. Simplification is permitted only where it removes compiler / forensic information that the reader does not need; it must never remove uncertainty in a way that converts provisional, approximate or unresolved information into apparently finished architecture.
