# PR-01 — Internal Adversarial Review Programme

**Status:** ACTIVE PROGRAMME — attack phase not yet complete  
**Date:** 2026-10-14  
**Parent:** Publication Credibility and Release Programme  
**Umbrella issue:** #71 — PUB-01 publication credibility and release programme  
**Frozen candidate content:** `f6b286e883f24b322f41f85d8d3ad796b103a53d`  
**Freeze control:** `publication/pr00h-candidate-book-freeze.md`

PR-01 exists to make the frozen candidate difficult to dismiss before scarce external attention is requested.

The frozen candidate is **read-only during attack**. Findings are recorded first. Corrections happen only after the attack set has been completed, classified and prioritised.

> **Do not defend the book while discovering what is wrong with it.**

## 1. Review constitution

Every material finding must answer five questions:

1. **What is the strongest fair criticism?**
2. **What exact proposition / chapter / pattern / figure does it affect?**
3. **Why would a competent sceptical architect care?**
4. **Which layer owns the defect?**
5. **What is the smallest legitimate consequence if the criticism is true?**

A finding is not weakened merely because the book can produce a rebuttal. The first task is to establish whether the criticism exposes a real defect, missing qualification, evidential debt or architectural contradiction.

### Attack dimensions

Use the parent programme’s eight dimensions without merging them into a generic “quality” score:

- `WRONG` — factually, technically, historically or logically false;
- `WEAK` — claim/evidence relationship is inadequate to its prominence or breadth;
- `DERIVATIVE` — contribution or novelty exceeds what intellectual / architectural lineage permits;
- `NAIVE` — proposition underestimates construction, regulation, procurement, cost, carbon, workmanship, occupation or maintenance reality;
- `OVERSTATED` — useful bounded proposition presented too generally;
- `INCOMPLETE` — important counterargument, edge case, competing good or consequence is missing;
- `INCONSISTENT` — conflict between doctrine, patterns, grammar, Reference House, evidence or publication language;
- `UNMEMORABLE` — useful idea fails to crystallise or transmit at the prominence it receives.

`UNMEMORABLE` may justify editorial change, but never at the price of making a proposition less true.

## 2. Finding classes and severity

Use the PR-00H ownership classes:

- `PUBLICATION DEFECT`;
- `QUALIFICATION`;
- `MATERIAL ARCHITECTURAL FINDING`;
- `EVIDENCE FINDING`;
- `OUTSIDE REVIEW SCOPE`.

Severity is independent of class:

- **S0 — fatal to current candidate:** central proposition or structure cannot survive as published;
- **S1 — material:** a serious reader could reasonably reject or materially downgrade the work unless corrected;
- **S2 — significant:** should be corrected before private critical edition but does not invalidate the core proposition;
- **S3 — minor:** useful hardening, local clarity or expression issue;
- **NOTE — recorded challenge:** worth preserving but no change currently earned.

The review should prefer a small number of real S0/S1 findings over a theatrical catalogue of minor objections.

## 3. Execution sequence

```text
R0  FREEZE RECOVERY + REVIEW MAP
 ↓
R1  CORE PROPOSITION ATTACK
 ↓
R2  PATTERN / METHOD ATTACK
 ↓
R3  REFERENCE HOUSE ATTACK
 ↓
R4  HOSTILE ARCHITECT TEST
 ↓
R5  STEELMAN OPPOSITION TEST
 ↓
R6  CROSS-BOOK CONTRADICTION + OMISSION PASS
 ↓
R7  FINDINGS REGISTER FREEZE + TRIAGE
 ↓
R8  CORRECTION PROGRAMME
 ↓
R9  RE-PROOF / RE-FREEZE
```

R0→R7 are **diagnostic**. Do not edit the frozen candidate during them.

R8 is corrective and must route every accepted finding through its owning layer. R9 exists only if substantive changes were made, which should be expected rather than treated as failure.

## 4. R0 — Freeze recovery and review map

### Purpose

Prove that the review is attacking the intended object and map every major claim surface before criticism begins.

### Required output

Create one findings register containing at minimum:

- stable finding ID;
- attack dimension;
- severity;
- affected publication unit;
- exact claim / proposition under attack;
- strongest fair criticism;
- evidence / precedent relevant to the criticism;
- owning layer;
- finding class;
- provisional consequence if sustained;
- disposition: `OPEN / SUSTAINED / PARTLY SUSTAINED / REJECTED / OUTSIDE SCOPE`;
- closure / correction reference once resolved.

The initial register must include the major claim surfaces from Parts I–V, Part III’s canonical pattern language, G-01’s role in Part IV, the Reference House and the evidence/back-matter constitution.

## 5. R1 — Core proposition attack

Attack Parts I–II and the governing principles as if the rest of the project did not exist to rescue them.

At minimum ask:

- Is **selective permanence** genuinely more precise/useful than ordinary adaptability, loose-fit, Open Building or service-life planning?
- Is “no service inside permanent fabric” defensible as doctrine, or is it an absolutist slogan whose exceptions reveal the real rule?
- Does lifespan separation reduce whole-life damage often enough to justify the spatial/material burden it creates?
- Does **designed interface** describe a distinctive architectural proposition or merely competent detailing?
- Does failure architecture add anything beyond robust design / safe-to-fail engineering translated into domestic architecture?
- Is maintenance geography genuinely architectural rather than facilities-management planning with architectural vocabulary?
- Does workmanship robustness survive contact with procurement and ordinary site tolerances without becoming expensive bespoke detailing?
- Does repose carry useful architectural force without importing weak neuroarchitecture rhetoric or conservative aesthetic preference?
- Does the work understate carbon, embodied resource and initial-material penalties created by extra zones, access, redundancy and replaceability?
- Are any propositions secretly dependent on detached-house scale / wealth / space availability while being written generally?

For every central claim, identify the strongest conventional alternative and ask whether Ovid’s House materially improves on it.

## 6. R2 — Pattern and method attack

Attack Part III as a reusable language rather than as a summary of ideas already liked by the project.

At minimum test:

- whether any of the 21 patterns are duplicates, implementation choices or ordinary specification advice rather than architectural patterns;
- whether pattern invariants are actually invariant across credible domestic morphologies;
- whether the language contains enough generative relationship to deserve “pattern language” rather than “pattern catalogue”;
- whether the three strategies sit at a coherent level relative to doctrine and patterns;
- whether held candidates are genuinely unresolved or merely postponed because evidence is inconvenient;
- whether stable `HSA-P-*` identities introduce an unnecessary systems-engineering tone into architectural material;
- whether evidence / `does not prove` fields clarify the pattern or bury its architectural force;
- whether the service-topology generative sequence overweights services relative to other architecture;
- whether Alexander is being used as an influence without adequately confronting the differences between his pattern ontology and this one.

Do not alter canonical pattern status during R2. Findings route to R8.

## 7. R3 — Reference House attack

Treat the Reference House as the easiest place for hidden contradictions to become visible.

At minimum test:

- whether the courtyard house is genuinely a good integration vehicle or conveniently accommodates the doctrine by giving services/access abundant perimeter and depth;
- whether the house is spatially/domestically persuasive independent of the maintenance thesis;
- whether G-01 conformance produces architecture or only rule satisfaction;
- whether project-specific Georgian/classical taste has leaked into apparently general arguments despite formal safeguards;
- whether serviceability has distorted plan efficiency, wall thickness, circulation, room proportion or floor-to-floor depth disproportionately;
- whether the maintenance geography is credible once real landscape, neighbours, planning constraints and site boundaries exist;
- whether outward roof drainage and other selected solutions merely relocate risk rather than reduce it;
- whether unresolved structural / envelope / service details are so central that the current architectural integration is premature;
- whether the Reference House is too bespoke to demonstrate ordinary workmanship robustness;
- whether its assumptions imply a cost / floor-area / material premium that would materially undermine the proposition for normal housing.

The house may fail a criticism without invalidating Ovid’s House. Conversely, a technically rescuable house may expose a deeper doctrinal problem.

## 8. R4 — Hostile architect test

Adopt the strongest legitimate sceptical posture of an experienced UK architect who expects the project to be amateurish.

Find the easiest attacks they could make after a serious reading, including:

- rediscovery of established practice presented as revelation;
- over-systematisation of ordinary good detailing;
- lack of cost / carbon / buildability evidence;
- unrealistic assumptions about client behaviour and maintenance;
- design complexity masquerading as repairability;
- fetishisation of legibility / disassembly;
- classical taste smuggled into universal claims;
- excessive abstraction from actual procurement / liability / regulation;
- software formalism adding confidence without physical authority;
- a Reference House that is more diagram than credible commission;
- an outsider voice insufficiently grounded in architectural practice.

The pass must distinguish cheap rhetorical attacks from legitimate substantive ones. Record only the latter as findings.

## 9. R5 — Steelman opposition test

For each major doctrine, write the strongest good-faith alternative position before defending Ovid’s House.

Required opposition positions include at least:

- **integrated permanence:** durable buildings often succeed because assemblies are materially integrated; separability can add joints, weakness and maintenance;
- **conventional concealed services:** ordinary embedded/concealed systems may be cheaper, spatially efficient and sufficiently maintainable given realistic replacement cycles;
- **minimum material:** adding access zones, redundant routes and replaceable interfaces may consume more carbon/resources than future interventions save;
- **specialist optimisation:** proprietary integrated systems can outperform commodity repairability and remain supportable over realistic building horizons;
- **architectural primacy:** the house should optimise life, space, delight and environmental performance before hypothetical future interventions;
- **adapt through renovation:** destructive renovation may sometimes be a rational and culturally productive form of building change rather than a design failure;
- **uncertainty humility:** designing heavily for unknown future change can be less rational than building simply and accepting later adaptation.

Ovid’s House does not pass by defeating straw men. It passes when its remaining claim is stronger after these positions are granted their best case.

## 10. R6 — Cross-book contradiction and omission pass

Read later material back against earlier claims.

At minimum compare:

- governing principles ↔ pattern invariants;
- pattern language ↔ Reference House occurrences;
- G-01 ↔ style-neutral Ovid’s House doctrine;
- Part II implementation examples ↔ Part V proportionality warnings;
- repose ↔ maintenance/service visibility and technical legibility;
- permanence ↔ embodied-carbon / resource claims;
- ordinary workmanship ↔ bespoke interface families;
- commodity repairability ↔ architecture-specific manufactured components;
- “accessible ≠ exposed” ↔ actual access provisions;
- failure tolerance ↔ added boundary debt;
- compiler claims ↔ publication language around proof / determination.

Also identify major omitted domains whose absence could mislead a reader about scope rather than merely leave future work undone.

## 11. R7 — Findings register freeze and triage

Before any correction:

1. deduplicate findings;
2. merge only findings that share the same underlying defect;
3. state the strongest fair version of each surviving finding;
4. assign severity, class and owner;
5. separate criticisms that are genuinely answered by existing text from criticisms that require change;
6. freeze the diagnostic register against the original candidate.

A rebuttal may support `REJECTED`, but rejection requires a concrete reason tied to evidence or scope. “The project already considered this” is not a closure rationale.

## 12. R8 — Correction programme

Execute sustained findings from highest consequence to lowest.

Correction preference:

1. **delete** a weak proposition where it is not needed;
2. **narrow** the claim to its earned scope;
3. **strengthen evidence** where appropriate evidence already exists;
4. **reframe** to distinguish architectural proposition from project choice;
5. **reopen upstream architecture / pattern / grammar / Reference House work** where the criticism is architectural rather than editorial;
6. add new research only when an accepted finding cannot be resolved honestly without it.

Do not protect favourite phrases, diagrams, patterns or the Reference House for sentimental reasons.

## 13. R9 — Re-proof and re-freeze

If R8 changes the argument materially:

- run the appropriate repository / publication validation;
- rebuild and inspect affected print surfaces;
- create a new candidate identity;
- record which findings caused the freeze to break;
- freeze the revised candidate before PR-02.

PR-01 passes only when all S0/S1 findings and every sustained finding that materially affects the thesis are resolved, narrowed, accepted explicitly as uncertainty, or rejected for recorded reasons.

A successful PR-01 should usually leave a smaller, harder book.

## 14. Stop rules

Stop and reopen the owning workstream rather than editorially patch around a finding when:

- the criticism invalidates a governing architectural proposition;
- two canonical authorities are materially incompatible;
- a pattern invariant fails a credible counterexample;
- the Reference House requires architectural change rather than explanation;
- a technical claim cannot be narrowed without making the architectural argument misleading;
- resolving the criticism requires evidence the project does not possess and the claim cannot honestly remain at its current prominence.

Do not use PR-01 to broaden the project by curiosity. New work must be finding-led.

## 15. Gate

PR-01 passes when the obvious serious internal attacks have been exhausted against a stable object and the resulting candidate has been corrected and re-frozen.

Only then proceed to **PR-02 — private object edition**.
