**Status:** implementation record

D4 asks which system/maintenance relationships genuinely need spatial explanation in the book. Three figures survive that test. A fourth scenario plate does not.

## D-F09 — Reference House service / maintenance geography

**File:** `docs/manuscript/figures/reference-house-service-maintenance-geography.svg`

**Classification:** ESSENTIAL ARGUMENT.

**Purpose:** show the concentration of plant, utility, wet-service and vertical distribution around the eastern service flank while principal rooms remain outside primary infrastructure paths.

**Source authority:** Part IV chapter 16; COA service register; COA plans; canonical RH service-topology records.

**Caption:** *Service and maintenance geography. High-consequence infrastructure is concentrated around the service flank: plant and utility functions below, family bathroom above, and a bounded vertical zone between them. The diagram shows owned geography rather than exact pipe, duct or cable routes.*

**Does not establish:** exact MEP routing, service sizes, pressure losses, ventilation duty, drainage falls, electrical design or product selection.

## D-F10 — Reference House water / roof failure geography

**File:** `docs/manuscript/figures/reference-house-water-failure-geography.svg`

**Classification:** ESSENTIAL ARGUMENT.

**Purpose:** show why the selected roof drains outward, why the courtyard manages principally its own rainfall, and how a separate higher-level emergency route makes abnormal water visible before hidden internal damage.

**Source authority:** Part IV chapter 17; RH08 roof/drainage coordination; COA boundary register.

**Caption:** *Water and failure geography. Four low-slope roof fields drain away from the courtyard toward inspectable external edges. The court manages principally its own open-to-sky area through a short primary route toward the service side, with an independent higher-level overflow for abnormal water. Geometry reduces consequence; it does not establish hydraulic capacity.*

**Does not establish:** design rainfall, outlet or pipe sizing, waterproofing performance, final drainage destination or product adequacy.

## D-F11 — Reference House external maintenance geography

**File:** `docs/manuscript/figures/reference-house-external-maintenance-geography.svg`

**Classification:** ESSENTIAL ARGUMENT.

**Purpose:** show external maintenance as site geometry: service-side replacement/utility access, west/rear working lines and a garden-to-courtyard logistics route for temporary tower access.

**Source authority:** Part IV chapter 16; RH08 external-maintenance planning; COA plan geometry.

**Caption:** *External maintenance geography. Working space is reserved around the house and inside the courtyard so façade, roof-edge, rainwater and plant tasks can be approached by ordinary temporary means. Landscape remains subordinate to those task-specific routes rather than consuming them after design.*

**Does not establish:** a final access method statement, CDM compliance, scaffold engineering, neighbouring land rights or real-site constructability.

## D-F12 — future intervention scenarios

**Disposition:** NO STANDALONE FIGURE AT D4.

Chapter 18 contains several qualitatively different interventions: plant replacement, window replacement, leak isolation, bathroom access, a new dry service route, roof/gutter work and household change. Reducing these to one schematic would either become a generic maintenance infographic or imply a common geometry that does not exist.

The scenarios are already short and spatially explicit in prose. D5 may use one or two small callouts only if trim-scale proof shows that a particular intervention is hard to follow. There is no obligation to visualise all scenarios for symmetry.

## D4 judgement

The retained figures answer three different spatial questions:

1. **where infrastructure is concentrated;**
2. **where water is sent when normal or abnormal;**
3. **where future workers and replacement components can physically go.**

They remain analytical publication diagrams rather than technical drawings. No new engineering or services authority is introduced.

Final placement, captions, monochrome/trim-scale QA and any selective scenario callout remain D5 work.
