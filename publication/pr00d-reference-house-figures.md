# PR-00D3 — Reference House Orientation and Architectural Argument

**Status:** implementation record  
**Date:** 2026-10-14  
**Parent gate:** PR-00D — Figure and Visual-Argument Completion  
**Source substrate:** COA successor family under `docs/reference-house/generated/`

PR-00D3 converts the Reference House from compiler-inspection material into three bounded publication arguments. It does not copy the COA plates wholesale and it does not increase the house's technical authority.

## D-F06 — Reference House type and orientation

**File:** `docs/manuscript/figures/reference-house-type-orientation.svg`

**Classification:** ESSENTIAL ORIENTATION.

**Purpose:** establish the compact two-storey courtyard type, the approximate 12.0 × 10.8 m outer reservation, the approximately 3.6 × 4.0 m open court, the concentrated service flank, the front → court → garden sequence and the A–A registration.

**Source authority:** Part IV chapter 13; COA ground/first plans; canonical Reference House morphology.

**Caption:** *Reference House type and orientation. The house is organised as a compact two-storey ring around an open court, with a concentrated service flank and a legible front-to-court-to-garden sequence. Dimensions describe the current virtual type; compass orientation, utilities and neighbouring conditions remain contingent on a real site.*

**Does not establish:** a commissioned site, true solar orientation, planning acceptability, utility location, boundary ownership or finished wall-face construction geometry.

## D-F07 — Reference House spatial and sectional order

**File:** `docs/manuscript/figures/reference-house-spatial-sectional-order.svg`

**Classification:** ESSENTIAL ARGUMENT.

**Purpose:** explain how ground-floor shared rooms and arrival sequence, upper private rooms, the courtyard and a modestly ranked ground storey form one relational hierarchy. The figure demonstrates the Reference House occurrence of G-01 R05 without turning that occurrence into the grammar itself.

**Source authority:** Part IV chapter 14; COA ground/first plans and A–A section; frozen G-01 v0.1.

**Caption:** *Spatial and sectional order. Rank is relational rather than owned by one proxy: shared rooms and arrival structure the ground floor, private rooms occupy the upper level, and the courtyard recentres both. The slightly more generous ground storey establishes domestic primacy without a piano-nobile effect.*

**Does not establish:** a universal Georgian plan, mandatory room sequence, mandatory piano nobile, fixed room sizes or a general Ovid's House morphology.

## D-F08 — Reference House structural and assembly logic

**File:** `docs/manuscript/figures/reference-house-structural-assembly-logic.svg`

**Classification:** USEFUL ORIENTATION retained after review.

**Purpose:** show the architectural value of the courtyard-generated short-span ring and the separation between permanent masonry, structural floor, deliberately coordinated crossings and faster-changing layers. This is clearer for chapter 15 than reproducing the full semantic structural inspection plate.

**Source authority:** Part IV chapter 15; COA `first-floor-structure.svg`; RH structural coordination and current floor-zone assumptions.

**Caption:** *Structural and assembly logic at pre-engineering scope. The courtyard turns the floor plate into comparatively short span fields between outer and inner permanent walls; local openings are treated as explicit interruptions rather than site cuts through a finished system. The hierarchy is architectural coordination, not structural adequacy.*

**Does not establish:** member sizing, bearing capacity, lintel/beam adequacy, connections, diaphragm design, global stability or foundations.

## Publication judgement

The three figures deliberately use analytical publication linework rather than compiler-report aesthetics. COA remains the forensic inspection source; these figures carry only the minimum geometry needed for chapters 13–15.

D-F08 remains in the book because chapter 15's central argument is architectural before it is numerical: the courtyard changes the structural problem itself. The full COA structural plate remains available online for inspection but is too semantically dense to serve as the primary monograph figure.

No elevation plate is added in D3 merely to make the Reference House look more complete. G-01 frontage order is already described in prose and can be reconsidered in D5 only if the whole-book proof exposes a real orientation/comprehension gap.

## D3 gate

D3 passes when all three figures remain legible in monochrome, preserve uncertainty and claim boundaries, and do not visually promote coordinated geometry into technical proof. Final trim-scale placement and manuscript insertion remain D5 work.
