# 2. New findings

## PR01-F021 — the project designs more seriously for the future life of systems than for the future life of occupants

- **Dimension:** `INCOMPLETE`
- **Severity:** **S1 — material**
- **Surface:** `P1-01 / P1-03 / P1-06 / P4-01 / P4-02 / P4-06 / X-05`
- **Claim under attack:** Ovid’s House is a long-life domestic architecture in which foreseeable change is given deliberate architectural places to occur.
- **Strongest fair criticism:** The book anticipates plant replacement, new cables, failed windows, drainage blockage, changing technology, service obsolescence and future maintenance in remarkable detail. It treats human life-course change much more lightly. The Reference House carries only the baseline Category-1/`M4(1)` visitability condition at current scope: substantially level entrance intent, entrance-storey WC and step-free ground floor. All four bedrooms and the family bathroom remain upstairs behind a permanent three-flight stair. Chapter 18 tests a “changed household” mainly through room-use flexibility, not reduced mobility, disability, care, temporary incapacity or ageing in place. Yet those are foreseeable changes with high consequence and potentially destructive retrofit—the project’s own proportionality criteria would appear to rank them highly.
- **Why it matters:** This is not a request for universal wheelchair housing. It is an internal-priority challenge. A doctrine that gives future pipe replacement architectural authority but does not explicitly ask what happens when a long-term occupant can no longer use the permanent stair has an asymmetry at the centre of its domestic claim.
- **Evidence / counterexample:** Approved Document M Volume 1 distinguishes Category 1 **visitable**, Category 2 **accessible and adaptable**, and Category 3 **wheelchair user** dwellings. Categories 2/3 apply where required by planning, so they are not automatic Reference House compliance duties. They are nevertheless useful established comparators showing that “visitable” and “adaptable for occupants” are materially different ambitions. The frozen Reference House explicitly protects M4(1) geometry but does not perform a comparable life-course adaptation test.
- **Owner:** doctrine / Reference House / evidence
- **Class:** `MATERIAL ARCHITECTURAL FINDING`
- **Provisional consequence:** R8 should add a **life-course adaptation test** before deciding whether architecture must change. At minimum test temporary/permanent reduced mobility, need for entrance-storey sleeping/bathing, assistance/care, and whether the stair/room/service geography permits a proportionate adaptation. Use M4(2) as one comparator, not as an automatic doctrine rule. Outcomes may include: existing layout adapts acceptably; a cheap passive provision is earned; a project-specific brief choice is exposed; or the publication claim is narrowed. Do not reserve lifts or enlarge the house by reflex.
- **Disposition:** `OPEN`

### R6 judgement

This is the first PR-01 finding likely to reopen **programme/plan architecture** rather than only evidence/publication. That is why it is S1.

The point is not that every long-lived house must keep every resident in every room forever. The point is that human change deserves the same explicit proportionality analysis already granted to technical change.

## PR01-F022 — “build for time” does not yet include a sufficiently explicit future-climate test

- **Dimension:** `INCOMPLETE`
- **Severity:** **S2 — significant**
- **Surface:** `P1-01 / P1-03 / P1-05 / P4-05 / P5-01 / X-06`
- **Claim under attack:** The long-lived permanent architecture is selected with enough regard for future context that faster-changing systems can adapt around it.
- **Strongest fair criticism:** Part I correctly says a building’s context can change while its fabric remains sound. Regulation and technology are named; climate change is not developed to equivalent architectural depth. Yet the long-lived layers Ovid’s House wants to preserve—orientation, room depth, glazing distribution, shading opportunity, thermal mass, envelope, roof/drainage geometry, landscape and purge routes—are precisely the layers whose future performance may be altered by hotter summers, more intense rainfall or other changing weather conditions. The Reference House plans current passive hierarchy and postpones actual simulation until a real site, but neither the doctrine nor future-scenario method asks explicitly whether the **permanent choices remain robust under future climate conditions**.
- **Why it matters:** A serviceable house that makes boilers and windows easy to replace but bakes a difficult-to-retrofit overheating or rainfall problem into its permanent geometry has failed its own temporal reasoning.
- **Evidence / counterexample:** England now has a dedicated Approved Document O for overheating in new residential buildings, while current English Housing Survey climate/weather-resilience work explicitly treats overheating, winter warmth and storm/moisture resilience as housing concerns under climate change. London policy likewise treats rising heat risk as a design problem for current and future homes. These do not dictate one architectural solution; they establish that future climate is not speculative in the same sense as an unknown gadget.
- **Owner:** doctrine / evidence / Reference House implementation
- **Class:** `EVIDENCE FINDING`
- **Provisional consequence:** R8 should add a future-climate condition to the **real-project environmental gate** and research agenda. The architectural question is whether permanent geometry remains adaptable/robust across a reasonable future weather range, not whether the current virtual Reference House can be certified without a site. For a real project, compare current and future weather scenarios for overheating, purge/shading, moisture/rainfall and any other site-relevant hazard before treating passive geometry as durable. No universal climate provision should be promoted upstream from one site.
- **Disposition:** `OPEN`

# 3. Earlier findings materially strengthened by R6

## PR01-F005 — Repose / apparent equilibrium

The cross-book read confirms that the apparent-equilibrium hypothesis is unusually prominent relative to other underdeveloped human questions. The project has a dedicated Repose evidence audit but no equivalent life-course accessibility audit. F005 therefore remains a local qualification while F021 becomes the larger human-factor priority.

## PR01-F006 — doctrine versus baseline competence

R6 reinforces F006. The governing-principle layer contains both distinctive propositions and broad good-practice disciplines, while an important long-life domestic concern such as life-course accessibility is not a principle at all. R8 should use this as evidence when testing the doctrine’s constitution: inclusion/exclusion must reflect what actually defines the project, not historical accretion.

## PR01-F009 — technical weighting of the pattern corpus

F021 provides an independent symptom of the same imbalance. The project’s reusable pattern work is much richer around technical/service change than around human/spatial change. R8 should **not** respond by inventing accessibility patterns. It should first resolve the doctrine/programme question and then only patternise relationships that recur independently.

## PR01-F016 — stewardship continuity

R6 confirms no contradiction: the book explicitly wants a physical index to survive digital/account loss. But the same “degraded stewardship” principle should apply to accessibility and climate adaptation: the permanent architecture should not require perfect future knowledge to remain habitable.

## PR01-F019 — magnitude of collateral destruction

The research agenda is admirably candid about long-duration evidence, but R6 confirms that the main narrative still uses collateral destruction as a major organising problem. Explicit uncertainty prevents false proof; it does not answer the comparative magnitude question.

# 4. Cross-book attacks that do not earn findings

## R6-RJ01 — “The book contradicts itself on services in permanent fabric”

**Rejected.**

Historical project language was more absolute, but the frozen candidate consistently uses the bounded rule: permanent fabric is not the **routine distribution or attachment zone**; sparse necessary crossings are allowed when deliberate and documented. Patterns and the Reference House follow that rule.

## R6-RJ02 — “Failure tolerance contradicts boundary integrity”

**Rejected as a direct contradiction.**

The book explicitly carries a boundary ledger and repeatedly warns that access is unsuccessful if fire/acoustic/air/water/thermal performance cannot be reinstated. The unresolved issue is empirical lifecycle reliability, already F004.

## R6-RJ03 — “Repose contradicts technical access and legibility”

**Rejected.**

The project consistently distinguishes accessibility from exposure. The Reference House uses quiet direct walls/floors as baseline, concentrates services and treats visible technical interfaces architecturally. There is no doctrine requiring rooms to display maintenance machinery.

## R6-RJ04 — “Passive-first contradicts MVHR / heat pump / possible active cooling”

**Rejected.**

Principle 9 says passive architecture does the **first** work, not all work. Part IV retains MVHR/MEV and heat-pump options and explicitly says active cooling should be added if measured performance requires it. This is hierarchy, not anti-technology ideology.

## R6-RJ05 — “Commodity repairability contradicts specialist fabrication”

**Rejected.**

Commodity preference applies most strongly to shorter-lived technical components; specialist/custom work may legitimately create architectural enclosures and interfaces. The real risk is too many custom interface families, captured by F017.

## R6-RJ06 — “G-01 has contaminated style-neutral Ovid’s House doctrine”

**Rejected again after whole-book read.**

G-01 is visibly downstream and Part IV repeatedly localises Georgian-derived order, courtyard morphology and project tectonics. No material upstream classical rule appears in Parts I–II or the pattern invariants. F005 remains the only plausible taste-adjacent doctrine concern.

## R6-RJ07 — “The compiler language makes the publication imply buildability”

**Rejected.**

The computational track is not promoted into a manuscript Part; Appendix A and the RH material state clearly that deterministic representation/calculation is not proof of structural, environmental, regulatory or physical adequacy. The bounded authority survives cross-book reading.

## R6-RJ08 — “Reference House pattern occurrences are laundered into pattern evidence”

**Rejected.**

The book explicitly says occurrences do not raise evidence. The pattern-language problem is F008/F010, not occurrence laundering.

# 5. Major domains considered but not promoted into findings

R6 deliberately checked several possible omissions and declined to broaden the project merely because they are important subjects.

- **End-of-life material recovery:** already bounded as secondary to maintenance/replacement during occupation; no misleading whole-circularity claim is made.
- **Universal future structural capacity:** explicitly rejected by proportional serviceability; no obligation to design for speculative extra storeys.
- **Every possible hazard / resilience domain:** a long-life doctrine cannot become a universal risk manual. F022 is retained because future climate directly bears on the permanent environmental geometry the book already claims as architecture.
- **Detailed affordability / housing policy:** F003/F020 already identify transfer/client-horizon limits. The monograph need not become housing-economics policy.
- **Universal accessibility rule:** R6 does not prescribe M4(2)/M4(3). F021 requires the project to perform its own life-course test before deciding what earns permanence/provision.
