## D-F01 — Selective permanence / architectural platform

**File:** `docs/manuscript/figures/selective-permanence-platform.svg`  
**Class:** ESSENTIAL ARGUMENT  
**Primary placement:** Part I, after the architectural-platform proposition is introduced in Chapter 4.  
**Source:** Chapters 2–4 and the governing principles.

**Publication caption**

*Selective permanence separates slower architectural commitments from faster-changing systems. The bands are qualitative rather than fixed service lives. Most ordinary renewal should terminate at a designed interface; unavoidable crossings into permanent fabric remain sparse, deliberate and documented.*

**Does not establish:** universal service-life bands, mandatory material families, or a requirement that all layers be physically separate.

## D-F02 — Designed interface

**File:** `docs/manuscript/figures/designed-interface-functions.svg`  
**Class:** ESSENTIAL ARGUMENT  
**Primary placement:** Part II, Chapter 6, immediately after the functional decomposition of an interface.  
**Source:** Chapter 6; PAT-008; PAT-022.

**Publication caption**

*A designed interface allocates duties rather than asking one buried joint to do everything. Support, restraint, movement, boundary continuity, tolerance and future removal are questions to assign deliberately. A real junction may combine some duties and omit others; this is a functional diagram, not a construction detail.*

**Does not establish:** an engineered junction, one required interface build-up, or technical adequacy of any Reference House detail.

## D-F03 — Failure architecture

**File:** `docs/manuscript/figures/failure-architecture-sequence.svg`  
**Class:** ESSENTIAL ARGUMENT  
**Primary placement:** Part II, Chapter 7, after the hierarchy of failure control.  
**Source:** Chapter 7; PAT-017; PAT-018; Fail-Safe Water Distribution strategy.

**Publication caption**

*Failure architecture designs the second consequence as deliberately as the first defence. Passive geometry and locality form the base layer; detection and isolation shorten the event; access, drying and reinstatement complete recovery. The sequence is a review logic, not a prescribed plumbing or control topology.*

**Does not establish:** a universal containment method, mandatory sensors or valves, or performance of an untested assembly.

## D-F04 — Maintenance geography

**File:** `docs/manuscript/figures/maintenance-geography-route.svg`  
**Class:** ESSENTIAL ARGUMENT  
**Primary placement:** Part II, Chapter 8, after the four maintenance-route geometries are defined.  
**Source:** Chapter 8 and `docs/manuscript/maintenance-geography-external.md`.

**Publication caption**

*Maintenance access is a continuous geography, not a hatch. Approach, working space, disconnection and withdrawal must all exist at the scale of the actual person, tool and component. The route may extend through rooms, stairs, roofs, courtyards, landscape and the site entrance; failure in any one geometry breaks the route.*

**Does not establish:** numerical clearances for a particular task, safe-work method, or compliance with project-specific access requirements.

## Graphic conventions

The D1 set is monochrome-first and intentionally non-metric. Solid outlines identify the objects or relationships being discussed; dashed lines identify movement, clearance or qualitative route conditions where the drawing is not asserting a physical construction line. Text inside the diagrams is explanatory, not specification language.

Final insertion, page-scale QA and any typography reduction belong to PR-00D5. If a figure cannot remain legible at candidate-book trim size without adding clutter, simplify the figure rather than shrinking it into technical illegibility.
