# PR-01 R8-C — Transfer Counter-Morphology + Hostile Intervention Test

**Status:** PASS — R7-C02 closed at analytical transfer / candidate-book scope  
**Date:** 2026-10-15  
**Parent:** `publication/pr01-r7-findings-freeze.md`  
**Root defect:** R7-C02 — Transfer beyond a favourable Reference House  
**Raw findings:** PR01-F003, F012, F013, F014

R7 retained the Reference House as a credible worked interpretation but rejected any inference that one generous detached courtyard house demonstrates transfer.

R8-C therefore does **not** redesign the Reference House to make it artificially awkward and does not create a second house project. It applies the smallest hostile counterfixture needed to ask what survives when the conveniences of the Reference House disappear.

The counterfixture is a **compact two-storey terrace condition** with party walls to both sides, front/rear external perimeter only, materially less floor area than the Reference House, no courtyard, no dedicated side maintenance strip and no assumed rear service alley.

The purpose is not to produce a buildable terrace design. It is to discover whether the Ovid’s House proposition transfers as a set of architectural decisions, or whether it secretly means “build something like the Reference House”.

---

# 1. Counterfixture constitution

## T-01 — Compact terrace analytical condition

Assume:

- two principal storeys;
- ordinary family dwelling scale, approximately **105–120 m² GIA** rather than the Reference House’s ~230 m² enclosed-plate reservation;
- frontage constrained to a typical narrow domestic order rather than a four-sided detached plan;
- party walls at east and west;
- front and rear are the only ordinary external envelope faces;
- no courtyard;
- no side route for plant, scaffold, utilities or replacement work;
- no assumption of rear vehicular access or shared alley;
- a conventional stair and ordinary room/circulation budget;
- wet rooms may be stacked, but a dedicated technical corridor is not assumed;
- legal, structural, fire, acoustic and environmental duties remain fully applicable.

The area range is a stress envelope, not a design target. It places the test near ordinary English family-house scale rather than reproducing the Reference House’s spatial generosity. No room schedule or regulatory-compliance claim is inferred from it.

## What the counterfixture deliberately removes

Compared with the Reference House, T-01 removes:

- two side façades as service/maintenance opportunities;
- the courtyard as a second perimeter and working zone;
- the court-generated short-span structural ring;
- the dedicated external plant replacement opening on a side flank;
- a generous east-side service suite;
- the ability to protect every principal room simply by moving technical work to another wing;
- the assumption that façade access can be reserved on private ground along every elevation.

If an Ovid’s House provision survives only by restoring one of those conveniences, it does not transfer economically to T-01.

# 2. Transfer tests

## TT-01 — service concentration

### Reference House expression

A dedicated flank combines utility, plant, WC, vertical service zone, kitchen adjacency and upper bathroom adjacency.

### Terrace pressure

There is not enough area to buy an equivalent flank without materially reducing ordinary rooms. Party walls also make indiscriminate chasing/penetration undesirable because acoustic/fire obligations are concentrated there.

### Smallest transferable response

Keep **wet and high-service uses adjacent/stacked**, but allow the architecture to collapse the dedicated service flank into a compact internal service cluster:

- kitchen/utility/WC/bathroom adjacency where the plan permits;
- one deliberate vertical wet/dry route where useful;
- ordinary cupboards/joinery/local ceiling zones where they already belong;
- no technical corridor merely to preserve a formal service topology.

### Result

**TRANSFER — reduced form.**

The transferable invariant is concentration/locality, not the Reference House’s room-sized service geography.

A separate service flank is therefore a **project response**, not an Ovid’s House requirement.

---

## TT-02 — plant and replacement

### Reference House expression

The Reference House currently reserves an approximately 2.9 × 2.2 m gross plant room and a nominal 1000 mm external replacement opening.

R8-A has already placed the *size* of that room on HOLD pending real equipment/area/cost evidence.

### Terrace pressure

A dedicated ~6 m² plant room can consume an implausibly large share of a compact terrace. A side replacement opening is impossible. If the rear garden has no independent route, every future plant item may also need to pass through ordinary domestic circulation.

### Smallest transferable response

- select plant families partly by ordinary replacement-route constraints;
- provide genuinely usable working clearance around service items;
- keep isolations and routine service components locally accessible;
- prefer a utility/plant cupboard or compact service room that has useful present-day storage/utility value as well as technical value;
- require major replaceable components to pass through a credible door/route rather than reserving a special opening by doctrine;
- if a particular system cannot be replaced without destructive work or a dedicated external route, compare another system before enlarging the house.

### Result

**TRANSFER — materially reduced.**

Plant maintainability survives; a dedicated plant room and special replacement aperture do not.

This is a direct conventional win over a literal transplant of the Reference House solution.

---

## TT-03 — horizontal service distribution

### Reference House expression

The service flank, galleries and selected room routes give services explicit horizontal geography outside primary structure.

### Terrace pressure

Shorter runs make conventional local ceiling/floor/joinery routes more competitive. Buying a continuous accessible spine may consume scarce section or room width for little avoided destruction.

### Smallest transferable response

Use the R8-A hierarchy:

1. coincident B0/B1 routes in skirting/joinery/ordinary ceiling zones where they already exist;
2. local access for recurrent components;
3. a dedicated B2 route only where several E2/E3 systems genuinely aggregate;
4. conventional integrated routing where access infrastructure would cost more than future local opening/making-good.

### Result

**TRANSFER — as a decision rule, not a fixed route.**

A coherent route may be ordinary construction rather than a visible or walkable service zone.

---

## TT-04 — wet-room adjacency and failure geography

### Terrace pressure

This is one of the doctrine’s strongest transfer cases because compactness rewards stacking rather than punishing it.

A bathroom over/near kitchen/utility/WC can shorten drainage and pressurised-water runs while reducing structural crossings. Dry-side access may be much harder because every square metre of circulation matters.

### Result

- **stacking / concentration:** TRANSFER strongly;
- **dry-side access to every wet component:** CONDITIONAL;
- **full accessible service wall:** only where several serviceable items share it and its area/boundary burden remains proportionate;
- **whole-room reversible lining:** REJECT as already established by R8-A.

The terrace therefore preserves the architectural logic while discarding the most spatially expensive implementation.

---

## TT-05 — structural penetrations

### Terrace pressure

A compact repetitive floor can make a conventional engineer-designed joist/service coordination solution simpler than a special penetration architecture.

### Result

The duty survives: do not improvise consequential structural holes. But no special Ovid-specific penetration family is automatically earned.

**TRANSFER AS OBLIGATION, NOT NECESSARILY AS PATTERN.**

This result is carried forward to R8-D’s P005 admission test.

---

## TT-06 — external maintenance geography

### Reference House expression

The detached plot preserves ground-supported working strips, courtyard logistics and a dedicated service side.

### Terrace pressure

Only front and rear façades are ordinarily accessible. Party-wall exterior maintenance is not a relevant task because the wall is not externally exposed. Upper front work may depend on pavement/highway permissions; rear work may depend on garden access through the dwelling if no alley exists.

### Smallest transferable response

- design front/rear openings, gutters, terminals and replaceable external components with the **actual available approach** in mind;
- avoid placing frequent-maintenance plant/terminals where every intervention requires exceptional access if a lower location is available;
- preserve useful rear working ground where the site has it;
- accept that some façade/roof work will rationally require temporary scaffold or specialist access;
- do not reserve non-existent side strips or permanent roof access merely to satisfy a pattern diagram.

### Result

**TRANSFER — site-relative.**

Maintenance geography remains architectural, but its content is whatever the site genuinely owns. “Ground-supported façade access” is not a universal occurrence.

---

## TT-07 — replaceable openings

Window/door replacement remains a strong low-burden case because the frame lifecycle differs from the masonry opening and because no extra plan area is required.

### Result

**TRANSFER strongly at B1**, subject to building-physics and product evidence.

The terrace condition strengthens rather than weakens this part of the proposition.

---

## TT-08 — life-course adaptation

R8-B found a low-burden entrance-storey sleeping/showering path in the generous Reference House.

In a compact terrace, the same outcome may compete directly with the only principal living room or with scarce service space.

### Result

**TRANSFER AS A SCENARIO TEST, NOT A GUARANTEED GEOMETRIC RESULT.**

A compact real project must test whether an entrance-storey adaptation path can be preserved proportionately. If not, the doctrine does not authorise shell growth without comparison; the brief, site, tenure and planning requirements decide the response.

This is an important limit: long-life intent cannot promise every later mode of occupation in every morphology.

| coherent horizontal route | coincident/local conventional routes likely preferred | **decision rule transfers** |
| dry-side wet-room access | local/selected only | **conditional** |
| courtyard maintenance geography | absent | **does not transfer** |
| detached side working strips | absent | **does not transfer** |
| opening/frame life separation | low-area replacement interface | **transfers strongly** |

The result is intentionally lossy. A transferable doctrine should lose project-specific apparatus when the morphology changes.

# 4. Hostile interventions against the Reference House

R7 also found Chapter 18 success-biased. The following scenarios are chosen because they do **not** follow the Reference House’s prepared success paths.

## HI-01 — real site removes the east-side service route

**Event:** a future real site has a tight boundary / neighbour / planning condition that prevents the current east working strip and external plant replacement approach.

**Current prepared path:** east service side.

**Result:** **ARCHITECTURAL / SITE REWIND before construction.**

The virtual house does not possess enough site authority to defend the current handedness. Mirror the service logic, relocate plant/replacement geography, change equipment or alter morphology before freezing the real project.

Do not solve this by claiming the virtual east route is portable evidence.

---

## HI-02 — future ventilation replacement requires materially different duct geometry

**Event:** the selected central ventilation system reaches end of life and the best later replacement family requires different main-duct geometry.

**Current prepared path:** accessible plant plus coordinated but not fully withdrawable ductwork.

**Result:** **DESTRUCTIVE BUT PROPORTIONATE may be acceptable.**

Plant replacement should remain local. Some long-lived duct routes may rationally stay in place, be adapted locally or require limited ceiling/boxing opening. R8-A already rejects a crawlable/accessible route around the entire duct network merely to avoid this event.

The house therefore knowingly accepts some future making-good rather than buying B3 access infrastructure now.

---

## HI-03 — owner wants to move the kitchen to the opposite flank

**Event:** a later household wants the kitchen in the west side of the house, far from the service cluster.

**Current prepared path:** kitchen remains beside the high-service geography.

**Result:** **MAJOR RENOVATION / OUTSIDE REASONABLE ANTICIPATION.**

The architecture should permit renovation in the ordinary sense, but Ovid’s House does not promise that any wet/high-service room can move anywhere without cost. The new owner may accept floor/wall opening, a new drainage route or a larger plan change.

Designing duplicate wet infrastructure across both flanks today would be disproportionate.

---

## HI-04 — vertical service zone becomes saturated after several generations

**Event:** successive alterations consume spare pulling/route capacity and a later service no longer fits.

**Current prepared path:** bounded ~900 × 700 mm zone; explicitly not a generic mixed-services shaft.

**Result:** **LOCAL NEW ROUTE / ACCEPTED LIMITATION.**

Do not enlarge the original riser indefinitely to serve unknown futures. The later project must remove obsolete services, reuse capacity, route a new service locally or accept some opening/making-good. If the new system is large enough to demand whole-house geometry, that is a new architectural project.

The finite riser is a feature, not a failure of future-proofing.

---

## HI-05 — a future envelope upgrade requires thickness outside the current stress-tested family

**Event:** future environmental performance or material replacement calls for an envelope build-up materially deeper than the current 450 mm geometry stress test.

**Current prepared path:** 400–425 mm working wall family; 450 mm geometry stress.

**Result:** **PROJECT REWIND / EXTERNAL ADDITION / SELECTIVE INTERNAL LOSS depending real constraints.**

The house has no universal right to preserve every internal dimension while accepting unlimited future envelope thickness. A later project must compare external build-up, internal loss, opening changes, roof/eaves implications and architectural order.

The correct response may alter the façade or room geometry. No current sacrificial void is reserved for an unknowable envelope family.

# 5. What R8-C establishes

The counterfixture produces a more credible transfer claim than the Reference House alone:

1. **Ovid’s House does not transfer as a kit of spaces.** Plant rooms, service flanks, side access and courtyard working zones can disappear.
2. **The strongest transfer is relational and decision-based:** concentrate wet/high-service work; preserve slower fabric where a low-burden interface earns it; make actual maintenance approach spatially real; keep high-burden future-proofing fail-closed.
3. **Compactness can strengthen some ideas.** Stacked wet rooms and short routes become more valuable.
4. **Compactness can defeat others.** Dedicated access/service infrastructure often loses to excellent conventional construction.
5. **Some future renovation should remain destructive.** The doctrine does not promise frictionless arbitrary change.
6. **Site-specific maintenance claims cannot be validated by a site-free fixture.** The Reference House remains a whole virtual building only at site-independent architectural scope.

---

# 6. Candidate-book consequence

No second worked house is added to Part IV. Doing so would falsely elevate an analytical stress test into a designed project and materially broaden the book.

The candidate-book conclusion to preserve at R8-F is:

> **The Reference House demonstrates coexistence in one deliberately supportive morphology. Transfer is not the replication of its service flank, courtyard or plant room; it is the survival of the decision rules when those devices are unavailable. In compact housing, some Ovid’s House provisions should shrink or disappear.**

The current Part IV already states that:

- there is no commissioned site;
- the house is a virtual integration project rather than a planning proposal;
- the courtyard is project morphology rather than doctrine;
- technical systems are project choices;
- the house is not proof.

Those boundaries remain correct. R8-F should add only the minimum transfer sentence necessary to prevent the worked house from acquiring rhetorical generality by weight of detail.
