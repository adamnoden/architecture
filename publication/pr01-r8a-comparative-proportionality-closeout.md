# PR-01 R8-A — Comparative / Proportionality Correction — Closeout

**Status:** PASS — R7-C01 closed at candidate-book architectural-screening scope  
**Date:** 2026-10-14  
**Parent:** `publication/pr01-r7-findings-freeze.md`  
**Root defect:** R7-C01 — Comparative proportionality and evidence  
**Raw findings:** PR01-F001, F002, F007, F019, F020

R8-A corrects the largest methodological weakness exposed by the adversarial review: Ovid’s House previously had a strong reason to care about future intervention but an insufficiently discriminating method for deciding **how much present building should be bought for that future**.

The correction does not attempt to manufacture a universal whole-life equation. It creates an architectural screening method that can reject Ovid’s House provisions, holds high-burden proposals when project evidence is missing, and restores excellent conventional construction as a positive baseline rather than a weak foil.

Canonical method:

`docs/development/proportional-serviceability-method-v01.md`

The method introduces:

Chapter 19 carries the architectural core without importing the whole research protocol into the book.

## 2. The method demonstrably says no

R7 required at least one conventional win and at least one rejected/reduced Ovid provision. R8-A deliberately exceeds that minimum.

The screening cases reject or bound:

- house-wide raised access flooring for possible future cabling — **REJECT**;
- house-wide reversible bathroom lining — **BASELINE / REJECT as default** because much of the apparent avoided work is rational co-renewal;
- distributed access infrastructure intended mainly to make complete long-lived duct replacement easy — **REJECT / HOLD**;
- structural reserve for an unknown future extra storey — **REJECT**;
- Reference House plant-room *size* — **HOLD** until equipment/area/cost review, despite the plant-withdrawal task itself being strongly justified.

The same cases retain lower-burden provisions where they earn themselves:

- coincident cable/draw routes;
- local service access;
- plant isolation and withdrawal geometry;
- accessible window replacement interfaces;
- clustered dry-side service geography where several real tasks aggregate.

This is the intended asymmetry: **the likelihood of a future event may justify B0/B1 without justifying B2/B3**.

## 3. Client / beneficiary horizon

R8-A also closes F020 conceptually.

Whole-life value is **not** identical to the initiating client’s investment case. For every B2/B3 provision, the option record must identify where relevant:

- who pays the present premium;
- who is likely to receive the future benefit;
- whether the benefit is retained by a long-term owner, transferred to a later owner/trade, or primarily social/environmental;
- whether the case depends on an assumed resale premium for which no evidence exists;
- whether the provision remains defensible if the original client captures none of the later maintenance saving.

A B0/B1 provision may remain rational despite uncertain value capture because its initial burden is negligible/local. A B2/B3 provision that depends on distant benefits requires either an explicit stewardship-oriented brief, a credible project business case, or sufficient non-financial architectural/environmental value to justify the present cost. **Future value is not treated as automatically capitalised into sale price.**

This rule belongs to the R8-A option method even where a later publication proof chooses to compress its wording.

## 5. What remains deliberately unresolved

R8-A does **not** claim exact whole-life cost or carbon savings for the Reference House.

Those require project information the current virtual house does not possess: cost plan, product selections, quantities, site conditions and specialist whole-life assessment. The correction is instead fail-closed:

> **Missing B2/B3 evidence leaves the provision HOLD or BASELINE. It does not grant adoption.**

This means the current Reference House plant-room area, riser area and other spatial service provisions can be re-tested later without the doctrine pre-approving them.

The RICS 60-year reference study period and indicative component lives used in the method are comparison scenarios, not predictions that the building or component will fail on a fixed date.
