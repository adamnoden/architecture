# PR-01 R8-B — Human Life-Course Adaptation Test

**Status:** PASS — R7-C05 closed at architectural-planning / candidate-book scope  
**Date:** 2026-10-15  
**Parent:** `publication/pr01-r7-findings-freeze.md`  
**Root defect:** R7-C05 — Human life-course adaptability  
**Raw finding:** PR01-F021  
**Reference House authority:** current RH-02 / RH-06 geometry and RH-C1 controlled fixture  
**Comparator:** Approved Document M, Volume 1, Category 2 (`M4(2)`) as an adaptable-dwelling comparator, not a compliance claim or automatic project requirement

R7 identified an internal asymmetry in Ovid’s House: the project anticipated the future life of pipes, windows, plant and interfaces more carefully than foreseeable change in the bodies and care needs of the people who occupy the house.

R8-B tests the Reference House against that criticism before changing doctrine or prose.

The result is not “make every Ovid’s House wheelchair housing” and not “reserve a lift because ageing exists”. The result is narrower:

> **Where a low- or moderate-burden early decision can preserve a credible entrance-storey life when stairs become difficult or unusable, human adaptation deserves the same proportionality test as technical adaptation.**

For this Reference House, that test earns a small passive provision in the existing service geography and rejects a more expensive vertical-access reserve.

---

## 1. Comparator boundary

The current public England guidance remains *Approved Document M: Access to and use of buildings, Volume 1: Dwellings*, 2015 edition with 2016 amendments. Category 2 is an optional accessible/adaptable-dwelling requirement where planning requires it. R8-B uses it as a disciplined comparator only.

Relevant Category-2 guidance includes:

- step-free access to rooms and facilities on the entrance storey (`2.23a`);
- a stair from the entrance storey with at least **850 mm clear width** measured 450 mm above the pitch line so a stair-lift can be fitted (`2.23c`);
- an entrance-storey living area (`2.24a`);
- bedroom access zones including **750 mm** access around the relevant bed positions (`2.25`);
- walls/ducts/boxings to required sanitary accommodation capable of supporting later adaptations (`2.26`);
- for a two- or three-storey dwelling with three or more bedrooms, an entrance-storey WC/basin room that also contains an installed or **potential level-access shower** (`2.27c`);
- an outward-opening door to that room (`2.27d`).

Official source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-and-use-of-buildings-approved-document-m

This record does **not** claim M4(2) compliance. The Reference House has no planning condition, detailed door schedule, final internal partitions, threshold package or Building Control review. M4(2) is used to prevent the life-course test from becoming an invented Ovid-specific accessibility standard.

M4(3) wheelchair-user dwelling guidance is not adopted as a default comparator. A client or planning brief requiring independent wheelchair occupation from the outset would be a materially different brief and could legitimately reopen the plan.

---

## 2. Existing Reference House geometry

The current controlled house is a two-storey, four-bedroom courtyard dwelling of approximately 230 m² enclosed plate across the principal storeys.

The relevant existing conditions are:

- `G-FAM-02`, the west/rear family room, has an approximate clear field of **3.5 × 5.3 m** at RH-02 planning assumptions;
- `G-SVC-02`, the ground service suite, occupies approximately **2.9 × 4.0 m gross** beside the internal service gallery;
- a ground WC already belongs to that high-service suite;
- wet, drainage and ventilation routes are deliberately concentrated at this flank;
- the principal stair has a current clear-width target of approximately **1000 mm**;
- the principal entrance is intended to be substantially level, but detailed Category-2 route geometry has not been proven.

The test therefore begins with a useful question: can life-course adaptation be obtained largely by **using geometry already bought for present architectural reasons**, rather than adding another permanent system?

# 3. Scenario tests

## LC-01 — temporary loss of stair use

**Scenario:** injury, surgery, illness or temporary reduced mobility makes the principal stair impractical for weeks or months.

### Current house

`G-FAM-02` is already a substantial enclosed habitable room. Its approximate clear field is large enough to test a double-bed arrangement with Category-2-like access zones without structural alteration. It remains connected to entrance-storey living space and the service flank through the ordinary ground-floor circulation sequence.

### Defect

The current record proves a WC on the entrance storey, not a credible showering path.

### Decision

Preserve `G-FAM-02` as a room capable of temporary sleeping occupation and preserve a step-free internal route between entrance, this room and the ground sanitary room.

This is **B0/B1**: no extra room and no added building area are required. The obligation is primarily not to destroy an already-useful option through later door/threshold/furniture coordination.

**Outcome: ADOPT.**

---

## LC-02 — permanent inability to use stairs

**Scenario:** an occupant can no longer use the principal stair reliably and wishes to remain in the house.

### Excellent conventional response

A good conventional house may adapt a ground living room to sleeping and remodel a nearby WC/utility area to create an accessible shower room. If drainage, structure and circulation are favourable, this is entirely rational and does not require a pre-installed domestic lift.

### Reference House response

The service suite is already the shortest, least destructive place to preserve future showering capability. It carries WC, water, drainage and extract functions now. The proposal is therefore **not** a new wet core.

Reserve, within the existing `G-SVC-02` gross envelope, a future arrangement in which the entrance-storey WC/basin can become or contain a level-access shower without moving the primary stack or extending wet services through a principal room.

This may require rebalancing the present plant / utility / WC subdivision. It does **not** justify enlarging the 2.9 × 4.0 m service-suite envelope. R8-A has already placed the present plant-room size on HOLD pending real equipment/area evidence; life-course adaptation is a legitimate competing use of that same finite service geography.

Exact internal dimensions remain a later planning/regulatory proof. The current conclusion is only that the gross service suite contains enough area and the correct service adjacency to make the option credible without shell growth.

### Decision

- preserve a potential level-access shower/WC geometry inside `G-SVC-02`;
- keep its future drainage/ventilation route local to the existing high-service flank;
- locally strengthen relevant sanitary-room wall fields for credible future grab rails / seats where that can be done at negligible or local burden;
- do not increase total house area to achieve the provision.

This is a **local B1/B2 adaptation provision** with high retrofit value and strong adjacency to infrastructure already required.

**Outcome: ADOPT at architectural-planning scope; detailed layout remains a professional/regulatory gate.**

---

## LC-03 — stair-lift rather than lift

**Scenario:** an occupant retains some transfer/mobility capacity but requires mechanical assistance between storeys.

The current stair target is approximately **1000 mm clear**. The Category-2 comparator asks for at least 850 mm clear at the stated measurement position to permit a stair-lift.

The Reference House therefore already carries the lower-burden vertical-adaptation option as a consequence of the stair’s ordinary architectural width.

The next stair design stage should avoid casually reducing that usable width below the adaptation comparator unless another architectural/regulatory obligation requires it.

**Burden:** B0 at current geometry.  
**Outcome: ADOPT / preserve.**

This is not evidence that a future proprietary stair-lift will fit every final stair detail. It is a reason not to destroy a low-cost option already present.

---

## LC-04 — reserve a domestic lift shaft now

**Scenario:** future occupant cannot use stairs and wishes to access the whole upper floor independently.

A lift could solve this elegantly in a specific brief. As a speculative universal provision, however, it would consume permanent plan/section, introduce structural and fire/door/interface obligations, predict future equipment geometry and compete directly with useful domestic space.

The lower-burden alternatives already available are:

- entrance-storey sleeping;
- entrance-storey WC / potential shower;
- stair-lift-compatible width;
- later project-specific lift intervention if a real need and viable location emerge.

Under the R8-A proportionality method, a permanent lift reservation is therefore a **B3-like whole-house spatial commitment** whose present burden is not earned by the generic possibility of future reduced mobility.

**Outcome: REJECT as Reference House default.**

A client brief, planning requirement or known occupant need can reverse that decision on a real project.

---

## LC-05 — long-term wheelchair occupation

**Scenario:** an occupant uses a wheelchair for most or all domestic movement and expects independent use of the complete dwelling.

R8-B does not claim that the Reference House satisfies M4(3), nor does it infer whole-dwelling wheelchair usability from the ground-floor adaptation path.

The entrance-storey strategy materially improves resilience, but full wheelchair-user occupation can impose different requirements on approach, parking, circulation, kitchens, bedrooms, storage, sanitary accommodation, doors and vertical movement.

**Outcome: OUTSIDE CURRENT BRIEF / REOPEN ON REAL REQUIREMENT.**

This boundary is important. A long-life house should preserve cheap options where it can; it should not claim every future body can be served without redesign.

---

## LC-06 — assistance and care

**Scenario:** an occupant remains at home but requires intermittent or live-in assistance.

The ground-floor adaptation path gives a sleeping room and sanitary provision without converting the entrance hall or technical spaces into care infrastructure. The rest of the ground floor retains ordinary domestic rooms, allowing family/care support to coexist without institutionalising the house in advance.

No dedicated carer room is reserved because no present brief earns one.

**Outcome: existing plan is adequate at scenario level; no new permanent provision.**

# 4. Proportionality register

| Provision | Event | Burden | Comparator result | Decision |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Preserve family room as possible entrance-storey sleeping room | reduced mobility / stair loss | B0/B1 | uses an existing proper room; no area premium | **ADOPT** |
| Preserve step-free entrance-storey route | reduced mobility | B0/B1 at current planning stage | aligns with good adaptable-dwelling practice and current level-threshold intent | **ADOPT; detailed proof later** |
| Potential level-access shower/WC within existing service suite | stair loss / ageing / temporary disability | B1/B2 | local high-service geography avoids later destructive wet-core creation | **ADOPT at planning scope** |
| Local strengthening for sanitary adaptations | later grab rails/seats | B0/B1 | cheap before finishes; disruptive later | **ADOPT where relevant** |
| Preserve current stair width against stair-lift comparator | reduced mobility | B0 | current architectural stair already exceeds comparator width | **ADOPT / preserve** |
| Permanent domestic lift shaft | possible future full vertical-access need | B3-like | lower-burden one-level + stair-lift paths exist | **REJECT as default** |
| Whole-house M4(3)-type geometry without brief | possible future wheelchair use | B3/systemic | changes present architecture materially without project requirement | **REJECT as universal default / reopen on brief** |

The method therefore says both **yes** and **no**. Human adaptability is not being used to justify unlimited future-proofing.

# 5. Architectural consequence

R8-B causes a **bounded Reference House correction**, not a morphology rewind.

No change is earned to:

- courtyard morphology;
- outer shell;
- four-bedroom upper programme;
- principal stair location;
- structural grid;
- G-01 grammar;
- service-flank location.

The changes earned are:

1. treat `G-FAM-02` as a deliberately retained future entrance-storey sleeping option as well as a present family room;
2. require detailed design to preserve a credible step-free route from principal entrance to entrance-storey living, `G-FAM-02` and sanitary provision;
3. require the ground service-suite subdivision to prove a WC/basin + potential level-access shower arrangement before the internal partitions are frozen;
4. treat the current plant-room reservation as competing for the same finite service-suite area rather than as untouchable infrastructure;
5. preserve the current generous stair width as a low-burden adaptation asset;
6. reject a speculative lift shaft.

The first four items must be carried into the Reference House implementation schedule / later detailed plan gate. They are not claims that the current schematic drawing has already demonstrated regulatory compliance.

---

# 6. Doctrinal consequence

The project’s future-change test now explicitly includes changes in **occupant capability and care needs**, not only changes in services, technology, regulation and ownership.

The general rule is still proportional:

> **Design for the foreseeable change in people where a modest present decision can prevent a severe future loss of use. Do not turn every possible future incapacity into permanent infrastructure.**

This belongs in scenario review and whole-life proportionality. R8-B does not add a twelfth governing principle. The constitution of the governing principles is owned later by R7-C07.

The correction is consistent with “accessible ≠ exposed”: latent wall strengthening, local service adjacency and retained room geometry need not make a house look adapted before adaptation is needed.

---

# 7. Evidence / uncertainty boundary

What R8-B establishes:

- the frozen criticism was valid;
- the Reference House has a credible low-burden one-level adaptation path;
- the ground sanitary provision must become more capable than a visitability-only WC;
- a lift reservation is not proportionate as a generic response;
- later detailed design has explicit adaptation geometry to prove.

What it does **not** establish:

- M4(2) compliance;
- M4(3) compliance;
- suitability for every disability or care model;
- detailed sanitary clearances;
- stair-lift product compatibility;
- planning applicability on a future site.

A real project must confirm its actual planning / Building Regulations route and the needs of its occupants.

---

# 8. Gate result

**R8-B — PASS.**

**R7-C05 / F021 — CLOSED at architectural-planning and candidate-book scope.**

The Reference House is not enlarged and no lift is reserved. A smaller, better-targeted intervention is earned instead: one-level occupation is kept credible by preserving a real ground-floor room, a step-free route and future showering capacity inside service geography the house already needs.

The candidate freeze remains broken from R8-A; R9 will create the revised candidate identity only after all R8 packages are complete.
