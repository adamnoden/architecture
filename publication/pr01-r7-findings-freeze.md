# 2. R7-C01 — Comparative proportionality and evidence

**Severity:** S1 — material  
**Raw findings:** F001, F002, F007, F019, F020  
**Owners:** doctrine / evidence / publication / delivery

## Frozen strongest criticism

The book is unusually good at explaining *why* coupling and future intervention matter, but it has not yet established the **magnitude and threshold** of the problem strongly enough to discipline its own response.

Three deficits are one underlying defect:

1. the comparator is not yet consistently an **excellent lean conventional house**;
2. the book’s proportionality rule is not operational enough to reject attractive Ovid’s House provisions reproducibly;
3. the central avoided-collateral-destruction mechanism lacks a bounded domestic comparative baseline showing how much sound slower fabric common interventions actually consume and whether avoiding that work repays present area/material/process burden.

The same discipline must apply to permanence: the architect should not be allowed to call a favoured room/stair/wall permanent without exposing the future constraints that choice creates.

A further boundary belongs here: whole-life value and client investment horizon are not identical. A provision may benefit a future owner without being financially rational to the person paying today.

## R8 closure condition

C01 closes only when the revised work contains a **falsifiable comparative decision method** and uses it on representative cases.

Minimum evidence expected:

- named excellent conventional baseline;
- representative domestic intervention cases;
- present provision burden: cost / material / carbon / area / complexity where material;
- future event assumptions and uncertainty;
- layers disturbed under conventional and Ovid responses;
- boundary/reinstatement consequences;
- client / beneficiary horizon where relevant;
- at least one case where the conventional answer wins;
- at least one case where an initially attractive Ovid provision is rejected or reduced.

R8 must not fabricate pseudo-precision. A bounded option record with ranges/uncertainty is sufficient if it genuinely permits rejection.

## Freeze consequence

**Substantive.** Changes to the central thesis, principle wording, evidence or major publication framing break the PR-00 candidate freeze and require R9 re-freeze.

# 3. R7-C02 — Transfer beyond a favourable Reference House

**Severity:** S1 — material  
**Raw findings:** F003, F012, F013, F014  
**Owners:** Reference House / evidence / publication

## Frozen strongest criticism

The Reference House is a credible integration vehicle but an unusually **supportive fixture** for the doctrine:

- detached perimeter;
- generous four-bedroom area;
- central courtyard creating extra perimeter and a maintenance working zone;
- short structure between outer and inner masonry;
- shallow multi-aspect room bands;
- concentrated service flank;
- no actual neighbours, planning boundary, utility position, drainage condition, orientation or site-access conflict.

Its internal coordination proves coexistence in **one favourable morphology**. It does not yet test transfer into the compact/constrained conditions where maintenance geography and serviceability become most expensive.

Chapter 18 also tests mostly interventions the selected topology was designed to handle. A serious worked-house test should expose where the platform deliberately stops.

The no-site issue is retained here at a lower force than F012: the frozen book already states the limitation well. R7 downgrades F014’s independent force; it becomes a scope correction inside C02 rather than its own S2 workstream.

## R8 closure condition

C02 closes when the project does **one** of the following honestly:

### Preferred

Create a small analytical **counter-morphology / transfer test** that is deliberately hostile to the Reference House conveniences. It must not become a second fully designed house.

Credible test types include:

- compact terrace;
- constrained rectangular detached/semi-detached house;
- apartment/maisonette condition where external perimeter/control is limited.

Test only relationships necessary to answer transfer:

- service concentration;
- plant/replacement route;
- wet-room adjacency;
- horizontal/vertical distribution;
- maintenance geography;
- boundary/access burden;
- area/section penalty.

### Plus

Add a small **hostile intervention set** to the Reference House itself, chosen from outside its prepared success paths. The test should be allowed to conclude:

- handled locally;
- destructive but proportionate;
- requires architectural rewind;
- unreasonable to anticipate.

### Scope wording

Where site reality remains absent, describe Reference House coordination at **site-independent architectural / virtual scope** rather than allowing “whole-building” to imply tested site performance.

## Freeze consequence

Likely substantive. An analytical counterfixture may not alter the Reference House itself, but any resulting doctrine/pattern/house correction requires R9 re-freeze.

# 4. R7-C03 — Pattern-language claim and taxonomy

**Severity:** S1 — material  
**Raw findings:** F008, F009, F010, F011  
**Owners:** pattern language / publication

## Frozen strongest criticism

The active corpus contains many strong recurring architectural responses, but the **language-level claim is ahead of the demonstrated generative structure**.

The service-topology family has genuine sequence and dependency. Outside it, many patterns are primarily standalone. The corpus is also strongly weighted toward serviceability, maintenance and technical interfaces relative to the wider architectural thesis.

One active entry, `HSA-P-005 — Designed Structural Penetration`, may not clear the project’s own pattern-versus-rule threshold: its core proposition is close to a coordination obligation rather than a generative spatial pattern.

Stable `HSA-P-*` identities are useful internal provenance but currently carry more reader-facing weight than architectural meaning warrants.

## R8 closure condition

C03 closes when the project chooses the **truthful smallest status** rather than defending the phrase “pattern language” institutionally.

R8 must:

1. rerun the admission test on P005;
2. inspect whether additional relationships/sequences among the **existing** patterns are genuinely evidenced by recurrence and design logic;
3. prohibit synthetic graph completion merely to preserve the language label;
4. state the scope of the corpus explicitly if it is primarily a long-life/serviceability architectural pattern set;
5. decide whether “pattern language” remains earned after that work;
6. retain stable IDs internally but demote them reader-facing where they compete with names.

A smaller catalogue/pattern set is an acceptable result. Pattern count must not be preserved.

## Freeze consequence

**Substantive.** Reclassifying/removing a canonical pattern or materially changing Part III’s status breaks the candidate freeze.

# 5. R7-C04 — Delivery, interface execution and stewardship

**Severity:** S1 — material  
**Raw findings:** F004, F015, F016, F017, F018  
**Owners:** delivery / doctrine / patterns / evidence / publication

## Frozen strongest criticism

The project may have designed an unusually coherent **professional process** around the house and then attributed too much of the resulting reliability to architecture alone.

The current implementation model expects, where applicable:

- explicit interface ownership;
- responsibility matrices;
- boundary registers;
- tolerance and movement schedules;
- option records;
- prototypes / first articles;
- representative-installer trials;
- hold points;
- broader substitution review;
- maintainability commissioning;
- building passport / physical index;
- later change control.

Those controls are individually sensible and often extensions of normal professional practice. Collectively they may require higher professional continuity, fees and procurement discipline than an ordinary domestic project can assume.

At the physical level, separability can also increase the number of boundaries that future trades must correctly reinstate. At the long-term level, the platform depends partly on later owners preserving access, labels, routes and records.

## R8 closure condition

C04 closes only when the project distinguishes three layers:

### A. Architectural robustness that survives weak stewardship

Geometry / passive organisation / obvious isolation / accessible routes / recoverable interfaces that remain intelligible even if records degrade.

### B. Minimum delivery constitution

The smallest set of controls required for an Ovid’s House project to be delivered credibly. Identify which controls are universal and which activate only because a non-standard detail is selected.

### C. Enhanced stewardship

Building passport, physical index, change control and later record updating. These are beneficial but must not be mistaken for inherent permanence.

R8 must also:

- audit the Reference House interface-family count / novelty budget;
- distinguish interfaces expected to cycle repeatedly from those merely replaceable in principle;
- identify procurement acceptance authority for critical substitutions / interface properties;
- run at least one **degraded-stewardship scenario**;
- preserve representative-installer testing only where it demonstrates ordinary executability rather than exceptional supervision.

## Freeze consequence

Substantive if it changes doctrine, patterns or major delivery claims. Argument-neutral implementation clarification alone is insufficient to close C04.

# 6. R7-C05 — Human life-course adaptability

**Severity:** S1 — material  
**Raw finding:** F021  
**Owners:** doctrine / Reference House / evidence

## Frozen strongest criticism

The project anticipates future technical change more seriously than predictable change in the bodies and care needs of the people who inhabit the house.

The Reference House currently protects baseline visitability but places all bedrooms and the family bathroom upstairs behind a deliberately permanent principal stair. No equivalent life-course scenario tests:

- temporary or permanent reduced mobility;
- ageing in place;
- entrance-storey sleeping/bathing;
- assistance/care;
- whether a cheap early spatial/service provision would make later adaptation proportionate.

This is internally material because the project’s own test—frequency, consequence and retrofit difficulty—would appear to give foreseeable human adaptation high priority.

## R8 closure condition

C05 must be attacked architecturally before it is edited rhetorically.

Run a bounded **life-course adaptation test** against the Reference House and doctrine. Use established accessible/adaptable dwelling guidance such as M4(2) as a comparator, not an automatic requirement.

Possible legitimate outcomes include:

- current plan adapts adequately with minor work;
- a small passive provision is earned;
- a Reference House project decision is exposed as too rigid and must rewind;
- the doctrine’s long-life domestic claim is narrowed.

Do not reserve a lift, enlarge circulation or redesign the house merely to make the finding disappear.

## Freeze consequence

**Potential architectural rewind.** Resolve C05 before lower-consequence editorial corrections because it may alter Reference House programme/plan or the doctrine’s definition of selective permanence.

# 7. R7-C06 — Repose / apparent equilibrium

**Severity:** S2 — significant  
**Raw finding:** F005  
**Owners:** principle / evidence / publication

## Frozen strongest criticism

Repose is a useful domestic criterion and survives broad attack. Its weakest element is the normative weight given to **apparent structural support/equilibrium**.

The project’s own evidence audit calls the comfort inference a doctrine hypothesis. Keeping it inside a canonical principle can make a preference-dependent aesthetic inference appear co-equal with stronger evidence around thermal comfort, acoustics, privacy, retreat and local control.

## R8 closure condition

Either:

- find materially stronger direct evidence; or, preferably unless that evidence exists,
- demote apparent equilibrium to an explicitly bounded architectural hypothesis / review prompt rather than a core evidence-backed component of Repose.

Preserve the broader Repose principle unless another finding independently defeats it.

# 8. R7-C07 — Governing-principle constitution

**Severity:** S2 — significant  
**Raw finding:** F006  
**Owners:** doctrine / publication

## Frozen strongest criticism

The eleven principles mix:

- propositions distinctive to Ovid’s House;
- broad competent-practice constraints;
- implementation disciplines.

The result risks making the doctrine comprehensive rather than discriminating. If everything admirable is constitutional, the project becomes harder to falsify and its distinctive contribution becomes harder to remember.

F021 adds pressure in the opposite direction: a major long-life domestic question was absent while some baseline professional disciplines have principle status.

## R8 closure condition

After C01/C05/C06 have clarified the thesis, rerun the eleven principles as a **constitution test**:

- What proposition disappears if this principle is removed?
- Is it doctrine, constraint, strategy or implementation discipline?
- Does it uniquely shape architecture?
- Is it independently falsifiable?

Do not target a smaller number aesthetically. Retain eleven if all eleven still earn constitutional status. Merge/demote only where the category is wrong.

Any principle add/remove/material rewrite breaks the freeze.

# 9. R7-C08 — Future-climate robustness

**Severity:** S2 — significant  
**Raw finding:** F022  
**Owners:** doctrine / evidence / Reference House implementation

## Frozen strongest criticism

The book says a building’s external context can change while fabric remains sound, but future climate does not receive the same explicit treatment as changing technology/regulation.

Long-lived decisions—orientation, opening distribution, shading opportunity, thermal mass, roof/drainage geometry, purge paths, landscape and envelope—are climate-dependent and expensive to change later.

## R8 closure condition

Add future climate as an explicit **real-project environmental robustness gate** and research question. A real project should test permanent geometry against a reasonable range of future weather/hazard conditions appropriate to its site.

Do not promote one future-weather assumption into universal doctrine and do not fictionalise a site for the Reference House.

This is primarily a scope/evidence correction unless future real-project testing later forces architecture.
