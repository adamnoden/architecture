# PR-01 R3 — Reference House Attack

**Status:** COMPLETE — diagnostic findings recorded; frozen candidate unchanged  
**Date:** 2026-10-14  
**Programme:** `publication/pr01-adversarial-review-programme.md`  
**Review map:** `publication/pr01-findings-register.md`  
**Frozen candidate content:** `f6b286e883f24b322f41f85d8d3ad796b103a53d`

R3 attacks the Reference House as evidence-bearing architecture rather than as an internally coordinated project.

The house receives no credit in this pass merely because:

- RH-00→RH-11 are complete at their declared virtual scope;
- G-01 v0.1 conformance passes;
- compiler outputs are deterministic;
- the publication carefully qualifies technical uncertainty.

Those controls are valuable, but R3 asks a different question:

> **Has the worked house actually made Ovid’s House harder to believe, or has a favourable morphology allowed the project to demonstrate its own preferences under unusually supportive conditions?**

No Reference House source, geometry, maturity or publication prose is changed in R3.

---

# 1. Adversarial baseline

The Reference House is explicitly one interpretation, not proof. That protects it from a false demand for representativeness.

But a worked integration vehicle still carries persuasive weight. A reader sees whether the doctrine can inhabit a real plan, section, structure and technical concept without collapsing. The quality of that test therefore depends on **how much resistance the fixture creates**.

The current fixture has several unusually supportive conditions:

- detached ownership and external perimeter access;
- a large four-bedroom programme;
- a central courtyard that creates a second perimeter;
- shallow occupied bands around the court;
- short structural spans between outer and inner masonry walls;
- one concentrated service flank;
- an open court capable of occasional maintenance occupation;
- freedom to reject an en-suite and other programme conveniences because there is no external client;
- no actual planning boundary, neighbour, utility, drainage, orientation or site-access constraint.

That does not make the design invalid. It defines what the house can and cannot demonstrate.

# 2. Existing findings strengthened by R3

## PR01-F002 — proportional serviceability is not yet operational enough

**R3 materially strengthens this R1 finding.**

The Reference House implementation schedule states directly that **house-specific whole-life cost/carbon comparison is incomplete**, including the area/material penalties of non-standard provisions. This is the exact evidence required to test whether the present service hub, riser, maintenance geography, interface provisions and future-access allowances earn themselves.

The house therefore demonstrates **coordination** of serviceability provisions, not yet their **proportionality**.

R3 does not create a duplicate finding. PR01-F002 should carry this Reference House evidence into R7.

## PR01-F003 — compact / ordinary-housing generality remains unproved

**R3 materially strengthens this R1 finding.**

The Reference House correctly stopped calling itself a small house. Its controlled gross enclosed plate proxy is approximately **230.4 m²** across two storeys; RH-10 gives a rough pre-partition internal floor field of approximately **188 m²** after the 450 mm envelope stress test.

For scale only—not as a design target—the England Nationally Described Space Standard gives **97–124 m²** minimum gross internal area for a two-storey four-bedroom dwelling depending on bedspaces. The comparison does not prove that the Reference House is wasteful; minimum standards are not quality benchmarks and the RH figure is not an exact final GIA. It does show that this is a generous fixture in which plant, utility, access and circulation space can be purchased more readily than in much ordinary housing.

R3 therefore strengthens the need either to test transfer into a materially tighter morphology or to keep the generality claim narrow.

Canonical external scale reference: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/technical-housing-standards-nationally-described-space-standard/technical-housing-standards-nationally-described-space-standard

# 3. New findings

## PR01-F012 — the Reference House is a favourable fixture, not a hard counter-morphology test

- **Dimension:** `INCOMPLETE`
- **Severity:** **S1 — material**
- **Surface:** `P4-01 / P4-03 / P4-04 / P4-05`
- **Claim under attack:** The Reference House meaningfully stress-tests the architectural consequences of Ovid’s House by forcing its propositions to coexist in one specific house.
- **Strongest fair criticism:** The fixture does force internal collisions, but its selected morphology solves several of the doctrine’s hardest problems simultaneously. The courtyard creates short structural spans, shallow/multi-aspect room bands, a strong orienting centre and a secondary external working zone. The detached type preserves ordinary external façade access. The dedicated east service flank lets wet rooms, plant, utility, riser, terminals and replacement access cluster with little conflict. These are good design decisions, but together they make the fixture unusually hospitable to maintenance geography and selective permanence.
- **Why it matters:** A self-compatible fixture can prove internal coherence without revealing how much the doctrine survives when perimeter, area, ownership, structure and service adjacency are hostile. The Reference House must not acquire implicit evidential weight for transfer merely because it is detailed.
- **Evidence / counterexample:** RH-10 itself records the courtyard as structurally useful, environmentally useful and spatially orienting; RH-05 uses its inner/outer support ring to achieve short spans; RH-08 uses the court as temporary maintenance geography; the service flank concentrates high-service uses; external maintenance assumes detached working lines. None of those is hidden, but their combination materially reduces resistance.
- **Owner:** Reference House / evidence
- **Class:** `EVIDENCE FINDING`
- **Provisional consequence:** Do **not** redesign the Reference House to make it artificially awkward. In R8, either (a) introduce one deliberately hostile counter-morphology / transfer exercise—e.g. compact terrace, constrained detached rectangle or apartment-like condition—at analytical scope, or (b) state more forcefully that the Reference House demonstrates coexistence in one favourable morphology only and cannot support transfer claims. The smallest credible counterfixture is preferred over a second full house programme.
- **Disposition:** `OPEN`

### R3 judgement

The finding attacks evidential role, not architectural merit. The courtyard and service flank may be excellent architecture precisely because they coordinate many concerns at once.

## PR01-F013 — the future-intervention suite is success-biased toward scenarios the selected topology already handles well

- **Dimension:** `INCOMPLETE`
- **Severity:** **S2 — significant**
- **Surface:** `P4-06`
- **Claim under attack:** Chapter 18’s future-maintenance scenarios provide a sufficiently adversarial test of the house’s long-life proposition.
- **Strongest fair criticism:** The scenario set is plausible but largely follows the architecture’s strongest prepared routes: plant through the plant-room opening, windows through designed frame interfaces, leaks in the service suite, bathroom work from the dry side, a new small dry service through selected route families, roof/gutter work through preserved access, and ordinary household change within enduring rooms. The suite contains few interventions chosen specifically because they **fight** the current topology.
- **Why it matters:** A long-life house should know where its platform stops. If scenarios are selected mainly where the architecture succeeds, the reader cannot see the cost of change that falls outside the anticipated graph.
- **Evidence / counterexample:** Credible hostile interventions include full replacement of a soil/vent stack; failure or renewal of the short buried courtyard drain; major MVHR duct replacement; service-riser saturation after several generations; moving a wet room to the opposite flank; deep envelope/insulation upgrade after performance standards change; a new technology whose plant geography conflicts with the 1000 mm replacement opening; or repair of a primary boundary after repeated access. The house need not make all of these easy. It should expose which ones trigger local destruction, accepted limitation or architectural rewind.
- **Owner:** Reference House / publication
- **Class:** `EVIDENCE FINDING`
- **Provisional consequence:** Add a bounded **hostile intervention set** at R8, explicitly chosen from outside the architecture’s prepared success paths. For each, record: handled locally / destructive but acceptable / requires project rewind / outside reasonable anticipation. Do not add permanent infrastructure merely to make the scenarios pass.
- **Disposition:** `OPEN`

## PR01-F014 — the no-site condition prevents several Reference House systems from functioning as genuine integration tests

- **Dimension:** `WEAK`
- **Severity:** **S2 — significant**
- **Surface:** `P4-01 / P4-04 / P4-05`
- **Claim under attack:** The Reference House has forced weather, environmental strategy, external maintenance and site-facing infrastructure to coexist strongly enough to support its worked-house role.
- **Strongest fair criticism:** The book is exemplary about admitting that there is no site, but some of the most important Ovid’s House claims are spatially external: scaffold/tower bearing and approach, neighbour/highway dependence, utility entry, heat-pump acoustics, solar control, actual daylight/overheating, rainwater discharge, threshold levels and planning relationships. A surrogate “medium London / Zone-4-type plot” cannot meaningfully create the conflicts that decide those questions.
- **Why it matters:** Explicitly labelling a limitation prevents overclaim, but it does not transform the missing test into evidence. The Reference House is a whole-building integration fixture only **inside the boundary of site-independent architecture**.
- **Evidence / counterexample:** The implementation schedule assigns site gates to planning, ground/foundations, utilities, external maintenance, heating location, cooling/overheating, PV, roof/site drainage and visitability. RH-08 likewise stops at building-side drainage and says final discharge cannot be selected without soil/groundwater/sewer data.
- **Owner:** Reference House / publication
- **Class:** `QUALIFICATION`
- **Provisional consequence:** R8 should make the integration boundary explicit wherever the book summarises Reference House maturity: “whole virtual building at site-independent architectural scope” or equivalent. Do not manufacture a fictional site merely to close this finding. A later real project is the legitimate test.
- **Disposition:** `OPEN`

### R3 judgement

This finding is intentionally not S1 because the publication already behaves honestly about the boundary. It is a precision correction to the phrase “whole-building integration”, not an accusation of hidden site fiction.

# 4. Attacks that did not earn findings

## R3-RJ01 — “The Reference House falsely claims to be buildable / compliant”

**Rejected.**

Part IV, the RH README and the implementation schedule repeatedly distinguish architectural coordination from structural capacity, building physics, Building Regulations compliance, product adequacy and physical proof. Open professional/physical/site gates are unusually visible. A hostile review should attack the actual bounded claim rather than demand that M3 virtual coordination already be construction documentation.

## R3-RJ02 — “G-01 was tuned to make the Reference House pass”

**Rejected at current internal scope.**

G-01 v0.1 was frozen before D12. The grammar-completion programme had already removed project-convenient universal ratios, centred entrances, mandatory double-height and the earlier roof-subordination wording. The Reference House trial could have required house rewind but did not. Same-project authorship remains a limitation for external validity, but procedural contamination is controlled well enough that R3 does not create a new finding.

## R3-RJ03 — “The courtyard has leaked upstream into Ovid’s House doctrine or G-01”

**Rejected.**

The authority stack is explicit and consistently maintained: courtyard = project morphology. Part IV repeatedly separates it from doctrine and G-01. R3 finds no material textual evidence that other houses are expected to use a courtyard.

## R3-RJ04 — “The low-slope outward-draining roof is being presented as the Ovid’s House roof”

**Rejected.**

The roof is repeatedly described as a project response to this courtyard’s failure geography; G-01 owns only integrated upper termination. A later hostile-architect pass may still challenge whether the option comparison was architecturally/technically wise, but no upstream generalisation is present in the frozen candidate.

## R3-RJ05 — “The Reference House proves the pattern language because it contains many patterns”

**Rejected as an attack on the frozen text because the book already rejects the inference.**

Part III, Part IV and the occurrence register state explicitly that project occurrence does not strengthen general pattern evidence. The more relevant R3 attack is PR01-F012: the house can still become rhetorically persuasive in ways its formal evidence rules forbid.

The Reference House survives R3 as a **coherent worked interpretation**, and its own records are notably candid about unresolved engineering/site/prototype gates. What R3 removes is any temptation to treat that coherence as a test of generality or proportionality.

The next pass must be harsher and less procedurally sympathetic.
