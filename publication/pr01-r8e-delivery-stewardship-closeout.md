# PR-01 R8-E — Delivery, Interface Execution + Stewardship Closeout

**Status:** PASS — R7-C04 closed at argument / delivery-constitution scope; candidate wording reconciliation remains R8-F  
**Date:** 2026-10-15  
**Parent:** `publication/pr01-r7-findings-freeze.md`  
**Root defect:** R7-C04 — Delivery, interface execution and stewardship  
**Raw findings:** PR01-F004, F015, F016, F017, F018

R7 found a serious attribution risk: Ovid’s House may describe a coherent professional process and then credit the resulting reliability to architecture alone.

R8-E accepts the criticism.

The corrected position separates three layers that must not be conflated:

> **A. architecture that remains useful when stewardship is weak; B. a minimum delivery constitution needed to realise consequential non-standard work; C. enhanced stewardship that improves later care but is not inherent permanence.**

---

# 1. Layer A — architectural robustness under weak stewardship

An Ovid’s House project should retain useful long-life properties even if the original BIM model, architect, manuals, cloud accounts or enthusiastic first owner disappear.

The strongest properties are physical and spatial:

- wet/high-service uses are geographically concentrated where proportionate;
- major plant and recurrent service items have real working/replacement space;
- principal isolation is in an ordinary discoverable service location;
- slower fabric is not the routine distribution zone for faster systems;
- important crossings are sparse rather than improvised everywhere;
- openings and replaceable frames are materially legible as separate assemblies;
- external maintenance tasks have an actual approach/working position where the site permits it;
- ordinary fasteners and commodity technical parts remain preferred;
- quiet conventional construction remains the baseline where serviceability infrastructure has no task.

These relationships should remain intelligible enough for a competent future trade to recover them even from an incomplete record.

This is the **weak-stewardship floor**. The architecture must not require perfect record continuity merely to avoid becoming dangerous or incomprehensible.

---

# 2. Layer B — minimum delivery constitution

Ovid’s House does require competent professional delivery. The correction is to identify the **smallest set of controls actually required**, not to universalise every desirable project-management technique.

## B1 — universal controls for consequential interfaces

For an interface that affects structure, fire, moisture, acoustics, weathering, major replacement or another high-consequence duty:

1. identify the designer/discipline responsible for the combined interface;
2. identify applicable boundary duties;
3. coordinate host, route/component and required geometry before concealment;
4. state acceptance evidence at the precision appropriate to the project;
5. preserve an as-built record of critical isolation / route / boundary changes.

These are extensions of competent professional coordination, not claims of a novel procurement system.

## B2 — controls activated only by non-standard or high-burden selections

Use only when the selected architecture creates the need:

- B2/B3 option record / whole-life comparison;
- prototype or first article;
- representative-installer trial;
- special tolerance schedule;
- hold point before concealment;
- manufacturing / remanufacturing record;
- substitution review beyond ordinary product equivalence;
- removal/reinstatement test.

A conventional wall, floor or standard engineered opening does **not** acquire this machinery merely because the project has a doctrine.

## B3 — representative-installer rule retained narrowly

The existing representative-installer test survives, but only for a **repeated non-standard assembly whose installation logic is not already ordinary trade practice** and where mistakes can be embedded before they become visible.

It is not a ritual second installer for every detail.

The test answers one question:

> **Can a competent person who did not invent the detail assemble it correctly from the issued information and parts without designer rescue?**

If the assembly is already conventional and well understood, ordinary inspection / workmanship control is sufficient.

---

# 3. Layer C — enhanced stewardship

The following can materially improve later maintenance and change:

- building passport;
- detailed digital model;
- physical service index;
- maintenance history;
- change-control record;
- component/fabrication archive;
- periodic record updating.

But these are **stewardship systems**, not evidence that the architecture itself is permanent.

The corrected doctrine therefore distinguishes:

- architecture that remains recoverable if records degrade;
- information that makes recovery faster/safer;
- optional long-term governance that requires future owners to participate.

A building passport can be lost. The building should not become unintelligible because the password was.

---

# 4. Reference House interface-family / novelty budget

R7 required an explicit audit because “ordinary competent workmanship” is not credible if every junction is a bespoke mechanism.

RH-09 already gives enough authority to classify the current occupied-interface burden.

## N0 — conventional baseline / no novelty budget consumed

- W0 direct mineral/plaster finish;
- continuous principal-room floors;
- ordinary commodity plant/components;
- ordinary access doors in technical spaces;
- standard structure/envelope construction subject to normal specialist design.

These remain the majority condition.

## N1 — conventional construction with explicitly designed lifecycle relationship

These may require careful detailing but need not require novel hardware or fabrication:

- `OW-01` permanent opening / replaceable window;
- `OD-01` permanent opening / replaceable doorset;
- `TH-01` external threshold;
- controlled service penetrations / P005 crossings;
- dry-side bathroom service access;
- W1 sparse attachment grounds;
- removable skirting / selected door-surround room route.

These consume **coordination attention**, not automatically a bespoke-component budget. An ordinary mechanical fixing, framed opening, timber ground or conventional access panel can satisfy them.

## N2 — current non-standard proof families

Two Reference House conditions presently deserve exceptional proof because the current proposition may differ materially from an excellent conventional detail:

1. **P03 — structural floor edge**;
2. **P04 — wall/ceiling movement junction**.

Each already has an engineering/prototype gate and may lose to the conventional comparator.

## N3 — optional challengers not selected

- W2 replaceable architectural lining;
- selective floor access.

These do **not** belong to the current Reference House architecture unless their independent evidence/prototype gates later pass and a real task earns them.

## Novelty-budget rule

For the present Reference House:

> **Do not add another N2/N3 interface family without identifying the task that existing N0/N1 construction cannot meet and applying R8-A proportionality before adoption.**

The count is not a universal maximum of two. It is a project control that prevents interface invention from multiplying silently.

---

# 5. Repeated-cycle versus merely replaceable

A second ambiguity is now removed.

## Repeated-cycle interfaces

These deserve easy ordinary access because intervention is expected during occupation:

- filters;
- fan/plant service items;
- isolators/valves;
- selected service panels;
- routine cleaning/rodding points;
- commonly altered low-level electrical/data routes where adopted.

## Long-cycle replaceable interfaces

These may be replaced once or a few times over a long building life:

- complete window frames;
- doorsets;
- major plant;
- selected roof components;
- large service equipment.

They need a credible removal/reinstatement path but not daily-style accessibility.

## Merely replaceable in principle

Some construction can technically be removed but does not earn special access infrastructure. The fact that an element will someday be demolished or renewed is not enough to make every fixing reversible or every boundary exposed.

This distinction reduces the risk of designing the whole house as a machine-room interior.

---

# 6. Procurement acceptance authority

A critical substitution is not accepted solely because a supplier or contractor calls it “equivalent”.

For any substitution that changes a controlled lifecycle/interface property:

- the **lead designer / architect** owns the architectural equivalence decision;
- the relevant competent specialist owns technical adequacy within their discipline;
- the contractor/supplier provides the product/system evidence and proposed deviation;
- Building Control / statutory approval remains independent where applicable;
- the client may accept cost/programme consequences but does not override unresolved safety/technical duties.

The acceptance record need only be as elaborate as the consequence. A commodity valve replacement does not require a design-panel hearing; a proprietary cassette replacing an intentionally repairable plant/interface family may.

# 7. Degraded-stewardship scenarios

## DS-01 — digital passport is lost

**Scenario:** thirty years after completion, the original digital model, cloud drive and design team are unavailable.

**Required surviving behaviour:**

- principal isolation is physically locatable;
- plant/service geography remains spatially legible;
- critical no-drill / concealed-route information that cannot safely be inferred has a restrained physical record where justified;
- ordinary components can be identified by direct inspection;
- a competent trade can recover the building without reverse-engineering a proprietary digital ecosystem.

**Result:** architecture survives; information quality drops. The building passport is helpful, not existential.

## DS-02 — records are stale after an alteration

**Scenario:** a later owner adds a service and fails to update the digital record.

**Required surviving behaviour:**

- the alteration should remain local rather than forcing unrelated systems to be reinterpreted;
- critical new isolation / safety information belongs with the work, not only in an old central model;
- future trades must treat records as evidence to verify, not unquestionable truth.

**Result:** stewardship failure increases investigation cost but should not cause whole-house opacity.

## DS-03 — future trade ignores the original doctrine

**Scenario:** a competent electrician/plumber/joiner approaches the house with ordinary tools and no knowledge of Ovid’s House.

**Required surviving behaviour:**

- access/release points use recognisable construction logic;
- special hidden clips/keys/software are avoided unless indispensable;
- ordinary service spaces look like service spaces;
- quiet conventional walls/floors behave conventionally;
- non-standard repeated details that remain in the building have already demonstrated representative-installer intelligibility.

**Result:** the building should reward ordinary competence rather than require doctrinal literacy.

## DS-04 — future owner removes a stewardship feature

**Scenario:** labels are painted over, a physical index is lost, or an access cover is cosmetically altered.

**Required surviving behaviour:**

The underlying isolation / service / boundary architecture should still be discoverable and safe to inspect. Where removal creates an actual safety or compliance problem, the feature was not merely enhanced stewardship and must instead be treated as a controlled component with an appropriate duty.

**Result:** distinguish informative overlay from safety-critical construction.

---

# 8. What the correction changes in the book

R8-F must reconcile Part V around this hierarchy:

1. **architecture first:** preserve physical locality, recoverability and ordinary construction logic;
2. **minimum delivery controls second:** activate additional process only where consequence or novelty earns it;
3. **enhanced stewardship third:** valuable, but contingent on future institutions/owners.

In particular:

- “important interface record” must not read as a new bureaucratic schedule for every junction;
- building passport language must not imply that passport continuity is required for the house to remain legible;
- representative-installer trial must remain conditional on repeated non-standard work;
- substitution acceptance must identify lead-designer + specialist authority;
- maintainability commissioning should test selected consequential tasks, not every replaceable component;
- the Reference House should state its current N2 novelty budget rather than merely calling itself conservative.
