# Making and testing the house

The proposals must earn their place through useful architecture, credible construction and proportionate cost. A route drawn on a plan or an access panel on a wall cannot establish that equipment can actually be isolated, disconnected, handled and removed.

## Test the awkward parts

Where an unfamiliar interface or assembly carries material risk, draw it at useful scale, build it, take it apart and record the result. Check tolerances, reachable fixings, handling space, sealing and reinstatement. Test credible failures as well as normal operation, including how damage is contained and the assembly repaired.

Ordinary construction does not need a prototype merely because it appears in this project. Concentrate trials on the claims that justify non-standard work. If an assembly cannot improve on excellent conventional construction in those respects, simplify or reject it.

After construction, verify the promised access, working space, isolation, replacement routes and operational information in the completed house.

## Use computation where it helps

Selected checks can expose inconsistent geometry, invalid crossings, missing relationships and unsupported assumptions. They support drawings and traceable decisions; they do not establish whole-house engineering adequacy, building-physics performance or regulatory compliance. Software earns its place where it catches a real architectural error or makes an obligation easier to inspect.
