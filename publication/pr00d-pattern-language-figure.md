# PR-00D2 — Pattern-Language Figure

**Figure:** `docs/manuscript/figures/pattern-language-fields-sequence.svg`  
**Register ID:** D-F05

**Primary placement:** publication-native Part III, after the language's five fields and before / alongside the service-topology generative sequence.

## Publication purpose

The figure clarifies a distinction that is easy to flatten in prose:

- the language contains **21 active patterns** grouped into five editorial reading fields;
- a **generative sequence** is a separate ordering structure over a relevant subset of those patterns;
- later technical complexity can trigger a **rewind** to an earlier architectural decision;
- strategies and held candidates remain outside the canonical 21-pattern count.

It deliberately does **not** draw an all-pattern dependency graph. The canonical language does not own one complete mandatory graph, and a dense invented network would create false authority rather than useful orientation.

## Publication caption

*The pattern language and its generative sequences are different structures. Twenty-one active patterns are grouped here into five editorial fields for reading; the groups are not a taxonomy. At right, service topology shows one sequence in which high-consequence spatial decisions precede detailed routes, with explicit rewind when later complexity exposes a weak earlier move. Strategies coordinate several patterns; held candidates remain unresolved and outside the canonical count.*

## Does not establish

The figure does not establish:

- one mandatory whole-house pattern order;
- a complete dependency graph among all twenty-one patterns;
- that every project should use every pattern;
- that strategies or held candidates are pattern nodes;
- technical adequacy of any service system selected through the sequence.

Stable `PAT-*` identities are shown in full. Pattern numbers remain identity only; they do not encode importance, sequence or maturity.
